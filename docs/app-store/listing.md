# Atlas: App Store listing

Bundle id `pub.gavin.atlas` · version 1.0 · build 5 · iPad only (`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 2`), so no iPhone screenshots are needed.
Researched 2026-11-09 against Apple's screenshot spec and age-rating definitions (links at the end). Character limits are checked by script; every block below is ready to paste.

Three things need Gavin before submission:

1. **The privacy policy page does not exist yet, and Apple requires one.** See "URLs".
2. **The age rating comes out at 13+, above the 10–12 readership.** See "Age rating".
3. **The app must stay free.** Content is CC BY-NC-SA (`LICENSE-CONTENT`); a paid app or in-app purchase would conflict with the NonCommercial term.

Wording rule used throughout: Guideline 2.3.8 reserves "For Kids" and "For Children" for the Kids Category, so the copy says "upper-primary pupils" and the app is not placed in that category.

The topic count is **61**, as the home page shows (Science 19, Biology 17, History 9, Geography 8, Maths 7, Computing 1). The 94 folders in `src/content/topics` include 33 `sample-*` engine fixtures that readers never see.

### Description

```
Atlas is a visual textbook you can turn, scrub through and take apart. It is written in English and Chinese for upper-primary pupils in Singapore, and for anyone who wants to see how things work.

61 TOPICS ACROSS SIX SUBJECTS
• Science: forces, magnets, circuits, light, heat, sound, states of matter, food webs, the solar system, how planes fly, the Saturn V
• Biology: cells, plants, the heart and lungs, digestion, bones and muscles, eyes and ears, insects, coral reefs
• History: World War I, World War II, the Warring States, Zheng He's voyages, the Industrial Revolution, the Space Race, Singapore from 1819 to today
• Geography: inside the Earth, plates and hazards, monsoons, Southeast Asia, Singapore's land and water
• Maths: fractions, decimals, multiplication and division, area and perimeter, measuring, telling the time, nets and solids
• Computing: inside an AI server

HOW A LESSON WORKS

3D models
Rotate a real-looking model of an air conditioner, a rocket or the human ear. Cut it open, pull it apart or switch to X-ray, then tap any part to find out what it does. Try-it controls let you change one thing and watch what happens.

Maps and timelines
History unfolds on a map, chapter by chapter. Territory, front lines and routes change as you move along the timeline. Maps are drawn from published historical data, and the sources are recorded.

Maths lessons
Each idea is explained first and then done by hand: shade a strip, fold it, cut it, place it on a number line. Every chapter ends with one short check, and each topic finishes with practice.

BUILT TO BE READ
• Switch between English and Chinese on any page
• Chapters are split into short beats that you move through at your own pace
• A presentation mode can read the captions aloud
• Key terms open a glossary, and facts and figures carry their sources
• Paper and dark themes

HISTORY, TOLD PLAINLY
The history topics are written from a global view and do not take sides. They describe war, massacres and bombing factually, with numbers and sources and without graphic images. Parents and teachers may wish to read these chapters with younger readers.

PRIVATE AND OFFLINE
• Everything is on your iPad: works with no internet connection
• No ads
• No accounts or sign-in
• No tracking and no data collection
• No in-app purchases

Atlas is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Ministry of Education, Singapore. Many topics are planned against the primary syllabus, and some go beyond it.
```

### The realistic-violence answer

Apple defines it as "aggressive physical conflict or harm involving humans in lifelike situations", with combat as an example.

**Case for Infrequent (recommended).** Atlas shows no combat scenes, but it does tell readers in plain terms that real people were killed: the Nanjing Massacre, the Holocaust, Sook Ching, Hiroshima, trench warfare. The project's own rule (CLAUDE.md) is not to look away from detail and numbers. That is harm involving humans, and describing it at length is what those chapters are for. It is infrequent across the app because it is concentrated in a handful of the 61 topics.

**Case for None.** Everything is text and maps; there are no characters fighting on screen, and "war" is explicitly listed under the 9+ Mature Themes row. This reading is arguable, but I would not choose it. It would be relying on a loophole to label massacre accounts as containing no violence, and it would not reach 9+ anyway because the opium references still give 13+.

**What 13+ costs.** Pupils aged 10–12 whose parents set a Screen Time limit under 13 will need a parent to allow the app. The listing copy deliberately says what the app contains so parents can decide. Reaching 9+ would mean removing the opium references and softening the atrocity chapters, which conflicts with the content rules, so it is Gavin's call and not something to settle in this form.

## App Privacy

Answer: **Data Not Collected.** In App Store Connect, under App Privacy, choose "No, we do not collect data from this app".

Checked 2026-11-09 at commit `594109ba`:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Analytics or crash SDK names in `src`, `ios/App/App`, `package.json`, `astro.config.mjs` (Google Analytics, gtag, Plausible, PostHog, Mixpanel, Segment, Sentry, Amplitude, Firebase, Umami, Fathom, Hotjar, Clarity) | No matches |
| The same names plus `sendBeacon` and `WebSocket(` in the built `dist/_astro` and `dist/en/index.html` | No matches |
| `XMLHttpRequest`, `sendBeacon`, `new WebSocket`, `EventSource(` in `src` | No matches |
| `fetch(` in `src` | 12 call sites, all loading the app's own bundled data (`/geo/*`, `catalogues/*`, engine JSON) |
| Absolute URLs in `src` | Citations only (Wikimedia, DOI, NLB, NASA and so on), shown as source references and not fetched |
| Native networking in `ios/App/App/*.swift` | No `URLSession`. `AtlasBundleHandler` serves files from the app bundle only |
| Native Content-Security-Policy | `connect-src 'self'`, `frame-src 'none'`, `form-action 'none'`: the web view cannot reach another host |
| Outbound navigation | `AtlasOfflineNavigation` cancels anything outside `capacitor://localhost` |
| Permissions in `Info.plist` | No `*UsageDescription` keys: no camera, microphone, location or photos |
| Capacitor's own `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` | `NSPrivacyTracking` false, collected data types empty |
| Runtime | Headless run of the 8 EN screenshot pages logged no request to any other host |
| Read-aloud | `src/lib/speech.ts` uses the system `speechSynthesis`; no audio is sent by the app |

Stored on the device only, which Apple does not count as collection: `localStorage` for language, theme, autoplay, voice and whether the guide has been seen; `sessionStorage` for panel layout. No answers or progress leave the device, and answers are not put in the URL.

## Export compliance

Answer: **No non-exempt encryption.** `ios/App/App/Info.plist` already sets `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` to `false`, so App Store Connect should not ask per build. If it does ask, choose "None of the algorithms mentioned above". The app makes no network connections, and `crypto.subtle`, CryptoKit and CommonCrypto do not appear in `src` or the app's Swift.
