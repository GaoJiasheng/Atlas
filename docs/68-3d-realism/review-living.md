# 3D model realism review: biology SpaceScene topics (2026-11-02)

Read-only review on a fresh build of `main` (dee51e3). I looked at the shots (`pnpm shoot <slug> --locale en --theme paper`, 1920×1080), one per chapter plus presets and close-up crops, at most 6 per topic. mangrove-food-web was shot with `--beats`. The other topics used the default chapter and preset run because the machine load was about 110. I also read the primitive inventory in each `data/parts.json` and the generator scripts in `scripts/geo/<slug>/`. This review judges form only. Ivory and graphite technical-plate colour is intentional.

Grades: A = convincing, keep · B = good, polish details · C = recognisable but crude, rebuild key parts · D = placeholder, rebuild model.
Priority: 1 = subject of the topic and visibly crude · 3 = minor or secondary.

## Factually misleading shapes (fix even if nothing else is rebuilt)

1. **fossils `myna-leg`**: 3 forward toes only (`dz -0.10, 0, 0.10`). Passerines are anisodactyl, with 3 forward and 1 back. This matters in a chapter that argues birds are dinosaurs, because theropod feet have a hallux too.
2. **fossils `sk-ribs`**: 8 identical hoops all rooted at y = 2.08, not on the vertebrae. Real dorsal ribs articulate with each vertebra and change length front to back. The count and attachment are wrong for any large theropod (about 10–12 dorsal pairs plus gastralia).
3. **fossils `sk-leg-*`**: the hindlimb is fleshy sweeps with no visible femur, tibia, fibula or metatarsal bones, on a model labelled "Direct record / original fossils".
4. **fossils `bone-sample`**: a cartoon dog-bone shape presented as a real body fossil.
5. **coral-reef `monitoring-quadrat`**: reads as an oval ring. Survey quadrats are square frames.
6. **mangrove `api-pencil`**: 6 fat cones. Real Avicennia pneumatophores are slender and number in the dozens to hundreds per tree. With so few, students under-read the "many breathing roots" idea.
7. **frog-and-chicken `chicken-adult`**: no wattles, and the tail is a single flat wedge. The frogs' hind feet lack webbing, which is a key aquatic adaptation and part of what the chapter is teaching.
8. **cells animal cell**: drawn as a perfectly circular flat disc. Cheek cells are irregular flattened polygons; a perfect circle invites the "cells are round" misconception.
9. **frog-and-chicken `chicken-egg`**: visible polygon facets (low `segments`). This is not a factual error, but at the close-up it reads as "low-poly", not "egg".

## Per-part notes

### mangrove-food-web (`scripts/geo/mangrove-food-web/build-parts.ts`)
- `api-tree` and `bakau-tree` (5 sweeps + 12 leaf extrudes each): replace with the tree generator. Avicennia: a short, often leaning trunk, a spreading crown, about 200+ leaves in clusters at the twig ends. Rhizophora: a straight trunk with stilt roots (`bakau-prop`) that arch outwards about 1–2 m before touching the mud, branching once or twice, with lenticels. Leaf count is the cheapest big win: instanced leaves, about 300–600 per tree in one draw call.
- `api-lateral` and `api-pencil`: lateral roots should run just under the mud surface with pencil roots rising every 10–20 cm (instanced, 40–80 visible, 0.008–0.012 m radius at model scale).
- `heron` (5 lathes + 8 sweeps): a faceted egg body (raise lathe segments) on stick legs. Rebuild from the bird rig with a long-neck preset. Legs need a tibiotarsus, a reversed "knee" (the ankle), a scaled tarsus and 4 toes.
- `fiddler-crabs` and `tree-crabs`: the counts are already right (8 walking legs + 2 chelipeds, eye stalks). The carapace is a faceted 6-sided extrude: use a lofted dome with front-wide trapezoid outline. Legs should be 4 segments with a flattened merus, not round sticks. The male claw should be about as long as the carapace is wide.
- `giant-mudskipper` and `small-fish`: cone body with a flat tail plate. Use the fish kit. Mudskipper specifics: eyes on top, a large sail-like first dorsal fin, muscular pectoral "limbs".
- `bacteria-inset` and `fungi-inset`: acceptable as schematic insets, but fungi should be branching hyphae (thin, many branches, with a few fruiting bodies), not 4 thick sticks.

### fossils-and-skeletons (`scripts/geo/fossils-and-skeletons/build-model.ts` lines 66–104)
- `sk-skull`: 2 parallel `extrude` plates (13-point outline, 4 holes) + 2 crossbars + 14 tooth extrudes. It reads as a hollow box. Loft a narrow, deep snout to wide temporal-region skull from about 6 sections, with fenestrae as through-holes and the teeth set in an alveolar margin.
- `sk-neck`, `sk-trunk`, `sk-tail-*`: one sweep each with `rings`. Replace with an instanced vertebra chain: about 10 cervical, 13 dorsal, 5 sacral and about 40 caudal for a tyrannosaurid. Neural spine height should vary along the chain, and the tail needs chevrons.
- `sk-ribs`: 16 tubes from one formula (`width = .38 + sin(...)*.30`, all `y = 2.08`). Root each rib at its vertebra's parapophysis, curve it backwards, and vary length. Add gastralia as a short basket.
- `sk-pelvis`: a flat plate. It needs an ilium blade, a forward pubis with a boot, and a backward ischium.
- `sk-leg-*`: rebuild as bones (femur with head and 4th trochanter, tibia, fibula, 3 long metatarsals, phalanges with claws). `sk-arm-*`: a short humerus, radius, ulna and 2 fingers.
- The sauropod studies (`prince-study` etc., 9 sweeps + sphere + 10 tubes each) can share the skeleton kit with a diplodocid preset. Keep them modest because they sit in the background of chapter 08, but give them real vertebra rhythm and limb bones.
- `myna-body`, `myna-wing`, `myna-leg`: the bird rig (see below). `myna-feather` is acceptable (vane extrude + rachis + barb tubes) but would benefit from an asymmetric vane, which is itself a flight-feather fact.
- `bone-sample`: replace the dog-bone with a partial long bone (broken shaft, a condyle end, mineral texture).
- `track-slab`: the footprint should have 3 toe impressions with claw tips, a heel pad and displacement rims. `leaf-slab`: needs an actual leaf impression (outline + midrib + secondary veins). The current shape is a V-groove.

## Cross-cutting issues

- **Low lathe segment counts** give visible facets: heron body, chicken egg, crab carapace, bird's-nest fern. A one-line win: default lathe `segments` of at least 48, and at least 64 for hero-scale ovoids.
- **Flat extrudes used as organic surfaces** (wings, leaves, tails, fronds, cell discs) are the main "made of primitives" signal. Organic sheets need curvature (loft or a curved panel) and a thickness taper.
- **Sticks for legs** on every vertebrate (heron, myna, hen, theropod feet, crabs). Any limb needs segments, joint bulges and a foot with the correct toe count and arrangement.
- **One identical repeat** (ribs, leaf pairs, pencil roots, tentacles): add per-instance jitter in length, angle and curl, plus a gradient along the series.

## Top 5 worst offenders (fix first)

1. **fossils-and-skeletons theropod** (`sk-*`): box skull, identical rib hoops that do not attach to the spine, fleshy tube legs on a "direct record" fossil.
2. **fossils-and-skeletons myna** (`myna-*`): sphere and egg body, grooved slab wing, plank tail, stick legs with no hind toe.
3. **mangrove-food-web trees and roots** (`api-*`, `bakau-*`): Y-stick trees with 12 giant leaves, 6 fat pencil roots, straight prop roots. These are the habitat-defining subjects.
4. **mangrove-food-web heron and crabs** (`heron`, `*-crabs`): faceted egg body and stick legs; hexagonal crab shells with stick legs.
5. **coral-reef polyp and colonies** (`polyp-*`, `colony-*`): finger tentacles in a white mug with hidden septa; smooth pink domes with no corallites.
