<Lang en>

Start at the skull. A <Term id="joint">joint</Term> is a connection where bones meet; it need not be freely movable. Many skull sutures allow little or no movement. Other connections allow small movements, while the joints of the limbs can allow much more. Looking for a bend is therefore not enough to decide whether a connection is a joint.

The separate specimen shows the shared plan of a synovial joint. Smooth articular <Term id="cartilage">cartilage</Term> covers the facing bone surfaces. A capsule encloses the joint cavity. Within it, <Term id="synovial-fluid">synovial fluid</Term> helps lubricate the surfaces as they move. The transparent capsule is a viewing choice: it lets us inspect a structure that would otherwise hide what is inside.

A <Term id="ligament">ligament</Term> supports the connection between bones and helps limit excess movement. It is not a little muscle that actively pulls the joint into motion. The surrounding tissues together help guide and support movement, so the shape of the bones is only part of the explanation.

At the elbow, compare the band carrying a muscle’s pull. This is a <Term id="tendon">tendon</Term>, which transmits tension from muscle to bone. A tendon and a ligament are both strong connective tissues, but their connections and roles differ. Follow each band to its attachments instead of identifying it only by colour or by its thin shape. The next chapter compares joint shapes; the later elbow chapter follows the pull through a tendon.

<More title={{"en": "Structure and movement", "zh": "结构分类与活动分类"}}>

Fibrous, cartilaginous and synovial describe how a joint is built. Immobile, slightly movable and freely movable describe how much it moves. These are related classifications, not interchangeable lists [S10].

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Teaching transparency", "zh": "教学透明度"}}>

This is a generic joint specimen, not a named ligament or a complete elbow. Its gap, cartilage thickness and transparency are adjusted for reading. Neither the soft layers nor their thickness should be treated as measurements [S11,S34].

</More>

先从颅骨看起。<Term id="joint">关节</Term>是骨相接的地方，不一定都能自由活动。许多颅骨缝几乎不动，有些骨连接只能小幅活动，四肢的一些关节则能动得更多。所以，不能只凭一个地方会不会明显弯曲，判断那里有没有关节。

独立标本展示滑膜关节的共同结构。相对的骨面覆有光滑的关节<Term id="cartilage">软骨</Term>，关节囊把关节腔围起来。腔内的<Term id="synovial-fluid">滑液</Term>有助润滑活动表面。图中把囊画成透明，只是让原本被挡住的内部显出来，不表示真实的囊可以直接看穿。

<Term id="ligament">韧带</Term>支持骨与骨之间的连接，并帮助限制过度活动。它不是会主动收缩、把关节拉动的小肌肉。骨的形状、关节囊及周围组织一起影响活动的方向和稳定程度；单看骨面，还不能解释全部运动。

转到肘部，追踪从肌肉通向骨的条带。这是<Term id="tendon">肌腱</Term>，负责把肌肉张力传给骨。肌腱与韧带都是坚韧的结缔组织，但连接对象和作用不同。辨认时应沿着条带看两端附在哪里，不只看颜色或细长外形。下一章比较关节面形状，之后再跟着肌腱的拉力看肘部动作。

<More title={{"en": "Structure and movement", "zh": "结构分类与活动分类"}}>

纤维性、软骨性、滑膜性描述连接结构；不动、微动、自由活动描述活动程度。两套分类有关联，却不是同一张名单[S10]。

</More>
