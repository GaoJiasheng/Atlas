<Lang en>

Six plants stand beside the tray. All of them are green, and all of them make their own food. The question here is whether a plant bears flowers.

A <Term id="flowering-plant">flowering plant</Term> is a plant that can bear flowers. Its flowers become fruits with seeds inside (<TopicLink slug="plant" ch="flowers-and-pollination">A Plant: flowers</TopicLink>). The ixora is in flower now: each small flower has exactly <Num s="S46">four</Num> petals in a cross, and the flowers grow in clusters.

A flowering plant does not have flowers all the time. The young chilli plant has only leaves so far; when it is older it will flower and then make chillies (<TopicLink slug="plant" ch="seed-to-plant">A Plant: from seed to plant</TopicLink>). So “has flowers now” is the wrong question: it puts the chilli plant with the ferns.

A <Term id="non-flowering-plant">non-flowering plant</Term> never bears flowers. Ferns and mosses are non-flowering plants, and they are green too. The bird’s nest fern and the staghorn fern are native to Singapore and grow on trees, high up on branches and trunks.

Looks can mislead. The rhu tree has thin, drooping green shoots like a pine, but NParks notes that it is a flowering plant and not related to pines at all. Its flowers are tiny, and its fruits form small cone-like clusters.

In the Try it card, sort by different questions and see which one puts the chilli plant with the ixora and the rhu.

<More title={{"en": "Not in flower yet", "zh": "还没开花"}}>

A flowering plant does not have flowers all the time: a young plant may not have flowered yet. A plant with no flowers today may still be a flowering plant. To decide, ask whether it ever bears flowers, not whether it has flowers now.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Spores (Primary 5)", "zh": "孢子（小五）"}}>

On the underside of a mature bird’s nest fern frond are brown, fuzzy-looking lines where the fern makes its spores; one frond of the model shows them. How plants reproduce, by spores or by seeds, is studied in Primary 5. A few non-flowering plants make seeds instead of spores: NParks calls cycads and pines “non-flowering seed plants”.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Plants on plants", "zh": "长在植物上的植物"}}>

The rain tree, a flowering plant, often carries large, rosette-shaped bird’s nest ferns on its branches: a non-flowering plant growing on a flowering one. The staghorn fern also grows on trees, without harming them.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Mosses", "zh": "苔藓"}}>

Mosses are small and low and grow in soft green cushions on stones, soil and bark. They have no true roots: thin threads called rhizoids hold them in place. So the rule “a plant has roots, stems and leaves” does not fit every plant.

</More>

分类盘旁边站着六株植物。它们都是绿色的，都自己制造食物。这一章要问的是：它会不会开花。

<Term id="flowering-plant">开花植物</Term>是会开花的植物。花会变成果实，里面有种子（见<TopicLink slug="plant" ch="flowers-and-pollination">“一株植物”的花一章</TopicLink>）。龙船花正在开花：每一朵小花正好有 <Num s="S46">4</Num> 片花瓣，排成十字，一簇一簇地开。

开花植物不是一直都有花。辣椒幼苗现在只有叶子，长大以后才会开花、结辣椒（见<TopicLink slug="plant" ch="seed-to-plant">“一株植物”的从种子到植物一章</TopicLink>）。所以“现在有没有花”问得不对：它会把辣椒苗和蕨分在一起。

<Term id="non-flowering-plant">不开花植物</Term>从来不开花。蕨和苔藓都是不开花植物，它们也是绿色的。鸟巢蕨和鹿角蕨是新加坡的本地植物，长在树上，高高地挂在树枝和树干上。

样子会骗人。木麻黄长着细长、下垂的绿色枝条，像松树；国家公园局却指出，它是开花植物，和松树毫无关系。它的花很小，果实聚成像小球果一样的一团。

<More title={{"en": "Not in flower yet", "zh": "还没开花"}}>

开花植物不是一直都有花：年幼的植株可能还没开过花。今天没有花的植物，仍可能是开花植物。要判断，就问它“会不会开花”，而不是“现在有没有花”。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Spores (Primary 5)", "zh": "孢子（小五）"}}>

鸟巢蕨成熟的叶子背面，有一条条褐色、毛茸茸的线，蕨就在那里产生孢子；模型上有一片叶子画出了这些线。植物怎样繁殖——用孢子还是用种子——到小五才学。有少数不开花植物结种子而不产孢子：国家公园局把苏铁和松树叫做“不开花的种子植物”。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Plants on plants", "zh": "长在植物上的植物"}}>

雨树是开花植物，枝上常常长着大大的、莲座状的鸟巢蕨：一株不开花植物长在一株开花植物上。鹿角蕨也长在树上，对树没有伤害。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Mosses", "zh": "苔藓"}}>

苔藓又小又矮，在石头、泥土和树皮上长成一团团柔软的绿垫。它们没有真正的根，是叫“假根”的细丝把它们固定住。所以“植物都有根、茎、叶”这条规矩，并不适用于所有植物。

</More>
