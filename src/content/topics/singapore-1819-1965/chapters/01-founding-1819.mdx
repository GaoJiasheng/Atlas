<Lang en>

The British East India Company wanted a port at the southern end of the Straits of Malacca, outside Dutch control, to protect its ships on the way to China, where it bought tea and sold Indian opium. Stamford Raffles, Lieutenant-Governor of Bencoolen in Sumatra, and William Farquhar, the former Resident of Malacca, anchored off St John's Island on 28 January 1819, with about <Num s="S364">120</Num> Indian soldiers. At the river mouth lived Temenggong Abdul Rahman and his followers, many of them Orang Laut. On 30 January he agreed to let the British stay.

The Temenggong could not give land away alone; a treaty needed a sultan. But the throne of Johor–Riau was disputed. The Bugis nobles in Riau, and later the Dutch, backed Abdul Rahman, a younger son of Sultan Mahmud III. Raffles sent for the elder son, Hussein, who was living quietly in Riau, and recognised him as Sultan of Johor.

On 6 February 1819 Raffles, Sultan Hussein and the Temenggong signed a treaty. The Company could build a trading post and would pay Hussein <Num s="S7,S351">5,000</Num> and the Temenggong <Num s="S7,S351">3,000</Num> Spanish dollars a year. The island was not yet British. The Dutch protested, and the governments in Calcutta and London were uneasy about the quarrel.

Farquhar stayed as Resident. Traders came from Malacca and Riau: Malays, Bugis, Chinese and Indians. With little money from the Company, Farquhar let the town grow quickly and raised money by selling licences to sell opium and spirits and to run gambling. Raffles, who wanted an orderly town, later objected, and in 1823 removed him.

<More title={{"en":"Two sultans","zh":"两个苏丹"}}>

In the Malay world a ruler needed the royal regalia, the drums, seals and other objects of office, to be installed. They were held by Engku Puteri Hamidah, Sultan Mahmud's widow, who refused to give them to Hussein. To the Riau court, Hussein was a sultan made by the British; to the British, the Riau sultan was a puppet of the Bugis and the Dutch. In 1822 Dutch troops took the regalia from Engku Puteri by force, and in November 1823 Abdul Rahman was formally installed with them. Each side had its own sultan, and each pointed to the other's as proof of foreign meddling.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The Temenggong and the sea people","zh":"天猛公与海人"}}>

The Temenggong was the Johor official in charge of order and of the sea peoples. Abdul Rahman had moved from Riau to the river mouth shortly before 1819, after a Dutch treaty with Johor cut his power and income. He governed settlements of Orang Laut, Orang Seletar and Orang Kallang, sea people who lived on boats. When the British arrived, it was the Temenggong who met them and agreed first, on condition that Hussein approved. By 1824 his following numbered between <Num s="S373">6,000 and 10,000</Num>. He moved to Telok Blangah, where his grandson Abu Bakar was born; Abu Bakar later became Sultan of Johor.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Abdullah, a witness with a view","zh":"阿都拉：有立场的见证人"}}>

Abdullah bin Abdul Kadir, a writer and teacher from Malacca, worked for Raffles as a scribe and interpreter and later settled in Singapore. His memoir, the Hikayat Abdullah, written in Malay in 1840–43, is one of the few accounts of early Singapore by someone who lived there and was not British. He was not present in January 1819 and pieced the landing together from others. He admired the British and was harsh about Malay rulers, and some later critics have called him pro-British. Like the British officials' letters, his book is evidence of what one man saw and believed.

</More>

<Lang zh>

英国东印度公司想在马六甲海峡南端找一个荷兰人管不到的港口，保护开往中国的商船；公司在中国买茶叶，卖印度鸦片。1819 年 1 月 28 日，苏门答腊明古连副总督莱佛士和前马六甲驻扎官法夸尔，带着约 <Num s="S364">120</Num> 名印度兵，在圣约翰岛外停泊。河口住着天猛公阿都拉曼和他的随从，其中许多是海人。1 月 30 日，天猛公同意让英国人留下。

天猛公无权独自出让土地，订约需要一位苏丹。可是柔佛—廖内的王位有争议：廖内的武吉斯贵族，以及后来的荷兰人，拥立苏丹马末沙三世的幼子阿都拉曼。莱佛士派人把在廖内过着平淡日子的长子胡先接来，承认他为柔佛苏丹。

1819 年 2 月 6 日，莱佛士、苏丹胡先和天猛公签约：公司可以兴建商站，每年付给胡先 <Num s="S7,S351">5,000</Num> 西班牙银元、天猛公 <Num s="S7,S351">3,000</Num> 西班牙银元。这时岛还不是英国的。荷兰提出抗议，加尔各答和伦敦的政府对这场争执也心存疑虑。

法夸尔留下来当驻扎官。商人从马六甲和廖内迁来，有马来人、武吉斯人、华人和印度人。公司拨款很少，法夸尔让城镇迅速发展，靠出卖鸦片、酒和赌博的执照筹钱。想要一座井然有序的城市的莱佛士后来表示反对，1823 年把他撤换。

在马来世界，统治者要有王室信物（鼓、印玺等象征王权的器物）才能登基。这些信物在苏丹马末的遗孀恩古·布特丽·哈米达手里，她不肯交给胡先。在廖内朝廷看来，胡先是英国人立的苏丹；在英国人看来，廖内的苏丹是武吉斯人和荷兰人的傀儡。1822 年，荷兰军队从恩古·布特丽手中强行夺走信物；1823 年 11 月，阿都拉曼凭这些信物正式登基。双方各有自己的苏丹，又各自指着对方的苏丹说是外国插手的证据。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The Temenggong and the sea people","zh":"天猛公与海人"}}>

天猛公是柔佛负责治安、管辖海人的官员。阿都拉曼在 1819 年前不久才从廖内迁到河口，因为荷兰人与柔佛订的一份条约削弱了他的权力和收入。他管辖着几处海人、实里达人和加冷人的聚落，这些人都以船为家。英国人来的时候，迎接他们、最先同意的，都是天猛公，条件是要胡先认可。到 1824 年，他的追随者有 <Num s="S373">6,000 到 10,000</Num> 人。他后来迁到直落布兰雅，孙子阿布巴卡就在那里出生；阿布巴卡后来成为柔佛苏丹。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Abdullah, a witness with a view","zh":"阿都拉：有立场的见证人"}}>

马六甲出身的文人和教师阿都拉（文西阿都拉），曾替莱佛士当文书和翻译，后来在新加坡定居。他在 1840 年至 1843 年用马来文写成的《阿都拉传》，是少有的由住在本地、又不是英国人的作者写下的早期新加坡记录。1819 年 1 月他并不在场，登陆的经过是听别人说后拼凑出来的。他钦佩英国人，对马来统治者却很严厉，后来有评论者说他亲英。和英国官员的书信一样，这本书记下的是一个人看到和相信的东西。

</More>
