<Lang en>

David Marshall's government sought greater authority through constitutional negotiations in London. The talks of 1956 failed, and Marshall resigned. Lim Yew Hock succeeded him. Negotiations continued alongside strikes, student protest and government action against organisations described as communist-influenced. These disputes concerned both the end of colonial rule and who would hold power after it.

In October 1956 Chinese-school students stayed in their schools after government action against organisations and student leaders. Police clearance was followed by wider disturbances. <Num s="S155">13</Num> people were killed. The government described its actions as necessary for security. Students and their supporters described restrictions on education, association and political participation. Counting the dead does not settle these competing explanations. It keeps the people affected visible alongside constitutional conferences.

On 31 August 1957 the Federation of Malaya became independent under Tunku Abdul Rahman. Singapore remained under British rule. Changes to citizenship and the constitution broadened participation in its political system. In the election of 30 May 1959 the People's Action Party won <Num s="S190">43</Num> of the <Num s="S190">51</Num> seats. The elected government could now claim a different mandate from the earlier limited councils.

Singapore became a self-governing state on 3 June. Lee Kuan Yew and his ministers were sworn in on 5 June. <Term id="self-rule">Self-government</Term> gave the elected ministry responsibility for domestic affairs while Britain retained important responsibilities, including defence and external relations. In December Yusof Ishak became head of state. The changes were substantial, but Singapore's relationship with the federation and the future of colonial bases remained unresolved.
