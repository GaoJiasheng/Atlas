On 27 May 1961 Tunku Abdul Rahman, Malaya's Prime Minister, proposed joining Malaya, Singapore and the British territories in northern Borneo. Each side had its reasons. Lee Kuan Yew's government wanted <Term id="merger-1963">merger</Term> to give Singapore a wider market and to end colonial rule. The Tunku feared a left-wing Singapore on his border, and adding Borneo kept the Malays and other indigenous peoples a majority. Britain wanted to leave its colonies while keeping its bases.

The plan split the People's Action Party. In July 1961 its left wing broke away and formed the Barisan Sosialis, which opposed these terms of merger. On 1 September 1962 voters were offered three ways of merging but no way to reject merger. About <Num s="S193">71</Num> percent chose the government's option, and about <Num s="S193">a quarter</Num> cast blank votes, as the Barisan had urged.

On 2 February 1963, in Operation Coldstore, the Internal Security Council ordered the arrest of opposition politicians, unionists and students: <Num s="S337">107</Num> were arrested that night, and later counts reach <Num s="S338">113</Num>. They were held without trial. The government said they were part of a communist plot. Detainees and some historians say the arrests removed the strongest opponents of the government and of merger. The two accounts are given below.

Sarawak and North Borneo had their own debates; Brunei, after an armed revolt in December 1962, stayed out. In January 1963 Indonesia announced its <Term id="konfrontasi">Confrontation</Term> against the new federation, and the Philippines claimed North Borneo. Malaysia was formed on 16 September 1963. Five days later the People's Action Party won <Num s="S340">37</Num> of Singapore's <Num s="S340">51</Num> seats, and the Barisan <Num s="S340">13</Num>.

<More title={{"en":"Coldstore: two accounts","zh":"冷藏行动：两种说法"}}>

The Singapore and Malayan governments, and the British, said that the detainees were part of a communist “united front” planning to seize power through the Barisan Sosialis and the unions, and that the revolt in Brunei showed the danger was real. A minister restated this view in 2014, citing documents released later.

Detainees such as Lim Chin Siong, and historians such as Thum Ping Tjin, argue that British files show the security case was thin, and that the arrests crippled the main opposition party before the 1963 election. Others, such as Kumar Ramakrishna, defend the original security case. The counts differ with the day and the list: <Num s="S337">107</Num> arrested that night, <Num s="S186">110</Num> by 12 February according to the British minister, <Num s="S338">113</Num> in another account.

<More title={{"en":"Three ways to say yes","zh":"三种“赞成”"}}>

Option A was the government's plan: Singapore would keep control of education, labour and some other matters, and Singapore citizens would be citizens of Malaysia. Option B was full merger as a state like the other eleven; option C was entry on terms no less favourable than those for the Borneo territories. David Marshall said voters were not allowed to answer yes or no: “You must say yes.” There was no option to reject merger, and the government added a rule that unmarked or uncertain ballots would follow the choice of the majority in the Assembly. The Barisan Sosialis called for blank votes in protest. The count was <Num s="S336">397,626</Num> for A, <Num s="S336">9,422</Num> for B, <Num s="S336">7,911</Num> for C and <Num s="S336,S192">144,077</Num> blank.

<More title={{"en":"Borneo and Brunei","zh":"婆罗洲与文莱"}}>

A commission led by Lord Cobbold visited Sarawak and North Borneo in 1962 and reported that about a third of the people strongly favoured Malaysia, another third would accept it with safeguards, and the remaining third were divided between those who wanted independence first and those who wanted British rule to continue. Brunei's revolt began on 8 December 1962 and was put down by British troops; the Sultan of Brunei then chose not to join. Malaysia was first meant to begin on 31 August 1963, but was moved to 16 September to allow a United Nations mission to survey opinion in Borneo.

1961 年 5 月 27 日，马来亚首相东姑阿都拉曼提议，把马来亚、新加坡和英国在婆罗洲北部的属地合在一起。各方各有理由。李光耀政府希望通过<Term id="merger-1963">合并</Term>为新加坡争取更大的市场，并结束殖民统治。东姑担心身边出现一个左翼的新加坡；加上婆罗洲，马来人和其他原住民仍会占多数。英国则想撤出殖民地，同时保住军事基地。

这个计划让人民行动党分裂。1961 年 7 月，党内左翼另组社会主义阵线，反对这样的合并条件。1962 年 9 月 1 日的公投给选民三种合并方式，却没有“反对合并”的选项。约 <Num s="S193">71%</Num> 的人选了政府的方案，约<Num s="S193">四分之一</Num>的人按照社阵的号召投了空白票。

1963 年 2 月 2 日，内部安全理事会下令展开“冷藏行动”，逮捕反对党人士、工会人士和学生：当晚逮捕 <Num s="S337">107</Num> 人，后来的统计达到 <Num s="S338">113</Num> 人。他们未经审判就被关押。政府说，他们是共产党阴谋的一部分；被扣押者和一些史家则说，这次逮捕清除了政府和合并的最强对手。两种说法见下面的细看。

砂拉越和北婆罗洲有各自的争论；文莱在 1962 年 12 月发生武装起事之后，没有加入。1963 年 1 月，印度尼西亚宣布对新联邦采取<Term id="konfrontasi">对抗</Term>政策，菲律宾则声索北婆罗洲。1963 年 9 月 16 日，马来西亚成立。五天后的新加坡大选，人民行动党在 <Num s="S340">51</Num> 席中赢得 <Num s="S340">37</Num> 席，社阵赢得 <Num s="S340">13</Num> 席。

新加坡、马来亚两地政府和英国当时说，被扣押的人属于共产党的“统一战线”，打算通过社阵和工会夺取政权；文莱起事说明危险是真的。2014 年，一位新加坡部长引用后来公开的文件，重申了这个看法。

被扣押者如林清祥，以及史家如覃炳鑫则认为，英国档案显示安全理由很薄弱，逮捕是在 1963 年大选前打垮最大的反对党。也有学者如古玛·拉玛克里斯南为当年的安全理由辩护。人数随日期和名单而不同：当晚逮捕 <Num s="S337">107</Num> 人；英国部长说到 2 月 12 日共 <Num s="S186">110</Num> 人；另一记载是 <Num s="S338">113</Num> 人。

A 方案是政府的方案：新加坡在教育、劳工等事务上保有自主权，新加坡公民就是马来西亚公民。B 方案是像其他十一个州一样完全合并；C 方案是以不逊于婆罗洲各邦的条件加入。马绍尔说，选民不能回答“是”或“否”，“只能说‘是’”。没有“反对合并”的选项，政府还加了一条规定：没有圈选或无法辨认的选票，算作跟随立法议院多数的选择。社阵号召投空白票表示抗议。计票结果是：A 方案 <Num s="S336">397,626</Num> 票，B 方案 <Num s="S336">9,422</Num> 票，C 方案 <Num s="S336">7,911</Num> 票，空白票 <Num s="S336,S192">144,077</Num> 张。

1962 年，由科博德勋爵率领的调查委员会走访砂拉越和北婆罗洲，报告说，当地约三分之一的人大力支持马来西亚，另有三分之一要求有保障才接受，其余三分之一分成两派：一派坚持先独立，一派希望英国继续统治。文莱起事在 1962 年 12 月 8 日爆发，被英军镇压；文莱苏丹随后决定不加入。马来西亚原定 1963 年 8 月 31 日成立，为了让联合国调查团了解婆罗洲的民意，改到 9 月 16 日。
