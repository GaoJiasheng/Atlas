<Lang en>

Most newcomers were Chinese, from Fujian and Guangdong: Hokkien, Teochew, Cantonese, Hakka and Hainanese speakers leaving poor harvests, crowded land and war. Merchants came with capital or relatives. Many labourers came under the <Term id="credit-ticket">credit-ticket system</Term>: a broker paid the passage, and the newcomer worked off the debt for an employer who bought it. Brokers lied, locked men in lodging houses and sold them on. Such workers were called “coolies”, a word now seen as insulting; this topic uses <Term id="coolie">coolie</Term> only when quoting the time.

From India came Tamil and Chulia traders, Sikh policemen and watchmen, moneylenders and labourers. Not all came by choice: from 1825 the British shipped convicts from India and Bencoolen, who built roads, bridges and public buildings in chains. Later, plantation workers were recruited through the <Term id="kangany">kangany system</Term>.

From the archipelago came Malays, Bugis, Javanese and Boyanese; many Muslims also passed through on the pilgrimage to Mecca. Hadhrami Arab families such as the Aljunieds and Alsagoffs became landowners and benefactors. Armenians, Jews, Europeans and Eurasians had their own churches and synagogues, and <Term id="peranakan">Peranakan</Term> families, born in the Straits, mixed Malay and Chinese ways.

Most migrants were young men, and the few women had fewer choices; some were trafficked into prostitution. In 1877, after years of complaints, the government set up the Chinese Protectorate under William Pickering to register brokers and lodging houses and to protect newcomers.

<More title={{"en":"The price of a passage","zh":"一张船票的代价"}}>

A credit-ticket labourer, the “sinkeh” or new arrival, owed his fare and his keep to the broker before he earned anything, and worked for whoever bought his debt until it was paid off, often on plantations. Men not yet sold were kept in crowded, filthy depots, and in the 1870s and 1880s some were shipped on to Sumatra against their will, where brokers got a higher price. Many turned to opium and gambling to bear the work. The Protectorate cut some of the abuse, but employers and brokers kept their hold over men in debt.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"A town built by convicts","zh":"罪犯修的城"}}>

The first Indian convicts arrived in April 1825; the sources disagree on whether it was the 18th or the 25th. Convicts cleared land, drained swamps and built roads, bridges, St Andrew's Cathedral and Government House. By early 1857 there were nearly <Num s="S367">3,000</Num> convicts in Singapore. Some learned trades and stayed after their sentences. Singapore stopped being a penal station when it became a Crown Colony in 1867, and the last Indian convicts were shipped to the Andaman Islands in May 1873.

</More>

<Lang zh>

新来的人大多是华人，来自福建和广东，讲福建话、潮州话、广府话、客家话和海南话，为了逃离歉收、人多地少和战乱而离乡。商人带着本钱或投靠亲戚而来。许多劳工靠<Term id="credit-ticket">赊单制</Term>南来：客头先垫付船费，新客上岸后被雇主买下这笔债，以做工抵债。客头常常行骗，把人关在客馆里，再转卖出去。这样的劳工当时被称为“苦力”，这个词今天带有贬义，本主题只在引述当时的说法时使用<Term id="coolie">苦力</Term>一词。

从印度来的有泰米尔和注辇商人、锡克籍警察和看守、放贷人和劳工。并不是人人都自愿来：1825 年起，英国人把印度和明古连的罪犯押到新加坡，他们戴着镣铐修路、架桥、盖公共建筑。后来，种植园劳工又通过<Term id="kangany">工头制</Term>招募而来。

从马来群岛来的有马来人、武吉斯人、爪哇人和巴韦安人；许多穆斯林去麦加朝圣也在这里中转。阿裕尼、阿尔沙哥夫等哈德拉毛阿拉伯家族成了大地主和慈善家。亚米尼亚人、犹太人、欧洲人和欧亚裔各有自己的教堂和会堂；在海峡出生的<Term id="peranakan">土生华人</Term>家族，融合了马来和华人的习俗。

移民大多是年轻男子，少数女性的处境更难，有的被拐卖为娼。1877 年，在多年的投诉之后，政府设立华民护卫司，由毕麒麟主持，登记客头和客馆，保护新来的移民。

<More title={{"en":"The price of a passage","zh":"一张船票的代价"}}>

赊单南来的“新客”，还没赚到一分钱，就先欠了客头的船费和食宿费，要替买下这笔债的雇主做工，直到还清为止，多半是在种植园。还没被“卖”出去的人，被关在拥挤肮脏的客馆里；1870、1880 年代，有些人被强行转运到苏门答腊，因为那里的“价钱”更高。许多人靠鸦片和赌博熬过苦工。华民护卫司减少了一些弊端，但雇主和客头仍然牢牢控制着欠债的人。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"A town built by convicts","zh":"罪犯修的城"}}>

第一批印度罪犯在 1825 年 4 月到达，到底是 18 日还是 25 日，记载不一。罪犯开荒、排干沼泽，修建道路、桥梁、圣安德烈座堂和总督府。到 1857 年初，新加坡有将近 <Num s="S367">3,000</Num> 名罪犯。有些人学会了手艺，刑满后留了下来。1867 年改为直辖殖民地后，新加坡不再接收罪犯；1873 年 5 月，最后一批印度罪犯被送往安达曼群岛。

</More>
