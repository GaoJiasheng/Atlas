<Lang en>

The British returned in September 1945 to a hungry, damaged city and ran it under a military administration until March 1946. Food was short, prices were high and the black market thrived. Not everyone saw the return the same way: many were relieved the occupation was over; others, after watching the colonial power lose in 1942, now wanted to govern themselves.

On 1 April 1946 Singapore became a separate Crown Colony, while the peninsula's states, Penang and Malacca were joined in the Malayan Union. Malays opposed the Union, which weakened the sultans and opened citizenship to many non-Malays; the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO) was founded in May 1946 to fight it, and in 1948 it was replaced by the Federation of Malaya. Singapore stayed outside: Britain wanted to keep its port and bases, and Malay leaders did not want its large Chinese population in the federation.

Singapore's first election, in March 1948, filled <Num s="S189">six</Num> seats. In June 1948 the Malayan Communist Party took up arms against British rule, and an Emergency was declared in Malaya and then Singapore. The colonial government used detention without trial, bans and searches against suspected supporters; in Malaya, hundreds of thousands of rural Chinese were moved into guarded “new villages”.

In December 1950 a court case over the custody of Maria Hertogh led to riots in which <Num s="S151">18</Num> people died and <Num s="S151">173</Num> were injured. In 1955 a new constitution brought a mostly elected assembly, and David Marshall of the Labour Front became Chief Minister. Weeks later a strike at the Hock Lee bus company became a riot in which <Num s="S153">four</Num> people died.

<More title={{"en":"Maria Hertogh: the case and the riot","zh":"玛丽亚·赫托：官司与暴乱"}}>

Maria Hertogh was born in Java to Dutch Catholic parents and was raised during the war by a Malay Muslim woman, Che Aminah, as her daughter Nadra. In 1950 her Dutch parents went to court to get her back; the court also ruled invalid her marriage, at 13, to a Malay teacher. Newspaper reports and photographs of her in a Catholic convent angered many Muslims. On 11 December, when an appeal failed, a crowd outside the Supreme Court attacked Europeans and Eurasians in the streets. Police and then troops opened fire. Of the <Num s="S151">18</Num> dead, some were people attacked by rioters and some were rioters shot by the security forces. The government arrested <Num s="S151">1,168</Num> people for rioting and held <Num s="S151">about 500</Num> more under emergency laws. A commission of inquiry was set up in 1951.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Hock Lee: four who died","zh":"福利巴士：四个死者"}}>

Workers of the Hock Lee Amalgamated Bus Company, members of a new left-wing union, went on strike in April 1955 after the company sacked union members; Chinese middle-school students joined their pickets. On 12 May police tried to break up the picket at the depot, and the crowd fought back. Corporal Andrew Teo Bok Lan of the Volunteer Special Constabulary was beaten to death; Detective Corporal Yuen Yau Pang died of his injuries; Gene Symonds, an American correspondent, was beaten and died; and Chong Lon Chong, a 16-year-old student, was shot and carried through the streets for hours before reaching hospital. Who fired the shot was never established. <Num s="S153">31</Num> people were injured. The government blamed communist agitators; the unions and students said they were fighting for workers' rights.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The Emergency, three ways","zh":"紧急状态的三种说法"}}>

To the British it was a campaign against communist terror. To the Malayan Communist Party, many of whose members had fought the Japanese, it was a war for independence. To many people in the villages, it meant guerrilla killings, army searches, curfews and forced resettlement. In Singapore it brought bans on many organisations and detentions without trial. It was declared on 18 June 1948 in Malaya and on 24 June in Singapore, and ended in 1960.

</More>

<Lang zh>

1945 年 9 月，英国人回到一座饥饿、残破的城市，用军政府管理到 1946 年 3 月。粮食短缺，物价高涨，黑市兴旺。人们对英国人回来的看法并不一样：许多人庆幸占领结束了；也有人在 1942 年亲眼看到殖民政府战败，现在想自己管理自己。

1946 年 4 月 1 日，新加坡成为单独的直辖殖民地，半岛各州与槟城、马六甲组成马来亚联邦。马来人反对马来亚联邦，因为它削弱苏丹的地位，又让许多非马来人取得公民权；1946 年 5 月，马来民族统一机构（巫统）成立，带头反对。1948 年，马来亚联邦被马来亚联合邦取代。新加坡留在外面：英国要保住这里的港口和基地，马来领袖也不愿把新加坡众多的华人纳入联合邦。

1948 年 3 月，新加坡举行第一次选举，只选出 <Num s="S189">6</Num> 个议席。同年 6 月，马来亚共产党拿起武器反抗英国统治，马来亚和新加坡先后宣布进入紧急状态。殖民政府对疑似支持者实行不经审判的扣押、查禁和搜查；在马来亚，几十万农村华人被迁进有人看守的“新村”。

1950 年 12 月，一宗玛丽亚·赫托监护权的官司引发暴乱，<Num s="S151">18</Num> 人死亡，<Num s="S151">173</Num> 人受伤。1955 年新宪制实施，立法议院大部分议席改为民选，劳工阵线的马绍尔出任首席部长。几个星期后，福利巴士公司的罢工演变成暴乱，<Num s="S153">4</Num> 人死亡。

<More title={{"en":"Maria Hertogh: the case and the riot","zh":"玛丽亚·赫托：官司与暴乱"}}>

玛丽亚·赫托在爪哇出生，父母是信天主教的荷兰人；战争期间，她由马来穆斯林妇女车·阿米娜抚养，名叫娜迪拉。1950 年，她的荷兰父母打官司要回女儿；法庭还裁定她 13 岁时与一名马来教师的婚姻无效。报章报道她住进天主教修道院，还刊登照片，激怒了许多穆斯林。12 月 11 日上诉失败，最高法院外的人群在街上袭击欧洲人和欧亚裔。警察和随后出动的军队开枪。<Num s="S151">18</Num> 名死者中，有被暴乱者袭击的人，也有被军警击毙的暴乱者。政府以暴乱罪逮捕了 <Num s="S151">1,168</Num> 人，另有<Num s="S151">约 500</Num> 人依紧急法令被扣押。1951 年，政府成立调查委员会调查这次暴乱。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Hock Lee: four who died","zh":"福利巴士：四个死者"}}>

福利联合巴士公司开除工会会员后，1955 年 4 月，加入新成立的左翼工会的工人开始罢工；华文中学的学生也来到纠察线声援。5 月 12 日，警察在车厂外驱散纠察线，人群还手。志愿特警队下士 Andrew Teo Bok Lan 被打死；便衣警探下士 Yuen Yau Pang 伤重不治；美国记者 Gene Symonds 被殴打致死；16 岁的学生 Chong Lon Chong 中枪后，被人抬着游行了几个小时才送医。是谁开的枪，始终没有查明。<Num s="S153">31</Num> 人受伤。政府指责共产党煽动；工会和学生则说，他们是在争取工人的权益。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The Emergency, three ways","zh":"紧急状态的三种说法"}}>

在英国人眼里，这是对付共产党恐怖活动的战役。在马来亚共产党看来，许多党员曾经抗日，这是一场争取独立的战争。对许多村民来说，它意味着游击队的杀戮、军队的搜查、宵禁和被迫搬迁。在新加坡，许多团体被查禁，许多人未经审判被扣押。紧急状态 1948 年 6 月 18 日在马来亚、6 月 24 日在新加坡宣布，1960 年结束。

</More>
