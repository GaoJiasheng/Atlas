<Lang en>

When Raffles returned in October 1822, he challenged the way Farquhar had allowed the settlement to develop. A Town Committee received instructions to reorganise the town. Lieutenant Philip Jackson prepared a plan showing government land, commercial streets and areas assigned to different communities. The plan placed European uses north of the river and the Chinese <Term id="kampong">kampong</Term> mainly to its south. Other labelled areas included Chulia, Bugis and Arab settlements and the Sultan's Kampong Glam.

This was a colonial scheme for allocating space by community. It could support familiar religious, trading and family networks, while making residents easier to classify and administer. A coloured area on the plan does not establish that everyone living there belonged to a single group. Residents crossed these boundaries, and the developing town did not reproduce every part of the proposal.

Land itself was being changed. Earth from a small hill was used to fill swampy ground on the south bank. Streets and commercial land replaced parts of the earlier waterfront. The historical shoreline is therefore essential to reading the plan: modern reclaimed ground cannot be treated as land that residents could use in 1822.

Agreements also changed authority. A convention in June 1823 reduced the Malay rulers' powers in the settlement. Britain and the Netherlands signed their treaty in London on 17 March 1824, exchanging claims and settlements without Malay rulers participating in the negotiations. On 2 August Crawfurd concluded a further treaty with Hussein and the Temenggong, transferring Singapore and nearby islands to the Company. Town planning and treaty making were connected ways of extending colonial control.

The Jackson Plan describes intended uses and allocations. Its legend must be read in its original wording. The town divisions on this topic's map represent that proposal rather than a census, a modern ethnic boundary or proof that each division was implemented exactly.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"What changed in 1824","zh":"【待译】What changed in 1824"}}>

The Anglo-Dutch Treaty transferred Malacca to Britain and Bencoolen to the Netherlands. The agreement helped separate British and Dutch spheres in the Malay world. The Crawfurd treaty was a different instrument signed with Hussein and the Temenggong. Its island cession should not be silently substituted for the more limited trading-post agreement of 1819.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"A committee and an instruction letter","zh":"【待译】A committee and an instruction letter"}}>

The surviving document dated 4 November 1822 contains Raffles's instructions to the Town Committee. The Roots collection description dates the committee's appointment to October. The instruction letter is therefore dated separately from the committee’s appointment.

</More>
