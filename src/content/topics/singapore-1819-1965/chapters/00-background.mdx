<Lang en>

Singapore stands beside sea passages linking the Indian Ocean with the South China Sea. Seasonal monsoon winds helped sailing ships travel between India, the Malay world and China. The island's history begins long before the British trading post. Excavations on Fort Canning Hill have uncovered Chinese ceramics, glass and other objects from a settlement connected to overseas trade. These objects show contact and exchange; they cannot tell the names or languages of everyone who lived here.

Chinese and Javanese writings describe places called Danmaxi and Tumasik. The Malay Annals tell a different kind of history, mixing royal genealogies, remembered events and stories. Its account of Sang Nila Utama seeing a lion and naming Singapura is a legend. Archaeological objects, royal stories and visitors' reports answer different questions and should be read alongside one another.

After the Portuguese took Malacca in 1511, rulers claiming the Malacca inheritance established centres around Johor and the Riau islands. Their authority depended on alliances as well as land. The <Term id="orang-laut">Orang Laut</Term>, maritime communities living along coasts and on boats, helped Malay rulers navigate and patrol the straits. The <Term id="temenggong">Temenggong</Term> was an official whose relationships with these communities mattered to the government of the coast.

By 1819 British officials, Dutch officials and rival Malay royal claimants were making competing agreements. Sultan Hussein and Sultan Abdul Rahman did not represent an empty island waiting for an owner. The trading post was established within this existing political world. On the map, colour identifies administrations and planned town divisions; it does not show the identity or allegiance of every resident.

Excavations at Fort Canning began in 1984. The Singapore Stone was a rock with an inscription near the river mouth; much of it was destroyed in 1843. A surviving fragment is held by the National Museum. Neither the inscription nor the objects establish a complete list of rulers or inhabitants.
