<Lang en>

In 221 BCE the King of Qin took a new title, huangdi, "emperor", and called himself the First Emperor. A minister proposed giving the conquered lands to the emperor's sons; Li Si, Lǐ Sī 李斯, argued that this would only bring back the wars, and the empire was divided into <Num s="S150">36</Num> commanderies run by appointed officials. Weapons were melted down, and <Num s="S150">120,000</Num> rich families were moved to the capital, Xianyang. Writing, weights, measures and the width of cart axles were made the same everywhere.

The empire went on fighting and building. In 214 BCE Meng Tian, Méng Tián 蒙恬, drove the Xiongnu from the Yellow River bend and built walls along the new frontier, while other armies conquered Lingnan in the far south. Roads ran out from Xianyang. According to the Shiji, more than <Num s="S150">700,000</Num> convicts were put to work on the emperor's palace and tomb.

In 213 BCE, on Li Si's advice, the emperor ordered private copies of the old histories and classics burned. In 212 BCE more than <Num s="S150">460</Num> scholars in Xianyang were killed. In 210 BCE the emperor died on a tour of the east. A year later two conscript leaders, Chen Sheng, Chén Shèng 陈胜, and Wu Guang, Wú Guǎng 吴广, rose in revolt, and rebellion spread through the old states. In 206 BCE the last Qin ruler, Ziying, surrendered.

Han writers condemned Qin; Jia Yi, Jiǎ Yì 贾谊, said it fell because it ruled by force once the fighting was over. Yet the Han dynasty kept Qin's commanderies, laws and registers, and later dynasties ruled China in much the same way. Whether the conquest was worth its cost is still argued.

<More title={{"en": "The books and the scholars", "zh": "焚书与坑杀诸生"}}>

The edict of 213 BCE spared copies held by court scholars and books on medicine, divination and farming. In 212 BCE, after two magicians who had served the emperor criticised him and fled, he had the scholars of Xianyang questioned, and the Shiji says more than 460 were "buried at Xianyang". It calls them zhusheng, "the scholars", and in another chapter "magicians"; only the emperor's son Fusu says they "all recite and follow Confucius".

Some historians, such as Li Kaiyuan, argue that the famous phrase "burning the books and burying the Confucians" was shaped by Han writers, and that the killing was aimed at the emperor's own magicians. Others defend the Shiji's account. The four-character phrase itself appears only centuries later.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Names on broken tiles", "zh": "残瓦上的名字"}}>

West of the First Emperor's tomb, at Zhaobeihu village, archaeologists in 1979 and 1980 located <Num s="S237">114</Num> graves of the people who built it and excavated <Num s="S237">32</Num>. Most of the dead were men aged twenty to thirty, buried without coffins, some with wounds. On <Num s="S237">18</Num> pieces of tile, someone scratched the names of <Num s="S237">19</Num> of them, with their home counties and ranks. One reads: "Dongwu, working off a debt, rank shangzao, Qingji". Qingji came from Dongwu, in the old state of Qi; the others came from Qi, Lu, Chu and the former Jin states.

</More>

Qin's governor of Shu, Li Bing, Lǐ Bīng 李冰, built the Dujiangyan works in the third century BCE to split the Min River and water the Chengdu plain; they still irrigate it today. The story of Meng Jiangnü, whose weeping for her husband, a wall labourer, brought down part of the Great Wall, grew over many centuries from a much older tale; it is a legend, not a record of Qin.

前221年，秦王改称“皇帝”，自号始皇帝。有大臣提议把被兼并的土地分封给皇子，李斯反对，认为分封只会重新引起战争。于是全国分为<Num s="S150">36</Num>郡，由朝廷任命的官吏治理。各地兵器被收缴熔化，<Num s="S150">12万</Num>户豪富人家被迁到都城咸阳。文字、度量衡和车轨宽度，都统一成一种标准。

帝国仍在不停地打仗和修建。前214年，蒙恬把匈奴赶出黄河河套，沿新边界修筑长城；另一路秦军攻取了遥远的岭南。一条条驰道从咸阳通向各地。据《史记》，修建宫殿和陵墓的刑徒多达<Num s="S150">70</Num>多万人。

前213年，秦始皇采纳李斯的建议，下令焚毁民间收藏的古代史书和经典；前212年，咸阳有<Num s="S150">460</Num>多名“诸生”，也就是读书人，被坑杀。前210年，秦始皇在东巡途中去世。第二年，戍卒首领陈胜、吴广揭竿而起，起义很快蔓延到原来的六国故地。前206年，秦朝最后一位君主子婴投降。

汉代人普遍批评秦朝。贾谊在《过秦论》里说，秦打天下靠武力，得了天下以后仍然只靠武力，所以很快灭亡。然而汉朝继承了秦的郡县、法律和户籍制度，后来的王朝也大体沿用这种统治方式。兼并六国值不值得付出那样的代价，至今仍有争论。

<More title={{"en": "The books and the scholars", "zh": "焚书与坑杀诸生"}}>

前213年的焚书令规定，朝廷博士所藏的书，以及医药、卜筮、种树之书，可以不烧。前212年，两名为始皇求仙药的方士批评他后逃走，始皇下令审问咸阳的读书人，据《史记》，“犯禁者四百六十余人，皆阬之咸阳”。《秦始皇本纪》称他们为“诸生”，《儒林列传》则说“坑术士”；只有始皇长子扶苏说他们“皆诵法孔子”。

有些学者，如李开元，认为“焚书坑儒”的说法是汉代人逐步塑造出来的，被杀的主要是为始皇求仙的方士；也有学者维护《史记》的记载。“焚书坑儒”这四个字连用，要到几百年后才出现。

</More>
