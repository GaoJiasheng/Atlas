<Lang en>

Duke Huan of Qi, Qí Huán Gōng 齐桓公, ruled from 685 BCE to 643 BCE. With his minister Guan Zhong, Guǎn Zhòng 管仲, he strengthened the government and organised alliances. Qi's position in present-day Shandong connected inland routes with the coast. Its influence depended on other rulers accepting its leadership as well as on its ability to deploy troops.

Later tradition associated Qi's policy with <Term id="zunwang-rangyi">“honour the king, repel the Yi”</Term>. The slogan presented Qi's actions as service to Zhou kingship. It also expressed the categories of writers within the Zhou political world. Communities called Rong or Di in those records left few accounts of these encounters in their own voices.

Qi campaigned against the Shanrong to assist Yan in 663 BCE. When Di forces attacked Xing and Wey, Qi helped their ruling houses establish new centres. Wey's capital had been taken in 660 BCE; its rebuilding at Chuqiu in 658 BCE placed survival alongside dependence on a stronger patron. For residents displaced by warfare, the restoration of a court did not necessarily restore lost homes or fields.

In 656 BCE, Qi led a coalition south and negotiated with Chu at Shaoling. The settlement, rather than a decisive battle, limited the confrontation. At Kuiqiu in 651 BCE, a royal representative honoured Duke Huan. A <Term id="hegemon">hegemon</Term> could summon meetings and support agreements while acknowledging the king's rank. Yet this was personal and negotiated authority: after Huan died, his sons fought over the succession and Qi's coalition weakened.

<More title={{"en": "What a covenant could promise", "zh": "盟约能承诺什么"}}>

A <Term id="covenant">covenant meeting</Term> brought rulers together to swear commitments. The Mencius recalls Kuiqiu's restrictions on harmful practices, including obstructing rivers and restricting grain trade. Such rules addressed disputes with consequences for people downstream or dependent on imported grain. Their preservation in a later text shows an ideal of political order; it does not prove that every participant obeyed them.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Different lists of hegemons", "zh": "不同的五霸名单"}}>

“The <Num s="S6">Five</Num> Hegemons” is a later grouping with competing lists. Xunzi names Huan of Qi, Duke Wen of Jin, Jìn Wén Gōng 晋文公, King Zhuang of Chu, Chǔ Zhuāng Wáng 楚庄王, King Helü of Wu, Hélǘ 阖闾, and Goujian of Yue, Gōujiàn 勾践. One account in the Baihu tong substitutes Duke Mu of Qin, Qín Mù Gōng 秦穆公, for Goujian. The list associated with Zhao Qi's Mencius commentary includes Huan, Wen, Mu, Duke Xiang of Song, Sòng Xiāng Gōng 宋襄公, and Zhuang. The variation reflects different judgments about leadership, rather than a single office filled in a fixed sequence.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "The view from Xing and Wey", "zh": "从邢与卫看联盟"}}>

The annals describe attacks and relocations through the fortunes of their ruling houses. A state's survival could depend on a neighbouring army and a new capital. The same sources often classify the attackers collectively as Di, concealing differences among their communities. This topic retains those terms only with attribution and does not give them invented borders or a single collective political purpose.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

齐桓公在前685年至前643年间在位，在管仲协助下加强治理、组织联盟。齐位于今山东，联系内陆与沿海交通。它的影响既来自出兵能力，也依靠其他君主接受其领导，不能只靠扩大领土来说明。

后世常以<Term id="zunwang-rangyi">尊王攘夷</Term>概括齐的政策，把齐的行动解释为维护周王。这个说法也反映周代记述者区别人群的方式。文献所称的戎、狄，很少留下自身对这些接触和冲突的叙述，因此不能只按齐的说法理解战争。

前663年，齐为援燕而攻山戎。狄进攻邢与卫后，齐帮助两国王室另建中心。卫都在前660年被攻破，前658年迁至楚丘。王室得以延续，也更依赖强国援助；对战争中离散的居民而言，复国未必意味着能够找回原来的房屋与田地。

前656年，齐率联军南下，与楚在召陵议和，谈判限制了冲突。前651年葵丘会盟，周王代表礼遇齐桓公。<Term id="hegemon">霸主</Term>承认王的名分，同时召集诸侯、维持盟约。这种权力仍须协商，并依赖个人关系。桓公死后，诸子争位，齐领导的联盟随之削弱。联盟既是军队之间的关系，也影响迁徙居民如何重新获得住所与生计。

<More title={{"en": "What a covenant could promise", "zh": "盟约能承诺什么"}}>

<Term id="covenant">会盟</Term>使君主聚集并宣誓承担义务。《孟子》追述葵丘盟约时，列举不得曲防水道、限制粮食贸易等规定，涉及下游居民及依靠外来粮食的人。后世文献保存这些条款，显示一种政治秩序的理想，不能证明所有参与者都照做。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Different lists of hegemons", "zh": "不同的五霸名单"}}>

“<Num s="S6">五</Num>霸”是后世形成且名单不同的归类。《荀子》列齐桓公、晋文公、楚庄王、吴王阖闾、越王勾践；《白虎通》的一说用秦穆公替代勾践。赵岐《孟子》注所见名单则为齐桓公、晋文公、秦穆公、宋襄公、楚庄王。差异反映对领导权的不同评价，并非一个按固定次序交接的官职。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "The view from Xing and Wey", "zh": "从邢与卫看联盟"}}>

史书常以王室的命运记述受攻与迁徙。一国能否延续，可能取决于邻国援军及新的都城。同样的记载又常把进攻者统称为狄，掩盖其内部差异。本主题只在注明文献称呼的前提下使用这些名称，不替他们编画边界或设定共同政治目标。

</More>

</Lang>
