<Lang en>

Ying Zheng, Yíng Zhèng 嬴政, became king of Qin in 247 BCE and assumed personal rule in 238 BCE. Ministers including Lü Buwei, Lǚ Bùwéi 吕不韦, and Li Si, Lǐ Sī 李斯, shaped government policy. The Zhengguo canal, begun in 246 BCE, expanded irrigation in the Wei valley. Resources from earlier conquests and administrative reforms supported further campaigns, without guaranteeing that every attack would succeed.

The final coalition attack on Qin failed in 241 BCE. Yet Zhao's commander Li Mu, Lǐ Mù 李牧, defeated Qin at Fei in 233 BCE. Zhao later executed him during another Qin invasion; the Shiji attributes the decision to Qin's efforts to sow distrust. Han Fei, Hán Fēi 韩非, died in Qin in 233 BCE. Jing Ke, Jīng Kē 荆轲, attempted to kill the Qin king in 227 BCE. Political persuasion, imprisonment and assassination accompanied the armies.

Qin conquered Han in 230 BCE and captured Zhao's king at Handan in 228 BCE. Zhao's remnant court survived in Dai until 222 BCE. In 225 BCE, Qin flooded Daliang; Wei's king surrendered. The accounts do not supply a reliable civilian death toll. Qin's first major attack on Chu failed, showing that its eventual annexations were not a predetermined sequence of uninterrupted victories.

Wang Jian, Wáng Jiǎn 王翦, led the renewed campaign. Chu's monarchy conventionally ends in 223 BCE, though the Shiji's chapters disagree on the king's capture year; operations south of the Yangtze continued in 222 BCE. Yan's last king was captured that year. Qi surrendered without a major battle in 221 BCE. Qin had removed rival courts, but their populations retained local ties and memories that an imperial title could not immediately replace.

<More title={{"en": "The defeated campaign against Chu", "zh": "攻楚失利与年代分歧"}}>

The Shiji says Li Xin, Lǐ Xìn 李信, proposed using <Num s="S152">200,000</Num> men, while Wang Jian requested <Num s="S152">600,000</Num>. Li Xin's force was defeated; Wang Jian then led the larger expedition. These are figures in a retrospective biography, not independently preserved mobilisation returns. The dialogue also illustrates a problem of royal trust: the story has Wang Jian repeatedly ask for property to reassure the king that he wanted retirement wealth rather than political power.

Li Xin's campaign is conventionally placed in 225 BCE, but this biography supplies no year. The First Emperor annals place the Chu king's capture in the twenty-third regnal year, 224 BCE, and Xiang Yan's death in the following year; the Wang Jian biography arranges events differently. The topic keeps 223 BCE as a conventional marker for Chu's fall rather than a securely established capture date.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Persuasion, philosophy and assassination", "zh": "劝说、思想与刺杀"}}>

Xunzi, Xúnzǐ 荀子, Han Fei and Li Si offered different accounts of education, law and authority. Later labels should not erase their disagreements. The Shiji presents Jing Ke's attack as a dramatic narrative of preparation, friendship and death. Its speeches and song are literary evidence for how the episode was remembered, not a recording. The failure of the assassination did not cause every later annexation by itself; campaigns and political decisions had already been developing.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why Qin?", "zh": "为何是秦"}}>

Geography gave Qin access to the Wei valley, passes and the resources of Ba and Shu. Administrative institutions supported taxation and mobilisation. Its rulers also exploited divisions among other courts, whose coalitions repeatedly broke apart. These explanations are complementary but place different weight on resources, institutions, diplomacy and contingency. Victoria Hui's comparison with European state formation asks why competitive systems produced different long-term outcomes. No single factor makes Qin's result inevitable.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What the conquered remembered", "zh": "被兼并者的记忆"}}>

The Shiji attributes to people of Chu the saying that even if only <Num s="S1">three</Num> households remained, Chu would bring Qin down. The saying belongs to a later account of resistance and cannot measure everyone's opinion. It does show that narratives of local loss survived annexation. At Daliang the Shiji describes a siege lasting <Num s="S150">three</Num> months. The surrendered king has a name in the record; most residents affected by the flood do not.

</More>

<Lang zh>

嬴政于前247年即秦王位，前238年亲政。吕不韦、李斯等臣下参与制定政策。前246年开始修建的郑国渠扩大了渭水流域灌溉。早先兼并的资源与行政改革支持继续出兵，却不保证每次进攻都能成功。

前241年，最后一次诸国联合攻秦未成。但赵将李牧在前233年于肥击败秦；秦再次入侵时，赵处死李牧，《史记》把这一决定归于秦的离间。韩非在前233年死于秦，荆轲在前227年刺秦王未成。劝说、囚禁与刺杀同军队行动交织。

前230年，秦灭韩；前228年攻邯郸、俘赵王，赵的残余王廷在代延续至前222年。前225年，秦水灌大梁，魏王投降，史料没有可靠的居民死亡总数。秦首次大举攻楚失利，显示后来兼并的结果并非从一开始就已注定。

王翦再度领军攻楚，通常以前223年概括楚王廷的终结，但俘王年份在《史记》不同篇章中不一致；江南战事延续至前222年。燕的末王也在这一年被俘。前221年齐未经历大规模会战即投降。秦移除了其他王廷，居民的地方联系与失国记忆却不会立即消失。此后的制度整合仍须面对不同地区的习惯，以及持续征调带来的负担。

《史记》记李信提出用兵<Num s="S152">20万</Num>，王翦则要<Num s="S152">60万</Num>。李信失利后，王翦带较大军队出征。这些是后世传记数字，不是独立保存的征兵清册；王翦反复求田的对话，也借此说明他如何使秦王相信自己没有夺权之心。

李信攻楚常系于前225年，但该传本身没有给年。《秦始皇本纪》把俘楚王置于秦王二十三年，即前224年，项燕死于次年；《白起王翦列传》的次序不同。本主题保留前223年作为楚亡的常用标记，区分它与俘王的确切定年。

<More title={{"en": "Persuasion, philosophy and assassination", "zh": "劝说、思想与刺杀"}}>

荀子、韩非与李斯对教育、法律、权力提出不同解释，不能用后世分类抹去分歧。《史记》把荆轲刺秦写成含有准备、友谊与死亡的戏剧性叙事；其中言辞与歌辞是后人如何记忆事件的文学证据，并非录音。刺杀失败并非后来所有兼并的单一原因，战争和政治决策早已展开。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why Qin?", "zh": "为何是秦"}}>

地理条件使秦能够利用渭水流域、关隘及巴蜀资源，行政制度支持征税与动员。秦王也利用其他王廷之间的分歧，诸国联盟屡次瓦解。不同研究分别重视资源、制度、外交与偶然选择，这些解释可以互补。许田波以欧洲国家形成作比较，追问竞争体系为何产生不同长期结果；没有单一因素使秦的结果必然发生。

</More>
