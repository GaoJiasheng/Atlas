<Lang en>

King Ping of Zhou, Zhōu Píng Wáng 周平王, set up his court at Luoyi, traditionally in 770 BCE. His own domain now covered little more than the Luoyang basin, and he relied on the lords of Jin and Zheng to protect it. A bamboo chronicle acquired by Tsinghua University in 2008, the Xinian, says that for some years there were two rival Zhou kings, and that the move east may have come later than the Shiji says.

According to the Shiji, Duke Xiang of Qin, Qín Xiāng Gōng 秦襄公, escorted King Ping east and was made a regional lord. He was promised the old Zhou lands in the west if he could take them from the Rong. Qin spent the next century fighting Rong communities for the Wei valley.

Zheng, just east of the new capital, profited most at first. Its ruler, Duke Zhuang, Zhèng Zhuāng Gōng 郑庄公, was also a minister at the royal court. In 720 BCE king and duke exchanged sons as hostages, and the same year Zheng troops cut the royal wheat and millet harvests at Wen and Chengzhou.

In 707 BCE King Huan of Zhou, Zhōu Huán Wáng 周桓王, led his own troops and those of Chen, Cai and Wey against Zheng. At Xuge they were routed, and an arrow fired by the Zheng officer Zhu Dan struck the king in the shoulder. The Zhou king remained the senior ritual figure for another five centuries, but from now on he had to rely on the regional lords. Fifteen years earlier, in 722 BCE, the court of Lu had begun the annals that give the period its name.

<More title={{"en": "Two kings?", "zh": "两个周王？"}}>

The Xinian, a bamboo history now kept at Tsinghua University, gives a different account of the end of Western Zhou. After King You's death, it says, some lords set up a rival, King Hui of Xie, and the Zhou had no generally accepted king for a time; Jin later killed King Hui and backed King Ping. Historians such as Li Feng and Yuri Pines read this as evidence of a longer struggle. This topic keeps 770 BCE as the traditional date, not a settled one.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why “Spring and Autumn”?", "zh": "为什么叫“春秋”"}}>

Court chronicles were dated by year and season, and "spring and autumn" came to mean "annals". Lu's chronicle runs from 722 to 481 BCE in short entries: a ruler's death, a covenant, an eclipse, an attack. The period is usually dated 770–476 BCE, but other historians end it in 481, 453 or 403 BCE. Each date marks a different change.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Hostages and harvests", "zh": "人质与庄稼"}}>

The Zuo Tradition tells how the king and the duke of Zheng, distrusting each other, exchanged a royal prince and Zheng's heir in 720 BCE. Within months Zheng troops had reaped the king's grain at Wen and Chengzhou. The account is told from the courts' side. For the farmers at Wen, an argument between their rulers meant losing a harvest.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

周平王把王廷安在雒邑，传统上定为前770年。此后周王直接掌握的土地，大致只剩洛阳盆地一带，要靠晋、郑等诸侯保护。2008年入藏清华大学的战国竹简《系年》却记载，西周灭亡后曾有两个周王并立多年，东迁的时间可能比《史记》所说的要晚。

据《史记》，秦襄公护送平王东迁，被封为诸侯。平王许诺，如果秦能从戎人手中夺回周的西部故地，那片土地就归秦所有。此后一百多年，秦一直和渭水流域的戎人作战。

起初得利最多的是紧邻新都的郑国。郑庄公既是一国之君，又在王廷任卿士。前720年，周王和郑庄公互相把儿子送去做人质；同一年，郑军割走了王室在温地的麦子和成周的谷子。周王和诸侯之间，已经要靠交换人质来维持信任了。

前707年，周桓王亲自率领王师，会同陈、蔡、卫三国军队讨伐郑国。双方在繻葛交战，王师大败，郑国大夫祝聃一箭射中周王的肩膀。此后五百年间，周王仍是礼仪上地位最高的人，但实际上只能依靠诸侯。在这十五年前，即前722年，鲁国开始记录这一时期得名的那部编年史。

<More title={{"en": "Two kings?", "zh": "两个周王？"}}>

清华简《系年》对西周末年的记述与《史记》不同。它说幽王死后，一部分诸侯另立携惠王，周有一段时间没有公认的君主；后来晋文侯杀了携惠王，扶立平王。李峰、尤锐等学者认为，这说明王位之争持续的时间更长。本主题把前770年作为传统纪年，而不当作定论。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why “Spring and Autumn”?", "zh": "为什么叫“春秋”"}}>

当时各国史官按年、按季记事，“春秋”于是成了编年史的代称。鲁国的《春秋》起于前722年，止于前481年，每条只有寥寥数字：某君去世、某处会盟、日食、某国攻某国。春秋时期今天通常定为前770—前476年，也有学者以前481、前453或前403年为界，每个年份代表不同的变化。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Hostages and harvests", "zh": "人质与庄稼"}}>

《左传》记载，周王与郑庄公互不信任，前720年互换人质：王子狐到郑，郑公子忽到周。几个月后，郑军就收割了王室在温和成周的庄稼。这段故事是从王廷的角度写的；对温地的农民来说，君主之间的争执意味着一季收成没了。

</More>
