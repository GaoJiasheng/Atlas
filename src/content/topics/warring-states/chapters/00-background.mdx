<Lang en>

For nearly three centuries before 771 BCE, Zhou kings ruled from Feng and Hao in the Wei valley, near present-day Xi'an, and kept an eastern centre at Luoyi, near present-day Luoyang. Through <Term id="fengjian">enfeoffment</Term> they gave land, and the people living on it, to kinsmen and allies. These <Term id="zhuhou">regional lords</Term> owed the king tribute, ritual service and troops, but governed their own states.

A state was a network of walled towns and the fields around them. Between the towns lay hills, marshes and forests that no state controlled, home to communities whom Zhou writers called Rong, Di, Yi or Man. These were outsiders' labels, not the names of peoples with one government, and those communities left no written records of their own. Blank land on this map means only that no state is drawn there.

Rivers carried grain, travellers and armies. Before 602 BCE the lower Yellow River flowed north through present-day Hebei to the Bohai Sea, and the Ji River, which no longer exists, crossed the eastern plain. Both are drawn dashed, as reconstructions. The coastline is today's.

In 771 BCE King You of Zhou, Zhōu Yōu Wáng 周幽王, was killed below Mount Li by the forces of the Marquis of Shen and the Quanrong, and Hao was sacked. The court moved east to Luoyi, traditionally in 770 BCE: the start of the Eastern Zhou. It is usually divided into the Spring and Autumn period, 770–476 BCE, and the Warring States period, 475–221 BCE. In these five and a half centuries <Num s="S2">more than a hundred</Num> states were reduced to seven, and in 221 BCE to one empire.

<More title={{"en": "How this map was made", "zh": "这张地图是怎么画的"}}>

No map of the Zhou states drawn at the time survives, and no open dataset of their borders exists. Each frame here is traced from a published map on Wikimedia Commons and fitted to real coordinates; the maps and their licences are listed in the sources.

The frames for 770, 650 and 600 BCE use an undated map of the whole Spring and Autumn period, so Chu, Qin, Wu and Yue look larger than they were in 770 BCE. The 403 BCE frame borrows the borders between Han, Zhao and Wei from a map of 350 BCE. The 260 and 230 BCE frames keep the outlines of a 280 BCE map and change only whole states that were annexed, so Qin's conquest of the Chu heartland in 278 BCE is not drawn. The 221 BCE empire is simply the union of the conquered states. Every border is dashed because ancient frontiers were zones between towns, not surveyed lines.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A river that moved", "zh": "改道的黄河"}}>

The Hanshu, written in the first century CE, quotes an older record that the Yellow River "moved" in 602 BCE. Before that, its lower course ran north through Hebei; afterwards it reached the sea further south, near present-day Cangzhou, until 11 CE. Its present course through Shandong dates only from 1855 CE and is not drawn. The ancient channels shown here come from a reconstruction by the Commons mapmaker Kanguole, based on Tan Qixiang's historical atlas and on archaeological surveys.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Who wrote the records?", "zh": "谁留下了记录"}}>

The Spring and Autumn Annals are the court chronicle of Lu for 722–481 BCE. The Zuo Tradition, compiled in the fourth century BCE, tells their stories in detail. Sima Qian finished the Shiji, the main source for the Warring States, around 90 BCE under the Han dynasty, which had overthrown Qin. Writers in the Zhou states called themselves <Term id="huaxia">Huaxia</Term> and set themselves against the Rong, Di, Yi and Man; none of those communities' own accounts survive. Bamboo texts excavated since the 1970s, such as the Qin legal slips from Shuihudi and the chronicle Xinian, sometimes correct these histories.

</More>

<Lang zh>

公元前771年以前将近三百年间，周王以渭水谷地的丰、镐两京（今西安附近）为都，并在东方的雒邑（今洛阳附近）经营另一个中心。周王通过<Term id="fengjian">分封</Term>，把土地连同土地上的人口授予亲族和功臣。受封的<Term id="zhuhou">诸侯</Term>要向周王纳贡、助祭、出兵，同时自己治理封国。

所谓“国”，其实是一串筑有城墙的城邑，加上城外的田地。城邑之间有山地、沼泽和森林，不归任何一国管辖，那里住着周人称为戎、狄、夷、蛮的人群。这些是外人给的称呼，并不是某个有统一政权的民族的名字；这些人群也没有留下自己的文字记录。所以图上空白的地方，只表示没有画出诸侯国，并不是没有人。

河流是运粮、行旅和行军的通道。前602年以前，黄河下游向北流经今河北，注入渤海；今天已经消失的济水，当时横贯东部平原。这两条河都按复原结果画成虚线。海岸线用的是今天的。

前771年，周幽王在骊山下被申侯和犬戎的军队杀死，镐京遭到洗劫。王室东迁雒邑，传统上定在前770年，这就是东周的开始。东周通常分为春秋（前770—前476年）和战国（前475—前221年）两段。在这五百五十年里，<Num s="S2">一百多个</Num>诸侯国逐渐被兼并为七国，前221年又归于一个帝国。

<More title={{"en": "How this map was made", "zh": "这张地图是怎么画的"}}>

周代没有留下当时绘制的地图，也没有公开的列国疆界数据集。这里的每一帧，都是描摹维基共享资源上已发表的地图，再按真实坐标配准而成；地图作者和许可证见来源列表。

前770、前650、前600年三帧，用的是一张不标年份、概括整个春秋时期的地图，所以楚、秦、吴、越都比前770年的实际范围大。前403年一帧中韩、赵、魏之间的分界，借用了前350年地图的画法。前260、前230年两帧沿用前280年地图的轮廓，只把整个被灭的国家改换颜色，因此前278年秦夺取楚国腹地并没有画出来。前221年的秦帝国，只是被兼并各国范围的合并。古代的“边界”是城邑之间的过渡地带，不是测量出来的线，所以全部画成虚线。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A river that moved", "zh": "改道的黄河"}}>

东汉成书的《汉书》引用更早的记载，说黄河在前602年“徙”，即改道。在此之前，黄河下游北流经今河北；改道后在偏南的地方入海，大致在今沧州一带，这条河道一直用到公元11年。今天经山东入海的黄河下游，是1855年才形成的，图上不画。图中的古河道采用维基共享资源作者Kanguole的复原图，它依据谭其骧主编的历史地图集和考古调查。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Who wrote the records?", "zh": "谁留下了记录"}}>

《春秋》是鲁国史官记录的编年史，起于前722年，止于前481年。《左传》大约成书于前4世纪，把这些事件讲得很详细。战国史主要依据《史记》，司马迁大约在前90年写成此书，当时在位的是推翻秦朝的汉朝。周代诸侯国的作者自称<Term id="huaxia">华夏</Term>，与戎、狄、夷、蛮相对；后者自己的说法没有流传下来。1970年代以来出土的竹简，如睡虎地秦律、清华简《系年》，有时能纠正这些史书的记载。

</More>
