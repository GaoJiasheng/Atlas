<Lang en>

In 354 BCE Wei besieged Handan, the Zhao capital. Qi's commanders Tian Ji, Tián Jì 田忌, and Sun Bin, Sūn Bìn 孙膑, marched on the Wei capital instead and beat the returning Wei army at Guiling in 353 BCE. In 342 BCE they lured Wei's army into an ambush at Maling, where, the Shiji says, ten thousand crossbows fired at once. Wei never recovered its lead.

The courts were now full of travelling advisers who argued for <Term id="vertical-alliance">vertical alliances</Term> of the states against Qin, or <Term id="horizontal-alliance">horizontal alliances</Term> of single states with Qin. In 318 BCE a coalition led by Chu attacked Qin and failed. In 316 BCE Qin conquered the kingdoms of Shu and Ba in the Sichuan basin. Zhang Yi, Zhāng Yí 张仪, a minister of Qin, promised Chu six hundred li of land if it broke with Qi, then gave it nothing; in 312 BCE Qin defeated Chu at Danyang, taking, by its own count, <Num s="S151">80,000</Num> heads. In 299 BCE King Huai of Chu went to a meeting in Qin, was held prisoner and died there three years later.

Every power expanded where it could. Qi invaded Yan in 314 BCE, Zhao annexed Zhongshan in 296 BCE, and Qi swallowed Song in 286 BCE. At Yique in 293 BCE Bai Qi, Bái Qǐ 白起, defeated Han and Wei; Qin's records claim <Num s="S151">240,000</Num> heads. In 284 BCE Yan, Qin, Han, Zhao and Wei turned on Qi. The Yan general Yue Yi, Yuè Yì 乐毅, took Linzi and more than <Num s="S169">70</Num> cities.

The philosopher Mencius, Mèngzǐ 孟子, who advised the kings of Wei and Qi, judged the whole age harshly: "In the Spring and Autumn Annals there are no righteous wars."

<More title={{"en": "Su Qin and Zhang Yi: who came first?", "zh": "苏秦与张仪：谁先谁后"}}>

The Shiji makes Su Qin, Sū Qín 苏秦, the great organiser of the alliance against Qin, active before Zhang Yi. Silk manuscripts found in a Han tomb at Mawangdui in 1973 contain letters and speeches of Su Qin that place most of his career between about 300 and 284 BCE, after Zhang Yi's death, working for Yan against Qi.

Many historians now follow the manuscripts. Others note that the Shiji's speeches were already literary set pieces, and that the manuscripts too were collected and edited. Either way, the neat contrast between the two men was shaped by later storytellers.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two occupations: Qi in Yan, Yan in Qi", "zh": "两次占领：齐在燕，燕在齐"}}>

In 316 BCE King Kuai of Yan handed his throne to his minister Zizhi; the civil war that followed, says the Shiji, killed <Num s="S214">tens of thousands</Num>. Qi invaded in 314 BCE and took the country within fifty days. Mencius told the Qi king that his soldiers were "killing fathers and elder brothers, tying up sons and younger brothers, destroying ancestral temples and carrying off the sacred vessels". Yan's people rose and Qi withdrew.

Thirty years later Yan's army occupied most of Qi. The Shiji says that at Jimo, Yan troops cut off the noses of captured Qi soldiers and dug up the graves outside the walls, burning the dead. It tells this inside a story of the Qi commander Tian Dan spreading rumours to provoke exactly that, so it may be shaped by the storyteller; but both occupations are recorded in the same plain terms.

</More>

A crossbow's bronze trigger let a soldier hold the drawn string and aim before shooting, so crossbows could be fired together on command. The Shiji says Qi lit fewer cooking fires each day to make Pang Juan, Páng Juān 庞涓, think its army was deserting, then ambushed him at Maling, where he killed himself. A bamboo military text from Yinqueshan instead says Pang Juan was captured earlier, at Guiling. Both versions are given here.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Northern dress and the end of Zhongshan", "zh": "胡服骑射与中山的覆亡"}}>

In 307 BCE King Wuling of Zhao, Zhào Wǔlíng Wáng 赵武灵王, ordered his soldiers to wear the trousers and short coats of the northern herders and to fight on horseback as mounted archers. Zhao then pushed north into the lands of the Linhu and Loufan and, in 296 BCE, conquered Zhongshan, a state founded by people the Zhou texts called the White Di. Royal tombs excavated at Pingshan in Hebei show that Zhongshan had its own bronze-casting, writing and court ritual.

<Lang zh>

前354年，魏军包围赵都邯郸。齐将田忌和军师孙膑不去救邯郸，而是直扑魏都，前353年在桂陵截击回救的魏军，这就是“围魏救赵”。前342年，齐军又把魏军诱入马陵的埋伏圈，据《史记》，当时“万弩俱发”。魏国从此失去了领先地位。

各国朝廷里聚集着四处游说的谋士，有的主张<Term id="vertical-alliance">合纵</Term>，即各国联合抗秦；有的主张<Term id="horizontal-alliance">连横</Term>，即各国分别与秦结盟。前318年，楚国领头的联军攻秦失败。前316年，秦灭了四川盆地里的蜀国和巴国。秦相张仪许诺，只要楚国与齐国断交，就割让六百里土地给楚国，事后却一寸也不给。前312年秦军在丹阳大败楚军，按秦方的记录斩首<Num s="S151">8万</Num>。前299年，楚怀王到秦国会盟，被扣押，三年后死在秦国。

各国都在能扩张的地方扩张：前314年齐攻燕，前296年赵灭中山，前286年齐吞并宋国。前293年，白起在伊阙击败韩、魏联军，秦方记录斩首<Num s="S151">24万</Num>。前284年，燕、秦、韩、赵、魏五国联合攻齐，燕将乐毅攻入临淄，连下齐国<Num s="S169">70</Num>多城。

曾向魏、齐两国国君进言的孟子，对整个时代评价很低：“春秋无义战。”在他看来，诸侯之间的战争，没有一场称得上正义。

<More title={{"en": "Su Qin and Zhang Yi: who came first?", "zh": "苏秦与张仪：谁先谁后"}}>

《史记》把苏秦写成合纵抗秦的组织者，活动年代在张仪之前。1973年长沙马王堆汉墓出土的帛书《战国纵横家书》，收有苏秦的书信和言论，显示他主要活动在约前300年到前284年之间，晚于张仪，而且是为燕国谋划对付齐国。

不少学者现在依从帛书的年代。也有人指出，《史记》里的说辞本来就是文学化的范文，帛书同样经过后人收集整理。无论如何，苏秦、张仪针锋相对的形象，是后来的讲述者塑造出来的。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two occupations: Qi in Yan, Yan in Qi", "zh": "两次占领：齐在燕，燕在齐"}}>

前316年，燕王哙把王位让给国相子之，随后爆发内乱，据《史记》，“死者数万”，即<Num s="S214">数万</Num>人。前314年齐军攻燕，五十天就占领了燕国。孟子对齐王说，齐军在燕国“杀其父兄，系累其子弟，毁其宗庙，迁其重器”。燕人起来反抗，齐军撤走。

三十年后，轮到燕军占领齐国大部。《史记》说，在即墨城外，燕军割掉被俘齐兵的鼻子，又挖开城外的坟墓，焚烧尸体。这段记载出自田单故意散布谣言、激燕军这样做的故事，可能经过讲述者的加工；但两次占领中的暴行，史书都是这样平实地记下来的。

</More>

弩有青铜扳机，士兵可以先拉好弦、瞄准，再扣动扳机，所以许多弩手能听号令一齐发射。《史记》说，齐军每天减少做饭的灶，让庞涓以为齐兵在大批逃亡，然后在马陵设伏，庞涓兵败自杀。银雀山出土的竹简兵书却说，庞涓早在桂陵之战就被擒了。两种说法这里并列。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Northern dress and the end of Zhongshan", "zh": "胡服骑射与中山的覆亡"}}>

前307年，赵武灵王下令士兵改穿北方牧人的短衣长裤，练习骑马射箭。此后赵国向北进入林胡、楼烦的地方，又在前296年灭了中山国。中山国由周人文献所称的白狄人建立，河北平山出土的中山王墓表明，它有自己的青铜铸造、文字和宫廷礼仪。

</More>
