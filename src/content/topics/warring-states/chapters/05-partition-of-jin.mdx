<Lang en>

By the fifth century BCE the rulers of Jin and Qi had lost power to great ministerial families. In Qi, Tian Chang, Tián Cháng 田常, killed Duke Jian in 481 BCE, and his descendants ruled the state from then on. In Jin, six families fought each other until four were left, led by the Zhi.

In 455 BCE Zhi Bo, Zhì Bó 智伯, head of the Zhi, demanded land from the Zhao family. When Zhao Xiangzi, Zhào Xiāngzǐ 赵襄子, refused, Zhi Bo and the Han and Wei families besieged him in Jinyang. In 453 BCE they dammed the Fen River and turned it against the city. According to the Shiji, the water rose to within three planks of the top of the walls; people cooked in pots hung up off the flooded ground, and starving families exchanged their children to eat. Zhao Xiangzi secretly persuaded Han and Wei to change sides. Together they broke the dykes onto Zhi Bo's own camp, killed him and divided his lands.

In 403 BCE the Zhou king recognised Han, Zhao and Wei as regional states. Sima Guang's great eleventh-century history, the Zizhi tongjian, begins with that year. The last Duke of Jin was deposed in 376 BCE, and in 386 BCE the king recognised the Tian as rulers of Qi.

Seven large states now dominated: Qin, Chu, Qi, Yan, Han, Zhao and Wei. Smaller ones, among them Song, Lu, Wey, Yue and Zhongshan, survived beside them. Mozi, Mòzǐ 墨子, a craftsman-philosopher of this time, taught that attacking other states was a crime like theft or murder, and trained his followers to defend besieged cities.

<More title={{"en": "When did the Warring States begin?", "zh": "战国从哪一年算起"}}>

This topic follows today's common usage and starts the Warring States in 475 BCE. Other historians choose 481 BCE, when the Spring and Autumn Annals end; 453 BCE, when the Zhi family fell; or 403 BCE, when Han, Zhao and Wei were recognised. The name itself comes from the Zhanguoce, the "Stratagems of the Warring States", compiled in the first century BCE.

</More>

The tomb of Marquis Yi, Zēng Hóu Yǐ 曾侯乙, excavated near Suizhou in Hubei in 1978, held a set of <Num s="S161">65</Num> bronze bells that can still play a full scale. One bell was a gift from the King of Chu, dated to about 433 BCE. Zeng is usually identified with the state called Sui in the texts. The tomb also held the coffins of <Num s="S27">21</Num> young women who were killed to accompany the marquis.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Water as a weapon", "zh": "以水为兵"}}>

Jinyang is the first well-recorded case of a city flooded on purpose, and it was not the last. The Shiji records that Zhao broke the Yellow River dykes against invading Qi and Wei armies in 332 BCE and again against Wei in 281 BCE; Qin flooded the Chu city of Yan in 279 BCE and the Wei capital, Daliang, in 225 BCE. In none of these cases does any source say how many townspeople drowned. After Jinyang, according to the Shiji, Zhao Xiangzi had Zhi Bo's skull lacquered and used it as a drinking cup.

<Lang zh>

到前5世纪，晋、齐两国的国君已经大权旁落，实权掌握在几个大家族手里。前481年，齐国的田常杀死齐简公，此后田氏世代执掌齐政。晋国的六家卿大夫互相攻伐，最后剩下四家，以智氏最强。

前455年，智氏的首领智伯向赵氏索要土地，赵襄子拒绝。智伯便联合韩、魏两家，把赵襄子围在晋阳。前453年，智伯筑坝截断汾水，引水灌城。据《史记》，城墙只剩三版高没有被淹，城里人把锅吊起来做饭，饿极了的人家互相交换孩子吃。赵襄子暗中说服韩、魏倒戈，三家反过来掘堤水淹智伯的军营，杀死智伯，瓜分了他的领地。

前403年，周王正式承认韩、赵、魏为诸侯，等于承认了臣下瓜分君主土地的既成事实。北宋司马光的《资治通鉴》就从这一年写起。前376年，晋国最后一位国君被废；前386年，周王承认田氏为齐国国君。

此后，秦、楚、齐、燕、韩、赵、魏七个大国并立，宋、鲁、卫、越、中山等小国夹在其间。这一时期的墨子出身工匠，他认为攻打别国和偷盗、杀人一样是罪行，又训练弟子帮助被围的城邑守城。他的门徒纪律严明，常常应弱小国家之请前去助守。

<More title={{"en": "When did the Warring States begin?", "zh": "战国从哪一年算起"}}>

本主题按今天通行的说法，以前475年为战国开端。也有人以《春秋》结束的前481年、智氏灭亡的前453年，或三家被承认为诸侯的前403年为起点。“战国”这个名称，来自西汉末年编成的《战国策》。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Water as a weapon", "zh": "以水为兵"}}>

晋阳是第一次有详细记载的人为水淹城邑，但绝不是最后一次。据《史记》，前332年齐、魏攻赵，赵掘开黄河堤防灌敌；前281年赵又决河水攻魏；前279年秦水灌楚国的鄢城，前225年水灌魏都大梁。这些事件中，没有一部史书记下城里淹死了多少百姓。又据《史记》，晋阳之战后，赵襄子把智伯的头骨涂上漆，做成饮酒的器具。

</More>
