<Lang en>

Bending the elbow is called <Term id="flexion">flexion</Term>. Three muscles share it. Biceps and brachioradialis both pull on the radius; brachialis pulls on the ulna. Because the ulna does not turn when you rotate your hand, **brachialis bends the elbow whatever your grip** [M87] [M63]. Changing from palm-up to thumb-up or palm-down shifts some of the work between biceps and brachioradialis, but it never switches brachialis off.

Biceps has a second job that many people miss: **biceps also turns the palm up**. This is <Term id="supination">supination</Term>. Its tendon wraps around the radius, so when it pulls, the radius rolls around the ulna. Gray calls biceps a powerful supinator, and OpenStax lists supination alongside elbow flexion as its action [M87] [M63]. The reverse turn, <Term id="pronation">pronation</Term>, is done by pronator teres, which pulls the radius across the front of the ulna [M87]. <FlyTo preset="close-arm">Watch the forearm turn</FlyTo>

Straightening the elbow, <Term id="extension">extension</Term>, belongs to triceps, with a little help from anconeus. **The long head of triceps crosses the shoulder as well as the elbow**, so it also helps pull the arm down and back. The lateral and medial heads start on the humerus and act only at the elbow [M87]. Biceps crosses the shoulder too and helps raise the arm forward, together with coracobrachialis [M87] [M63].

At the wrist, the flexors on the palm side bend the hand forward and the extensors on the back lift it. The muscles on the thumb side also tilt the hand towards the thumb, called <Term id="radial-deviation">radial deviation</Term>; the two ulnar muscles tilt it towards the little finger. Moving straight forward or back needs both sides to pull together [M87].

<More title={{ en: "Holding still also takes work", zh: "保持不动也要用力" }}>

When you grip hard, the finger flexors also tend to bend the wrist. The wrist extensors pull against them so the wrist stays straight. A joint that does not move can still have muscles working hard on both sides of it [M72].

<Lang zh>

弯曲肘关节叫<Term id="flexion">屈</Term>，有三块肌肉分担：肱二头肌和肱桡肌拉的是桡骨，肱肌拉的是尺骨。转动手掌时尺骨不转，所以**不管怎么握，肱肌都在屈肘** [M87] [M63]。从掌心朝上换成拇指朝上或掌心朝下，肱二头肌和肱桡肌之间的分工会变，但肱肌始终在工作。

肱二头肌还有一项常被忽略的任务：**它也让掌心转向上**，这叫<Term id="supination">旋后</Term>。它的肌腱绕在桡骨上，一收缩，桡骨就绕着尺骨转。格雷的解剖学称它为有力的旋后肌，OpenStax 也把旋后和屈肘并列为它的作用 [M87] [M63]。反方向的转动叫<Term id="pronation">旋前</Term>，由旋前圆肌完成，它把桡骨拉到尺骨前面 [M87]。<FlyTo preset="close-arm">看前臂转动</FlyTo>

把肘伸直叫<Term id="extension">伸</Term>，由肱三头肌负责，肘肌稍作帮助。**肱三头肌长头既跨肘，也跨肩**，所以它还帮助把手臂往下、往后拉；外侧头和内侧头起于肱骨，只作用于肘 [M87]。肱二头肌同样跨过肩关节，和喙肱肌一起帮助把手臂向前抬 [M87] [M63]。

腕部，掌侧的屈肌把手往前弯，背侧的伸肌把手抬起。拇指侧的肌肉还能把手偏向拇指一侧，叫<Term id="radial-deviation">桡偏</Term>；两块尺侧肌肉则把手偏向小指一侧。要让手腕笔直地前后动，两侧肌肉得一起拉 [M87]。

<More title={{ en: "Holding still also takes work", zh: "保持不动也要用力" }}>

用力握东西时，屈指肌也会把腕往前拉弯。伸腕肌反向用力，手腕才保持平直。关节不动，两侧的肌肉照样可以在使劲 [M72]。
