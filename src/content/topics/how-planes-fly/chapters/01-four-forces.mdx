<Lang en>

Four forces act on an aircraft in flight. <Term id="weight">Weight</Term> pulls it towards the centre of the Earth. The aircraft, its fuel, its passengers and its cargo all contribute to its weight. As fuel is used, weight falls. Lift comes from air flowing past the wings, and thrust comes from the engines. <Term id="drag">Drag</Term> opposes the aircraft’s movement through the air.

Look at the arrows around the airliner. They show steady, level flight: the wings are level and thrust points straight ahead. Lift balances weight, and thrust balances drag. The aircraft keeps moving at a steady speed. In <TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">Gravity and weight</TopicLink>, a falling sheet of paper also reaches a steady speed when the air’s push balances its weight.

The wings and engines do different jobs. A glider has no engine, yet it has lift and drag as it moves through the air. On an airliner, the wings provide most of the lift. The engines supply thrust to overcome drag. The engines briefly disappear from the picture to help you compare their job with the wings’ job. <FlyTo preset="aircraft-overview">Look at the wings and engines</FlyTo>

The direction of the incoming air matters. Lift acts at right angles to it. Drag acts along it, opposing movement through the air. In level flight, lift points upwards. In a climb, descent or turn, lift can point in a different direction. Look at the incoming-air line beside the wing experiment to see how the directions are measured.

<More title={{ en: "Reading the four arrows", zh: "怎样看四力箭头" }}>

This picture shows level flight at steady speed, without a bank, and with thrust along the flight path. Chapter 04 shows how banking changes lift’s direction. Each pair of arrows uses its own scale: compare lift with weight, or thrust with drag. Comparing lengths between the two pairs does not tell you the forces on a real airliner.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Speed through the air", zh: "相对空气的速度" }}>

Lift and drag depend on speed relative to the surrounding air. Speed over the ground is a different measurement. In chapters 02, 03 and 06, air moves past a wing section held in place. The wing and the air still move relative to each other.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Moving with balanced forces", zh: "力平衡时的运动" }}>

Balanced forces leave motion unchanged. A still object stays still. A moving object keeps the same speed and direction. An aircraft in steady level flight is the second case. Changing speed or direction needs an unbalanced force. Forces can also balance during a steady climb.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

飞行中的飞机受到四种力。<Term id="weight">重力</Term>把飞机拉向地心。飞机本身、燃油、乘客和货物都计入重量，燃油用掉以后，重力就会减小。流过机翼的空气产生升力，发动机提供推力。<Term id="drag">阻力</Term>阻碍飞机在空气中运动。

看客机周围的箭头。这里画的是稳定的水平飞行：两翼保持水平，推力朝正前方。升力与重力平衡，推力与阻力平衡，飞机仍以不变的速度向前飞。<TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">“重力与重量”</TopicLink>里下落的纸也一样。空气的推力与重力平衡以后，纸就保持稳定的下落速度。

机翼和发动机各有作用。滑翔机没有发动机，在空气中运动时仍有升力和阻力。客机的大部分升力来自机翼，发动机则提供推力，克服阻力。画面暂时隐藏发动机，方便比较两者的作用。<FlyTo preset="aircraft-overview">看看机翼和发动机</FlyTo>

迎面而来的空气叫来流。升力垂直于来流，阻力沿着来流方向，阻碍飞机相对空气运动。水平飞行时，升力向上。飞机爬升、下降或转弯时，升力可以指向别的方向。看看机翼实验旁的来流线，就能明白这些方向从哪里量起。无论飞机还是空气在移动，关键都是它们之间的相对运动。

<More title={{ en: "Reading the four arrows", zh: "怎样看四力箭头" }}>

这里画的是速度稳定、两翼水平、推力沿着航迹的水平飞行。倾斜会怎样改变升力方向，见第 04 章。两对箭头各有自己的比例尺：升力和重力比，推力和阻力比。把两对箭头互相比长短，得不出真实客机受到的力。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Speed through the air", zh: "相对空气的速度" }}>

升力和阻力取决于飞机相对周围空气的速度。相对地面的速度是另一种量。第 02、03、06 章让翼段固定，让空气流过它，两者之间仍有相对运动。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Moving with balanced forces", zh: "力平衡时的运动" }}>

力平衡时，物体的运动状态不变。原来静止，就保持静止；原来在动，就保持同样的速度和方向。稳定水平飞行的飞机属于后一种。改变速度或方向，需要不平衡的合力。稳定爬升时，各力也可以平衡。

</More>

</Lang>
