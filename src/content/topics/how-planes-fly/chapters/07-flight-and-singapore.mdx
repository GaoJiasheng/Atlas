This chapter picks out a few dates; it is not a full history of flight. On <Num s="S17">17 December 1903</Num> the Wright brothers made the first powered, controlled and sustained flights of a heavier-than-air machine, after years of tests with gliders. Orville Wright’s first flight that day lasted <Num s="S59">12 seconds</Num>. Balloons and gliders had flown long before; what was new was the combination of an engine, control and sustained flight. The airliner on the stage is a generic comparison model, not their aircraft. <FlyTo preset="aircraft-overview">Look at the comparison model</FlyTo>

Singapore Airlines received its first A380 on <Num s="S56">15 October 2007</Num> and began commercial services with it to Sydney on <Num s="S56">25 October 2007</Num>. Taking delivery of an aircraft and starting passenger services with it are different events, with different dates.

Singapore’s civil airport has moved twice. Kallang Airport was officially opened on <Num s="S24">12 June 1937</Num>. In <Num s="S24">1955</Num> its role as the civil airport passed to Paya Lebar Airport, officially opened on <Num s="S25">20 August 1955</Num>. Changi Airport began commercial operations on <Num s="S22">1 July 1981</Num> and was officially opened on <Num s="S22">29 December</Num> that year: an airport can start working before its opening ceremony.

Before Changi was chosen, planners also considered expanding Paya Lebar. They weighed capacity, land for growth, flight paths and the noise of aircraft over homes. Building Changi had a cost on the ground as well. Site preparation, which began in <Num s="S22">1975</Num>, involved demolishing <Num s="S22,S23">558</Num> buildings and exhuming <Num s="S22,S23">4,096</Num> graves. These numbers count buildings and graves, not people, and the work was not all done at once.

The full date is <Num s="S17">17 December 1903</Num>, at the Wright brothers’ camp on the coast of North Carolina in the United States. Their first flights followed several years of glider tests. People had already gone up in balloons and flown gliders; the Wrights’ achievement was a machine heavier than air that took off under its own power, could be steered and kept flying.

Kallang Airport was officially opened on <Num s="S24">12 June 1937</Num>, and Paya Lebar on <Num s="S25">20 August 1955</Num>. For Changi, the start of commercial operations on <Num s="S22">1 July 1981</Num> and the official opening on <Num s="S22">29 December 1981</Num> are two different events. The list in this chapter follows Singapore’s civil airport; it is not a list of every airfield there has been on the island.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why airports move", zh: "机场为什么搬迁" }}>

An airport has to grow with the number of flights, needs land around it for new runways and buildings, and sends aircraft over the places under its flight paths. Those reasons appear in the planning for Changi. So does the cost: the buildings that were demolished and the graves that were exhumed to make way for it. The counts come from government and National Library sources; they are given as counts of buildings and graves because that is what the sources count.
