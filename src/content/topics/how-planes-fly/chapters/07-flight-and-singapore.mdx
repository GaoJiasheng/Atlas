On <Num s="S17">17 December 1903</Num>, the Wright brothers made the first powered, controlled and sustained flights in a heavier-than-air machine. Orville Wright’s first flight that day lasted <Num s="S59">12 seconds</Num>. The brothers had spent years testing gliders. Balloons and gliders had already flown; their achievement combined an engine, control and sustained flight. More than a century later, Singapore Airlines received its first A380 on <Num s="S56">15 October 2007</Num>. It began commercial services to Sydney on <Num s="S56">25 October 2007</Num>. The two dates mark delivery and passenger service.

Singapore’s civil airport has moved twice. Kallang Airport officially opened on <Num s="S24">12 June 1937</Num>. In <Num s="S24">1955</Num>, its role passed to Paya Lebar Airport, which officially opened on <Num s="S25">20 August 1955</Num>. Changi began commercial operations on <Num s="S22">1 July 1981</Num>. Its official opening followed on <Num s="S22">29 December</Num> that year. Flights can begin before an airport’s opening ceremony.

Planners considered expanding Paya Lebar as well as moving to Changi. They weighed capacity, land for growth, flight paths and aircraft noise over homes. Building Changi also affected places on the ground. Site preparation began in <Num s="S22">1975</Num>. The work involved demolishing <Num s="S22,S23">558</Num> buildings and exhuming <Num s="S22,S23">4,096</Num> graves: digging up graves to move the remains. These count buildings and graves, not people. The work took place over time.

More flights can require more airport capacity. New runways and buildings need land, while flight paths bring aircraft noise over nearby places.
