<Lang en>

Look at the airliner. It has two engines under its wings and a tail at the back. The wings provide most of its <Term id="lift">lift</Term>, the force from the air that holds it up in level flight. The engines provide <Term id="thrust">thrust</Term>, which pushes it forwards. This is a general example of an airliner, with no particular maker or model. <FlyTo preset="aircraft-overview">Start with the whole aircraft</FlyTo>

Beside the aircraft is a short slice of wing. Its cross-section is called an <Term id="airfoil">airfoil</Term>. Here, it has a simple mathematical shape. Air flows past it while it stays in place. Change the speed or tilt the wing to see how its lift changes. The readings marked SIM are calculated by the computer. <FlyTo preset="section-view">Look at the experiment</FlyTo>

The third model is an engine cut open along its length. A large fan sits at the front, with a core behind it. Shafts connect the machinery inside. Some air passes through the core, and some flows around it. Follow the two routes to see how the engine produces thrust. <FlyTo preset="engine-section-view">Open the engine</FlyTo>

Each model has its own scale. The whole aircraft shows where the wings, engines and tail sit. The wing experiment shows airflow close up. The engine cutaway shows parts hidden inside its casing. Solid lines show calculated airflow; dashed lines show a simple picture of air breaking away from the wing. Chapter 03 explains this change.

Explore pushes, pulls and gravity in <TopicLink slug="forces">Forces</TopicLink>. The same ideas explain how an aircraft moves through the air.

<More title={{ en: "A simplified view of air", zh: "把气流简化来看" }}>

The experiment treats air as having no stickiness and the wing as having no ends. This is called ideal two-dimensional flow. It shows how a wing turns the air, but cannot calculate air breaking away from its surface. Beyond <Num s="S58">12°</Num> of tilt, a simple rule supplies the dashed part of the curve. The fans and shafts turn at <Num s="S58">30 rpm</Num> and <Num s="S58">45 rpm</Num> on screen, slowed down so you can follow them.

</More>

The wing experiment offers two views of the same flow. Streamlines follow the air’s direction. The pressure view shows how the air pushes on the wing’s surface. Switch between them while keeping the inputs fixed.

</More>

<Lang zh>

先看这架客机。两台发动机装在机翼下方，尾翼位于机身后部。机翼提供大部分<Term id="lift">升力</Term>。水平飞行时，空气给机翼的这个力托住飞机。发动机提供<Term id="thrust">推力</Term>，推动飞机向前。这是客机的一种常见外形，不代表某个厂家或型号。<FlyTo preset="aircraft-overview">先看整架飞机</FlyTo>

客机旁边放着一小段机翼。机翼剖面的形状叫<Term id="airfoil">翼型</Term>，这里选用的是一种简单的数学形状。翼段固定不动，空气从它旁边流过。改变速度，或把翼段转动一些，就能观察升力怎样变化。标着“模拟”的读数由计算机算出。<FlyTo preset="section-view">看看这个实验</FlyTo>

第三个模型是一台沿长度方向剖开的发动机。前面是大风扇，后面是核心，里面的轴连接着不同的机械部件。有些空气穿过核心，有些从核心外面绕过。沿着这两条路线看，就能了解发动机怎样产生推力。<FlyTo preset="engine-section-view">打开发动机</FlyTo>

几个模型各有各的比例。整架客机让你看清机翼、发动机和尾翼的位置。机翼实验让你近看气流，发动机剖面则展示外壳里面的零件。实线表示计算出的气流，虚线示意空气离开翼面的情形。第 03 章会说明这个变化。

<TopicLink slug="forces">“力”</TopicLink>介绍了推、拉和重力。同样的道理也能解释飞机怎样在空气中运动。

<More title={{ en: "A simplified view of air", zh: "把气流简化来看" }}>

实验把空气看成完全没有黏性，把机翼看成没有两端。这叫理想二维流。它能说明机翼怎样让空气转向，却算不出空气离开翼面后的情形。翼段倾斜超过 <Num s="S58">12°</Num> 时，曲线的虚线部分改用简单规则算出。画面中风扇和轴的转速是 <Num s="S58">30 rpm</Num> 和 <Num s="S58">45 rpm</Num>，已放慢，方便观察。

</More>
