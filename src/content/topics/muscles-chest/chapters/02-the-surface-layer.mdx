<Lang en>

With the other muscles faded, pectoralis major stands alone on the bones. It is one muscle in three parts. The **clavicular part** starts on the front of the inner half of the collarbone. The **sternocostal part**, the largest, starts on the front of the breastbone and the <Term id="costal-cartilage">costal cartilages</Term> beside it. The small **abdominal part** starts on the <Term id="aponeurosis">aponeurosis</Term> of the external oblique, the flat tendon sheet at the top of the belly wall [M1] [M2].

All three converge on one flat tendon about <Num s="M1">5 cm</Num> wide, which ends on a ridge just below the head of the humerus [M1]. A muscle shaped like this, with fibres fanning in from a wide start to a narrow end, is a <Term id="fan-shaped-muscle">fan-shaped muscle</Term>: each fibre pulls in a slightly different direction.

The tendon is two layers. The collarbone and upper breastbone fibres make the front layer and end lowest on the bone. The lower fibres turn behind them and end highest, so the tendon looks twisted [M1]. <FlyTo preset="close-pec">See the tendon close up</FlyTo>

This explains a common gym idea. There is no separate "inner chest": every fibre runs from its start on the collarbone, breastbone or rib cartilages all the way to the arm [M1]. Squeezing your hands together works the whole fan. What changes from one exercise to another is which part is pulling hardest, which depends on where the arm starts [M5].

<More title={{ en: "Gym names and anatomical names", zh: "健身俗名与解剖学名" }}>

Upper chest roughly means the clavicular part, middle chest the sternocostal part and lower chest its lowest fibres with the abdominal part. These are useful as places on the body, but they share one tendon, so none of them can be trained completely on its own.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why the twist matters", zh: "止腱为什么扭转" }}>

Because the lowest fibres wrap behind the others, they are the ones stretched most when the arm is pulled far back, as at the bottom of a deep bench press. A cadaver study found these short lower fibres lengthen the most in the final part of that movement [M15]. Chapter 6 comes back to this.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

把其他肌肉调淡后，骨骼上只剩下胸大肌。它是一块肌肉，分三部分。**锁骨部**起于锁骨内侧半的前面。**胸肋部**面积最大，起于胸骨前面和旁边的<Term id="costal-cartilage">肋软骨</Term>。小小的**腹部**起于腹外斜肌的<Term id="aponeurosis">腱膜</Term>，也就是腹壁上端那层扁平的腱性薄膜 [M1] [M2]。

三部分汇聚成一条约 <Num s="M1">5 厘米</Num>宽的扁平止腱，止在肱骨头正下方的一条骨嵴上 [M1]。像这样从宽阔的起点向狭窄的止点收拢的肌肉，叫<Term id="fan-shaped-muscle">扇形肌</Term>：每一根纤维拉的方向都略有不同。

这条止腱分前后两层。锁骨和胸骨上部的纤维组成前层，在骨上止得最低；下部的纤维转到它们后面，止得最高，所以止腱看起来是扭转的 [M1]。<FlyTo preset="close-pec">近看止腱</FlyTo>

这也回答了健身房里常听到的一种说法：并没有一块单独的“内胸”。每一根纤维都从锁骨、胸骨或肋软骨上的起点，一直长到上臂 [M1]。双手往中间夹，练到的是整把扇子。不同动作之间真正变化的，是哪一部分拉得最用力，这取决于手臂从什么位置开始动 [M5]。

<More title={{ en: "Gym names and anatomical names", zh: "健身俗名与解剖学名" }}>

“上胸”大致指锁骨部，“中胸”指胸肋部，“下胸”指胸肋部最下面的纤维加上腹部。作为身体上的位置，这些叫法很好用；但它们共用一条止腱，所以哪一部分都不可能被完全单独地练到。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why the twist matters", zh: "止腱为什么扭转" }}>

最下面的纤维绕到了其他纤维后面，所以当手臂被拉到很靠后的位置，比如很深的卧推最低点时，被拉长最多的就是它们。一项尸体研究发现，在这个动作的最后一段，这些下部短纤维拉长得最多 [M15]。第 6 章会再讲到这一点。

</More>

</Lang>
