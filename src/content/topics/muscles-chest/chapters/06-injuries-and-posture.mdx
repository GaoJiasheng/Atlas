<Lang en>

The best-known chest injury in the gym is a tear of pectoralis major, usually at or near its tendon on the humerus. It is rare. A review in 2012 found <Num s="M14">365</Num> cases reported worldwide since 1822, <Num s="M14">75 %</Num> of them in the previous 20 years and <Num s="M14">48 %</Num> during weight training [M14]. Most happened during a heavy lift, often as the bar was lowered.

The twisted tendon from chapter 2 helps explain why. In a cadaver study, the short lowest fibres stretched the most in the final <Num s="M15">30°</Num> of the arm moving back, which is the bottom of a bench press [M15]. That is the point to lower slowly, use safety bars or a spotter, and stop if you feel sharp pain or a sudden tearing sensation. <FlyTo preset="close-pec">See the tendon</FlyTo>

Deep dips and very deep flies stretch the front of the shoulder under load. A comfortable depth that you can reverse smoothly is enough to train the chest.

"Rounded shoulders" are often blamed on a tight pectoralis minor. In <Num s="M12">50</Num> pain-free people, those with a shorter pectoralis minor did keep the shoulder blade tipped further forward as the arm rose [M12]. But a review in 2026 found that none of the tests used to measure its length has enough evidence behind it [M13]. A slouched posture on its own says little about this muscle, and it is not a diagnosis.

Strength training helps more than it harms: across <Num s="M27">25</Num> trials, strength training cut sports injuries to about a third [M27]. This chapter is general information, not medical advice. For pain or an injury, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

<More title={{ en: "Lowering is where control matters", zh: "下放时最需要控制" }}>

The lowering half of a lift is the <Term id="eccentric-phase">eccentric phase</Term>: the muscle is working while it lengthens. Lowering the bar slowly, pausing lightly on the chest instead of bouncing, and never training heavy without a way to escape a failed repetition keep the stretched fibres under control.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What this chapter does not do", zh: "这一章不做什么" }}>

It does not diagnose. Pain at the front of the shoulder, a lump or bruise in the armpit after a lift, a shoulder blade that sticks out, or weakness are reasons to see a professional, not to stretch or train through.

</More>

健身房里最为人知的胸部伤，是胸大肌撕裂，通常发生在它肱骨止腱处或附近。这种伤很少见。2012 年的一篇综述找到自 1822 年以来全世界报告的 <Num s="M14">365</Num> 例，其中 <Num s="M14">75 %</Num> 在此前 20 年里，<Num s="M14">48 %</Num> 发生在力量训练中 [M14]。大多发生在大重量练习中，常常是在杠铃下放的时候。

第 2 章讲过的扭转止腱，有助于解释原因。一项尸体研究发现，手臂向后移动的最后 <Num s="M15">30°</Num> 里，最下面的短纤维被拉长得最多，这正是卧推的最低点 [M15]。所以在这里要慢慢下放、用保护杠或请人保护；一旦感到刺痛或突然的撕裂感就停下。<FlyTo preset="close-pec">看止腱</FlyTo>

很深的双杠臂屈伸和很深的飞鸟，会在负重下拉伸肩前部。练胸只需要一个舒服、能平稳推回的幅度。

“圆肩”常被归咎于胸小肌太紧。在 <Num s="M12">50</Num> 名没有疼痛的人中，胸小肌较短的人抬臂时，肩胛骨确实更向前倾 [M12]。但 2026 年的一篇综述发现，现有测胸小肌长度的方法，证据都还不够充分 [M13]。只看含胸的姿势，说明不了这块肌肉怎么样，更不能当作诊断。

力量训练利大于弊：综合 <Num s="M27">25</Num> 项试验，力量训练让运动损伤减少到约三分之一 [M27]。本章是一般性知识，不是医疗建议。有疼痛或受伤，请咨询医生或物理治疗师。

<More title={{ en: "Lowering is where control matters", zh: "下放时最需要控制" }}>

动作的下放那一半叫<Term id="eccentric-phase">离心阶段</Term>：肌肉一边用力一边被拉长。慢慢下放杠铃，在胸口轻轻停一下而不是砸胸反弹，没有保护措施时不做大重量，这些都能让被拉长的纤维保持在控制之中。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What this chapter does not do", zh: "这一章不做什么" }}>

它不做诊断。肩前部疼痛、练完后腋窝出现肿块或淤青、肩胛骨翘起、手臂无力，都应该去看专业人员，而不是继续拉伸或硬练。
