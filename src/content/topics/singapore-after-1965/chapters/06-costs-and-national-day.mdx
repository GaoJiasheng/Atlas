Earlier chapters have already shown choices that some people opposed: young men who would not serve (chapter 01), the labour laws and the election of 1968 (chapter 04), and the end of dialect programmes (chapter 05). This chapter adds two more: newspapers, and detention without trial. For each it gives what the government said, what those on the other side said, and, where there is one, what a historian has written. It does not decide between them.

On 2 May 1971 four senior staff of the Chinese newspaper Nanyang Siang Pau were detained under the <Term id="internal-security-act">Internal Security Act</Term>, which allows detention without trial. The government said the paper had glamorised communism and stirred up communal feeling; in June the four denied this in sworn statements. On 28 May the government revoked the printing licence of the English Singapore Herald. In 1974 Parliament passed the Newspaper and Printing Presses Act, and in 1986 an amendment allowed the government to restrict foreign publications.

In May and June 1987 <Num s="S461">22</Num> people, many of them active in Catholic church groups, were detained without trial. The government called it a Marxist conspiracy. In April 1988 nine of them, by then released, issued a statement denying it and describing their treatment in detention; eight were detained again. In 1989 the law was changed so that courts could review such detentions only on questions of procedure. A historian who later studied the case, Michael Barr, concluded that there was no conspiracy.

The chapter ends with the days that schools mark today. National Day began with the parade on the Padang on 9 August 1966, and every school day still begins with the Pledge.

<More title={{ en: "Newspapers and the law: two accounts", zh: "报纸与法律：两种说法" }}>

**What the government said.** In May 1971 it said that the Nanyang Siang Pau was “glamorising the communist system” and working up communal emotions, and on 22 May it stated that the four detained men had admitted this. It revoked the Singapore Herald's printing licence on 28 May 1971. The Newspaper and Printing Presses Act, passed on 28 August 1974, came into force on 1 January 1975. In 1986 the government said that an amendment was needed to stop slanted reporting by foreign publications.

**What others said.** In June 1971 all four Nanyang Siang Pau men denied the government's statement in sworn affidavits. After the 1986 amendment was introduced, foreign newspapers criticised it, one writing that the country was “veering towards authoritarianism”.

前面几章已经写到一些人反对的选择：不愿服役的男青年（第 01 章）、1968 年的劳工法令和大选（第 04 章）、停播方言节目（第 05 章）。这一章再写两件事：报纸，以及未经审判的拘留。每一件都写政府怎么说、另一方怎么说，有史家研究的，也写史家怎么看，不替读者下结论。

1971 年 5 月 2 日，华文报《南洋商报》四名高层依<Term id="internal-security-act">《内部安全法令》</Term>被拘留；这部法律允许不经审判拘留。政府说，该报美化共产主义、煽动种族情绪；6 月，四人发誓章否认。5 月 28 日，政府吊销英文报《新加坡先驱报》的印刷执照。1974 年，国会通过《报章与印刷新闻业法令》；1986 年的修正，让政府可以限制外国刊物。

1987 年 5 月和 6 月，<Num s="S461">22</Num> 人未经审判被拘留，其中不少人活跃于天主教会的团体。政府称这是马克思主义阴谋。1988 年 4 月，其中九名已获释的人发表声明，否认有阴谋，并描述了拘留期间受到的对待；其中八人再次被拘留。1989 年，法律作了修改，法院对这类拘留只能审查程序。后来研究这件事的史家 Michael Barr 认为，并没有什么阴谋。

这一章最后写学校今天纪念的日子。国庆日从 1966 年 8 月 9 日在大草场的检阅开始；每个上课日，学生仍以诵读信约开始。

**政府的说法**：1971 年 5 月，政府说《南洋商报》“美化共产制度”、煽动种族情绪；5 月 22 日，政府说被拘留的四人已经承认。1971 年 5 月 28 日，政府吊销《新加坡先驱报》的印刷执照。《报章与印刷新闻业法令》1974 年 8 月 28 日通过，1975 年 1 月 1 日生效。1986 年，政府说必须修改法令，制止外国刊物的偏颇报道。

**另一方的说法**：1971 年 6 月，《南洋商报》的四人分别发誓章，否认政府的说法。1986 年修正案提出后，外国报刊提出批评，其中一份说这个国家“正走向威权”。
