<Lang en>

On 9 August 1965 Singapore left Malaysia and became an independent country. Why it happened, and how the governments in Kuala Lumpur and Singapore each explained it, is told in <TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="separation">Singapore, 1819–1965: riots and separation</TopicLink>, which also lists the first Cabinet of the Republic.

The new country faced three questions, and they are the three threads of this topic. How would it defend itself? It had <Num s="S421">two</Num> infantry battalions of about <Num s="S421">1,000</Num> men, <Num s="S421">two</Num> ships and no air force. Indonesia's <Term id="konfrontasi">Confrontation</Term> against Malaysia had not ended, and Britain still kept large bases on the island. How would people earn a living? In 1966 <Num s="S433">8.9</Num> per cent of the workforce had no job, and many families depended on work at the British bases. How would people of many races, languages and religions live together as one country? The riots of 1964 were only a year old, and the flag, crest and anthem had been in use only since 1959.

Each chapter follows one of these threads, from the first years after 1965 to the commemorative days that schools mark today. Where people disagreed about a decision, the chapter gives each side in its own words.

The map marks places as points. Where a decision had no single place, such as a law for the whole country or a national campaign, the point stands at the centre of the island. Look at the <FlyTo preset="island">whole island</FlyTo>, the <FlyTo preset="west">west</FlyTo>, where the first soldiers trained, and the <FlyTo preset="civic">Civic District</FlyTo>, where the national symbols were first used.

<More title={{ en: "Why numbers often come in pairs", zh: "数字为什么常常有两个" }}>

Different official sources sometimes give different numbers for the same thing. For the number of people out of work in 1959, the National Heritage Board gives <Num s="S434">200,000</Num>, while a National Library Board article gives <Num s="S435">46,000</Num>. Because the gap is so large, this topic prints neither in the chapters and says only what another government source says: unemployment was “in the double digits”. For Singapore's navy at independence, the Ministry of Defence says “two ships”; SG101 says “two wooden hull boats”. Where two sources differ, this topic shows both, each with its source.

1965 年 8 月 9 日，新加坡脱离马来西亚，成为独立国家。为什么会分家，吉隆坡和新加坡两地政府各怎么说，见<TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="separation">《新加坡 1819–1965》的“骚乱与分家”</TopicLink>；那一章也列出了共和国第一届内阁。
