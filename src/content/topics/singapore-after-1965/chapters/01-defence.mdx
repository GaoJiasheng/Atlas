<Lang en>

After separation, Singapore had to defend itself, and in 1967 Britain announced that its forces would leave. The country could not pay for a large army of full-time soldiers. Its planners chose another way, described in a National Library article as making sure that “despite our small population, we could mobilise a large fighting force at short notice”. Goh Keng Swee was in charge of defence. He looked abroad for instructors, and in the end persuaded Israel to send military advisers.

The first step was to train the trainers. On 14 February 1966 the Singapore Armed Forces Training Institute, <Term id="safti">SAFTI</Term>, began its first course at Pasir Laba Camp, for <Num s="S422">60</Num> officers and NCOs. On 16 July 1967 its first <Num s="S422">117</Num> officer cadets were commissioned into the new <Term id="saf">Singapore Armed Forces</Term>.

<Term id="national-service">National Service</Term> began in 1967. Parliament passed the National Service (Amendment) Act on 14 March. Young men born in the first half of 1949 had to register, or face up to six months in jail or a fine of <Num s="S420">2,000</Num> dollars. About <Num s="S421">9,000</Num> registered. On 17 August about <Num s="S425">900</Num> of them began two years of full-time service at Taman Jurong Camp, in blocks of flats turned into barracks; the others served part-time. Not everyone welcomed it: the Ministry of Defence says most people had long thought little of soldiering.

By 1971 there was an air defence command, which started with <Num s="S421">eight</Num> small training aircraft, and a navy. The first Armed Forces Day was held in 1969. In 1970 the Enlistment Act made National Service permanent. Today <Term id="nsman">NSmen</Term> serve in the armed forces, the police and the civil defence force.

<More title={{ en: "Numbers that differ: 9,000 or 900?", zh: "说法不一的数字：9,000 人还是 900 人？" }}>

A BiblioAsia article says that “the first batch of 9,000” young men were enlisted on 17 August 1967. The Ministry of Defence says <Num s="S421">9,000</Num> registered and “only the top 10%” began full-time training, <Num s="S421">450</Num> in each of two battalions; the Prime Minister's Office also speaks of “the first <Num s="S425">900</Num>”. This topic follows the second account. The same article gives 17 March 1967 for the passing of the Act, while the Ministry of Defence, SG101 and the National Heritage Board give 14 March. And for the navy at independence, the Ministry of Defence counts <Num s="S421">two</Num> ships, while SG101 speaks of <Num s="S419">two</Num> wooden-hulled boats.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Goh Keng Swee", zh: "吴庆瑞" }}>

When the new Cabinet was announced on 9 August 1965, the Straits Times called Goh Keng Swee “Minister for Defence and Security”; the Ministry of Defence's history today says that the Ministry of the Interior and Defence was set up in August 1965 with him as its first minister. Both titles are in use. On 17 August 1967 he and Lim Kim San swapped posts, and Goh became Minister for Finance. On 11 August 1970 he took charge of defence again, and Hon Sui Sen took over finance. In 1970 the ministry was split into a Ministry of Defence and a Ministry of Home Affairs. Critics once called the Jurong industrial estate “Goh's Folly” (chapter 04).

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Those who did not want to serve", zh: "不愿服役的人" }}>

National Service was not new to Singapore. In 1954 the British colonial government had brought in a National Service law, and on 13 May 1954 about <Num s="S419">500</Num> students from Chinese schools protested against it. They clashed with the police; the National Heritage Board's account gives <Num s="S420">26</Num> injured and <Num s="S420">45</Num> arrested. In March 1967 there were protests again, which a BiblioAsia article describes as “not as serious as the riots that took place in 1954”.

The law has no exception for people who refuse to serve on grounds of conscience or religion. On 14 January 1972 the government de-registered the Singapore congregation of Jehovah's Witnesses; the Ministry of Home Affairs said then that its continued existence was “prejudicial to public welfare and good order”. A United States government report says that Jehovah's Witnesses refuse military service for religious reasons and that, according to the Witnesses' own website, <Num s="S428">11</Num> of them were held in the armed forces' detention barracks at the end of 2023 (<Num s="S428">14</Num> in 2022).

分家以后，新加坡必须自己保卫自己；1967 年，英国又宣布要撤军。国家养不起一支庞大的职业军队，规划的人选了另一条路。国家图书馆管理局的一篇文章这样形容这个想法：“人口虽少，也能在短时间内动员大批兵力。”负责国防的是吴庆瑞。他向外国寻找教官，最后说服以色列派军事顾问来协助训练。

第一步是训练教官。1966 年 2 月 14 日，<Term id="safti">新加坡武装部队军训学院</Term>在巴西拉峇军营开办第一期课程，学员是 <Num s="S422">60</Num> 名军官和士官。1967 年 7 月 16 日，首批 <Num s="S422">117</Num> 名军官学员授衔，加入新成立的<Term id="saf">新加坡武装部队</Term>。

<Term id="national-service">国民服役</Term>从 1967 年开始。国会在 3 月 14 日通过《国民服役（修正）法令》。1949 年上半年出生的男青年必须登记，否则可被判监最多六个月，或罚款 <Num s="S420">2,000</Num> 元。约 <Num s="S421">9,000</Num> 人登记。8 月 17 日，其中约 <Num s="S425">900</Num> 人在 Taman Jurong 军营开始两年全职服役，营房是改装过的组屋；其余的人部分时间服役。并不是人人都欢迎：国防部说，当时大多数人一向看低当兵。

到 1971 年，新加坡已有空防司令部，起初只有 <Num s="S421">8</Num> 架小型教练机，也有了海军。1969 年举行第一个武装部队日。1970 年的《征兵法令》让国民服役固定下来。今天，<Term id="nsman">战备军人</Term>在武装部队、警察部队和民防部队服役。

<More title={{ en: "Goh Keng Swee", zh: "吴庆瑞" }}>

1965 年 8 月 9 日公布新内阁时，《海峡时报》称吴庆瑞为“国防与安全部长”；国防部今天的历史网页则说，内政与国防部在 1965 年 8 月成立，他是第一任部长。两种职称都有人用。1967 年 8 月 17 日，他与林金山对调，改任财政部长。1970 年 8 月 11 日，他再度主管国防，财政部长由韩瑞生接任。同一年，内政与国防部分为国防部和内政部。裕廊工业区曾被批评者叫作“吴庆瑞的傻事”（见第 04 章）。

<More title={{ en: "Those who did not want to serve", zh: "不愿服役的人" }}>

国民服役在新加坡并不是新事。1954 年，英国殖民地政府推出国民服役法令；5 月 13 日，约 <Num s="S419">500</Num> 名华校学生起来抗议，与警察发生冲突。据国家文物局的记述，<Num s="S420">26</Num> 人受伤，<Num s="S420">45</Num> 人被捕。1967 年 3 月又有抗议，《BiblioAsia》的文章说，“不如 1954 年的骚乱严重”。

法律没有为基于良心或宗教理由拒绝服役的人设例外。1972 年 1 月 14 日，政府撤销耶和华见证人新加坡会众的注册；内政部当时说，这个团体继续存在“有损公共福利与良好秩序”。美国政府的一份报告说，耶和华见证人出于宗教理由拒绝服兵役；报告又引述见证人自己的网站说，2023 年底有 <Num s="S428">11</Num> 名信徒被关在武装部队拘留所（2022 年是 <Num s="S428">14</Num> 人）。
