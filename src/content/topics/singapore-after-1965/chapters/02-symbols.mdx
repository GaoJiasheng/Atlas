<Lang en>

On 3 December 1959, at City Hall, Yusof Ishak was installed as Singapore's head of state, and a new flag, crest and anthem were used for the first time. The Legislative Assembly had approved them on 18 November, and a committee led by Toh Chin Chye had worked on the flag and crest. When Singapore became independent in 1965, they became the national flag, the national coat of arms (also called the State Crest) and the national anthem: three of today's <Term id="national-symbols">national symbols</Term>.

Each has a meaning. On the flag, red stands for universal fellowship and equality, white for purity and virtue; the crescent moon is a young nation on the rise, and the <Num s="S411">five</Num> stars are democracy, peace, progress, justice and equality. On the crest, the lion stands for Singapore and the tiger for its historical ties with Malaysia. The anthem, “Majulah Singapura” (“Onward Singapore”), was written in Malay by Zubir Said in 1958.

In 1966 came the <Term id="national-pledge">National Pledge</Term>. It was proposed in the Ministry of Education in October 1965; S. Rajaratnam, the foreign minister, rewrote a draft in February 1966. On 24 August about <Num s="S413">500,000</Num> pupils in <Num s="S413">529</Num> schools recited it for the first time. That month also saw the first National Day Parade, on the Padang.

Three more symbols followed: the national flower, an <Term id="orchid">orchid</Term>, in 1981; the Lion Head symbol in 1986; and the <Term id="public-seal">Public Seal</Term>. A law that came into force on 1 August 2023 names all <Num s="S411">seven</Num>. There is no national dress among them. The law also sets rules for using the symbols, so this topic describes them in words and does not draw them.

<More title={{ en: "The seven national symbols", zh: "七个国家象征" }}>

| Symbol | Since | What it shows or means |
|---|---|---|
| National flag | 1959; national flag from 1965 | red and white, a crescent and five stars |
| National coat of arms (State Crest) | 1959; national from 1965 | a lion and a tiger holding a shield; the motto Majulah Singapura |
| National anthem | 1959; national from 1965 | “Majulah Singapura” by Zubir Said; a new arrangement from 19 January 2001 |
| National Pledge | 24 August 1966 | a promise to build a united, just and equal society |
| National flower | 15 April 1981 | Vanda Miss Joaquim, bred in Singapore by Agnes Joaquim in 1893 |
| Lion Head symbol | 1986 | for everyone to use; the five partings of the mane stand for the flag's five ideals |
| Public Seal | named as a symbol by the 2022 Act | the coat of arms inside the words “Republic of Singapore”; kept by the President for state documents |

The National Symbols Act 2022 and the Ministry of Education's syllabus count seven. The Ministry of Culture, Community and Youth's web page still speaks of six and leaves out the Public Seal. No source read for this topic gives the year the Public Seal was first used.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How the Pledge was written", zh: "信约是怎样写成的" }}>

In October 1965 William Cheng, an official of the Ministry of Education, suggested that pupils should take a pledge. The Minister for Education, Ong Pang Boon, asked Philip Liau and George Thomson to write drafts. S. Rajaratnam sent back a rewritten version in February 1966. Officials of the ministry and the Prime Minister, Lee Kuan Yew, changed it further, and the Cabinet approved it. One phrase changed along the way:

| Earlier draft | The Pledge recited today |
|---|---|
| “forget differences of race, language and religion” | “regardless of race, language or religion” |

Pupils at first raised the right hand. From 1988 they have held the right fist over the heart, because the Ministry of Defence wanted one way of taking the Pledge for everyone. One National Library page says the Pledge was written “just after” independence; the National Archives and the National Heritage Board give the steps above.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What the red stands for: two wordings", zh: "红色代表什么：两种写法" }}>

The National Heritage Board's older wording says that red stands for “universal brotherhood and equality of man”. The law of 2022 says “universal fellowship and equality”; the Bill's explanatory statement says the description was updated “so as to be gender neutral”. The meaning is the same; this topic uses the wording of the law.

</More>

<Lang zh>

1959 年 12 月 3 日，尤索夫伊萨在政府大厦就任新加坡元首，新的旗、徽、歌在典礼上第一次使用。立法议院在 11 月 18 日通过了它们；旗和徽由杜进才领导的委员会设计。当时称为邦旗、邦徽、邦歌。1965 年新加坡独立后，它们成为国旗、国徽和国歌，也就是今天<Term id="national-symbols">国家象征</Term>中的三个。

每一个都有意思。国旗上，红色代表普世的友爱与平等，白色代表纯洁与美德；新月代表一个正在上升的年轻国家，<Num s="S411">五</Num>颗星代表民主、和平、进步、正义和平等。国徽上，狮子代表新加坡，老虎代表新加坡与马来西亚的历史渊源。国歌《前进吧，新加坡！》是朱比赛在 1958 年用马来文写的。

1966 年有了<Term id="national-pledge">信约</Term>。教育部在 1965 年 10 月提出这个构想；1966 年 2 月，外交部长拉惹勒南改写了草稿。8 月 24 日，<Num s="S413">529</Num> 所学校约 <Num s="S413">50 万</Num>名学生第一次诵读。同一个月，第一次国庆检阅在大草场举行。

后来又有三个：1981 年的国花，是一种<Term id="orchid">胡姬</Term>；1986 年的狮头标志；还有<Term id="public-seal">公共印章</Term>。2023 年 8 月 1 日生效的法令，把<Num s="S411">七</Num>个国家象征都列了出来。其中没有“国服”。法令也规定了这些象征的使用办法，所以本主题只用文字介绍，不画出来。

<More title={{ en: "How the Pledge was written", zh: "信约是怎样写成的" }}>

1965 年 10 月，教育部官员 William Cheng 提议让学生宣誓。教育部长王邦文请 Philip Liau 和 George Thomson 起草。1966 年 2 月，拉惹勒南寄回改写稿。教育部官员和李光耀总理又作了修改，内阁批准。改动中有一句是这样变的：

| 早先的草稿 | 今天诵读的信约 |
|---|---|
| “忘记种族、语言和宗教的差别” | “不分种族、言语、宗教” |

学生起初举起右手宣誓；1988 年起改为右手握拳放在心口，因为国防部希望大家用同一种姿势。国家图书馆管理局有一个网页说，信约是独立后“不久”写成的；国家档案馆和国家文物局写的是上面这些步骤。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What the red stands for: two wordings", zh: "红色代表什么：两种写法" }}>

国家文物局的旧写法说，红色代表“人类普遍的兄弟情谊与平等”。2022 年的法令写的是“普世的友爱与平等”；法案说明书说，这是为了让描述“不分性别”。意思没有变，本主题用法令的写法。

</More>
