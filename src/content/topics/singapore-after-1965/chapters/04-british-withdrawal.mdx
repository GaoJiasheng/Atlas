<Lang en>

On 18 July 1967 Britain announced that it would withdraw its forces from Singapore by the mid-1970s. On 16 January 1968 it brought the date forward to 1971. The bases produced over <Num s="S430">20</Num> per cent of Singapore's national income, and tens of thousands of people worked for the British forces, as civilian staff or as domestic helpers in British homes.

The Economic Development Board, set up in 1961 on the advice of a United Nations team led by Albert Winsemius, had begun an industrial estate at Jurong; how swamp became factory land is in <TopicLink slug="singapore-land-use" ch="new-towns">Singapore's Land: new towns and industrial estates</TopicLink>. A law of 1967 gave new factories years of lower taxes. Foreign companies came: in 1968 the American firm National Semiconductor was in business in Singapore within two weeks of its first visit. On 1 June 1968 the Jurong Town Corporation took over the estates, and from September 1968 people could use their savings in the Central Provident Fund to buy flats (<TopicLink slug="singapore-land-use" ch="crowded-city">Singapore's Land: the first flats</TopicLink>).

In April 1968, after the news of the withdrawal, there was a general election. The main opposition party, the Barisan Sosialis, boycotted it; only <Num s="S430">seven</Num> of the <Num s="S430">58</Num> seats were contested, and the People's Action Party won every seat. In July and August Parliament passed two labour laws. The government said they would return the right to manage to employers and bring investment; union leaders in Parliament raised concerns.

By the government's figures, unemployment fell from <Num s="S433">8.9</Num> per cent in 1966 to <Num s="S433">4.5</Num> per cent in 1973, and the economy grew by about <Num s="S433">13</Num> per cent a year. By October 1971 most British troops had left, and their air bases passed to Singapore's air defence command. The last British soldier left in March 1976.

<More title={{ en: "How many jobs were there at the bases?", zh: "基地里有多少份工作？" }}>

The National Library Board gives two accounts in two articles. One says that about <Num s="S430">25,000</Num> base workers would lose their jobs in 1971. The other says the British forces employed <Num s="S435">30,000</Num> civilians and <Num s="S435">10,000</Num> domestic helpers, and that about half were expected to lose their jobs. The two counts may include different people, so both are given. Other figures often quoted are left out: the number of people out of work in 1959 (two official sources differ by more than four times) and the size of manufacturing in 1965 (government publications give different shares).

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The labour laws of 1968: two views", zh: "1968 年的劳工法令：两种看法" }}>

The Employment Act and the Industrial Relations (Amendment) Act set out working conditions and gave employers more say in how work was managed. The government said that they would “restore the rights of management to the employers”, that strikes and stoppages had cost <Num s="S435">1,284,029</Num> working days between 1960 and 1967, and that the laws would help to bring investment as the bases closed. In Parliament two members who were leaders of the National Trades Union Congress raised concerns, among them the limits placed on the Industrial Arbitration Court, which settled disputes between workers and employers. The laws were passed. Unemployment fell in the following years, but the figures alone do not show how much of the change came from these laws and how much from other causes.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Jurong's first years", zh: "裕廊的头几年" }}>

When the Jurong estate was being prepared, critics called it “Goh's Folly”, after Goh Keng Swee, who had pushed for it. At first only <Num s="S434">two</Num> investors came, a steel mill and a textile company. By the end of the 1960s <Num s="S434">181</Num> factories were in production, with about <Num s="S434">20,000</Num> workers. Flats were built nearby for the workers.

</More>

<Lang zh>

1967 年 7 月 18 日，英国宣布在 1970 年代中期以前从新加坡撤军。1968 年 1 月 16 日，又把期限提前到 1971 年。这些基地占新加坡国民生产总值的 <Num s="S430">20</Num> % 以上；数以万计的人为英军工作，有的是文职人员，有的在英国人家里帮佣。

经济发展局 1961 年按照温思敏率领的联合国专家团的建议成立，在裕廊开辟工业区；沼泽怎样变成工业用地，见<TopicLink slug="singapore-land-use" ch="new-towns">《新加坡的家、粮、路》的“新镇与工业区”</TopicLink>。1967 年的一项法令，让新工厂可以享受多年的税务优惠。外国公司陆续进来：1968 年，美国的国家半导体公司第一次来考察，两周后就在新加坡开工生产。1968 年 6 月 1 日，裕廊镇管理局接管各个工业区；同年 9 月起，人们可以用公积金存款买组屋（见<TopicLink slug="singapore-land-use" ch="crowded-city">《新加坡的家、粮、路》的“拥挤的城市与第一批组屋”</TopicLink>）。

撤军消息传出后，1968 年 4 月举行大选。主要反对党社会主义阵线抵制大选；<Num s="S430">58</Num> 个议席中只有 <Num s="S430">7</Num> 席有竞选，人民行动党赢得全部议席。7 月和 8 月，国会通过两项劳工法令。政府说，这能把管理权还给雇主、吸引投资；工会领袖出身的议员则在国会提出疑虑。

按政府的数字，失业率从 1966 年的 <Num s="S433">8.9</Num> % 降到 1973 年的 <Num s="S433">4.5</Num> %，经济每年平均增长约 <Num s="S433">13</Num> %。到 1971 年 10 月，大部分英军已经撤走，空军基地交给新加坡的空防司令部。最后一名英军在 1976 年 3 月离开。

<More title={{ en: "How many jobs were there at the bases?", zh: "基地里有多少份工作？" }}>

新加坡国家图书馆管理局的两篇文章给出两种说法。一篇说，约 <Num s="S430">2.5 万</Num>名基地工人会在 1971 年失业。另一篇说，英军雇用了 <Num s="S435">3 万</Num>名文职人员和 <Num s="S435">1 万</Num>名家佣，预计约一半会失业。两种算法包括的人可能不同，所以两个都列出。常被引用的另外两组数字这里不用：1959 年的失业人数（两个官方来源相差四倍以上），以及 1965 年制造业的比重（不同的政府出版物给出不同的比例）。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The labour laws of 1968: two views", zh: "1968 年的劳工法令：两种看法" }}>

《雇佣法令》和《劳资关系（修正）法令》规定了工作条件，也让雇主在管理工作上有更大的决定权。政府说，这是要“把管理权还给雇主”；1960 年至 1967 年，罢工和停工损失了 <Num s="S435">1,284,029</Num> 个工作日；基地关闭之际，这两项法令有助于吸引投资。国会里，两名全国职工总会领袖出身的议员提出疑虑，其中包括负责处理劳资纠纷的劳资仲裁庭受到的限制。两项法令都通过了。之后几年失业率下降，但单看数字，看不出多少是这两项法令的作用，多少是别的原因。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Jurong's first years", zh: "裕廊的头几年" }}>

裕廊工业区还在开辟的时候，批评者把它叫作“吴庆瑞的傻事”，因为是吴庆瑞力主兴建的。起初只来了<Num s="S434">两</Num>家投资者，一家钢铁厂和一家纺织厂。到 1960 年代末，已有 <Num s="S434">181</Num> 家工厂投产，工人约 <Num s="S434">2 万</Num>名。附近也为工人建了组屋。

</More>
