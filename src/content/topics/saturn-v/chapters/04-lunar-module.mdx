<Lang en>

The lunar module is a separate craft with a lower descent stage and an upper ascent stage. In the landing configuration, <Num s="S26">four</Num> legs support the lower stage. A ladder runs down <Num s="S26">one</Num> front leg. The angular cabin above is built for work in space and near the Moon, not for flying like an aircraft through Earth's air.

The descent engine beneath the lower stage controls the approach to the surface. The Moon has a very thin exosphere rather than an Earth-like atmosphere. A parachute cannot provide the atmospheric braking used on Earth. The model therefore identifies an engine and landing structure; it does not show a parachute lowering the cabin.

During Apollo 11, <Num s="S21">two</Num> astronauts, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin, descended in the lunar module while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit. The command module is placed beside the lander here only as a reminder. Their screen distance does not represent the distance from the surface to orbit.

For departure, the upper cabin and its own ascent engine rise away. The descent stage, landing legs and ladder stay behind. Watch the docking port move with the upper cabin: it belongs to that departing section, not to the structure left on the ground.

The ascent craft later meets and docks with the command and service modules. This requires a changing connection between spacecraft, not the entire lander returning to Earth. The beat poses show that sequence without claiming to calculate a landing trajectory, fuel consumption or orbital rendezvous.

<More title={{"en": "Surface and orbit are separate places", "zh": "月面与轨道不是同一处"}}>

The command module is shown beside the lander to keep both crew roles visible. This is an explanatory layout. It does not place the orbiting craft at landing-leg height or use screen distance as an altitude measurement.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "The Moon and parachutes", "zh": "月球与降落伞"}}>

The lunar exosphere is extremely thin. A parachute needs enough surrounding gas to brake a falling craft. Do not transfer an Earth landing mechanism to the Moon just because both journeys end at a surface.

</More>

<Lang zh>

登月舱是一艘独立飞行器，下部为下降级，上部为上升级。着陆构型中，<Num s="S26">四支</Num>腿支撑下部结构，只有前方 <Num s="S26">一支</Num>腿带着梯子。上方乘员舱外形有棱有角，它在太空和月球附近工作，不需要像飞机那样穿过地球空气飞行。

下降发动机位于下部，控制接近月面的过程。月球只有非常稀薄的外逸层，没有地球那样的大气，因此降落伞不能提供地球上常用的空气减速。图中要辨认的是发动机与着陆结构，而不是靠降落伞把舱体放下来。

阿波罗 11 号的阿姆斯特朗和奥尔德林共 <Num s="S21">两人</Num>下降，科林斯留在月球轨道。模型把指令舱放在旁边作提醒，屏幕上的距离不代表月面到轨道的距离，也不能据此判断真实的相对位置。

离开月面时，上部舱体和独立的上升发动机升起，下降级、支腿与梯子留在原处。顶部的对接口跟着上部移动，说明它们属于离开的飞行器。留下的硬件不再随乘员加速。随后上升级与指令舱、服务舱交会对接。节拍解释部件归属和连接顺序，不计算着陆轨迹、燃料消耗或轨道交会。

指令舱摆在登月舱旁边，是为同时提醒不同乘员的任务。这不表示轨道飞船就在支腿旁，也不能拿屏幕距离测量轨道高度。

</More>
