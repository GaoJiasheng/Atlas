<Lang en>

The tall object on the stage is an assembled launch stack. It includes the Saturn V <Term id="term-launch-vehicle">launch vehicle</Term>, which launches and accelerates the spacecraft, and the Apollo spacecraft above it. The complete stack stood about <Num s="S24">111 m</Num> tall. Its narrow top is not another large propulsion section: it contains the crew cabin and the escape system.

Saturn V had <Num s="S24">three</Num> main propulsion sections, called <Term id="term-stage">stages</Term>, which could separate. Read upward from the broad lower body to the narrower upper body. The letter V is part of its name, not a count of propulsion sections. During the mission these sections separated after their work was done. The whole tower did not travel to the lunar surface.

Above the launch vehicle are several spacecraft sections with different jobs. The <Term id="term-cm">command module</Term> carries the crew. The <Term id="term-sm">service module</Term> supplies propulsion and support. The <Term id="term-lm">lunar module</Term> travels inside the tapered adapter at launch, rather than beside the rocket. Follow the shell of the adapter before looking through it: a hidden part has not ceased to exist.

The cone-shaped command module alone comes back through Earth's atmosphere. Before following that journey, predict what happens when an engine stops: the craft does not immediately stop moving. Gravity still acts in space. Orbiting motion and the absence of an engine burn can occur together.
