<Lang en>

Read the rocket from the bottom up. Each stage is a propulsion section that can separate. The first has <Num s="S25">five</Num> F-1 engines, the second has <Num s="S25">five</Num> J-2 engines, and the third has <Num s="S25">one</Num> J-2. The stages work in sequence.

The first burns kerosene with liquid oxygen. Both upper stages burn liquid hydrogen with liquid oxygen. When a lower stage finishes its work, it separates with its tanks and equipment. The remaining craft no longer has to accelerate that structure.

The third stage first helps establish a <Term id="term-parking-orbit">parking orbit</Term> around Earth. Later, the same engine restarts for <Term id="term-tli">translunar injection</Term>: the acceleration that sends Apollo towards the Moon. The ring-shaped <Term id="term-iu">instrument unit</Term> guides and controls the launch vehicle.

Try a mass comparison with an imaginary small rocket. Keep payload and propellant fixed, then add <Num s="S90">100 kg</Num> of hardware that supplies no thrust. The calculated speed change falls from <Num s="S90">3295.84 m/s</Num> to <Num s="S90">2748.87 m/s</Num>. Carrying extra structure reduces how much the rocket can change its speed.

This helps explain why rockets discard used stages. The ideal calculation compares masses of the imaginary rocket; it does not predict Saturn V's flight speed.

第一级燃烧煤油和液氧，上面两级燃烧液氢和液氧。下级完成工作后，连同储箱和设备一起分离。剩下的飞行器便不用继续带着这些结构加速。
