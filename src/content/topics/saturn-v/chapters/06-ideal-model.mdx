<Lang en>

Try a mass experiment with an imaginary small rocket. Its <Term id="term-delta-v">ideal speed change</Term> tells us how much the rocket could change its velocity under simplified conditions. The SIM reading belongs to this rocket, not Saturn V's flight.

The experiment fixes effective exhaust speed at <Num s="S90">3000 m/s</Num> and dry hardware at <Num s="S90">100 kg</Num>. Dry hardware is the structure and equipment without propellant. Payload is the useful load. Final mass includes the hardware and payload left after the propellant is expelled. Initial mass includes that propellant too. Any extra inert hardware stays aboard throughout.

With <Num s="S90">200 kg</Num> of payload, <Num s="S90">600 kg</Num> of propellant and no extra structure, initial mass is <Num s="S90">900 kg</Num> and final mass is <Num s="S90">300 kg</Num>. The ideal speed change is <Num s="S90">3295.84 m/s</Num>. Keep propellant fixed and increase payload. More mass remains, so the speed change falls.

Predict the next reading, reveal it, then record a row. Change one input at a time. At the same payload, more propellant raises the ideal result; extra unused structure lowers it. Real rockets also need structure to hold their propellant, so they cannot add unlimited amounts.

The table leaves out air resistance, gravity losses and changes in exhaust conditions. It does not calculate an orbit or landing safety. Engineering helps explain a mission. Its wider history also includes other programmes, crews, risks and costs.

<More title={{"en": "The calculation", "zh": "计算方法"}}>

NASA's ideal rocket equation is <Num s="S47">Δv = ve × ln(m0/mf)</Num>. Divide initial mass by final mass, take the natural logarithm, then multiply by effective exhaust speed. The equation sets aside weight and drag. With the experiment's default inputs, the mass ratio is <Num s="S90">3</Num>, giving <Num s="S90">3000 × ln(3) = 3295.84 m/s</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Explaining your result", "zh": "说明实验结果"}}>

Write the inputs beside each recorded result. This lets someone else repeat your comparison. A result describes the imaginary rocket under these assumptions; it does not measure a historical rocket's payload capacity, reliability or cost.

</More>

<Lang zh>

用一枚小型假想火箭做质量实验。<Term id="term-delta-v">理想速度变化</Term>表示在简化条件下，火箭能让速度改变多少。SIM 读数属于这枚假想火箭，不是土星五号的飞行速度。

实验固定有效排气速度为 <Num s="S90">3000 米/秒</Num>，干结构为 <Num s="S90">100 千克</Num>。干结构是不含推进剂的结构和设备，载荷是有用的携带物。推进剂排出后，留下的结构和载荷构成最终质量；再加上推进剂，就是初始质量。额外携带的不提供推力的结构，始终留在火箭上。

载荷为 <Num s="S90">200 千克</Num>、推进剂为 <Num s="S90">600 千克</Num>，且不带额外结构时，初始质量是 <Num s="S90">900 千克</Num>，最终质量是 <Num s="S90">300 千克</Num>，理想速度变化为 <Num s="S90">3295.84 米/秒</Num>。固定推进剂、增加载荷，留下的质量增大，速度变化就减小。

先猜读数，再显示结果，最后记录一行。每次只改一个输入。同样载荷下，增加推进剂会提高理想结果，多带无用结构则会降低它。真实火箭还要用结构盛装推进剂，不能无限添加。

表格省略空气阻力、重力损失和排气条件的变化，不计算轨道或着陆安全。工程帮助我们理解任务，历史还包括其他项目、乘组、风险和投入。

<More title={{"en": "The calculation", "zh": "计算方法"}}>

NASA 的理想火箭方程为 <Num s="S47">Δv = ve × ln(m0/mf)</Num>：先用初始质量除以最终质量，取自然对数，再乘以有效排气速度。方程暂不计重量和阻力。默认输入的质量比为 <Num s="S90">3</Num>，得到 <Num s="S90">3000 × ln(3) = 3295.84 米/秒</Num>。

</More>
