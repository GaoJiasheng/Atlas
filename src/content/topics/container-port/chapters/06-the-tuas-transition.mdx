The machines on the stage remain a generic port study. They help explain work discussed in the Tuas story, but they do not become a surveyed reconstruction when this chapter opens. Container handling in Singapore has developed over time: Tanjong Pagar container terminal opened in <Num s="S27">1972</Num>, long before Tuas began operating.

Tuas’s first <Num s="S5">two berths</Num> began operations in December <Num s="S5">2021</Num>. Its official opening followed on <Num s="S5">1 September 2022</Num>. Beginning operations and holding an official opening are different milestones. Neither means that every planned stage of the port was complete on that date.

Automation changes how work is done. Tuas uses automated guided vehicles, electric automated yard cranes and remote management from a control centre. Select the carrier, yard crane and control room to connect those descriptions to the model. Automatic movement still belongs to a system involving people, planning, maintenance and supervision; the model does not count workers or show that the port is empty of them.

Read the two evidence entries separately. <Term id="throughput">Throughput</Term> is the amount actually handled within a stated time and scope. <Term id="capacity">Capacity</Term> describes what could be handled under the stated conditions. A future capacity for Tuas and a past result for all Singapore describe different things. They must not be joined into a single measured trend or read as a forecast that the real annual total will equal the planned capacity.
