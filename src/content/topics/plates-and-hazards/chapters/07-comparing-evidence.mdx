The last view shows the full earthquake sample for 2000–2024: <Num s="S604">3,729</Num> reviewed catalogue events with magnitude at least <Num s="S604">6</Num>. The files were collected on 26 October 2026. The sample leaves out smaller earthquakes and keeps different magnitude types. Older historical disasters were chosen separately. Comparing their sparse records with this sample cannot show that earthquakes are becoming more frequent.

Look across the boundary lines, volcanoes and earthquake points. Many lie near plate edges. Others, such as Kilauea inside an oceanic plate, show that there are exceptions. A plate model gives a broad view; a local fault map gives finer detail. A place without catalogue points can still face hazards.

The <Term id="term-ring-of-fire">Ring of Fire</Term> is a popular name for a region around the Pacific. Its extent depends on the definition. MSS broadly includes Indonesia. GVP's Pacific-basin definition excludes most Indonesian volcanoes along the Australia–Sunda boundary. The region's volcanoes do not form one connected structure or share one magma reservoir.

Singapore is part of this wider region. Distance from active boundaries reduces some exposure, but distant events can still have effects here. Return to the 2004 event: its loss of life still refers to the October 2024 source, even as the map date changes. Compare definitions and missing evidence before drawing conclusions. The map cannot give exact earthquake predictions or current public warnings.

<More title={{ en: "The Ring of Fire: two definitions", zh: "火环的两种范围定义" }}>

MSS includes Indonesia in its broad description. GVP excludes most volcanoes along the Australia–Sunda boundary. GVP v5.4.0 reports <Num s="S11">687</Num> of <Num s="S11">1,214</Num> Holocene catalogue volcanoes, or <Num s="S11">57%</Num>, inside its definition. This is GVP's published count for that version; no ring boundary has been drawn on this map.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why catalogue counts change", zh: "目录数量为什么会变？" }}>

New observations, reviews and changed definitions can alter a catalogue. The files used here keep one dated snapshot so the selection can be checked. Volcano locations do not tell us which volcanoes were erupting on each historical date. This earthquake sample gives neither a uniform set of moment magnitudes nor the probability of a future disaster.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What do gaps mean?", zh: "空白说明什么？" }}>

A record needs usable coordinates to become a map point. Records without them remain in the data audit. Historical observations do not cover every coast. A missing value means “not recorded”, not zero, and scattered dots cannot trace the whole flooded area.

</More>
