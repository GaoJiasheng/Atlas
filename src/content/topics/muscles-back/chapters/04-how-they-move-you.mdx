<Lang en>

Each beat of this chapter moves one joint so you can watch which muscles shorten. Start with the shoulder blade. A shrug lifts the shoulder girdle with the upper trapezius and levator scapulae. Drawing the blades toward the spine uses the middle trapezius and the rhomboids. When the arm rises, the upper and lower trapezius work with serratus anterior to turn the blade so that its socket faces up, a movement called <Term id="upward-rotation">upward rotation</Term>; the rhomboids and levator scapulae turn it back down [M47] [M2].

Now the arm. Latissimus dorsi and teres major pull a raised arm down to the side and back; moving the arm backward at the shoulder is called <Term id="extension">extension</Term>. Because both end on the front of the humerus, they also turn the arm inward. Infraspinatus and teres minor turn it the other way, outward, and help keep the head of the humerus in its socket [M47] [M48].

**This is why latissimus dorsi, not the arms, is the prime mover in pull-ups and lat pulldowns.** The main work happens at the shoulder, where latissimus and teres major pull the upper arm down. The biceps and the other elbow flexors bend the elbow at the same time and work hard too, but they move a different joint [M47] [M51] [M52]. Tired forearms do not mean the back did nothing.

Last, the spine. The erector spinae can bend it backward, and one side helps bend it sideways [M47] [M49]. **In a deadlift with a <Term id="neutral-spine">neutral spine</Term>, though, they hardly change length: they work <Term id="isometric">isometrically</Term> to stop the back rounding while the hips and knees do the lifting** [M55]. <More title={{ en: "What the model can and cannot move", zh: "模型能动什么、不能动什么" }}>

The shoulder blade moves on a simplified chain through the collarbone: it turns a little rather than sliding over the ribs. The trunk tilts as one rigid piece, so spinal extension and side bending are shown as a direction, not vertebra by vertebra. There are no hip or knee joints, which is why the deadlift and the back extension are shown by their sketches rather than in full motion. The angles are chosen to make the direction clear; they are not measured ranges of movement.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The hand can be the fixed end", zh: "手也可以是固定端" }}>

In a lat pulldown the trunk is held still and the arms move. In a pull-up the hands hold a fixed bar and the body rises toward them. Either way latissimus dorsi pulls between the same attachments on the trunk and the arm [M47] [M91].

<Lang zh>

这一章每一拍只动一个关节，方便你看清是哪些肌肉在缩短。先看肩胛骨。耸肩时，斜方肌降部和肩胛提肌把肩带提起来。把两块肩胛骨拉向脊柱，靠的是斜方肌横部和菱形肌。手臂抬起时，斜方肌上部、下部和前锯肌一起把肩胛骨转一下，让关节盂朝上，这叫<Term id="upward-rotation">上回旋</Term>；菱形肌和肩胛提肌则把它转回来 [M47] [M2]。

再看手臂。背阔肌和大圆肌把抬起的手臂往身体侧面、往后拉；手臂在肩关节处往后走，叫<Term id="extension">伸</Term>。两块肌肉都止在肱骨前面，所以还会让上臂内旋。冈下肌和小圆肌让上臂往反方向转，也就是外旋，并帮着把肱骨头稳在关节盂里 [M47] [M48]。

**所以，引体向上和高位下拉的主动肌是背阔肌，而不是手臂。**主要的工作发生在肩关节：背阔肌和大圆肌把上臂往下拉。肱二头肌等屈肘肌同时把手肘弯起来，也出了很大力，但它们动的是另一个关节 [M47] [M51] [M52]。前臂累了，不代表背没干活。

最后看脊柱。竖脊肌能让脊柱向后伸，单侧收缩还帮着侧屈 [M47] [M49]。**但在<Term id="neutral-spine">脊柱中立位</Term>的硬拉里，它们的长度几乎不变：它们做<Term id="isometric">等长收缩</Term>，不让背弓起来，把杠举起来的是髋和膝** [M55]。最后一拍就是这种支撑：什么都没动，肌柱却在用力。<FlyTo preset="left-side">从侧面看</FlyTo>

<More title={{ en: "What the model can and cannot move", zh: "模型能动什么、不能动什么" }}>

肩胛骨通过锁骨上的一条简化关节链活动：它只能稍微转动，不会真的在肋骨上滑。躯干作为一整块倾斜，所以脊柱后伸和侧屈只表示方向，不是一节一节地动。模型没有髋关节和膝关节，所以硬拉和背伸展靠示意图来演示，没有完整的动作。演示的角度是为了把方向看清楚，不是测量出来的活动范围。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The hand can be the fixed end", zh: "手也可以是固定端" }}>

做高位下拉时，躯干不动，手臂在动。做引体向上时，双手抓住固定的单杠，是身体往手的方向升上去。不管哪种，背阔肌都是在躯干和上臂的同一对附着点之间拉 [M47] [M91]。

</More>
