<Lang en>

You already read money in <Term id="dollars-and-cents">dollars and cents</Term>, such as 2 dollars 35 cents, and count coins to find a total. Now look at how the writing records what you count.

<Figure src="figures/decimals-coin-tray" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of a wooden cash tray on a drinks stall: plain coins of four sizes stacked in separate compartments, the fifth compartment empty, a folded paper note under a clip at the back and a cup of iced drink with a straw beside the tray.", zh: "一个木制零钱盒的铅笔画：四种大小的素面硬币分格摞着，第五格空着，后面夹着一张折好的纸币，旁边放着一杯插着吸管的冰饮料。" }} caption={{ en: "Coins sorted by kind, one compartment each: the same idea as sorting dollars, ten cents and cents into places. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "硬币按种类分格放，就像把元、10 分、分按位分开。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

Look at the <Beat n={1}>coins in the tray</Beat>. Count two one-dollar coins, three 10-cent coins and one 5-cent coin. The smaller coins make 35 cents, so the total is $2.35. The <Term id="decimal-point">decimal point</Term> separates the whole dollars from the part worth less than a dollar. Read this amount as "2 dollars 35 cents".

Next, <Beat n={2}>sort the coins into columns</Beat>. The amount stays the same because you have only moved the coins. The 2 records two whole dollars. The 3 records three lots of 10 cents, and the 5 records the remaining 5 cents. You can see each part in $2.35 = $2 + $0.30 + $0.05. Each digit has its own place.

Watch <Beat n={3}>ten 10-cent coins become one dollar</Beat>: 10 × $0.10 = $1. Each coin is one of ten equal parts of a dollar, so 10 cents is one <Term id="tenth">tenth</Term> of a dollar. A dollar is also 100 cents, so 1 cent is one <Term id="hundredth">hundredth</Term> of a dollar. This is why the first place after the point counts tenths and the second counts hundredths.

Now <Beat n={4}>compare $2.05 with $2.50</Beat>. Both have two whole dollars. Look at the next column: $2.05 has no 10-cent coins and has 5 cents left; $2.50 has five 10-cent coins, worth 50 cents. The amounts are different: $2.05 ≠ $2.50.

One common mistake is leaving out the placeholder zero in $2.05: it keeps the 5 in the hundredths place. Another is reading $2.5 as 2 dollars 5 cents; the 5 is in the tenths place, so it means 50 cents, written $2.50 for money.

<Lang zh>

钱你天天在用。$2.35 这样的写法就是一个<Term id="decimal">小数</Term>。这一章从你熟悉的钱讲起，看看小数点到底在做什么。

<Figure src="figures/decimals-coin-tray" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of a wooden cash tray on a drinks stall: plain coins of four sizes stacked in separate compartments, the fifth compartment empty, a folded paper note under a clip at the back and a cup of iced drink with a straw beside the tray.", zh: "一个木制零钱盒的铅笔画：四种大小的素面硬币分格摞着，第五格空着，后面夹着一张折好的纸币，旁边放着一杯插着吸管的冰饮料。" }} caption={{ en: "Coins sorted by kind, one compartment each: the same idea as sorting dollars, ten cents and cents into places. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "硬币按种类分格放，就像把元、10 分、分按位分开。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

先看<Beat n={1}>托盘里的硬币</Beat>：熟食中心的一杯饮料 $2.35，读作 2 元 35 分。托盘上是两枚 1 元、三枚 10 分和一枚 5 分。

把硬币<Beat n={2}>按列排好</Beat>：元一列，10 分一列，分一列。元 2 枚，10 分 3 枚，最后是 5 分。每一列正好对着 2.35 的一位数字。<Term id="decimal-point">小数点</Term>前面是整元，小数点后面第一位数 10 分硬币，第二位数分。所以 $2.35 = $2 + $0.30 + $0.05。

为什么 10 分硬币要放在小数点后第一位？<Beat n={3}>10 枚 10 分硬币</Beat>是 1 元，所以 10 分是 1 元的十分之一。100 分是 1 元，1 分就是 1 元的百分之一。小数点后面第一位是十分之几，第二位是百分之几，这就是<Term id="dollars-and-cents">元和分</Term>能写成小数的原因。

最后<Beat n={4}>把 $2.05 和 $2.50 放在一起</Beat>比一比。$2.05 是 2 元 5 分，10 分那一列一枚也没有，所以要写一个 0，让 5 留在“分”那一位。$2.50 是 2 元 50 分，10 分那一列有 5 枚。数字一样，钱数却差了 45 分。

常见的错误有两个。一是漏写占位的 0，把 2 元 5 分写成 $2.5，可是 $2.5 和 $2.50 一样多，是 2 元 50 分。二是把小数点后面的数直接当成分：看到 $2.5，就以为是 2 元 5 分。小数点后第一位数的是 10 分，不是 1 分。

准备好了，就<Task id="dollars-and-cents">做一道小题</Task>：换一换元和分，或者数一数硬币。
