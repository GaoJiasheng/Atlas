<Lang en>
By the 1st century CE four large states stretched across Eurasia from east to west: Han China, the Kushan Empire, Parthia (called Anxi in Chinese records) and Rome. None of them controlled the whole route. Parthia and the Kushans sat in the middle, and goods passed through many hands between the two ends.

In 53 BCE the Roman general Crassus led an army against Parthia and was defeated at Carrhae. The Greek writer Plutarch says that <Num s="S40">20,000</Num> Romans were killed and <Num s="S40">10,000</Num> captured in the campaign.

Each end knew the other mostly by name. About 77 CE the Roman writer Pliny complained that India, the Seres and the Arabian peninsula drained at least <Num s="S42">100 million</Num> sesterces a year from the empire. In Chinese records Rome was Da Qin. In 97 CE the Protector-General Ban Chao sent Gan Ying to find it; at the sea, sailors from Parthia's western border warned him that the crossing could take <Num s="S14">three</Num> months to <Num s="S14">two</Num> years, and he turned back. In 166 men claiming to be envoys of “Andun”, king of Da Qin, arrived by sea; the Chinese historian himself noted that their gifts were nothing special.

The Kushans ruled from Bactria into northern India. Kanishka, whose reign began about 127 CE, left an inscription at Rabatak that lists Indian cities under his control. In Gandhara, artists combined Greek, Iranian and Indian styles. A Greek writer, Isidore of Charax, listed the stations on a road across Parthia; the dashed line in the west of the map joins those that can be placed.

<More title={{ en: "A road across Parthia", zh: "一条穿过帕提亚的路" }}>

Isidore's *Parthian Stations*, written around the turn of the era, counts the distance between stations in schoeni, a Greek measure. It starts at Zeugma on the Euphrates, follows the river down to Seleucia on the Tigris, climbs past Behistun and Kangavar to Ecbatana (Hamadan) and Rhaga, and ends at Alexandropolis, the chief city of Arachosia (Kandahar). Stations whose modern place is disputed are left off the line. [S97]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Carrhae: where the numbers come from", zh: "卡莱：数字从哪里来" }}>

Plutarch's figures are what “are said” to have died, and they are for the whole campaign, not one day. A story that the Romans first saw silk at Carrhae, on the Parthians' banners, rests on the later writer Florus, who mentions “gold and silken pennons” on the Parthian standards. [S40, S41]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Envoys or traders?", zh: "使者还是商人" }}>

The Chinese record of 166 calls the visitors envoys of the king of Da Qin, but adds that their gifts were nothing rare and that the report may have been exaggerated. Modern historians think they were probably traders acting on their own rather than official envoys of the Roman emperor. [S14, S15]

</More>

<Lang zh>

到公元 1 世纪，亚欧大陆上由东到西有四个大国：汉朝、贵霜帝国、帕提亚（中国史书称安息）和罗马。没有哪一国控制整条路。帕提亚和贵霜在中间，货物在两端之间要经过许多人的手。

公元前 53 年，罗马将军克拉苏率军进攻帕提亚，在卡莱战败。希腊作家普鲁塔克说，这场战役中罗马人被杀<Num s="S40">两万</Num>、被俘<Num s="S40">一万</Num>。

两端对彼此大多只知道名字。约公元 77 年，罗马作家普林尼抱怨，印度、赛里斯和阿拉伯半岛每年至少从帝国拿走<Num s="S42">一亿</Num>塞斯特斯。在中国史书里，罗马叫“大秦”。公元 97 年，西域都护班超派甘英出使大秦；到了海边，安息西界的船人告诉他，渡海要<Num s="S14">三</Num>个月到<Num s="S14">两</Num>年，他就折回了。166 年，有人自称大秦王“安敦”的使者，从海上来到汉廷；史家自己也写道，他们献的东西“并无珍异”。

贵霜从巴克特里亚一直统治到北印度。约公元 127 年即位的迦腻色伽，在罗巴塔克留下碑文，列出他控制的印度城市。在犍陀罗，工匠把希腊、伊朗和印度的风格融在一起。希腊作者伊西多尔列出了穿过帕提亚的一条路上的驿站，地图西部的虚线连接的是其中能定位的地方。

伊西多尔的《帕提亚驿程》写于公元前后，用希腊的长度单位“斯科伊诺斯”记驿站之间的距离。它从幼发拉底河边的宙格玛出发，顺河而下到底格里斯河边的塞琉西亚，经贝希斯敦、康加瓦尔上到埃克巴坦那（哈马丹）和拉盖，终点是阿拉霍西亚的首府亚历山大城（坎大哈）。今地有争议的驿站不连进线里。[S97]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Carrhae: where the numbers come from", zh: "卡莱：数字从哪里来" }}>

普鲁塔克的数字是“据说”的死亡人数，而且指整场战役，不是一天。另有一种说法，说罗马人第一次见到丝绸是在卡莱战场上帕提亚人的旗帜上；这个说法依据的是更晚的作者弗洛鲁斯，他写到帕提亚军旗上“金色和丝绸的旗幡”。[S40, S41]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Envoys or traders?", zh: "使者还是商人" }}>

中国史书把 166 年的来客记作大秦王的使者，又补了一句：他们献的东西并不稀罕，传话的人可能夸大了。现代史家认为，他们多半是自行前来的商人，不是罗马皇帝派出的正式使节。[S14, S15]

</More>
