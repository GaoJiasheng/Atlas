<Lang en>

In 971 the Song government set up a Maritime Trade Office at Guangzhou, and in 1087 another at Quanzhou. When a ship came in, the office took one part in ten of the cargo and bought part of the rest. Quanzhou, which Arab and European writers called Zayton, was a great port of the Song and Yuan; in 2021 it was added to the World Heritage List. A Song ship found in Quanzhou Bay in 1974 had <Num s="S23">13</Num> watertight compartments. In 1225 Zhao Rugua, the official in charge of Fujian's sea trade, wrote a book about foreign countries, their goods and the routes to them, from what foreign merchants told him.

Across the Indian Ocean, letters kept in a synagogue in Cairo follow Abraham Ben Yiju, a Jewish merchant from Tunisia who went to Aden and lived for years in India, from the 1130s to the 1150s. In the straits, power and trade went together: in 1025 the Chola kingdom of south India seized Palembang, Srivijaya's chief city (see <TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">Southeast Asian kingdoms</TopicLink>).

Travellers rode these ships. Marco Polo says that on his way home from China, about 1292, he waited <Num s="S72">five</Num> months for the weather at Samara on Sumatra. Around 1345 the traveller Ibn Battuta sailed from Samudra-Pasai on Sumatra to China; Quanzhou was the first Chinese city he reached.

In 1349 the Chinese traveller Wang Dayuan described Danmaxi, or Temasek, and Longyamen, the strait at today's Singapore. He wrote that people there plundered Quanzhou junks on their way home. Digs on Fort Canning Hill have found gold ornaments, coins and porcelain of his time. Later, the Malay Annals told of a prince, Sang Nila Utama, founding Singapura; historians treat it as a legend. The Ming fleets that later sailed into these seas are the subject of <TopicLink slug="zheng-he" ch="setting-sail">Zheng He</TopicLink>.

<More title={{ en: "What the Maritime Trade Office did", zh: "市舶司做什么" }}>

The Song history says that when a ship arrived, the office first took one part in ten; the government then bought some of the goods at prices set by their value. Merchants sailing from Fujian had to register their goods and destination and find guarantors before they were given a permit. [S22]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A 12th-century merchant's letters", zh: "一位 12 世纪商人的信" }}>

The Cairo Geniza is a store of old papers kept in a synagogue in Cairo. About <Num s="S64">80</Num> documents concern Ben Yiju, dated between 1133 and 1156; some are letters from his business partner in Aden, Madmun ibn Hasan. They follow one man's business from North Africa to India. [S64]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Temasek: digs and writing", zh: "淡马锡：考古与文字" }}>

Wang Dayuan's account appeared in 1349 and on its own in 1350 as *Daoyi zhilüe*. Besides the settlement, he describes pirate junks waiting in Longyamen for ships returning to China. The finds on Fort Canning Hill match his time. The story of Sang Nila Utama comes from the *Malay Annals*, a court chronicle. <Lang zh>

971 年，宋朝在广州设市舶司，1087 年又在泉州设了一个。海船到港，市舶司先抽取货物的十分之一，再收买其余货物的一部分。阿拉伯和欧洲作者称之为“刺桐”的泉州，是宋元时期的大港，2021 年列入世界遗产名录。1974 年在泉州湾发现的一艘宋代海船，有<Num s="S23">13</Num>个水密隔舱。1225 年，主管福建海外贸易的官员赵汝适根据外国商人的讲述，写了一本书，记下海外各国的物产和去那里的路程。

在印度洋的另一边，开罗一座犹太会堂保存的信件，记下突尼斯犹太商人亚伯拉罕·本·伊居的经历：从 12 世纪 30 年代到 50 年代，他到过亚丁，又在印度住了多年。在海峡上，权力和贸易分不开：1025 年，南印度的朱罗王朝夺取了室利佛逝的主要城市巨港（见<TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">东南亚诸王国</TopicLink>）。

旅行者也搭这些船。马可·波罗说，约 1292 年他从中国回国途中，在苏门答腊的萨马拉等天气等了<Num s="S72">五</Num>个月。约 1345 年，旅行家伊本·白图泰从苏门答腊的须文答剌—巴赛航行到中国，到的第一座中国城市是泉州。

1349 年，中国旅行者汪大渊记下了“单马锡”，也就是淡马锡，以及今天新加坡那里的海峡“龙牙门”。他写道，那里的人劫掠回航的泉州船。福康宁山的发掘出土了他那个时代的金饰、钱币和瓷器。后来的《马来纪年》讲到王子山尼拉乌他玛建立新加坡拉，史家把它看作传说。后来明朝船队驶入这片海域的故事，见<TopicLink slug="zheng-he" ch="setting-sail">郑和下西洋</TopicLink>。

<More title={{ en: "What the Maritime Trade Office did", zh: "市舶司做什么" }}>

《宋史》说，海船一到，市舶司“十先征其一”，官府再按货物轻重定价收买一部分。从福建出海的商人，要报明货物数量和去处，找人作保，才能领到出海的凭证。[S22]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A 12th-century merchant's letters", zh: "一位 12 世纪商人的信" }}>

开罗的吉尼扎，是一座犹太会堂里保存下来的大批旧文书。与本·伊居有关的文书约有<Num s="S64">80</Num>件，年代在 1133 到 1156 年之间，有些是他在亚丁的生意伙伴马德蒙写给他的信。从这些信可以看到一个人的生意怎样从北非一直做到印度。[S64]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Temasek: digs and writing", zh: "淡马锡：考古与文字" }}>

汪大渊的记载 1349 年先作为附录出版，1350 年单独刊为《岛夷志略》。除了这个聚落，他还写到在龙牙门等候回航船只的海盗船。福康宁山的出土物与他的时代相合。山尼拉乌他玛的故事出自宫廷编年史《马来纪年》。新加坡自己的历史，见<TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="background">新加坡 1819—1965</TopicLink>。[S78, S91, S92]
