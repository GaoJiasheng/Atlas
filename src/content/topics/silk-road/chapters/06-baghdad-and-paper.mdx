<Lang en>

In 751 a Tang army under Gao Xianzhi met an army sent by the Abbasid rulers of the <Term id="term-caliphate">caliphate</Term> near Taraz, on the Talas River. During the battle the Karluks, a Turkic people fighting on the Tang side, changed sides, and the Tang army was badly defeated. Chinese and Arabic records both mention the battle.

An 11th-century Arabic writer, al-Tha'alibi, says that Chinese prisoners taken in this battle taught papermaking at Samarkand. The historian Jonathan Bloom points out that paper documents written in Sogdiana around 722 survive from decades before the battle, so the story is a later explanation, not a record.

In 762 the caliph al-Mansur founded Baghdad. At the other end of Asia, the Tang official Jia Dan recorded a sea route from Guangzhou through the Strait of Malacca, past Sri Lanka and India to the Gulf, ending at Baghdad. In the 9th century Ibn Khurradadhbih described Jewish merchants who travelled by land and sea between the Frankish lands and China, carrying slaves, silk, furs and swords east and musk, camphor and cinnamon west.

Not all who moved on these routes chose to. In 869 enslaved East Africans, the Zanj, who had been brought to drain the salt marshes east of Basra, rose against the caliphate; the war lasted until 883. In China, in 868, a copy of the Diamond Sutra was printed. Later it was sealed in a cave at Dunhuang with many other documents, which were found again in 1900.

<More title={{ en: "How a story became “history”", zh: "一个故事怎样变成“史实”" }}>

The link between Talas and paper first appears in al-Tha'alibi, writing more than two centuries after the battle. Paper letters from Sogdiana survive from centuries earlier, and documents from Mount Mug near Panjikent are dated 722–723. The battle did happen; the paper story is a tale told about it. [S54, S55]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Who were the Zanj?", zh: "僧祇人是谁" }}>

“Zanj” was the Arabic name for people from the East African coast. Landowners in Basra brought <Num s="S59">several thousand</Num> of them to drain the salt marshes east of the city. Their revolt against the Abbasid caliphate lasted fourteen years. No reliable total of the dead on either side survives in the sources read here. [S59]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Where the Dunhuang manuscripts are today", zh: "藏经洞的写本今天在哪里" }}>

The Library Cave was sealed around the beginning of the 11th century. In 1900 the Daoist monk Wang Yuanlu found its entrance. In 1907 Aurel Stein and in 1908 Paul Pelliot bought large numbers of manuscripts from him and took them to London and Paris; the remaining Chinese manuscripts were later sent to Beijing, and other collections went to St Petersburg and Japan. How the manuscripts were obtained is still argued over today. [S60, S61]

</More>

<Lang zh>

751 年，高仙芝率领的唐军在怛罗斯河畔的塔拉兹附近，遇上阿拔斯<Term id="term-caliphate">哈里发国</Term>派来的军队。战斗中，站在唐军一边的突厥部族葛逻禄倒戈，唐军大败。中国和阿拉伯的史书都记下了这场战斗。

11 世纪的阿拉伯作者塔阿利比说，这一仗中被俘的中国人在撒马尔罕传授了造纸术。史学家 Jonathan Bloom 指出，粟特地区约 722 年的纸文书至今还在，比这场战斗早几十年，所以这个故事是后人的解释，不是当时的记录。

762 年，哈里发曼苏尔建立巴格达。在亚洲的另一头，唐朝官员贾耽记下一条海路：从广州出发，经马六甲海峡、斯里兰卡和印度到波斯湾，终点是巴格达。9 世纪的伊本·胡尔达兹贝赫写到在法兰克人的地方和中国之间走陆路、走海路的犹太商人，他们向东运奴隶、丝绸、皮毛和刀剑，向西运麝香、樟脑和肉桂。

走在这些路上的，并不都是自愿的人。869 年，被运来排干巴士拉以东盐沼的东非奴隶“僧祇人”起来反抗哈里发国，战争持续到 883 年。在中国，868 年印出了一部《金刚经》。它后来和许多文书一起被封进敦煌的一个洞窟，1900 年才重新被发现。

把怛罗斯和造纸连在一起的说法，最早见于塔阿利比，他写作时离那场战斗已经两百多年。粟特地区更早几个世纪的纸信件今天还在，片治肯特附近穆格山出土的文书写于 722—723 年。战斗确有其事，造纸的故事则是后人附会上去的。[S54, S55]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Who were the Zanj?", zh: "僧祇人是谁" }}>

“僧祇”是阿拉伯人对东非沿海居民的称呼。巴士拉的地主把<Num s="S59">几千</Num>名东非人运来，排干城东的盐沼。他们反抗阿拔斯王朝的战争持续了十四年。本主题读到的资料里，没有双方死亡人数的可靠总数。[S59]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Where the Dunhuang manuscripts are today", zh: "藏经洞的写本今天在哪里" }}>

藏经洞约在 11 世纪初被封起来。1900 年，道士王圆箓发现了洞口。1907 年斯坦因、1908 年伯希和先后从他手里购得大批写本，带到伦敦和巴黎；剩下的中文写本后来被运到北京，另有一些收藏去了圣彼得堡和日本。这批写本是怎样被取得的，至今仍有争议。[S60, S61]
