<Lang en>

In 1877 the German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen used the name *Seidenstraße*, “Silk Road”, in a book on China, and made it famous. He did not invent it: the German word had been printed decades earlier, and Richthofen himself also wrote of “Silk Roads” in the plural. The people who travelled these routes never had one name for them.

The <Term id="term-silk-roads">Silk Roads</Term> were not one road. They were a web of steppe tracks, desert crossings between <Term id="term-oasis">oases</Term>, mountain passes and seaports. Most merchants worked one stretch, and goods changed hands many times on the way. Few people travelled the whole distance; the travellers in this topic were unusual.

Silk was only one of the things that moved. Horses went east to the Han court; Buddhism and Christianity reached China; Chinese ceramics sailed west; paper was in use in Sogdiana, around Samarkand, by the early 8th century; silkworms reached Constantinople. People were bought and sold along many of these routes, and in the 14th century plague travelled along them. The sea lanes of the <Term id="term-monsoon">monsoon</Term> mattered as much as the roads.

The chapters follow time. Two chapters are about the sea; each starts at the date of its first event and runs alongside the land chapters. The topic ends in 1368, when the Yuan court left Khanbaliq; the Ming voyages that came after are told in <TopicLink slug="zheng-he" ch="background">Zheng He and the Maritime Silk Routes</TopicLink>. What came before the Han is in <TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="after-221">the Warring States</TopicLink> and <TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="china">Ancient Civilisations</TopicLink>.

<More title={{ en: "Where the name comes from", zh: "名字的来历" }}>

Richthofen used the term in the first volume of his book *China* (1877), and he was long said to have coined it. Daniel Waugh showed that the name played a small part in his work and that later writers made it famous; Matthias Mertens found the German word in print decades before Richthofen. The safe statement is that Richthofen made the name known. [S1, S2]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How this map is drawn", zh: "这张地图是怎么画的" }}>

- **Coloured areas** come from Cliopatria, a dataset of past states. Its authors call their maps one version of history, so every boundary here is dashed and pale. Only states that the chapters talk about are coloured. [S3]
- **Places** are points from Wikidata. A city's point is today's city; a point for a region, such as Ferghana or Bactria, is only a reference point. [S93]
- **Route lines** join stops in the order a written source gives them: the Han History, the Tang geographer Jia Dan, a Florentine merchant's handbook. On land the line between two stops is straight; at sea a computer program finds the shortest way through the water on today's map. Neither is a path anyone actually took, so the lines are dashed.
- **Travellers' journeys** join only the places their own accounts name, and leave out places whose location is disputed.

</More>

<Lang zh>

1877 年，德国地理学家李希霍芬在一本讲中国的书里用了 Seidenstraße，即“丝绸之路”这个名字，让它出了名。这个词并不是他发明的：几十年前已经有人用过这个德语词，李希霍芬本人也写过复数的“丝绸之路”。当年走在这些路上的人，从来没有一个统一的名字。

<Term id="term-silk-roads">丝绸之路</Term>不是一条路，而是一张网：草原上的道路、<Term id="term-oasis">绿洲</Term>之间的沙漠通道、山口和海港连在一起。大多数商人只走其中一段，货物在路上一次又一次转手。走完全程的人极少，本主题里讲到的几位旅行者都是少数。

路上走的也不只有丝绸。马向东送到汉朝的宫廷；佛教和基督教传到中国；中国的瓷器装船西去；8 世纪初撒马尔罕一带的粟特地区已经在用纸；蚕卵到了君士坦丁堡。许多条路上都有人被买卖，14 世纪鼠疫也沿着这些路传开。<Term id="term-monsoon">季风</Term>海上的航路，和陆路一样重要。

各章按时间排列。讲海路的有两章，各从自己第一件事的年份开始，和陆路的章节并行。本主题到 1368 年元廷撤出大都为止；此后明朝船队下西洋的故事，见<TopicLink slug="zheng-he" ch="background">郑和下西洋与海上丝路</TopicLink>。汉代以前的事，见<TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="after-221">春秋战国</TopicLink>和<TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="china">古文明</TopicLink>。

<More title={{ en: "Where the name comes from", zh: "名字的来历" }}>

李希霍芬在《中国》第 1 卷（1877）里用了这个词，长期被说成是他首创的。Daniel Waugh 指出，这个词在他书里分量很小，是后人让它出了名；Matthias Mertens 找到了比李希霍芬早几十年的德语用例。稳妥的说法是：李希霍芬让这个名字出了名。[S1, S2]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How this map is drawn", zh: "这张地图是怎么画的" }}>

- **色块**来自 Cliopatria，一个记录历代国家范围的数据集。它的作者说这些地图只是一种版本，所以这里的边界都画成虚线、颜色较淡；只给各章讲到的国家上色。[S3]
- **地点**是维基数据里的点。城市的点是今天的城市；费尔干纳、巴克特里亚这类地区的点只是参照点。[S93]
- **路线**按文献给出的顺序连接站点：《汉书》、唐代地理学者贾耽、一位佛罗伦萨商人的手册。陆上两站之间画直线；海上由程序在今天的地图上找出穿过水面的最短路线。这两种线都不是真正走过的路，所以画成虚线。
- **旅行者的行程**只连他们自己的记载里写到的地方，今地有争议的地方不用。

</More>
