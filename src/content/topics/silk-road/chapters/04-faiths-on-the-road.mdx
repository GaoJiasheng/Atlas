<Lang en>

Buddhism reached China along these routes, and many of the people who carried it were not Chinese. Early records say that An Shigao, a man from Parthia, reached Luoyang in 148 and began turning Buddhist texts into Chinese; he is the earliest translator we know of. Kumarajiva, born in the oasis kingdom of Kucha, was captured when Chinese troops took Kucha in 384 and reached Chang'an in 401 to translate Buddhist texts. At Dunhuang, an inscription of 698 says the first cave temple was cut into the cliff in 366.

In Iran a new dynasty, the Sasanians, took power in 224. There, from about 240, a man called Mani taught a religion of his own. <Term id="term-manichaeism">Manichaeism</Term> spread far to the east: about 762 the Uyghur ruler Bögü Khan adopted it, and it stayed a state religion of the Uyghurs for centuries.

<Term id="term-sogdians">Sogdian</Term> merchants from the region around Samarkand worked as far east as China. In 1907 Aurel Stein found a bundle of their letters in a watchtower just west of the Jade Gate. Written about 313, they describe war in northern China: the emperor had fled Luoyang, and “Luoyang is no more”. Two letters come from a woman named Miwnay, left in Dunhuang without money.

At the other end, silk-making itself moved west. Procopius, a Greek historian of the Eastern Roman Empire, says that in the early 550s monks brought silkworm eggs to Constantinople and raised them on mulberry leaves.

<More title={{ en: "A letter that never arrived", zh: "一封没有送到的信" }}>

Miwnay writes that she has been in Dunhuang for <Num s="S94">three</Num> years and lives “wretchedly, without clothing, without money”; she asks for a loan and to be taken to her husband. The letters were probably taken by a Chinese garrison and never reached the people they were written to. [S94]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Translators from the west", zh: "从西边来的译经人" }}>

Two of the most important early translators of Buddhist texts into Chinese were not Chinese: An Shigao came from Parthia and Kumarajiva from Kucha, an oasis kingdom of the Tarim Basin. [S47, S49]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How silkworms travelled", zh: "蚕是怎样传过去的" }}>

Procopius says the monks came “from India” and had lived in a country called Serinda, where they learned how silk was made; they carried the eggs back to Byzantium for the emperor Justinian. He gives no exact year, and where Serinda was is not known. [S51]

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

佛教沿着这些路传到中国，而传教的人很多不是中国人。据早期记载，安息人安世高 148 年到达洛阳，开始把佛经译成中文，他是已知最早的佛经汉译者。龟兹人鸠摩罗什在 384 年中国军队攻下龟兹时被俘，401 年到达长安译经。在敦煌，698 年的一块碑说，366 年有人在崖壁上凿出了第一个石窟。

在伊朗，萨珊王朝在 224 年掌权。约从 240 年起，一个叫摩尼的人在那里传播自己创立的宗教。<Term id="term-manichaeism">摩尼教</Term>一路向东传：约 762 年，回鹘的牟羽可汗皈依摩尼教，此后几百年它都是回鹘的国教。

来自撒马尔罕一带的<Term id="term-sogdians">粟特人</Term>商人，一直走到中国做生意。1907 年，斯坦因在玉门关以西的一座烽燧里发现了一包他们的信。这些信约写于 313 年，讲到中国北方的战乱：皇帝逃离了洛阳，“洛阳已经没有了”。其中两封出自一位叫米薇的妇女，她被留在敦煌，身无分文。

在另一头，养蚕的技术本身也传到了西方。东罗马帝国的希腊文史家普罗科匹厄斯说，6 世纪 50 年代初，几位僧人把蚕卵带到君士坦丁堡，用桑叶养大。

<More title={{ en: "A letter that never arrived", zh: "一封没有送到的信" }}>

米薇写道，她在敦煌已经<Num s="S94">三</Num>年，“没有衣服，没有钱，过得很惨”；她请求借钱，请求把她送到丈夫身边。这些信大概被中国的守军扣下，没有送到收信人手里。[S94]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Translators from the west", zh: "从西边来的译经人" }}>

早期最重要的两位佛经汉译者都不是中国人：安世高来自安息，鸠摩罗什来自塔里木盆地的绿洲王国龟兹。[S47, S49]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How silkworms travelled", zh: "蚕是怎样传过去的" }}>

普罗科匹厄斯说，这几位僧人“从印度来”，曾住在一个叫赛林达的地方，在那里学会了养蚕缫丝；他们为皇帝查士丁尼把蚕卵带回拜占庭。他没有写确切的年份，赛林达在哪里也不清楚。[S51]

</More>
