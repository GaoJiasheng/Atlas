## Quantities, dates and sensitive wording

- The only main/More historical quantity sources are **S34** (two schooling hours and four inspectors in 1833), **S70** (about four million / exact 3,953,760 enslaved people in the 1860 United States census), **S28** (New Harbour 1869: 99 steamers / 65 sailing vessels; 1879: 541 / 91), and **S22** (34 km in the railway More). Dates, chapter numbers and descriptive figure topology are not presented as statistical observations.
- The census is neither a cotton-workforce count, a New Orleans population, a cumulative enslaved population nor deaths. No casualties field is used for it. Census and factory legislation indexes are regional reading anchors; their point locations do not locate all affected people or the passage of a national law.
- Harbour counts retain their annual wharf-visiting scope. They do not identify distinct vessel identities, describe all Singapore shipping or establish the canal's independent causal effect.
- Slavery's property status, dangerous child work, long hours, fines and physical punishment are stated directly, without graphic scenes or invented accident rates. A law is not treated as proof of universal compliance.
- Indenture, kangany recruitment and free migration remain distinct. Poor conditions, recruitment abuses and extended debt are qualitative claims grounded in Periasamy's Indian-migration account, not claims about every migrant or every Chinese miner.
- Coal-bearing strata are geology. No exposure surface, individual fixed lifespan loss or “Industrial Revolution death toll” is inferred. Production, wages, safety, schooling, health and freedom are not combined into a progress score.

Excluded per §0.3: 1733 shuttle priority/speed claim; eight-thread jenny count; Whitbread modification chronology; trade money totals; disputed 1833 daily-hours summaries; Luddite case; 22/11 rubber seedling comparison; exact smoke lifespan numbers; curriculum/learning-study assertions and quotations; New Orleans domestic slave-trade subclaim based on the failed Smithsonian page. Changed claims use 1770 patent, 1776 operating engines, 1881 Tangxu construction, Gupta/Roy for Indian industries and Malik for Russian capital.
