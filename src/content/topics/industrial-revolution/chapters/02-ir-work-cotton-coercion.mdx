<Lang en>

In December 1790 Slater got cotton-spinning machinery running in Pawtucket. Water supplied the power. The surviving Slater Mill building dates to 1793, so the start of machine production and the date of that building must be kept apart. Children worked in this factory system, earning wages while living under employers' control. [S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

Cotton linked these mills to a wider economy. <Term id="term-slavery">Slavery</Term> treated people as property and compelled them to work. Beckert connects mechanised manufacturing to American cotton grown through enslaved labour. New Orleans, Liverpool and Manchester locate regions in that economy. The map does not claim to trace a particular ship or cargo between their city points. [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0)

Enslaved workers and wage-earning children had different legal positions, but both belong in the history of industrial production. For British textile mills, the National Archives' documents describe dangerous machinery, long working days, fines and physical punishment. Such evidence tells us about workers' conditions, which cannot be read from a machine’s output alone. [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

The 1833 Factory Act restricted child employment in covered textile mills. It provided <Num s="S34">two</Num> hours of schooling and <Num s="S34">four</Num> inspectors. Passing the law did not end child labour. Reports from 1836 recorded breaches: a legal rule and an employer’s practice were different kinds of evidence. [S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

This chapter finally looks ahead to 1860. The United States census counted about <Num s="S70">four million</Num> enslaved people nationally. This is neither a count of cotton workers alone nor a death toll. The next chapter returns to the railway story in 1830; these overlapping histories need not share the same sequence of milestones. [S70](https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2025/june-2025.html)

<More title={{ "en": "Law and enforcement", "zh": "法律与执行" }}>

The Act covered particular textile mills, rather than every kind of work done by children. Inspector reports recorded breaches after it passed. These records prevent us from treating the passing of a law as proof that all working conditions immediately changed. [S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Schooling and inspection", "zh": "学校与监察" }}>

Parliament records <Num s="S34">two</Num> hours of schooling and <Num s="S34">four</Num> inspectors under the 1833 law. Those provisions describe what the law required. They do not tell us that each child received the required schooling or that inspectors reached every workplace.

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "The denominator is the whole country", "zh": "全国人数的分母" }}>

The exact 1860 census count was <Num s="S70">3,953,760</Num> enslaved people in the United States. The figure covers a national population. It is not a count of people in New Orleans, a cotton-field workforce, a cumulative total or a number of people killed. The event is dated 1860, not 1830.

</More>

The final chapter compares these interpretations with other historians. Disagreement about a mechanism or its weight does not erase the documented coercion. [S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0)

<Lang zh>

1790 年 12 月，斯莱特在波塔基特让棉纺机器运转起来，动力来自流水。今天保留的斯莱特厂房建于 1793 年，因此机器开始生产与现存建筑的年代要分开。这套工厂制度雇用儿童；他们领取工资，也受雇主控制。[S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

棉花把工厂连到更广的经济体系。<Term id="term-slavery">奴隶制</Term>把人当作财产，强迫他们劳动。Beckert 的研究把机械化棉纺制造，与美国被奴役者种植的原棉联系起来。新奥尔良、利物浦与曼彻斯特只是地区锚点，地图没有记录某艘货船怎样航行。[S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0)

被奴役者与领工资的童工，法律地位不同，却都不能从生产史中删去。英国国家档案馆的文献写到危险机器、长工时、罚款和体罚。机器产量增加，不能单独说明工人的生活变好了。[S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

1833 年《工厂法》限制适用纺织厂的童工劳动，并规定<Num s="S34">两</Num>小时学校教育、设置<Num s="S34">四</Num>名监察员。但法案并未结束童工；1836 年的报告仍记录违规。法律条文与实际执行，必须分别看。[S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

本章最后展望 1860 年：美国普查记录全国约<Num s="S70">四百万</Num>名被奴役者。他们并非全部种棉，这也不是死亡数。下一章将回到 1830 年的铁路主线。[S70](https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2025/june-2025.html)

</More>
