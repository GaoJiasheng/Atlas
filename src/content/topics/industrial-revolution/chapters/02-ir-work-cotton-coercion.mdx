<Lang en>

In December 1790 Slater got cotton-spinning machinery running in Pawtucket. Water supplied the power. The surviving Slater Mill building dates to 1793, after the machinery first began running. Children worked in this factory system, earning wages while living under employers' control. [S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

Cotton linked these mills to a wider economy. <Term id="term-slavery">Slavery</Term> treated people as property and compelled them to work. Beckert connects mechanised manufacturing to American cotton grown through enslaved labour. New Orleans, Liverpool and Manchester locate regions in that economy. These places were linked by the cotton trade. [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0)

Enslaved workers and wage-earning children had different legal positions, but both belong in the history of industrial production. For British textile mills, the National Archives' documents describe dangerous machinery, long working days, fines and physical punishment. Such evidence tells us about workers' conditions, which cannot be read from a machine’s output alone. [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

The 1833 Factory Act restricted child employment in covered textile mills. It provided <Num s="S34">two</Num> hours of schooling and <Num s="S34">four</Num> inspectors. Passing the law did not end child labour. Reports from 1836 still recorded employers breaking the law. [S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

This chapter finally looks ahead to 1860. The United States census counted about <Num s="S70">four million</Num> enslaved people nationally. This is neither a count of cotton workers alone nor a death toll. The next chapter returns to railways in 1830, while cotton production and child labour continued to change. [S70](https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2025/june-2025.html)

<More title={{ "en": "Law and enforcement", "zh": "法律与执行" }}>

The Act covered particular textile mills, rather than every kind of work done by children. Inspector reports recorded breaches after it passed. Changing working conditions also depended on employers obeying the law and inspectors enforcing it. [S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Schooling and inspection", "zh": "学校与监察" }}>

Parliament records <Num s="S34">two</Num> hours of schooling and <Num s="S34">four</Num> inspectors under the 1833 law. Schooling and inspection became legal duties, but the continuing breaches show how much depended on enforcement.

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "People counted across the United States", "zh": "美国全国的人数" }}>

The exact 1860 census count was <Num s="S70">3,953,760</Num> enslaved people in the United States. This was the number living in slavery across the whole country in that census year. It included people in different kinds of work, beyond cotton fields and beyond New Orleans.

</More>

<Lang zh>

1790 年 12 月，斯莱特在波塔基特让棉纺机器运转起来，动力来自流水。今天保留的斯莱特厂房建于 1793 年，比机器开始运转的时间晚。这套工厂制度雇用儿童；他们领取工资，也受雇主控制。[S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

棉花把工厂连到更广的经济体系。<Term id="term-slavery">奴隶制</Term>把人当作财产，强迫他们劳动。Beckert 的研究把机械化棉纺制造，与美国被奴役者种植的原棉联系起来。新奥尔良、利物浦与曼彻斯特，通过棉花贸易相连。[S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0)

被奴役者与领工资的童工，法律地位不同，却都不能从生产史中删去。英国国家档案馆的文献写到危险机器、长工时、罚款和体罚。机器产量增加，不能单独说明工人的生活变好了。[S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

1833 年《工厂法》限制适用纺织厂的童工劳动，并规定<Num s="S34">两</Num>小时学校教育、设置<Num s="S34">四</Num>名监察员。但法案并未结束童工；1836 年的报告仍记录违规。改善工作条件，还需要雇主守法、监察员执行法律。[S34](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/factoryact/) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf)

本章最后展望 1860 年：美国普查记录全国约<Num s="S70">四百万</Num>名被奴役者。他们并非全部种棉，这也不是死亡数。下一章将回到 1830 年的铁路主线。[S70](https://www.census.gov/about/history/stories/monthly/2025/june-2025.html)
