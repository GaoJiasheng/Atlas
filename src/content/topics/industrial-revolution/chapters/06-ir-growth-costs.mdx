<Lang en>

Around 1900, foreign investors were putting money into Russia. Malik studies this investment, which came long after the public railway opening of 1837. Building industry needed money; who supplied it and who earned the profits also mattered. [S82](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv301gmn)

Laws, workers joining together, and water and sewage systems also changed daily life. British legislation in 1844 required dangerous machinery to be guarded; the 1847 Act limited hours for women and young people in textile factories. The TUC describes the 1871 Trade Union Act as securing unions' legal status and protecting their funds. Public Health Acts in 1848 and 1875 gave public authorities responsibilities for clean water and sewage systems. By 1900, these laws formed part of a longer history of changing work and urban life. [S35](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/laterfactoryleg/) [S37](https://www.tuc.org.uk/research-analysis/reports/section-tucs-first-victories) [S38](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/towncountry/towns/keydates/)

Coal smoke added another cost. Hanlon’s historical study links pollution from burning coal in industry with a higher risk of death. The risk varied with how much pollution people breathed. Greater machine output alone did not bring better health or higher earnings. [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/)

In India, some crafts and industries made fewer goods or employed fewer people. Historians call a decline of manufacturing in a particular trade or place <Term id="term-deindustrialisation">deindustrialisation</Term>. Gupta and Roy discuss artisanal decline alongside the rise of modern cotton, jute and tea industries. Hand spinning, weaving and other crafts did not all follow the same path. A country could have expanding industries alongside declining crafts. [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209)

In Malaya and Singapore, migrant labour helped link production and trade. Contracts, recruitment and debt could constrain migrant workers, while slavery involved treating people as property. Better transport or greater production alone cannot tell us how free, safe or well paid a worker was. [S59](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue3-oct-2007-indian-migration-british-malaya-singapore/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html)

Hanlon’s research links historical industrial coal use with a higher risk of death. People’s exposure differed, so the study does not give every resident the same loss of life. Studying that risk requires evidence of pollution as well as the location of coal-bearing rocks. [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/)

<More title={{ "en": "Output and wages need different evidence", "zh": "产量与工资须分别找证据" }}>

Allen calls a period in early nineteenth-century Britain “Engels’ pause”: workers produced more, while their pay rose more slowly. The pattern changed later. Production and workers’ earnings could grow at different speeds. More railway lines alone do not show how much workers earned or how healthy they were. [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Indian industries followed different paths", "zh": "印度各行业的不同变化" }}>

Gupta and Roy compare crafts that declined with modern industries that grew. They discuss Clingingsmith and Williamson’s explanation: political crises, environmental problems and changing world prices could affect production. Different trades could therefore grow or shrink at the same time. [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Coal underground and smoke in the air", "zh": "地下的煤与空气中的烟" }}>

A coal-bearing rock layer answers a geological question. It does not tell us the smoke concentration in Manchester in 1900. Coal-bearing rocks lie underground; smoke reaches people through the air. To study health, historians must also ask where and how much coal was burned, and who breathed the polluted air. [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/) [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf)

<Lang zh>

1900 年前后，外国投资者把资金投入俄国。Malik 研究这些投资；此时距离 1837 年公共铁路开通已过去多年。建工厂需要钱，谁出钱、谁得到利润，也很重要。[S82](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv301gmn)

机器以外，法律、工人团结起来争取权益，以及供水和排污设施，也改变生活。英国 1844 年立法要求危险机器加防护；1847 年法案限制纺织厂妇女与年轻人的工时。工会大会把 1871 年《工会法》描述为确立工会法律地位、保护基金的法律。1848 与 1875 年公共卫生法规定公共部门要负责供水和环境卫生。到 1900 年，这些法律已经成为工作与城市生活变化的历史。[S35](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/livinglearning/19thcentury/overview/laterfactoryleg/) [S37](https://www.tuc.org.uk/research-analysis/reports/section-tucs-first-victories) [S38](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/towncountry/towns/keydates/)

煤烟带来另一种代价。Hanlon 的历史研究把工业煤烟与死亡风险联系起来。人们接触污染的程度不同，承受的风险也不同；机器产量提高，并不自动带来更好的健康或更高的收入。[S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/)

在印度，有些手工业和制造业生产减少，或者雇用的工人减少。史家把某个行业或地区的制造业衰退称为<Term id="term-deindustrialisation">去工业化</Term>（deindustrialisation）。Gupta 与 Roy 并列讨论手工业衰退，以及现代棉纺、黄麻和茶业的发展。手纺、织布与其他行业并非同进同退。[S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209)

回到马来亚与新加坡，契约、招工和债务可能限制移民劳工的选择；奴隶制则把人当作财产。运输更快、产量更多，并不能单独证明劳工更自由、更安全或工资更高。[S59](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue3-oct-2007-indian-migration-british-malaya-singapore/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html)

<More title={{ "en": "What the smoke study can tell us", "zh": "煤烟研究能够说明什么" }}>

Hanlon 的研究把历史上的工业用煤与较高的死亡风险联系起来。人们接触污染的程度不同，因此研究并不是说每个人都少活相同的年数。研究健康风险，既要知道哪里有煤，也要知道污染怎样影响人。[S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Output and wages need different evidence", "zh": "产量与工资须分别找证据" }}>

Allen 把英国十九世纪早期的一段时期称为“恩格斯停顿”：工人生产更多，工资却增长得较慢，后来情况又有变化。生产与工人的收入可能以不同速度增长。铁路更多，也不一定说明工人工资更高、身体更健康。[S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Indian industries followed different paths", "zh": "印度各行业的不同变化" }}>

Gupta 与 Roy 比较衰退的手工业和增长的现代产业。他们讨论 Clingingsmith 与 Williamson 的解释：政治危机、环境问题和世界价格变化，都可能影响生产。因此，同一时期有的行业增长，有的行业衰退。[S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Coal underground and smoke in the air", "zh": "地下的煤与空气中的烟" }}>

含煤地层回答地质问题，不能给出 1900 年曼彻斯特的煤烟浓度。煤藏在地下，烟则进入空气。研究健康影响，还要了解哪里烧了多少煤，以及哪些人呼吸了受污染的空气。[S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/) [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf)
