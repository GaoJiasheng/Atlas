The <Term id="term-industrial-revolution">Industrial Revolution</Term> names changes in production and power that this topic begins by following in Britain. Before these changes, skilled workers already made textiles in homes and workshops. <Term id="term-spinning">Spinning</Term> made yarn; <Term id="term-weaving">weaving</Term> crossed yarns to make cloth. Neither skill began with a <Term id="term-steam-engine">steam engine</Term>. Different places specialised in different kinds of work. [S3](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/zh-cn/articles/industrial-revolution/) [S75](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/glossary-of-weaving-terms.htm)

<Term id="term-mechanisation">Mechanisation</Term> means using machines for production tasks. A <Term id="term-factory">factory</Term> brought machinery and workers together, but its power could come from flowing water. Slater’s cotton-spinning machinery in Pawtucket is an example. The words “factory” and “steam power” describe different things: the organisation of work and a source of power. [S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

The dates from about 1760 to 1914 frame the cases selected here. They do not divide everyone’s history into the same before and after. British textile regions, European railways, American cotton, Asian workshops and Singapore’s port enter the story through particular evidence. A new machine or railway was a milestone in a place, not a switch that changed a whole country at once. [S3](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/zh-cn/articles/industrial-revolution/)

Start with <FlyTo preset="view-britain">Britain</FlyTo>, then use the wider views to compare places. A <Term id="term-coalfield">coalfield</Term> contains coal-bearing rocks. The shading comes from modern geology, not a register of working mines in 1760. City points locate regions; they do not mark exact historical workshops. A blank railway layer leaves a gap in this map’s evidence, rather than erasing the people and work beyond it. [S64](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/datasets/coal-resources-for-new-technologies/) [S68](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) [S63](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/rshapes/)

<Term id="term-industrial-revolution">工业革命</Term>是生产与动力发生变化的一段历史。本题先从英国讲起。变化以前，人们早已在家庭和作坊里制造纺织品，各地有不同的专长。<Term id="term-spinning">纺纱</Term>是做出纱线，<Term id="term-weaving">织布</Term>是把纱线交织成布；这些技能都不是有了<Term id="term-steam-engine">蒸汽机</Term>才出现的。[S3](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/zh-cn/articles/industrial-revolution/) [S75](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/glossary-of-weaving-terms.htm)

用机器完成生产任务，称为<Term id="term-mechanisation">机械化</Term>。<Term id="term-factory">工厂</Term>把工人和机器集中起来，却不一定烧煤。波塔基特（Pawtucket）的斯莱特棉纺机器就靠水力运转。工厂说的是怎样组织生产，蒸汽动力说的是机器靠什么工作，两者不能画等号。[S20](https://home.nps.gov/blrv/learn/historyculture/slatermill.htm)

约 1760—1914 年只是本题选定的观察时段，不是全世界共同的起止线。英国纺织地区、欧洲铁路、美洲棉花与亚洲工商业，会按各自的证据进入故事。某地出现新机器或新铁路，并不表示全国人的生产与生活同时改变。[S3](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/zh-cn/articles/industrial-revolution/)
