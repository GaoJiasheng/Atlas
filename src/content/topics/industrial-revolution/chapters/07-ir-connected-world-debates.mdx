By 1914 the cases visited here were connected, but their changes were uneven. Shanghai’s arsenal, Japanese technical adaptation and Indian industries belonged to different histories. Singapore brought shipping, tin smelting, rubber and cable insulation into a regional story. Ships carried materials between these places, and people shared knowledge. The people doing this work faced different rules and conditions. [S24](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6364) [S25](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gmww/202104/t20210406_249554.shtml) [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209) [S28](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=15fcae20-2e3d-4eec-b5ab-2384f1aac2b9) [S29](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue2-jul-2007-tapping-history/) [S30](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/sbg/research/publications/-/media/sbg/gardenwise/2005-jul-gardenwise-vol-25.ashx) [S32](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/chapters-of-asia/chapters-on-asia-2015-on-the-waterfront-the-building-of-singapore-s-first-high-rise-skyline-1918-1928/)

The continental railway layer is denser by this final date. Railway building connected more places, while income, power sources and working conditions varied. Across Asia too, different regions and industries followed different paths. [S63](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/rshapes/)

Why did industry change? Robert Allen asks whether high wages and cheap fuel made it worthwhile to invent machines that needed fewer workers. Joel Mokyr asks how useful knowledge and the sharing of ideas helped people invent. Kenneth Pomeranz asks how coal and resources grown on American land helped some regions overcome shortages. Each looks at a different part of the question. [S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711) [S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

Eric Williams argued that slavery and slave-trade wealth helped finance British industrial development. Sven Beckert links mechanised cotton manufacture to slave-grown supplies. Trevor Burnard and Giorgio Riello also ask what people bought and when changes happened. Alan Olmstead and Paul Rhode question some of the evidence and explanations used in recent histories of slavery and capitalism. The debate concerns how slavery helped shape industry, and how large its role was. [S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)

Industrial production involved slavery, dangerous child work and restrictions on colonial migrant labour. It also brought pollution, while its gains were shared unequally. A map helps locate connections in time and place. Understanding a machine’s motion answers another question; neither account alone describes the lives of all the people who worked around it. [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf) [S59](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue3-oct-2007-indian-migration-british-malaya-singapore/) [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html) Explore the <TopicLink slug="steam-engine" ch="before-you-start">steam engine</TopicLink> next to follow its moving parts and energy transfers.

到 1914 年，这些案例彼此相连，变化却不均衡。上海制造局、日本的技术改造与印度各行业，各有历史。新加坡把航运、炼锡、橡胶与电缆绝缘材料带进区域故事；船只运送原料，人们传播知识；从事这些工作的人面对不同的规定和劳动条件。[S24](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6364) [S25](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gmww/202104/t20210406_249554.shtml) [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209) [S28](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=15fcae20-2e3d-4eec-b5ab-2384f1aac2b9) [S29](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue2-jul-2007-tapping-history/) [S30](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/sbg/research/publications/-/media/sbg/gardenwise/2005-jul-gardenwise-vol-25.ashx) [S32](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/chapters-of-asia/chapters-on-asia-2015-on-the-waterfront-the-building-of-singapore-s-first-high-rise-skyline-1918-1928/)

到这一时期，欧洲大陆的铁路更加密集，连接起更多地方；各地收入、动力与劳动条件仍有差别。亚洲各地区、各行业也走过不同道路。[S63](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/rshapes/)

工业为什么改变？Allen 问：工资高、燃料便宜，会不会让人觉得发明省人力的机器很划算？Mokyr 问：实用知识和思想传播怎样帮助人们发明？Pomeranz 问：煤和美洲土地上生产的资源，怎样帮助一些地区应对资源短缺？他们各自研究问题的不同方面。[S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711) [S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

Williams 认为奴隶制和奴隶贸易赚来的钱帮助英国发展工业；Beckert 强调被奴役者种植的原棉供应。Burnard 与 Riello 还问人们买了什么、变化何时发生。Olmstead 与 Rhode 质疑近年有关奴隶制与资本主义的一些史著所用的证据和解释。争论关乎奴隶制怎样影响工业，以及作用有多大。[S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)
