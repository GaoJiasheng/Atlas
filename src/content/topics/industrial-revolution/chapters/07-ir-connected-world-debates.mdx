By 1914 the cases visited here were connected, but their changes were uneven. Shanghai’s arsenal, Japanese technical adaptation and Indian industries belonged to different histories. Singapore brought shipping, tin smelting, rubber and cable insulation into a regional story. Materials and knowledge travelled through different institutions and labour systems. [S24](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6364) [S25](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gmww/202104/t20210406_249554.shtml) [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209) [S28](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=15fcae20-2e3d-4eec-b5ab-2384f1aac2b9) [S29](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue2-jul-2007-tapping-history/) [S30](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/sbg/research/publications/-/media/sbg/gardenwise/2005-jul-gardenwise-vol-25.ashx) [S32](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/chapters-of-asia/chapters-on-asia-2015-on-the-waterfront-the-building-of-singapore-s-first-high-rise-skyline-1918-1928/)

The continental railway layer is denser by this final date. Railway building connected more places, while income, power sources and working conditions varied. Across Asia too, different regions and industries followed different paths. [S63](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/rshapes/)

Historians also ask different questions. Robert Allen emphasises wages relative to cheap energy as incentives for labour-saving inventions. Joel Mokyr stresses useful knowledge, Enlightenment culture and the circulation of ideas. Kenneth Pomeranz highlights accessible coal and land-intensive resources from the Americas when comparing advanced Eurasian regions. These explanations need not form opposing teams. [S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711) [S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

Eric Williams argued that slavery and slave-trade wealth helped finance British industrial development. Sven Beckert links mechanised cotton manufacture to slave-grown supplies. Trevor Burnard and Giorgio Riello emphasise multiple causes, consumption and chronology; Alan Olmstead and Paul Rhode criticise parts of the evidence and mechanisms in the new history of capitalism. Their arguments examine different ways in which slavery, trade and industrial production were connected. [S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)

Industrial production involved slavery, dangerous child work and restrictions on colonial migrant labour. It also brought pollution, while its gains were shared unequally. A map helps locate connections in time and place. Understanding a machine’s motion answers another question; neither account alone describes the lives of all the people who worked around it. [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf) [S59](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue3-oct-2007-indian-migration-british-malaya-singapore/) [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html) Explore the <TopicLink slug="steam-engine" ch="before-you-start">steam engine</TopicLink> next to follow its moving parts and energy transfers.

<More title={{ "en": "Incentives and knowledge", "zh": "激励与知识" }}>

Allen asks why particular inventions paid under British wage and energy prices. Mokyr asks how useful knowledge and its circulation helped sustain innovation. These are different explanatory tasks. Together, they draw attention to both the reasons to invent and the knowledge needed to do so. [S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Coal and land-intensive resources", "zh": "煤与土地密集型资源" }}>

Pomeranz compares advanced Eurasian regions and stresses accessible coal and resources from the Americas. This is an explanation about resource constraints and connections, not a claim that all Europeans or all Asians shared one experience. [S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Slavery, supplies and disputed mechanisms", "zh": "奴隶制、供应与有争议的机制" }}>

Williams’s financing argument and Beckert’s cotton-supply account address related but distinct mechanisms. Burnard and Riello draw attention to chronology, consumption and sugar before large American cotton exports. Olmstead and Rhode challenge evidence and mechanisms in parts of the new history of capitalism. None of these comparisons makes the existence of slavery or compelled labour uncertain. [S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "A comparison needs more than one template", "zh": "比较不能只用一个模板" }}>

Horn’s French case, Tilly and Kopsidis’s regional German history, Malik’s foreign-capital account and Gupta and Roy’s Indian industries examine different processes. They remind us to specify region, industry and time before comparing change. The map and the machinery story each supply only part of that evidence. <Lang zh>

到 1914 年，这些案例彼此相连，变化却不均衡。上海制造局、日本的技术改造与印度各行业，各有历史。新加坡把航运、炼锡、橡胶与电缆绝缘材料带进区域故事；原料和知识经过不同机构与劳动制度流动。[S24](https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/6364) [S25](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gmww/202104/t20210406_249554.shtml) [S49](https://academic.oup.com/book/7358/chapter/152152209) [S28](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=15fcae20-2e3d-4eec-b5ab-2384f1aac2b9) [S29](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue2-jul-2007-tapping-history/) [S30](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/sbg/research/publications/-/media/sbg/gardenwise/2005-jul-gardenwise-vol-25.ashx) [S32](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/chapters-of-asia/chapters-on-asia-2015-on-the-waterfront-the-building-of-singapore-s-first-high-rise-skyline-1918-1928/)

到这一时期，欧洲大陆的铁路更加密集，连接起更多地方；各地收入、动力与劳动条件仍有差别。亚洲各地区、各行业也走过不同道路。[S63](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/rshapes/)

史家提出的问题也不同。Allen 强调工资相对廉价能源的关系，如何鼓励节省劳动的发明；Mokyr 重视实用知识、启蒙文化与思想流通；Pomeranz 则强调煤和美洲土地密集型资源。这些解释不必分成对立阵营。[S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711) [S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

Williams 讨论奴隶制、奴隶贸易财富与英国工业融资；Beckert 强调原棉供应。Burnard 与 Riello 重视多种原因、消费和年代；Olmstead 与 Rhode 质疑“资本主义新史”部分证据和机制。他们讨论奴隶制、贸易与工业生产以哪些方式相连。[S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)

工业生产涉及奴役、危险童工，以及殖民移民劳工受到的限制；它也带来污染，收益的分配并不均衡。地图说明何时、何地相连，机器原理说明怎样运动，两者都不能单独替所有劳动者讲完他们的生活。[S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S80](https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/education/factory-actdoc.pdf) [S59](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-3-issue3-oct-2007-indian-migration-british-malaya-singapore/) [S81](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/documents/areas/ctr/ziman/2015-18WP.pdf) [S50](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v46y2009i4p418-435.html)接着到<TopicLink slug="steam-engine" ch="before-you-start">蒸汽机</TopicLink>，沿着运动部件与能量传递继续阅读。

<More title={{ "en": "Incentives and knowledge", "zh": "激励与知识" }}>

Allen 追问英国工资与能源价格怎样使某些发明有利可图；Mokyr 追问实用知识及其流通怎样支持持续创新。把两种问题一起考虑，可以同时认识发明的动力与发明所需的知识。[S40](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-0289.2010.00532.x) [S41](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.40336711)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Coal and land-intensive resources", "zh": "煤与土地密集型资源" }}>

Pomeranz 比较欧亚较发达地区，强调易于取得的煤与美洲资源。这个解释讨论资源限制及联系，不是说所有欧洲人或所有亚洲人都有相同经历。[S42](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7sv80)

</More>

<More title={{ "en": "Slavery, supplies and disputed mechanisms", "zh": "奴隶制、供应与有争议的机制" }}>

Williams 的融资论点，与 Beckert 的原棉供应解释有关联，机制却不同。Burnard 与 Riello 注意年代、消费，以及美国大量棉花出口之前的糖业；Olmstead 与 Rhode 则质疑“资本主义新史”部分证据与机制。这样的比较不使奴隶制或强迫劳动的存在变成疑问。[S43](https://uncpress.org/9781469663678/capitalism-and-slavery-third-edition/) [S44](https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/empire-cotton-0) [S45](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/slavery-and-the-new-history-of-capitalism/6885E5697E7151B50FDEFC65ED96C79E) [S46](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/exehis/v67y2018icp1-17.html)
