<Lang en>

Find the mouth of the Singapore River on the south coast. The City of Singapore spreads around it, with shophouses, offices, markets and the port packed close together. Outside the city, most of the island is countryside. A <Term id="kampong">kampong</Term> is a village. Kampongs stood among vegetable plots, fishponds, pig and chicken farms, and rubber and coconut estates. The Singapore Food Agency counts about <Num s="S102">20,000</Num> farms in 1959.

Some land had other jobs. The British naval base filled a stretch of the north coast, and there were airfields at Seletar, Sembawang, Tengah and Changi. Paya Lebar was the civil airport. In the middle of the island, forest was kept around three reservoirs that held the town's water.

This is a map of <Term id="land-use">land use</Term>: what each piece of land is used for. Its shapes come from a map printed by the British government in April 1959. The sand colour puts villages, farms and plantations together, because that map does not separate them. It does not mean every patch was a farm, or that every village lived the same way.

The dashed line is today's coast. Wherever it lies out in the sea, land was added later. <More title={{ en: "Where the 1959 map comes from", zh: "1959 年的地图从哪里来" }}>

The map was printed in *The State of Singapore*, a booklet published by British Information Services in April 1959, two months before Singapore became self-governing. It marks the City of Singapore and the naval base with fine dots, which have clear edges and could be traced. Forest and swamp are drawn as scattered symbols with no edges, so they are not coloured here. The coast of 1959 comes from British sea charts surveyed between 1954 and 1961; the smaller islands, which those charts leave out, come from a satellite image of 1973.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What a kampong was like", zh: "甘榜是什么样的" }}>

An <Term id="attap-house">attap house</Term> was a timber house with a sloping roof of palm leaves and a floor raised off the ground. Kampongs were not all alike. Some had piped water and electricity; others had neither. Kampong Wak Sumang in Punggol, for example, got piped water, electricity, paved roads and drains only during the 1960s. People still speak of the “kampong spirit” of neighbours helping one another. The word kampong means village; it does not mean a slum.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Airfields and the naval base", zh: "机场和海军基地" }}>

Seletar began flying in 1928 and was officially opened in 1930. Paya Lebar Airport opened in 1955. The naval base opened in 1938. The markers for Sembawang and Changi airfields are dated by the 1959 map, not by their opening days, which were not confirmed for this topic.

</More>

</Lang>

先在南岸找到新加坡河的河口。新加坡市区就围着它展开，店屋、办公楼、巴刹和港口挤在一起。市区以外，岛上大部分是乡村。<Term id="kampong">甘榜</Term>就是村子。甘榜散落在菜园、鱼塘、猪寮鸡寮，以及橡胶园和椰园之间。据新加坡食品局统计，1959 年岛上约有 <Num s="S102">2 万</Num>个农场。

有些土地另有用途。英国海军基地占了北岸的一大段，实里达、三巴旺、登加和樟宜都有机场，巴耶利峇是民用机场。岛的中部，三个蓄水池四周留着森林，池里存着市区用的水。

这是一张<Term id="land-use">土地用途</Term>图：每块地拿来做什么。图上的形状取自英国政府 1959 年 4 月印行的一张地图。沙色把村子、农场和种植园画在一起，因为原图没有把它们分开。这不表示每一块沙色的地都是农场，也不表示每个甘榜的生活都一样。

<More title={{ en: "Where the 1959 map comes from", zh: "1959 年的地图从哪里来" }}>

这张图印在英国新闻处 1959 年 4 月出版的小册子《新加坡邦》里，两个月后新加坡就自治了。图上用细密的小点标出新加坡市区和海军基地，边界清楚，可以描下来。森林和沼泽只画成零散的符号，没有边界，所以这里不上色。1959 年的海岸取自 1954 到 1961 年间测量的英国海图；海图上缺的小岛，取自 1973 年的卫星影像。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What a kampong was like", zh: "甘榜是什么样的" }}>

<Term id="attap-house">亚答屋</Term>是木屋，斜斜的屋顶铺着棕榈叶，地板离地架高。甘榜各不相同：有的通了自来水和电，有的两样都没有。比如榜鹅一带的 Kampong Wak Sumang，到 1960 年代才陆续有了自来水、电、柏油路和沟渠。今天人们还常说“甘榜精神”，指的是邻里互相帮忙。“甘榜”的意思是村子，不是贫民窟。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Airfields and the naval base", zh: "机场和海军基地" }}>

实里达机场 1928 年开始起降飞机，1930 年正式启用。巴耶利峇机场 1955 年启用。海军基地 1938 年启用。三巴旺和樟宜机场的标记，日期取自 1959 年的地图，不是启用日期；这两个日期本主题没能核实。

</More>
