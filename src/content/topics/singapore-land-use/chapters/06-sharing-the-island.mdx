The <Term id="master-plan">Master Plan</Term> is the government's plan for every piece of land: what it may be used for and how much may be built on it. The Urban Redevelopment Authority reviews it every <Num s="S212">five</Num> years. The coloured map is the 2019 plan. It shows **planned use**, so a plot coloured for housing may still be empty, and a plot marked as a reserve site is being kept for a decision later.

All the needs in this topic meet on one small island. Homes need to be near work and transport. Factories need roads and ports. Farms need flat, open land. Reservoirs need a <Term id="water-catchment">water catchment</Term>: land where the rain is collected and led into them, much larger than the water you can see.

A <Term id="nature-reserve">nature reserve</Term> is protected by law. The forest in the centre shelters plants and animals and also keeps rainwater clean for the reservoirs. Sungei Buloh in the north-west protects mangroves and mudflats, and Labrador on the south coast protects a strip of coastal forest. The reserves are drawn with today's boundaries.

Now find a place you know. Drag the timeline back to 1959 and look again: you may see countryside, sea or an old shoreline where homes and railways stand today. A part of the map with nothing on it does not mean nothing was there; it may mean no open map of that time exists.

<More title={{ en: "Why nature reserves are left alone", zh: "为什么自然保护区不能开发" }}>

Rain falling on the forest around the first three reservoirs runs into them with little to pollute it, which is why no major building is allowed in these catchments. The forest is also home to many plants and animals. Bukit Timah became a forest reserve in the 1880s. Since 1951 Bukit Timah and the forest around the central reservoirs have been protected by law as nature reserves. Sungei Buloh, opened as a nature park in 1993, and Labrador became nature reserves in 2002. The reservoirs on this map have the same shapes, names and years as in <TopicLink slug="four-taps" ch="the-first-reservoirs">The Four National Taps</TopicLink>.

</More>

<Term id="master-plan">总蓝图</Term>是政府为每一块地定下的规划：可以拿来做什么，可以建多密。市区重建局每 <Num s="S212">5</Num> 年检讨一次。地图上的颜色是 2019 年的总蓝图，画的是**规划用途**：涂成住宅色的地，可能还空着；标为“预留地”的，是留待以后再决定的地。

本主题讲到的各种需要，都挤在同一座小岛上。住家要靠近工作地点和交通，工厂需要道路和港口，农场需要平坦开阔的土地，蓄水池需要<Term id="water-catchment">集水区</Term>：把雨水收集起来、引进蓄水池的那片土地，比看得见的水面大得多。

<Term id="nature-reserve">自然保护区</Term>受法律保护。中部的森林是动植物的家，也让流进蓄水池的雨水保持干净。西北的双溪布洛保护红树林和泥滩，南岸的拉柏多保护一小片海边的森林。图上的保护区用的是今天的边界。

现在找一个你熟悉的地方，把时间轴拖回 1959 年再看一次：今天的住家和铁路那里，当年可能是乡村、海，或者一条旧海岸线。地图上空着的地方，不一定当时什么都没有，也可能只是找不到可以公开使用的那个年代的地图。

<More title={{ en: "Why nature reserves are left alone", zh: "为什么自然保护区不能开发" }}>

落在最早三个蓄水池四周森林里的雨水，流进蓄水池时几乎没有受到污染，所以这些集水区不准大规模开发。森林里也住着许多动植物。武吉知马在 1880 年代成为森林保留地。1951 年起，武吉知马和中部蓄水池四周的森林依法受到保护，成为自然保护区。双溪布洛 1993 年开放为自然公园，它和拉柏多都在 2002 年成为自然保护区。图上蓄水池的形状、名称和年份，与<TopicLink slug="four-taps" ch="the-first-reservoirs">“四大水喉”</TopicLink>主题相同。
