<Lang en>

The <Term id="master-plan">Master Plan</Term> sets out the planned use of land and how densely it may be developed. The Urban Redevelopment Authority reviews it every <Num s="S212">five</Num> years. On this map, Master Plan 2019 planned use groups homes, industry, transport, green space and other needs into broad colours. It does not show what was actually standing on every plot on the publication day.

Land serves needs that can support one another or compete. Homes need routes to workplaces. Factories need services and access for goods. Food production needs suitable sites. Reservoirs need places to collect water. A <Term id="water-catchment">water catchment</Term> is land that sends rain towards a reservoir; it is much larger than the blue water surface itself.

A <Term id="nature-reserve">nature reserve</Term> protects habitats and wildlife under law. Forest in the centre also helps protect water quality. Sungei Buloh protects coastal wetlands, while Labrador protects a different coastal environment. These places cannot simply be treated as empty spaces waiting for buildings. The map uses today’s reserve boundaries approximately; legal protection and earlier forest-reserve designations have their own histories.

Find a familiar place, then compare the dated layers around it. Travel back to 1959 and look again. Some changes are visible, but a blank layer is a gap in the evidence, not proof that nothing was there. Sharing a small island requires choices about homes, jobs, food, travel, water and living habitats, as well as the people already using each place.

The countryside category belongs to the earlier map, so it is not a separate Master Plan 2019 planned-use group. “City, shops and services” combines several detailed planned uses, including reserve sites. A reserve site is kept for a future decision; it is different from a nature reserve. The green group also includes open space and beaches, rather than only protected forest.
