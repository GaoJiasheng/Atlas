<Lang en>

People need to travel between homes, schools, shops and workplaces. Roads make those journeys possible, but roads also take land. Adding lanes cannot be the only response to traffic on a small island. In 1973 several bus companies merged into Singapore Bus Service. On 2 June 1975 the Area Licensing Scheme began charging vehicles entering the restricted city zone during charging hours.

Connections beyond the island changed too. Changi Airport opened for commercial operations on 1 July 1981. Reclamation had enlarged its site at the eastern tip. Choosing the location gave the airport room to grow, while moving civilian flights away from Paya Lebar. An airport needs much more land than the terminal building alone: runways, access roads and other facilities also need space.

On 7 November 1987 passenger trains began running between Toa Payoh and Yio Chu Kang. That first MRT section had <Num s="S154">five</Num> stations. Lines then extended towards more towns and workplaces. Some tracks run underground and others on viaducts, allowing railways to cross the city without a continuous strip of land at street level.

The MRT network grew from <Num s="S156">67</Num> kilometres in 1990 to <Num s="S156">242.5</Num> kilometres in 2024. Light rail connects neighbourhoods inside some new towns. Vehicle quotas and Electronic Road Pricing also manage demand for road space. The map’s lines appear by first passenger-service dates; a ceremonial opening can happen later.

<More title={{"en": "Different ways of managing traffic", "zh": "【待译】Different ways of managing traffic"}}>

A Certificate of Entitlement controls registration of a new vehicle through a quota. ERP charges for using busy roads at specified times [S153]. The Area Licensing Scheme used a paid paper licence for entry into a restricted zone [S157]. These measures act at different points: owning a vehicle, using a road, or entering an area.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Passenger service and official opening", "zh": "【待译】Passenger service and official opening"}}>

The MRT’s first passenger section opened on 7 November 1987 [S154]. The official opening was on 12 March 1988 [S155]. The railway layer uses passenger service, so a line can already be visible before an opening-ceremony event.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Light rail and the map", "zh": "【待译】Light rail and the map"}}>

Light rail carries passengers around neighbourhoods and links them with MRT interchanges. On the map, sections appear when passenger service begins. A line may later gain new stations, so a railway’s first opening does not date every stop along it. MRT length and LRT length are counted separately; adding them gives a different network total.

</More>
