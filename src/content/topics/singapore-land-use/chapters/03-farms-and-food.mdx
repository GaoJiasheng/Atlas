<Lang en>

In 1959 Singapore had about <Num s="S102">20,000</Num> farms. Families kept pigs, chickens and ducks, grew vegetables and fruit, and raised fish in ponds. As new towns and factories spread, farmland shrank: from about <Num s="S102">14,000</Num> hectares to about <Num s="S102">8,400</Num> as the 1970s began. Between 1975 and 1984 about <Num s="S101">8,000</Num> farms closed. For a farming family this meant losing a home, a living and neighbours, as well as land.

Water mattered too. Waste from pig farms polluted streams and the sea. In the 1970s many pig farms were moved to Punggol, partly to keep them away from the water catchments. Then in March 1984 the government announced that pig farming would end altogether, pointing to the pollution, the cost of treating the waste, the shortage of land and complaints from new housing estates nearby. The last pig farms closed in 1989.

From 1986 the government set aside land for <Term id="agrotechnology-park">agrotechnology parks</Term> and rented it to farms that used machines, greenhouses and science to grow more on less land. In <Term id="hydroponics">hydroponics</Term>, plants grow with their roots in water that carries nutrients, without soil. Singapore's first indoor farm, set up in 2014, grows vegetables in stacked trays under lamps.

In 2024 Singapore had <Num s="S202">153</Num> food farms on land and <Num s="S202">72</Num> in the sea, yet it still imported more than <Num s="S104">90</Num> per cent of its food. Local farms are one part of a food supply that also depends on buying from many countries. The olive shapes on the map are today's farmland in the agrotechnology parks, not the farms that disappeared.

The government said pig waste polluted the water, that treating it would cost a great deal, that the land was needed and that the farms were now close to homes. Earlier, pig farmers from other areas had been moved to Punggol, and many had spent money on new pens there. In Parliament in 1988, the Member of Parliament Koh Lip Lin criticised the change of policy, coming after these farmers had invested in new farms at Punggol. A study by the Centre for Liveable Cities describes families who started new businesses such as shops, and others who felt they had lost a way of life and the ties that came with it. Pig-farm water and the reservoirs are explained in <TopicLink slug="four-taps" ch="damming-the-rivers">Damming the rivers</TopicLink>.

The government's earlier goal, called “30 by 30”, was that by 2030 farms in Singapore should be able to produce <Num s="S201">30</Num> per cent of the nutrition people need. On 4 November 2025 it was replaced by new goals for 2035, called Singapore Food Story 2: local farms should be able to supply <Num s="S201,S220">20</Num> per cent of the leafy and fruit vegetables, bean sprouts and mushrooms eaten, and <Num s="S201,S220">30</Num> per cent of the eggs and seafood. These are goals for what farms can produce, not the share of all food that Singapore grows.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The first indoor farm", zh: "第一个室内菜园" }}>

The Singapore Food Agency dates the first indoor farm to 2014. The company that built it says the farm was set up in October 2013, and on 31 July 2014 it announced a trial supply of its vegetables to a restaurant chain. Indoor farms control light, water and temperature, so crops do not depend on the weather, but the lamps and pumps use a lot of electricity.

1959 年，新加坡约有 <Num s="S102">2 万</Num>个农场。农家养猪、养鸡、养鸭，种菜种果，还在池塘里养鱼。新镇和工厂越建越多，农地越来越少：1970 年代初，从约 <Num s="S102">1 万 4000</Num> 公顷减到约 <Num s="S102">8400</Num> 公顷。1975 到 1984 年间，约 <Num s="S101">8000</Num> 个农场关闭。对农家来说，失去的不只是土地，还有住处、生计和邻里。

水也是原因。养猪场的污水污染了溪流和海水。1970 年代，许多养猪场被迁到榜鹅，部分原因是让它们远离集水区。1984 年 3 月，政府进一步宣布全面停止养猪，理由是污染严重、处理污水成本高、土地不足，附近新建的住宅区也有居民投诉。最后一批养猪场在 1989 年关闭。

从 1986 年起，政府划出土地设立<Term id="agrotechnology-park">农业科技园</Term>，租给用机器、温室和科学方法耕作的农场，在较少的土地上种出更多。<Term id="hydroponics">水耕</Term>不用泥土，植物的根泡在含养分的水里。2014 年设立的第一个室内菜园，把蔬菜种在一层层的架子上，用灯光照着生长。

2024 年，新加坡有 <Num s="S202">153</Num> 个陆上食物农场和 <Num s="S202">72</Num> 个海上渔场，但仍有 <Num s="S104">90</Num>% 以上的食物靠进口。本地农场只是粮食供应的一部分，另一部分靠向许多国家购买。地图上的橄榄色区块是今天农业科技园里的农业用地，不是那些已经消失的农场。

政府的理由是：养猪的污水污染水源，处理费用很高，土地另有需要，而且养猪场已经离住宅区很近。在这之前，其他地区的养猪户已被迁到榜鹅，不少人在那里花钱建了新猪寮。1988 年，国会议员 Koh Lip Lin 在国会批评这项政策转变：这些农户才刚在榜鹅投资建了新农场。宜居城市中心的研究写到，有的家庭改行开店，也有人觉得失去了原来的生活方式和随之而来的人情联系。养猪污水和蓄水池的关系，见<TopicLink slug="four-taps" ch="damming-the-rivers">“在河上筑坝”</TopicLink>。

</More>

政府原先的目标叫“30·30 愿景”：到 2030 年，本地农场要有能力生产国人所需营养的 <Num s="S201">30</Num>%。2025 年 11 月 4 日，“新加坡食物故事 2”提出新的 2035 年目标，取而代之：本地农场要能供应国人所吃的叶菜、果菜、豆芽和菇类的 <Num s="S201,S220">20</Num>%，以及鸡蛋和海产的 <Num s="S201,S220">30</Num>%。这些都是“生产能力”的目标，不是新加坡自己生产了全部食物的几成。

</More>

新加坡食品局把第一个室内农场定在 2014 年。建造它的公司说，这个农场 2013 年 10 月设立，2014 年 7 月 31 日宣布开始试行向一家连锁餐馆供应蔬菜。室内农场控制光、水和温度，作物不靠天气，但灯和水泵很耗电。
