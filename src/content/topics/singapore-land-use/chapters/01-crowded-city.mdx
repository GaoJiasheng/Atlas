<Lang en>

In 1959 Singapore had about <Num s="S63">1.6 million</Num> people. Some city families lived in small shophouse cubicles, sharing a building with many other households. In some wooden-house settlements, water, electricity and toilets were inadequate. Conditions differed between places. A village could also offer familiar neighbours, room for a small business and help from nearby relatives.

On 1 February 1960 the Housing and Development Board replaced the Singapore Improvement Trust. It began building <Term id="hdb-flat">HDB flats</Term> quickly, including small rental homes. In Queenstown, blocks begun under the earlier board were completed in October 1960. Families received those flats the following year. Tall buildings allowed many households to share a smaller piece of land.

On 25 May 1961 a fire swept through Bukit Ho Swee. About <Num s="S55">16,000</Num> people lost their homes and <Num s="S55">four</Num> people died. The cause remains unknown. The government arranged temporary shelter and rehousing. By February 1962 the remaining affected families had been rehoused, while construction continued on and around the burnt land.

HDB began its home-ownership scheme on 12 February 1964. By 1965 about <Num s="S65">23%</Num> of the total population lived in HDB flats. The dots on this map are blocks standing today, appearing by their year of completion. They leave out early blocks that were later demolished. A sparse patch of dots does not mean that nobody lived there then.
