<Lang en>

Homes need more than walls and roofs. A <Term id="new-town">new town</Term> brings housing together with shops, schools, clinics, transport and places to work. HDB prepared land at Toa Payoh in 1964 and describes it as its first comprehensively planned town. Queenstown had already been planned by the earlier Singapore Improvement Trust. The word “first” depends on what is being compared.

Building towns required land that people already owned or occupied. Parliament approved the Land Acquisition Bill in October 1966, and the law took effect in June 1967. <Term id="land-acquisition">Land acquisition</Term> let the government take private land with compensation. Officials argued that land was needed for public housing and development. Some owners said the compensation left them unable to buy a suitable replacement property.

In the west, Jurong developed as an <Term id="industrial-estate">industrial estate</Term>, with factories and housing nearby. Ang Mo Kio’s first slab blocks were built in 1973, and construction at Tampines began in 1979. New housing offered services and safer buildings. Yet <Term id="resettlement">resettlement</Term> could also separate neighbours, interrupt livelihoods and make everyday routines unfamiliar.

By 1990 about <Num s="S65">87%</Num> of Singapore’s residents lived in HDB flats. Follow the growing dots across the map. They show blocks standing today, rather than all the blocks that existed at the time. Industrial shapes also show today’s extent from an approximate starting year; they do not trace each estate’s historical boundary.

In the Founders’ Memorial account, Au Seck Ngee’s family describe leaving their familiar kampong for their first flat. They needed help from HDB to understand English contracts [S69]. This is one family’s experience; residents did not all describe relocation in the same way.

<More title={{"en": "Compensation and two views", "zh": "【待译】Compensation and two views"}}>

CLC records the government’s argument that publicly created increases in land value should benefit the community. It also records owners’ difficulties finding replacement property when compensation used historical values [S54]. Rules changed: from 1973 compensation could use the lower of current and statutory historical values; reforms in 2007 made current market value primary. “Market value” alone hides this history.

</More>
