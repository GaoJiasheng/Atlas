<Lang en>

A <Term id="new-town">new town</Term> is planned as a whole: blocks of flats around a town centre, with shops, markets, schools, clinics and buses. The Singapore Improvement Trust had planned Queenstown in the early 1950s. Toa Payoh, begun at the end of 1964, was the first town planned and built entirely by HDB. Ang Mo Kio followed in 1973 and Tampines in 1979.

New towns needed land that people already owned, farmed or lived on. The Land Acquisition Act, passed on 26 October 1966, let the government take private land for public use and pay compensation set by the law. The government argued that the land was needed for homes and factories, and that a rise in land value created by public spending should benefit everyone, not a few landowners. Many owners felt the compensation was too low to buy a similar property. The state's share of the land grew from about <Num s="S54">44</Num> per cent in 1960 to over <Num s="S54">85</Num> per cent by 2007.

In the west, planning for the Jurong Industrial Estate began in 1961 on swamps and low hills, and the Jurong Town Corporation was set up on 1 June 1968 to run it, with flats nearby for the workers. Why factory jobs mattered so much when British forces left in 1971 is told in <TopicLink slug="singapore-after-1965" ch="british-withdrawal">Singapore after 1965: the British leave</TopicLink>.

<Term id="resettlement">Resettlement</Term> brought gains and losses. Families who moved got piped water, electricity and proper sanitation in buildings safer from fire, and many later bought their flats. They also left behind neighbours, farms, animals and ways of earning a living, and some found high-rise life hard at first. By 1990 about <Num s="S65">87</Num> in every 100 residents lived in HDB flats. The dots are blocks still standing today; the indigo shapes are today's industrial land.

<More title={{ en: "Moving day", zh: "搬家的那一天" }}>

In an exhibition by the Founders' Memorial, Au Seck Ngee remembers his family's move from their kampong into their first HDB flat, just before he began national service. As the eldest son he signed the purchase contract with his mother. Leaving the familiar kampong was a big change. To save money they first applied for a three-room flat and only later bought a bigger one. The family spoke a Chinese dialect, so an HDB officer explained the English contract to them. Other families remember it differently; NHB notes that for some people the move to a high-rise flat was at first difficult. There is no single story of resettlement.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Land acquisition: two views", zh: "土地征用：两种看法" }}>

The government's view, set out by Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew, was that when public spending on roads, drains and housing raised the value of land, the gain should go to the public, not to a few landowners, and that the state needed land quickly and cheaply to house the people. Landowners and some residents saw it differently. Compensation was often based on the value of the land at an earlier date, not on the price at the time, so owners could not afford a comparable property. The rules changed over the years: from 1973 compensation was the lower of the current value and the value on a fixed earlier date, and since 2007 it has been based on market value.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Who is counted?", zh: "分母是谁？" }}>

HDB's figures for the share of people living in its flats are counted against different totals. For 1960 and 1965 they use the whole population: <Num s="S65">9</Num> and <Num s="S65">23</Num> per cent. From 1970 they use residents only (citizens and permanent residents): <Num s="S65">35</Num> per cent in 1970, <Num s="S65">67</Num> in 1980 and <Num s="S65">87</Num> in 1990. At the end of March 2025 the figure was <Num s="S66">76.0</Num> per cent of residents. The numbers show a trend, but they are not exactly comparable.

</More>

<Term id="new-town">新镇</Term>是整体规划的：组屋围着镇中心，有商店、巴刹、学校、诊所和巴士。新加坡改良信托局在 1950 年代初规划了女皇镇。1964 年底动工的大巴窑，是第一个完全由建屋局规划和兴建的新镇。宏茂桥 1973 年、淡滨尼 1979 年相继动工。

建新镇需要土地，而这些土地原本有人拥有、耕种或居住。1966 年 10 月 26 日通过的《土地征用法令》，让政府可以为公共用途征用私人土地，按法令规定付补偿。政府的理由是：建住屋和工厂需要土地；公共开支让地价上涨，好处应该归全体人民，而不是少数地主。许多地主则认为补偿太低，买不到差不多的房地产。国家拥有的土地，从 1960 年约 <Num s="S54">44</Num>%，增加到 2007 年的 <Num s="S54">85</Num>% 以上。

西部的裕廊工业区从 1961 年开始规划，建在沼泽和小山上；1968 年 6 月 1 日，政府成立裕廊镇管理局来管理，附近也建了组屋给工人住。1971 年英军撤离时，工厂的工作为什么那么重要，见<TopicLink slug="singapore-after-1965" ch="british-withdrawal">《1965 年后的新加坡》的“英军撤离：找工作”</TopicLink>。

<Term id="resettlement">搬迁</Term>有得也有失。搬进组屋的家庭有了自来水、电和卫生设施，楼房也不容易着火，许多人后来还买下了自己的单位。他们也离开了邻里、田地、牲口和原来的谋生方式，有些人一开始不习惯住高楼。到 1990 年，每 100 名居民中约有 <Num s="S65">87</Num> 人住在组屋。地图上的点是今天还在的楼；靛蓝色是今天的工业用地。

<More title={{ en: "Moving day", zh: "搬家的那一天" }}>

在建国一代纪念馆的展览里，欧摄毅回忆，在他入伍服役之前，全家从甘榜搬进了第一间组屋。身为长子，他和母亲联名签了购屋合约。离开熟悉的甘榜，是人生中的一件大事。为了省钱，他们先申请三房式组屋，后来才买了较大的单位。一家人平时讲方言，合约上的英文条款要请建屋局的职员解释。别的家庭有不同的回忆；国家文物局也写到，对有些人来说，搬进高楼起初并不容易。搬迁的故事不止一种。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Land acquisition: two views", zh: "土地征用：两种看法" }}>

政府的看法，由李光耀总理提出：政府花钱修路、建沟渠、盖组屋，让地价上涨，这份好处应该归公众，而不是归少数地主；国家需要又快又便宜地取得土地，让人民有屋可住。地主和一些居民有不同的感受。补偿常按较早某一天的地价计算，而不是按当时的市价，地主拿到的钱买不到相当的房地产。规定后来几经修改：1973 年起，补偿取当时价值和某一固定日期价值两者中较低的一个；2007 年起，改以市价为准。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Who is counted?", zh: "分母是谁？" }}>

建屋局统计住组屋的人口比例，不同年份用的分母不同。1960 和 1965 年用的是总人口：<Num s="S65">9</Num>% 和 <Num s="S65">23</Num>%。1970 年起只算居民（公民和永久居民）：1970 年 <Num s="S65">35</Num>%，1980 年 <Num s="S65">67</Num>%，1990 年 <Num s="S65">87</Num>%。2025 年 3 月底，这个比例是居民的 <Num s="S66">76.0</Num>%。这些数字看得出趋势，但不能完全直接比较。

</More>
