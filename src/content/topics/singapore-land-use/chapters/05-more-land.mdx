<Lang en>

A small island can use its land in new ways, and it can also make more of it. <Term id="land-reclamation">Land reclamation</Term> turns shallow sea into land: a wall of rock and sand closes off an area, and sand is poured in until it rises above the water. Reclamation along the East Coast began in 1966 and went on in stages for about thirty years. Changi, Marina Bay, Tuas and the islands off Jurong were all enlarged the same way.

Off Jurong, work began in 1993 to join <Num s="S208">seven</Num> small islands into one, Jurong Island, for oil refineries and chemical plants. It was officially opened on 14 October 2000, and that stage of reclamation was finished in 2003. The coasts on this map come from satellite images taken on particular days, so each shows the shore on that day, not the day each project was finished.

Land can also be found below ground and higher up. Under Jurong Island, the Jurong Rock Caverns opened on 2 September 2014 to store oil in rock far below the seabed, freeing about <Num s="S210">60</Num> hectares of land at the surface. Deep tunnels carry used water to treatment plants. Homes went upwards: the seven towers of Pinnacle@Duxton, finished in 2009, have <Num s="S51">50</Num> storeys.

Singapore's official land area grew from <Num s="S200">581.5</Num> square kilometres at the end of 1960 to <Num s="S200">735.7</Num> at the end of 2024, about a quarter more. The way it is measured changed in 2002, so the figures are not exactly comparable. New land has costs too: reclamation changes coasts and sea life, and the sand has to come from somewhere.

First a seawall of rock is built around the area. Sand is pumped or dumped inside until it rises above the sea. The new ground is soft and must be left to settle, and often pressed or drained, before large buildings can stand on it. For the East Coast the fill came from Singapore's own hills, at Siglap, Bedok and Tampines; at Changi it was dredged from the nearby seabed; for later projects, such as Pulau Tekong, sand was also imported from neighbouring countries.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Where the sand came from: two views", zh: "沙从哪里来：两种看法" }}>

Reclamation added about a quarter to Singapore's land, for homes, an airport, industry and a port. The National Library Board also lists its costs: imported sand grew more expensive, and filling the sea harms the living things of the shore. Where the sand comes from is argued about. In 2010 the campaign group Global Witness said that sand dredged in Cambodia for Singapore was harming the environment there, including inside a wildlife sanctuary. Singapore's Ministry of National Development replied that sand is imported by private companies and that it checks where imports come from, including licence papers from Cambodia. In 2007 Singapore's foreign minister told Parliament that Indonesia had stopped exporting sea sand in 2003. These questions are still debated.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How these coastlines were drawn, and how high the blocks go", zh: "海岸线怎么画的，组屋有多高" }}>

Each coast from 1973 on was traced from a Landsat satellite image by a fixed rule: water reflects green and infrared light differently from land, and the shore is drawn where one changes into the other. Clouds, tides and the size of a pixel (60 metres in the 1970s images, 30 metres later) limit the result. Along shores that have not changed, half of the traced points lie within about 20 to 50 metres of the true coast, but some lie a few hundred metres out.

小岛可以换个方式用土地，也可以多造一些土地。<Term id="land-reclamation">填海</Term>就是把浅海变成陆地：先用石块和沙筑一道堤把一片海围起来，再往里面填沙，直到高出水面。东海岸的填海从 1966 年开始，分几期进行了约 30 年。樟宜、滨海湾、大士和裕廊外海的岛屿，也都是这样扩大的。

1993 年起，裕廊外海的 <Num s="S208">7</Num> 个小岛被逐步连成一个岛，就是裕廊岛，岛上建了炼油厂和化工厂。裕廊岛在 2000 年 10 月 14 日正式启用，这一期填海到 2003 年完成。这张地图上的海岸取自某几天拍的卫星影像，所以画的是那一天的海岸，不是每项工程完工的日子。

土地也可以往地下找、往高处找。2014 年 9 月 2 日，裕廊岛下的裕廊岩洞启用，在海床下深处的岩石里储油，腾出地面上约 <Num s="S210">60</Num> 公顷的土地。深层隧道把用过的水送到处理厂。住家也往上发展：2009 年落成的达士岭组屋，七座楼都有 <Num s="S51">50</Num> 层。

新加坡的官方陆地面积，从 1960 年底的 <Num s="S200">581.5</Num> 平方公里，增加到 2024 年底的 <Num s="S200">735.7</Num> 平方公里，多了约四分之一。2002 年起计算方法改了，前后的数字不能完全对比。多出来的土地也有代价：填海改变了海岸和海里的生物，填海的沙也得从别的地方来。

先用石块在要填的海域四周筑起海堤，再把沙泵进去或倒进去，直到高出海面。新填的地很软，要放着让它慢慢沉实，常常还要压实或排水，才能盖大型建筑。东海岸填海用的土，取自新加坡本地的实乞纳、勿洛和淡滨尼一带的山丘；樟宜用的是附近海床挖上来的沙；后来的工程，比如德光岛，也从邻国进口沙。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Where the sand came from: two views", zh: "沙从哪里来：两种看法" }}>

填海让新加坡的陆地多了约四分之一，用来建住屋、机场、工业区和港口。国家图书馆管理局也列出了代价：进口的沙越来越贵，填海也伤害海岸的生物。沙从哪里来，各方说法不一。2010 年，倡议组织“全球见证”指出，在柬埔寨为新加坡挖的沙破坏了当地的环境，连野生动物保护区里也有人挖沙。新加坡国家发展部回应说，沙由私人公司进口，政府会查核进口的来源，包括柬埔寨方面的执照文件。2007 年，新加坡外交部长在国会说，印尼自 2003 年起已停止出口海沙。这些问题至今仍有争议。

</More>
