<Lang en>

Every living thing needs water: people and animals drink it, plants need it to make food, and without it nothing grows. Singapore uses about <Num s="S11">440 million gallons</Num> of water a day, or about <Num s="S11">2 million cubic metres</Num>: enough to fill about <Num s="S9">800</Num> Olympic swimming pools. Yet the island is small and has no large rivers or lakes.

So Singapore gets its water from four sources, which PUB, the national water agency, calls the <Term id="four-national-taps">Four National Taps</Term>. The model on the stage is a slice of the island that holds all four. <FlyTo preset="hero">See the whole slice</FlyTo>

The first tap is local catchment. Rain that falls on forest and on the city runs into <Term id="reservoir">reservoirs</Term>; about two-thirds of Singapore's land is <Term id="catchment">catchment</Term> today, and there are <Num s="S3">17</Num> reservoirs. The second is imported water from the Johor River in Malaysia, which comes across the Causeway in large pipes. The third is <Term id="newater">NEWater</Term>, made by cleaning <Term id="used-water">used water</Term> many times over. The fourth is <Term id="desalination">desalination</Term>: taking the salt out of seawater.

Each tap depends on different things. Catchment depends on rain, imported water on an agreement with Johor, and NEWater and desalinated water on treatment plants and electricity. Having all four means a dry spell or a problem with one source does not leave the taps dry. The next chapter starts where all fresh water starts: with the Sun and the sea. The companion map topic <a href="/en/topics/four-taps/">Singapore's Four Taps</a> shows where the reservoirs and plants are on the island.

<More title={{ en: "What the model leaves out", zh: "模型省略了什么" }}>

The model is not to scale. Clouds a kilometre or more above the ground are drawn just above the hills; the hills are far higher than real ones, Singapore's highest point being only <Num s="S31">163 m</Num>; buildings and plants are enlarged against the land, and the deep tunnel lies much closer to the surface than its real <Num s="S15">35–55 m</Num>. Real reservoirs, waterworks and plants are spread across the whole island, not side by side. No plant is drawn with its name or logo.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How much is 440 million gallons", zh: "4.4 亿加仑有多少" }}>

PUB counts water in millions of imperial gallons a day. One million gallons is about <Num s="S11">4,546 cubic metres</Num>, so <Num s="S11">440 million gallons</Num> a day is about <Num s="S11">2 million cubic metres</Num> a day. A cubic metre is a thousand litres. PUB's exhibition compares the daily total with about <Num s="S9">800</Num> Olympic swimming pools.

</More>

<Lang zh>

一切生物都需要水：人和动物要喝水，植物要靠水来制造养料，没有水什么都长不出来。新加坡每天用水约 <Num s="S11">4.4 亿加仑</Num>，也就是约 <Num s="S11">200 万立方米</Num>，足以装满约 <Num s="S9">800</Num> 个奥运标准游泳池。可是，这座岛很小，也没有大河大湖。

所以新加坡从四个水源取水，国家水务局 PUB 把它们叫做<Term id="four-national-taps">四大水喉</Term>。舞台上的模型是这座岛的一块切片，四个水喉都在里面。<FlyTo preset="hero">看看整块切片</FlyTo>

第一个水喉是本地集水。落在森林和城市里的雨流进<Term id="reservoir">蓄水池</Term>；今天新加坡约三分之二的土地是<Term id="catchment">集水区</Term>，全国有 <Num s="S3">17</Num> 个蓄水池。第二个是进口水，来自马来西亚的柔佛河，用大水管经长堤送过来。第三个是<Term id="newater">新生水</Term>，把<Term id="used-water">用过的水</Term>一遍又一遍地净化制成。第四个是<Term id="desalination">海水淡化</Term>，把海水里的盐去掉。

每个水喉依靠的东西不一样。本地集水靠下雨，进口水靠与柔佛的协议，新生水和淡化海水靠处理厂和电力。四个水喉都有，碰上干旱或某一个水源出问题，水龙头也不会断水。下一章从所有淡水的起点讲起：太阳和大海。配套的地图主题<a href="/zh/topics/four-taps/">《新加坡的四大水喉》</a>画出了蓄水池和各个水厂在岛上的位置。

<More title={{ en: "What the model leaves out", zh: "模型省略了什么" }}>

模型不按比例。离地一公里以上的云，画在山顶上方不远处；山画得比真实的高得多，新加坡的最高点其实只有 <Num s="S31">163 米</Num>；楼房和水厂相对地面放大了；深隧道也画得比真实的 <Num s="S15">35–55 米</Num>浅得多。真实的蓄水池、水厂分布在全岛各处，并不挨在一起。模型不画任何厂名和标志。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How much is 440 million gallons", zh: "4.4 亿加仑有多少" }}>

PUB 用"百万英制加仑每天"来计算水量。一百万加仑约 <Num s="S11">4,546 立方米</Num>，所以每天 <Num s="S11">4.4 亿加仑</Num>约等于每天 <Num s="S11">200 万立方米</Num>。一立方米是一千升。PUB 的展览把一天的用水量比作约 <Num s="S9">800</Num> 个奥运标准游泳池。

</More>
