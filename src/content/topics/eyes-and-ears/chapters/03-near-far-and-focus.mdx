<Lang en>

Start with the far object and far-focus settings. The light paths meet on the model receiver. Its position stays fixed throughout this comparison. Bring the object nearer without changing focus. The geometrical meeting point now lies beyond the receiver, so the light reaches the receiver as a spread-out spot rather than a sharply focused point.

Use the card to predict where the focus will lie after changing to the near-focus setting. Keep the object near and leave the opening unchanged. Reveal the result: the paths meet on the receiver again. Record both states, including the description of focus position. The receiver has not moved to collect the image; the effective focusing power has changed.

In an eye, the lens changes shape during accommodation. For near viewing it becomes rounder; for distant viewing it is flatter. The ring of ciliary muscle around the lens changes the tension on the fibres holding it. The stage shows the lens-shape difference clearly, while the separate optical bench combines cornea and lens into its effective lens. The bench's distances are teaching choices, not measurements of a child.

Changing focus for a nearby object is different from <Term id="myopia">myopia</Term>, or short-sightedness. In myopia, light from distant objects focuses in front of the retina and distant things can look blurred. An eye-care professional can prescribe lenses to help a person see clearly. This simulation cannot tell whether someone needs glasses or what prescription would suit them. <FlyTo preset="focus-bench">Compare near and far focus</FlyTo>
