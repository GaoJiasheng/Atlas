<Lang en>

The cochlea is shown with its enclosing surface opened so that the receiving process can be discussed. Movement transferred at the oval window disturbs the fluid inside. A travelling wave develops along the basilar membrane, a flexible partition supporting the sensory structures. Air does not travel through the fluid and then pour into the auditory nerve.

The nearby strip is an enlarged, unrolled study of that membrane. It is separated from the curled organ to make local motion visible. Follow the strip towards the enlarged cell patch. This arrangement is a teaching layout: it is not another organ beside the ear, and its distance from the cochlea is not an anatomical measurement.

A <Term id="hair-cell">hair cell</Term> is a sensory cell with microscopic hair-like projections above it. The projections form a bundle; they are not ordinary hairs growing out of the ear. When the structures move, the bundles bend. Their bending helps start electrical signalling. The patch shows only a few enlarged examples, without claiming a total number of cells or a magnification value.

The final beats introduce the signal marker and then the auditory nerve. Follow the marker from the sensory region towards the nerve, keeping it distinct from the mechanical motion shown earlier. What travels along the nerve is an electrical signal. The brain processes incoming activity so that sounds can be recognised and understood. The model stops at this pathway and does not draw all the connections inside the brain. <FlyTo preset="cochlea">Examine the sensory region</FlyTo>

Higher pitches produce their strongest response nearer the cochlear base; lower pitches peak nearer the apex. These are qualitative locations. This stage does not map an input frequency to a measured distance along a real cochlea. [S7, S8]

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Balance beside hearing", "zh": "听觉旁边的平衡结构"}}>

The vestibular region and semicircular canals have roles in balance. They are shown as neighbouring context, not as extra stations along the sound-to-signal route. The cochlea is the hearing structure followed here. [S8]

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Marks show signals", "zh": "用标记表示信号"}}>

The moving markers show signalling, not air, liquid or light particles. The enlarged strip shows local up-and-down movement, not a computed spatial travelling wave. The cell bodies stay on the strip while the enlarged bundle strokes tilt together as a schematic cue; individual stereocilia are not independently simulated. These studies use a separate scale. 耳蜗的包围表面打开，可以讨论里面的接收过程。卵圆窗传来的运动使内部液体产生扰动，支撑感受结构的柔性分隔——基底膜——上出现行波。这里不是空气穿过液体，再灌进听神经；从外界声音到神经信号之间，经历了不同形式的变化。

旁边的长条是基底膜的放大、展开示意。把它与卷曲的耳蜗分开，局部运动才容易看清。沿长条找到放大的细胞区：这是一种教学摆放，不是耳朵旁边又长出一个器官，工位之间的距离也不表示真实解剖距离。

<Term id="hair-cell">毛细胞</Term>是感受细胞，顶部有成束的微小毛发状突起，不是普通耳毛。有关结构运动时，这些束会弯曲，帮助启动电信号传递。放大窗只画少量例子，既不表示毛细胞总数，也没有给出精确放大倍数。把细胞、顶端小束和下方传信号的位置分开看，便能顺着过程读图。
