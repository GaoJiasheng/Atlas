<Lang en>

The eye and ear occupy separate study stations on the stage. They are enlarged and turned so that their inner parts can be followed. They are not shown at their real relative sizes or positions in a head. Start with the whole view, then select a named part to read what it does.

Imagine watching someone clap. Light reflected from the moving hands enters your eyes. Inside each eye, the <Term id="retina">retina</Term> is a thin layer containing light-sensitive cells. These cells begin the conversion of light into electrical signals. The <Term id="optic-nerve">optic nerve</Term> carries signals towards the brain. Nothing has to shine out of your eyes towards the hands.

The clap also creates a disturbance in the surrounding air. At the other station, follow the ear canal to the <Term id="eardrum">eardrum</Term>, a thin membrane that vibrates. Movement passes through the middle ear towards the <Term id="cochlea">cochlea</Term>, the curled, fluid-filled structure of the inner ear. Sensory cells there help turn movement into electrical signalling. The <Term id="auditory-nerve">auditory nerve</Term> carries signals towards the brain.

Both routes involve receiving something from the surroundings and sending nerve signals. Their inputs and receiving structures differ. The eye receives light; the ear responds to vibration. Follow those changes separately before comparing them. 舞台把眼睛和耳朵分开摆在不同工位，放大并转向观者，方便看清内部。它们在头部里的真实位置和大小比例并不是这样。先看全貌，再点选名称，便能读到各部分的作用。透明和剖开的地方是观察安排，不表示身体里面原本就有这些开口。

想象你正在看别人拍手。手反射的光进入眼睛，来到内壁上含有感光细胞的薄层——<Term id="retina">视网膜</Term>。这些细胞开始把光转成电信号，<Term id="optic-nerve">视神经</Term>再把信号传向大脑。看见手，并不需要眼睛向手发出光；先有光入眼，才有这条路线后面的过程。

拍手也使周围空气产生扰动。在另一工位，沿外耳道找到薄薄的<Term id="eardrum">鼓膜</Term>。它会随传来的声音振动，运动再经过中耳，传向内耳里卷曲、充满液体的<Term id="cochlea">耳蜗</Term>。耳蜗里的感受细胞帮助把机械运动转成电信号，<Term id="auditory-nerve">听神经</Term>把信号传向大脑。

两条路线都有接收外界输入、传递神经信号的阶段，但输入和接收结构不同：眼接收光，耳响应振动。接下来的章节分别跟随这些变化，再回头比较。全课静音也能学完；画面中的运动用于说明过程，并不是检查你的视力或听力。<FlyTo preset="overview">比较两个工位</FlyTo>
