<Lang en>

The eye station is open along a cut surface so that the entrance can be seen. At the front is the <Term id="cornea">cornea</Term>, a clear curved layer. Incoming light passes through it, and its curvature helps bend the light. The coloured ring just behind it is the <Term id="iris">iris</Term>. The <Term id="pupil">pupil</Term> is the opening in that ring, not a black solid object filling the centre.

The iris controls how much light enters through the pupil. In bright light it makes the pupil smaller; in dim light the pupil becomes larger, letting more light in [S2, S24]. Behind the opening sits the clear <Term id="lens">lens</Term>. Light continues through the lens towards the retina. A larger opening can admit more light when the illumination and other conditions stay the same. That does not mean the lens has moved or changed its focusing setting.

Use the card to compare the model's openings. Keep incoming light, object position and focus unchanged. First predict whether the relative admitted-light reading will rise, fall or stay the same. Reveal the change, then record the reading for each setting. Look at the opening itself as well as the number: the outer edge of the iris stays in place.

At the last beat, the separate optical study makes the comparison visible. In this ideal model, changing the aperture changes the admitted-light proxy without moving the geometrical image position. Real eyes have automatic responses and more complicated optics. This hand-operated model separates opening size from focus so that their different effects can be compared. <FlyTo preset="eye">Look through the opening</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Opening area in this model", "zh": "模型里的开口面积"}}>

The diameter changes from <Num s="S31">2 mm</Num> to <Num s="S31">4 mm</Num>. A circular area depends on the square of its diameter, so the area and relative admitted-light proxy become <Num s="S31">4 times</Num> the starting values. This assumes uniform illumination and unchanged transmission. It does not predict perceived brightness.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Geometry is not brightness", "zh": "几何位置不等于明暗"}}>

The separate study uses a single effective lens representing the combined focusing action of cornea and lens. Its image distance depends on object distance and effective focal length. Aperture size is not in that ideal image-distance equation. It can affect the size of an out-of-focus spot. [S11, S31]

</More>

虹膜控制通过瞳孔进入眼内的光量：光线强时瞳孔变小，光线暗时瞳孔变大，让更多光进入 [S2, S24]。开口后方有透明的<Term id="lens">晶状体</Term>，光经过这里继续朝视网膜前进。照明和其他条件相同时，较大的开口能够放进更多光；这不表示晶状体同时移动了，也不表示聚焦设置已经改变。
