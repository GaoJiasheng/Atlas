<Lang en>

Look through the cut surface at the front of the eye. The <Term id="cornea">cornea</Term> is a clear curved layer. Light passes through it and bends. Behind it sits the coloured ring called the <Term id="iris">iris</Term>. The <Term id="pupil">pupil</Term> is the opening in that ring.

The iris controls how much light enters. In bright light, the pupil becomes smaller. In dim light, it becomes larger and lets more light in. [S2, S24] Behind the opening is the clear <Term id="lens">lens</Term>. Light passes through it towards the retina. A larger opening admits more light when other conditions stay the same; focus can stay unchanged.

Compare the openings on the card. Keep the incoming light, object position and focus the same. Predict whether the relative light reading will rise, fall or stay the same. Then reveal and record each reading. Watch the opening too: the iris’s inner edge changes while its outer edge stays fixed.

In the last beat, compare the light paths on the optical bench. A wider opening admits more light, but the image stays in the same place. You control opening size and focus separately here to see their different effects. Real eyes adjust automatically and have more complex optics. <FlyTo preset="eye">Look through the opening</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Opening area in this model", "zh": "模型里的开口面积"}}>

The opening’s diameter changes from <Num s="S31">2 mm</Num> to <Num s="S31">4 mm</Num>. Doubling a circle’s diameter makes its area <Num s="S31">4 times</Num> as large. The relative light reading rises by the same factor, with even illumination and unchanged light transmission. This reading measures the model’s admitted light, rather than how bright something feels.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Light and image position", "zh": "进光与成像位置"}}>

One lens on the optical bench stands for the combined focusing action of cornea and lens. Image distance is the distance from lens to image. It depends on the distance from lens to object and on focal length, which describes how strongly the lens bends light. In this ideal calculation, opening size does not change image distance. It can change the width of a blurred spot. [S11, S31]

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Use the screen model", "zh": "只操作屏幕模型"}}>

Use the screen controls to compare openings. Do not shine a torch into an eye or look at the Sun to test a pupil. The switch controls the model; it does not measure a person’s pupil response. [S19, S31]

</More>
