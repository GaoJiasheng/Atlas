<Lang en>

Follow the folds of the outer ear towards the canal. The canal ends at the sloping eardrum. Incoming sound makes this thin membrane vibrate. The vibration passes to the middle-ear bones without air going through a hole in the drum.

Behind the drum are <Num s="S7">three</Num> connected bones called the <Term id="ossicles">ossicles</Term>. The <Term id="malleus">malleus</Term> has a handle joined to the drum. Its head meets the <Term id="incus">incus</Term>, which connects to the <Term id="stapes">stapes</Term>. Select each bone in order and follow their contact points.

The stapes is shaped like a stirrup. Its flat base, called the footplate, moves at the oval window. This passes the bone chain’s movement into the fluid-filled inner ear. The chain transfers mechanical energy; it does not create energy or send air into a nerve.

Watch the bones rock and move back and forth. The movements are enlarged and slowed so you can follow the contacts, rather than measure real ear movements or delays. In the last beat, follow the motion towards the cochlea.
