<Lang en>

This bench is set up for testing magnets. Two bar magnets lie on a sheet of paper in the middle. To their left are a horseshoe magnet and a pair of ring magnets on a wooden post. In front are a compass and a tray of everyday objects, and on the right is the equipment for making a magnet. Each <Term id="magnet">magnet</Term> has two painted ends. The bar magnets are <Num s="S1">10 cm</Num> long.

Slide the second bar magnet towards the first, with a south pole facing a north pole. While there is still a gap, it is pulled towards the first magnet. To <Term id="attract">attract</Term> means to pull towards. The smaller the gap, the stronger the pull. <FlyTo preset="two-magnets">Look between the bar magnets</FlyTo>

Turn the second magnet round so that two north poles face each other, and it is pushed away instead. To <Term id="repel">repel</Term> means to push away. A push or a pull is a force, and a magnet can push or pull another magnet without touching it. Use the Try it card to change which ends face each other while the gap stays the same. Watch the arrows and the reading: a plus sign means a pull, a minus sign a push.

A magnet does not attract everything. Held over the test tray, it lifts the iron nail and the steel paper clips, but the wooden block and the plastic ruler stay where they are. Magnets can be made of iron or steel, and they come in many shapes: bars, horseshoes, rings and the thin, bendy sheets on a fridge door. The next chapter looks at their ends, where the pull is strongest.

<More title={{"en": "A force is a push or a pull", "zh": "力是推或拉"}}>

A push or a pull is called a force. Most pushes and pulls need contact, like a hand pushing a door. A magnet's push or pull reaches across a gap of air. On this bench the second bar magnet slides along a straight line, so its pull or push only moves it closer or further away.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About this model", "zh": "关于这个模型"}}>

The bench is a design study of school science equipment, not a particular product, and the sizes are chosen for it. The two colours on each magnet only mark which end is which; paint does not make a magnet work. In this model, north is towards the back of the bench. Readings marked SIM, such as the pull in percent, come from a simple model of two magnets. Use them to compare settings, not as measurements.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

这张实验台是专门用来做磁铁实验的。中间的纸上放着两根条形磁铁；左边是一块马蹄形磁铁和套在木柱上的一对环形磁铁；前面有一个指南针和一盘日常物品；右边是制作磁铁的器材。每块<Term id="magnet">磁铁</Term>的两端都涂了不同的颜色。条形磁铁长 <Num s="S1">10 cm</Num>。

让第二根条形磁铁的南极对着第一根的北极，慢慢推近。两根磁铁之间还隔着空隙，第二根就被拉了过去。拉向自己叫<Term id="attract">吸引</Term>。间隙越小，拉力越大。<FlyTo preset="two-magnets">看两根条形磁铁之间</FlyTo>

把第二根磁铁调个头，让两个北极相对，它反而被推开。把东西推开叫<Term id="repel">排斥</Term>。推和拉都是力；磁铁不用碰到另一块磁铁，就能推它或拉它。在「试一试」里改变相对的两端，保持间隙不变，看箭头和读数怎样变：正号表示拉，负号表示推。

磁铁并不是什么都吸。把它拿到测试盘上方，铁钉和钢回形针被吸了起来，木块和塑料尺却留在原处。磁铁可以用铁或钢制成，形状也有很多种：条形、马蹄形、环形，还有贴在冰箱门上的薄薄的软磁片。下一章看磁铁的两端，那里的吸力最强。

<More title={{"en": "A force is a push or a pull", "zh": "力是推或拉"}}>

推或拉叫作力。大多数推和拉要靠接触，比如用手推门。磁铁的推和拉却能隔着一段空气起作用。在这张实验台上，第二根条形磁铁只能沿一条直线滑动，所以拉力或推力只会让它靠近或离开。

</More>
