<Lang en>

Lay the two bar magnets on the paper with a north pole facing a south pole. A north pole and a south pole are <Term id="unlike-poles">unlike poles</Term>, and they attract. Turn one magnet round so that north faces north, and the magnets repel. Two north poles, or two south poles, are <Term id="like-poles">like poles</Term>. So like poles repel and unlike poles attract. It does not matter which magnet you move; what matters is which poles face each other.

Keep unlike poles facing and change only the gap. In this model, a gap of <Num s="S1">6 cm</Num> gives a pull of <Num s="S1">12 %</Num> of the strongest setting, <Num s="S1">3 cm</Num> gives <Num s="S1">41 %</Num> and <Num s="S1">1.5 cm</Num> gives <Num s="S1">100 %</Num>. Halving the gap from 6 to 3 cm makes the pull more than three times as strong. A push between like poles grows in the same way as they come closer. <FlyTo preset="two-magnets">Compare the gaps</FlyTo>

In the Try it card, record a row for each gap. Only the gap changes; the magnets and the poles facing each other stay the same. That makes it a <Term id="fair-test">fair test</Term> of how the gap affects the pull. Before you look, predict what will happen if magnet B is turned round.

The iron filings show the difference too. Between unlike poles they join up in a bridge. Between like poles they turn away from each other and leave an empty patch in the middle. On the wooden post, two ring magnets with like poles facing push apart so strongly that the top ring floats in the air; the post only stops it sliding off sideways. Turn the top ring over and it drops onto the bottom one.

<More title={{"en": "How the arrows are drawn", "zh": "箭头是怎样画的"}}>

The arrows are drawn shorter than the force would make them, so that a weak pull can still be seen: the arrow for <Num s="S1">100 %</Num> is only about three times as long as the one for <Num s="S1">12 %</Num>. Compare the numbers, not the arrows. The numbers are percentages of the pull at <Num s="S1">1.5 cm</Num> in this model, not measurements.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Finding an unknown pole", "zh": "怎样认出一个未知的磁极"}}>

Hold the north pole of a magnet near one end of a bar. If that end is pushed away, it must be a north pole. If it is pulled, it could be a south pole, but it could also be a plain piece of iron or steel that is not a magnet at all, because a magnet attracts both. Only a push proves that the end is a pole.

</More>

<Lang zh>

把两根条形磁铁放在纸上，让北极对着南极。北极和南极叫<Term id="unlike-poles">异极</Term>，它们互相吸引。把一根磁铁调头，让北极对北极，两根磁铁就互相排斥。两个北极，或两个南极，叫<Term id="like-poles">同极</Term>。所以同极相斥，异极相吸。移动哪一根磁铁都一样，要看的是哪两个磁极相对。

保持异极相对，只改变间隙。在这个模型里，间隙 <Num s="S1">6 cm</Num> 时，拉力是最强设置的 <Num s="S1">12 %</Num>；<Num s="S1">3 cm</Num> 时是 <Num s="S1">41 %</Num>；<Num s="S1">1.5 cm</Num> 时是 <Num s="S1">100 %</Num>。间隙从 6 cm 缩到 3 cm，拉力变成原来的三倍多。同极之间的推力，也是靠得越近越大。<FlyTo preset="two-magnets">比较不同的间隙</FlyTo>

在「试一试」里，每种间隙记一行。只改变间隙，两根磁铁和相对的磁极都不变，这样的实验叫<Term id="fair-test">公平测试</Term>，能看出间隙怎样影响拉力。揭晓之前，先猜一猜：把磁铁 B 调个头，会发生什么？

铁屑也显出不同。异极之间，铁屑连成一座桥；同极之间，铁屑各自拐开，中间空出一块。木柱上的两只环形磁铁同极相对，互相推得很用力，上面那只就悬在半空；木柱只是不让它从旁边滑下来。把上面的环翻过来，它就落到下面那只上。

<More title={{"en": "Finding an unknown pole", "zh": "怎样认出一个未知的磁极"}}>

拿一块磁铁的北极，靠近另一根条子的一端。如果那一端被推开，它一定是北极。如果被吸住，它可能是南极，也可能根本不是磁铁，只是一块普通的铁或钢，因为磁铁对这两种都会吸引。只有“推开”才能证明那一端是磁极。
