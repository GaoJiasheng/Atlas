<Lang en>

The steel needle on the bench is made of a magnetic material, but it is not a magnet: it picks up no paper clips. The <Term id="stroke-method">stroke method</Term> turns it into one. Touch one pole of a bar magnet to one end of the needle and slide it along to the other end. Then lift the magnet clear, bring it back to the start and stroke again, always in the same direction and with the same pole.

In this model the needle holds <Num s="S1">2 clips</Num> after <Num s="S1">10 strokes</Num>, <Num s="S1">4</Num> after <Num s="S1">20</Num> and <Num s="S1">6</Num> after <Num s="S1">30</Num>. Real needles and magnets give different numbers, so try it and count.

The <Term id="electrical-method">electrical method</Term> uses electricity. Insulated wire is wound round a steel nail to make a coil, and the coil is joined to two batteries through a switch. Close the switch and current flows round the coil. The nail becomes a magnet and lifts paper clips. A magnet made by an electric current like this is called an <Term id="electromagnet">electromagnet</Term>. Current only flows when the circuit is closed, as <TopicLink slug="circuits">Electric circuits</TopicLink> explains. <FlyTo preset="make-bench">Look at the electromagnet</FlyTo>

In this model two batteries lift <Num s="S1">6 clips</Num> and one battery lifts <Num s="S1">3</Num>. In the Try it card, change only the number of batteries, keep the same coil, nail and clips, and record a row each time. Then open the switch. The current stops and most clips drop, but the steel nail keeps a little magnetism and still holds <Num s="S1">1 clip</Num>. A core of soft iron keeps almost none, so an electromagnet with a soft iron core lets go of everything when it is switched off.

<More title={{"en": "Why the wire is insulated", "zh": "导线为什么要绝缘"}}>

The wire has a thin coat that does not let electricity through. Without it, turns of wire touching one another would let the current take a short cut instead of going round and round the nail, and the nail would hardly become a magnet. The coil and the batteries can get warm, so use only dry cells and switch off between tests.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "More turns, more current", "zh": "圈数多，电流大"}}>

An electromagnet also gets stronger with more turns of wire round the nail, or with more current through the wire. The coil in this model has a fixed <Num s="S1">30 turns</Num>, so only the number of batteries changes.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How a magnet can be spoilt", "zh": "磁铁怎样会失去磁性"}}>

Heating a magnet strongly, or dropping and knocking it hard, can weaken it. Iron stops being magnetic at about <Num s="S4">770 °C</Num>. Store magnets carefully and do not drop them.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

台上的钢针是用磁性材料做的，但它不是磁铁：一只回形针也吸不起来。用<Term id="stroke-method">摩擦法</Term>可以把它变成磁铁：把条形磁铁的一个磁极放在钢针的一端，沿着针滑到另一端；然后把磁铁抬起来，回到起点，再摩擦一次。每次都用同一个磁极，朝同一个方向。

在这个模型里，摩擦 <Num s="S1">10 次</Num>后钢针吸住 <Num s="S1">2 只</Num>回形针，<Num s="S1">20 次</Num>后吸住 <Num s="S1">4 只</Num>，<Num s="S1">30 次</Num>后吸住 <Num s="S1">6 只</Num>。真的钢针和磁铁会得出不同的数目，可以自己试一试、数一数。

<Term id="electrical-method">电流法</Term>要用电。把绝缘导线绕在钢钉上，做成线圈，再经过开关接到两节电池上。合上开关，电流就绕着线圈流动，钉子变成磁铁，能吸起回形针。像这样靠电流产生磁性的磁铁，叫<Term id="electromagnet">电磁铁</Term>。电路要闭合，电流才会流动，<TopicLink slug="circuits">《电路》</TopicLink>里讲过这一点。<FlyTo preset="make-bench">看电磁铁</FlyTo>

在这个模型里，两节电池能吸起 <Num s="S1">6 只</Num>回形针，一节电池吸起 <Num s="S1">3 只</Num>。在「试一试」里只改电池的节数，线圈、钢钉和回形针都不换，每次记一行。然后断开开关：电流停了，大部分回形针掉下来，但钢钉还留着一点磁性，仍吸住 <Num s="S1">1 只</Num>。软铁芯几乎不留磁性，所以用软铁芯的电磁铁一断电，就把吸着的东西全部放下。

<More title={{"en": "Why the wire is insulated", "zh": "导线为什么要绝缘"}}>

导线外面包着一层不导电的薄皮。没有它，挨在一起的导线就会让电流抄近路，不再一圈一圈地绕着钉子走，钉子也就几乎变不成磁铁。线圈和电池会发热，所以只用干电池，每次测完就断开开关。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "More turns, more current", "zh": "圈数多，电流大"}}>

导线在钉子上绕的圈数越多，或者流过导线的电流越大，电磁铁也越强。这个模型的线圈固定是 <Num s="S1">30 圈</Num>，所以只改电池的节数。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How a magnet can be spoilt", "zh": "磁铁怎样会失去磁性"}}>

把磁铁加热到很高的温度，或者让它重重地摔、撞，都会让它的磁性变弱。铁在约 <Num s="S4">770 °C</Num> 时就不再有磁性。磁铁要好好存放，别摔它。

</More>

</Lang>
