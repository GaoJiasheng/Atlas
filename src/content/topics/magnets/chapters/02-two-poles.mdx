<Lang en>

Iron filings are sprinkled on the paper around a bar magnet. Most of them crowd at the two ends, and only a few lie near the middle. The pull of a magnet is strongest at its ends, which are called <Term id="pole">poles</Term>. Every magnet has two poles, a north pole and a south pole.

The filings also line up. They form curves that leave one pole, sweep round through the space beside the magnet and come back to the other pole. The thin moving lines in the model trace the same curves; they are a way of picturing the pattern, not threads in the air. The pattern shows the space around the magnet where its pull acts. <FlyTo preset="field-top">Look down at the filings</FlyTo>

A <Term id="compass">compass</Term> has a small magnetic needle that can turn. Near the bar magnet the needle lines up with the curves, so it points in a different direction at each place. Use the Try it card to move the compass round the eight spots on its ring, keeping the bar magnet where it is. Read the needle when it has stopped swinging.

Now hang the bar magnet from a thread, away from other magnets. Hanging like this, it can turn without being held or blocked: it is <Term id="freely-suspended">freely suspended</Term>. It swings, slows down and comes to rest pointing north and south. The end that points north is the north pole, and the other end is the south pole. A compass needle does the same, which is how a compass finds north. <FlyTo preset="compass-view">Look at the compass</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Earth is a giant magnet", "zh": "地球是一块巨大的磁铁"}}>

Earth behaves like a huge magnet. The space around it where its pull acts is called a <Term id="magnetic-field">magnetic field</Term>, a word you will meet properly in secondary school. Because unlike poles attract, the north pole of a compass needle is pulled towards a magnetic south pole, and that magnetic south pole lies near Earth's geographic North Pole. A compass only points roughly north: the direction changes a little from place to place and from year to year.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Cut a magnet in half", "zh": "把磁铁切成两半"}}>

Cut a bar magnet in half and you do not get a north pole on its own and a south pole on its own. Each half becomes a smaller magnet with a north pole and a south pole of its own. In this model each bar is drawn as two coloured halves only so that each pole can be picked and labelled.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About the needle reading", "zh": "关于磁针读数"}}>

The Needle reading gives the needle's direction in degrees, measured clockwise from north as seen from above: <Num s="S1">0°</Num> is north and <Num s="S1">90°</Num> is east. In this model the needle feels the bar magnet and a steady pull towards north, standing in for Earth. Close to the magnet the magnet wins, so the needle points along the curves; far away it swings back towards north.

</More>

<Lang zh>

在条形磁铁周围的纸上撒一层铁屑，大部分铁屑挤在两端，中间只有稀稀的几粒。磁铁两端的吸力最强，这两端叫<Term id="pole">磁极</Term>。每块磁铁都有两个磁极：北极和南极。

铁屑还会排队。它们排成一条条弧线，从一个磁极出发，绕过磁铁旁边，回到另一个磁极。模型里细细流动的线描的是同样的弧线；它们只是画出图样的方法，空气里并没有真的线。这个图样显出磁铁周围吸力能起作用的地方。<FlyTo preset="field-top">从上方看铁屑</FlyTo>

<Term id="compass">指南针</Term>里有一根能转动的小磁针。靠近条形磁铁时，磁针会顺着弧线转，所以在不同的地方指向不同的方向。在「试一试」里把指南针移到圆圈上的八个位置，条形磁铁保持不动。等磁针停稳了再读它的方向。

再用细线把条形磁铁挂起来，远离别的磁铁。这样挂着，它不被手握住、也不被东西挡住，能自由转动，这叫<Term id="freely-suspended">自由悬挂</Term>。它摆几下，慢下来，最后指着南北停住。指向北方的一端是北极，另一端是南极。指南针的针也是这样，所以指南针能找到北方。<FlyTo preset="compass-view">看指南针</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Earth is a giant magnet", "zh": "地球是一块巨大的磁铁"}}>

地球就像一块巨大的磁铁。它周围吸力能起作用的地方叫<Term id="magnetic-field">磁场</Term>，这个词到中学会正式学。异极相吸，所以指南针磁针的北极被一个磁南极吸引，而这个磁南极就在地球的地理北极附近。指南针只是大致指北：在不同的地方、不同的年份，方向会有一点不同。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Cut a magnet in half", "zh": "把磁铁切成两半"}}>

把条形磁铁切成两半，并不会得到单独的北极和单独的南极。每一半都会变成一块小磁铁，各有自己的北极和南极。模型把每根条形磁铁画成两段不同颜色，只是为了让两个磁极能分别点选和标注。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About the needle reading", "zh": "关于磁针读数"}}>

「磁针」读数用度数表示磁针的方向：从上往下看，从北方起按顺时针量，<Num s="S1">0°</Num> 是北，<Num s="S1">90°</Num> 是东。在这个模型里，磁针同时受到条形磁铁和一个指向北方的固定拉力（代表地球）。离磁铁近时磁铁占上风，磁针顺着弧线指；离得远了，它就转回北方。

</More>
