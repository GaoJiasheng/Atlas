At the right-hand end of the bench is a cut-off piece of a fridge. A fridge magnet holds a note against the door, which has a steel front. Inside the soft rubber seal round the door is a flat magnetic strip. When the door closes, the strip pulls the seal against the steel body of the fridge, so the door stays shut without a catch and the seal fits tightly all round. <FlyTo preset="uses-bay">Look at the fridge door</FlyTo>

A magnetic cabinet catch works the same way. A small magnet fixed in the frame attracts a steel plate screwed to the door. The plate has to be made of a magnetic material: a magnet would not hold an aluminium plate.

A compass needle is a magnet that can turn freely. Away from other magnets it comes to rest pointing north and south, which is why a compass can be used to find the way. Keep magnets away from a compass: a magnet close by makes the needle point the wrong way.

Electromagnets do jobs that need a magnet you can switch on and off. In a scrapyard, a large electromagnet on a crane lifts steel and drops it when the current is switched off. In an electric bell, an electromagnet pulls a small hammer against the bell again and again. Some fast trains, called maglev trains, are held up by electromagnets instead of wheels. Use the Try it card to switch the bench electromagnet on and off and watch the clips. <FlyTo preset="make-bench">Look at the electromagnet</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Inside an electric bell", "zh": "电铃里面"}}>

When the bell is pressed, current flows through an electromagnet, which pulls a springy iron strip carrying a small hammer. The hammer strikes the bell. But as the strip moves, it breaks the circuit at a contact, so the current stops, the electromagnet lets go and the spring pulls the strip back. That remakes the circuit, and the whole thing happens again, many times a second.

<More title={{"en": "Maglev trains", "zh": "磁浮列车"}}>

In one kind of maglev train, the train has arms that curl round under the edges of its track. Electromagnets on these arms pull upwards towards steel rails on the underside of the track. The pull holds the train up, and a control system keeps the gap between them the same. Other maglev trains use magnets that push instead of pull.

指南针的针是一根能自由转动的磁铁。远离其他磁铁时，它指着南北停住，所以指南针能用来辨认方向。用指南针时要让磁铁离远一点：旁边有磁铁，针就会指错方向。

需要“能开能关”的活儿，就用电磁铁。在废钢场，起重机上的大电磁铁通电时吊起钢铁，断电时把它放下。电铃里的电磁铁，一次又一次地把小锤拉向铃。有些高速列车叫磁浮列车，它们不靠车轮，而是靠电磁铁托起来。在「试一试」里接通、断开台上的电磁铁，看回形针怎样变化。<FlyTo preset="make-bench">看电磁铁</FlyTo>

<More title={{"en": "Inside an electric bell", "zh": "电铃里面"}}>

按下电铃，电流流过电磁铁，电磁铁吸动一片带小锤的弹性铁片，小锤敲响铃。可是铁片一动，就在一个触点处把电路断开了：电流停止，电磁铁松开，弹簧把铁片拉回原处。电路又接通了，于是一切重新来一遍，一秒钟能来回很多次。

<More title={{"en": "Maglev trains", "zh": "磁浮列车"}}>

有一种磁浮列车，车身两侧伸出“手臂”，弯过来抱在轨道边缘的下面。手臂上的电磁铁向上吸轨道底面的钢轨，这股吸力把列车托起来，控制系统让车和轨道之间的空隙保持不变。另一些磁浮列车则用相互推开的磁铁。
