<Lang en>

The tray holds eight everyday objects. Bring a magnet close to each one in turn. The iron nail, the steel paper clips and the steel can are attracted. Iron and steel are <Term id="magnetic-material">magnetic materials</Term>: a magnet attracts them, even though they are not magnets themselves. <FlyTo preset="tray-view">Look at the test tray</FlyTo>

The aluminium can tab and the copper disc are metals too, but they stay where they are, and so do the wooden block and the plastic ruler. They are made of <Term id="non-magnetic-material">non-magnetic materials</Term>. So a shiny metal object is not always attracted by a magnet. The only way to find out is to test it.

The stainless steel spoon needs care. Stainless steel comes in several kinds: a magnet attracts some of them, while others are hardly attracted at all. The spoon in this model is made of a kind that is hardly attracted. It was chosen to show that the word “steel” on its own does not tell you what a magnet will do. Test the spoon in your own kitchen to see which kind it is.

A magnet's pull passes through materials that are not magnetic, such as paper, plastic and wood. Hold cards between the magnet and a chain of paper clips, and the magnet still holds some clips. Each card makes the gap <Num s="S1">2 mm</Num> wider and the pull weaker. In this model, through <Num s="S1">3 cards</Num> it holds <Num s="S1">2 clips</Num>, and through <Num s="S1">6 cards</Num> it holds none. In the Try it card, change only the number of cards, keep the same magnet and the same clips, and record a row for each setting.

<More title={{"en": "The full card table", "zh": "完整的卡片表"}}>

In this model, <Num s="S1">0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6</Num> cards let the magnet hold <Num s="S1">10, 5, 3, 2, 1, 1 and 0</Num> clips. The numbers are only an example: a different magnet, other clips or an unsteady hand would give different counts. The pattern stays the same: the more cards, the fewer clips.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Kinds of stainless steel", "zh": "不锈钢有好几种"}}>

The spoon in this model is an austenitic stainless steel, the kind that a magnet hardly attracts. Other kinds, called ferritic and martensitic stainless steels, are attracted. Bending or hammering an austenitic steel can make it a little magnetic, so two spoons that look alike may behave differently.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Sorting metal for recycling", "zh": "回收时把金属分开"}}>

A large electromagnet hanging from a crane can lift steel scrap, such as old steel cans and bicycle frames. Aluminium is not attracted and stays behind. So a magnet can separate magnetic metals from non-magnetic ones.

测试盘里有八件日常物品。拿磁铁一件一件地靠近它们，铁钉、钢回形针和钢罐被吸住了。铁和钢是<Term id="magnetic-material">磁性材料</Term>：它们本身不是磁铁，却会被磁铁吸引。<FlyTo preset="tray-view">看测试盘</FlyTo>

铝拉环和铜片也是金属，却留在原处，木块和塑料尺也没动。它们是用<Term id="non-magnetic-material">非磁性材料</Term>做的。可见，闪亮的金属不一定会被磁铁吸引，要知道答案，只能试一试。

不锈钢勺要特别留意。不锈钢有好几种：有的会被磁铁吸引，有的几乎吸不动。这个模型里的勺子用的是几乎吸不动的那一种。选它，是为了说明单看“钢”这个字，并不能判断磁铁会怎样。拿你家厨房里的勺子试试，就知道它是哪一种。

磁铁的吸力能穿过纸、塑料、木头这些非磁性材料。在磁铁和一串回形针之间夹上卡片，磁铁仍能吸住一些回形针。每多一张卡片，间隙就宽 <Num s="S1">2 mm</Num>，吸力也就弱一些。在这个模型里，隔着 <Num s="S1">3 张</Num>卡片，磁铁吸住 <Num s="S1">2 只</Num>回形针；隔着 <Num s="S1">6 张</Num>，一只也吸不住。在「试一试」里只改卡片的张数，磁铁和回形针都不换，每种设置记一行。

在这个模型里，隔着 <Num s="S1">0、1、2、3、4、5、6</Num> 张卡片，磁铁依次吸住 <Num s="S1">10、5、3、2、1、1、0</Num> 只回形针。这些数字只是举例：换一块磁铁、换一种回形针，或者手不稳，数目都会不同。但规律不变：卡片越多，吸住的回形针越少。

<More title={{"en": "Kinds of stainless steel", "zh": "不锈钢有好几种"}}>

模型里的勺子是奥氏体不锈钢，磁铁几乎吸不动这一种。另外几种叫铁素体不锈钢和马氏体不锈钢，会被磁铁吸引。奥氏体不锈钢经过弯折、锤打，也可能带上一点磁性，所以两把看起来一样的勺子，结果可能不同。

</More>
