<Lang en>

A fruit holds and protects the seeds. Cut a ripe chilli open and the hollow fruit holds many flat, pale seeds, each <Num s="S10">3–5 mm</Num> across, on a soft central column. NParks lists more than <Num s="S12">20</Num> seeds in a chilli; one study counted between <Num s="S27">14 and 186</Num> per fruit across <Num s="S27">28</Num> kinds of chilli. <FlyTo preset="tray">Look at the fruits and seeds</FlyTo>

<Term id="seed-dispersal">Seed dispersal</Term> is the carrying of seeds away from the parent plant. A seedling that comes up far from its parent does not have to share the same patch of light, water and soil with it. The shape and make-up of a fruit help it to be carried, in one of four main ways.

Wind: the fruit of the angsana, a tree planted along many of Singapore's roads, is a flat disc <Num s="S16">3–5 cm</Num> across with a papery wing round the seed, and drifts on the breeze. Water: a coconut has a thick fibrous husk round a hard shell; it is adapted to be carried by sea currents. Splitting: some dry fruits burst open when ripe and fling out their seeds, as garden balsam does. Animals: hooked fruits catch in fur and drop off somewhere else, and juicy fruits are eaten and their seeds passed out in droppings.

The chilli is an animal-dispersed fruit. Its hot taste comes from capsaicin, which keeps many mammals away but does not put birds off. Birds eat the ripe fruit and pass the seeds out whole, and those seeds can still germinate.

<More title={{ en: "Birds and chillies", zh: "鸟和辣椒" }}>

In a study published in 2001, wild chillies were eaten by a bird, the curve-billed thrasher, while pack rats and cactus mice avoided them. Seeds that had passed through the birds still germinated. The authors suggest that capsaicin keeps away the animals that would destroy the seeds while leaving the fruit to the birds that spread them.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Structure helps dispersal", zh: "结构帮助传播" }}>

Wings, floating husks, hooks and bursting pods are structures that help fruits and seeds to be carried away. The P6 syllabus calls such features structural adaptations.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it", zh: "试一试" }}>

Collect three different fruits or seeds, such as an angsana pod, a bean pod and a piece of coconut husk. Guess how each is dispersed. Drop each one from the same height, on a breezy day and on a still one, and measure how far from your feet it lands.

</More>

果实保护着里面的种子。剖开一只成熟的辣椒，中空的果实里有很多扁平的浅色种子，每粒宽 <Num s="S10">3–5 毫米</Num>，长在柔软的中轴上。NParks 说一只辣椒里有 <Num s="S12">20</Num> 粒以上的种子；一项研究统计了 <Num s="S27">28</Num> 个辣椒品种，每个果实里有 <Num s="S27">14 到 186</Num> 粒。<FlyTo preset="tray">看看果实和种子</FlyTo>

<Term id="seed-dispersal">种子传播</Term>就是种子离开母株、被带到别处。在离母株较远的地方长出来的幼苗，不必和母株挤在同一块地方分享光、水和土。果实的形状和结构帮助它被带走，主要有四种方式。

风：青龙木种在新加坡许多路边，它的果实是直径 <Num s="S16">3–5 厘米</Num>的扁圆盘，种子四周是一圈纸一样的薄翅，能随风飘走。水：椰子坚硬的壳外包着一层厚厚的纤维，适应于随海流传播。弹裂：有些干果成熟时会突然裂开，把种子弹出去，凤仙花就是这样。动物：带钩的果实挂在动物毛上，在别处掉下来；多汁的果实被动物吃掉，种子随粪便排出。

辣椒靠动物传播。它的辣味来自辣椒素，辣椒素让许多哺乳动物不吃它，却挡不住鸟。鸟吃下成熟的果实，种子完整地排出来，还能发芽。

<More title={{ en: "Birds and chillies", zh: "鸟和辣椒" }}>

2001 年发表的一项研究中，野生辣椒被一种鸟（弯嘴嘲鸫）吃掉，而林鼠和仙人掌鼠都不碰它。经过鸟肠道的种子仍然能发芽。研究者认为，辣椒素挡住了会毁掉种子的动物，却把果实留给了会传播种子的鸟。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Structure helps dispersal", zh: "结构帮助传播" }}>

翅、能漂浮的纤维层、钩刺和会弹裂的果荚，都是帮助果实和种子被带走的结构。小六课纲把这样的特点叫作结构上的适应。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it", zh: "试一试" }}>

收集三种不同的果实或种子，比如青龙木的翅果、豆荚和一块椰子纤维。猜一猜它们各靠什么传播。在有风和没风的日子，分别从同样的高度让它们落下，量一量落在离脚多远的地方。

</More>
