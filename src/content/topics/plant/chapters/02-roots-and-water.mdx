<Lang en>

Here the pot is cut open. A thick main root grows straight down from the base of the stem. Side roots branch off it and spread through the soil, and a mat of fine roots fills the lower half of the <Num s="S1">18 cm</Num> pot. NParks suggests a pot at least <Num s="S13">15 cm</Num> deep for chilli. <FlyTo preset="roots-view">Look at the roots</FlyTo>

Roots do two jobs. They hold the plant firmly in the soil, so wind and rain do not knock it over. And they take in water from the soil, with <Term id="root-hair">root hairs</Term> doing much of the work. A root hair is a long, thin outgrowth of one outer cell near a root tip. Thousands of them reach between the soil grains and give the root a far larger surface than it would have without them.

The water carries mineral salts dissolved in it from the soil. Plants need these in small amounts to grow healthy leaves, flowers and fruit; garden fertiliser adds more of them. From the fine roots, water passes into the main root and up the stem, and on the stage it shows as blue dots.

When the soil dries out, the roots cannot take in enough water. The leaves lose water faster than it arrives, so they go soft and droop, like those of the small pot beside the main one. Watered in time, the roots take in water again and the leaves usually stand up again.

<More title={{ en: "Root hairs up close", zh: "细看根毛" }}>

The root tip on the bench is drawn about <Num s="S1">30</Num> times larger, with fifteen root hairs; a real tip carries many hundreds. Root hairs are delicate. When a plant is pulled up and replanted, many are torn off, and for a few days it takes in less water.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: a fair test with water", zh: "试一试：关于水的公平测试" }}>

Take two young plants of the same kind and size, in the same pots and soil, side by side in the same place. Water one as usual and do not water the other for three days. At the same time each day, take a photo of both. What did you change? What did you keep the same? What did you look at to compare them? Water the dry one afterwards and see whether it recovers.

</More>

<Lang zh>

这里把花盆剖开了。一条粗壮的主根从茎基部笔直向下长，侧根从主根分出，伸进四周的土里，细根在 <Num s="S1">18 厘米</Num>高的花盆下半部结成一团。NParks 建议种辣椒的花盆至少深 <Num s="S13">15 厘米</Num>。<FlyTo preset="roots-view">看看根</FlyTo>

根有两项工作。第一，把植物牢牢固定在土里，风吹雨打也不会倒。第二，从土里吸收水分，其中大部分工作由<Term id="root-hair">根毛</Term>完成。根毛是根尖附近一个外层细胞长出的细长突起，细得肉眼几乎看不见，所以台上把根尖放大来看。成千上万根根毛伸进土粒之间，让根的表面比没有根毛时大得多。

水里溶着从土里带来的矿物盐。植物只需要少量矿物盐，就能长出健康的叶、花和果实；园艺用的肥料就是给土里补充矿物盐。水从细根进入主根，再沿着茎往上走，在舞台上显示为蓝色的点。

盆土干了，根就吸不到足够的水。叶子散失水分比得到水分快，于是变软、垂下来，就像主株旁边那个小盆里的植株一样。及时浇水，根重新吸到水，叶子通常又会挺起来。

<More title={{ en: "Root hairs up close", zh: "细看根毛" }}>

台上的根尖放大了约 <Num s="S1">30</Num> 倍，画了 15 根根毛；真实的根尖上有成百上千根。根毛很娇嫩。把植株拔起来再种下，很多根毛会被扯断，之后几天它吸进的水就少了。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: a fair test with water", zh: "试一试：关于水的公平测试" }}>

找两株同种、同样大小的幼苗，种在同样的盆和土里，并排放在同一个地方。一株照常浇水，另一株三天不浇水。每天在同一时间给两盆拍一张照片。你改变了什么？哪些保持不变？你看什么来比较它们？之后给干的那盆浇水，看看它能不能恢复。

</More>
