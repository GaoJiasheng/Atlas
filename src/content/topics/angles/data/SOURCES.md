# Sources · angles

The syllabus lines quoted in the lesson come from S1 only (wording and page numbers checked against the PDF on
2026-11-16, docs/76 §1.1). Steps 01–04 follow Primary Three (right angles, angles greater or smaller than a right
angle, perpendicular and parallel lines), with no degrees; steps 05–07 follow Primary Four (naming, measuring and
drawing angles in degrees); steps 08–11 follow Primary Five (angles on a straight line, at a point, vertically
opposite, finding unknown angles). The angle sum of a triangle and the angles of quadrilaterals are later syllabus
lines and are not in this lesson.

The mistakes the beats name and the checks diagnose (judging an angle by the length of its arms, missing a tilted
right angle, reading the other row of a protractor, using 360° on a straight line, and so on) are this lesson's own
diagnoses, written from the facts of the mathematics; the lesson does not claim how often any of them happens. Two
sources read on 2026-11-16 show that children do think this way: S2 (Keiser 2004, p. 292: longer arms or more space
between them taken for a bigger angle) and S3 (the Common Core geometric measurement progression, p. 23: a right angle
"points to the right", arm lengths, a protractor read as if an arm lay on its base). The other candidates in docs/76 §9
(Mitchelmore & White 2000, Clements & Battista 1989, Browning et al. 2007, Close 1982, Hart 1981, the PSLE 0008
syllabus) could not be read for the claims they were listed for and are left out; the PSLE 0008 document (2026) was
read and does not mention "not drawn to scale". The facts used are definitions and arithmetic: a full turn is 360°, a right angle is a quarter turn (90°),
angles on a straight line add up to 180°, angles at a point to 360°, vertically opposite angles are equal; the minute
hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, so 6° a minute and 90° in 15 minutes.

Every drawing on the stage is drawn by the engine from its directions; a protractor's two rows are placed as on a
real one (the outer row starts at 0 on the left, the inner row at 0 on the right). Drawings marked "not drawn to scale"
are drawn at least 12° away from their true sizes on purpose (docs/76 D8). The names in the questions are made up.
