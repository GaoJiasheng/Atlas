<Lang en>

Hang the <Num s="S1">50 g</Num> mass hanger on the spring. The spring stretches, bounces a few times and settles. In this model it is now <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num> longer than its own length of <Num s="S1">8.0 cm</Num>. That extra length is the <Term id="extension">extension</Term>. Read it on the ruler as the change in the pointer's position, not as the spring's whole length. <FlyTo preset="spring-view">Look at the spring stand</FlyTo>

The hanger hangs still, yet two forces act on it. Gravitational force pulls it down. The stretched spring pulls it up: that pull is the elastic spring force. Once the hanger has settled, the two are the same size. A stretched spring always pulls back towards its own length; a squashed spring pushes back outwards.

Add the slotted masses one at a time, counting the hanger in the load. In this model each <Num s="S1">50 g</Num> adds <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num> to the extension, up to <Num s="S1">300 g</Num>: <Num s="S1">200 g</Num> gives <Num s="S1">4.0 cm</Num>. Record each load with its extension. On the graph the points lie on a straight line, so you can read between them: <Num s="S1">175 g</Num> would give about <Num s="S1">3.5 cm</Num>.

A spring balance works the same way. A bigger pull stretches its spring further, and the scale beside the pointer is marked in newtons instead of centimetres.

A spring can be damaged. With <Num s="S1">400 g</Num> hanging from it, this model spring stretches <Num s="S1">9.2 cm</Num>, much more than the straight line would give. Take the masses off and it stays <Num s="S1">1.2 cm</Num> longer than before. Pulled too far, a spring does not go back to its own length.

<More title={{"en": "Elastic limit", "zh": "弹性限度"}}>

The load beyond which a spring no longer goes back to its own length is its elastic limit. In this model it is <Num s="S1">300 g</Num>; after <Num s="S1">350 g</Num> the spring stays <Num s="S1">0.4 cm</Num> longer, and after <Num s="S1">400 g</Num>, <Num s="S1">1.2 cm</Num> longer. Real springs have their own limits. Do not test a classroom spring by overloading it.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Springs that push", "zh": "往外推的弹簧"}}>

Squash a spring and it pushes outwards as it tries to get back to its own length. A click pen uses a small squashed spring to push its tip back in, and a sprung mattress has hundreds of them.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Stored energy", "zh": "储存的能量"}}>

A stretched or squashed spring stores energy: let it go and it can throw something or set something moving. How stored energy changes into motion, sound and heat is in <TopicLink slug="energy-conversion" ch="stored-energy">Stored energy</TopicLink>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About this model", "zh": "关于这个模型"}}>

The rule that the extension goes up in equal steps for equal loads, up to a limit, is named after Robert Hooke; you will meet it again in later science. The model spring's numbers, <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num> for each <Num s="S1">50 g</Num> and a limit at <Num s="S1">300 g</Num>, are illustrative, not measured on a particular spring. The bounce after each new load is drawn so that you can see the spring settle.

</More>

<Lang zh>

把 <Num s="S1">50 g</Num> 的砝码挂钩挂上弹簧。弹簧伸长，弹了几下，停了下来。在这个模型里，它比原来的长度 <Num s="S1">8.0 cm</Num> 长了 <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num>。多出来的这段长度叫<Term id="extension">伸长</Term>。在竖尺上读的是指针位置的变化，而不是弹簧的总长度。<FlyTo preset="spring-view">看弹簧架</FlyTo>

挂钩静静地挂着，身上却有两个力。重力把它往下拉；被拉长的弹簧把它往上拉，这个拉力就是弹簧的弹力。挂钩停稳以后，这两个力一样大。被拉长的弹簧总想缩回原来的长度；被压短的弹簧则往外推。

一片一片加上槽码，挂钩本身也算在载荷里。在这个模型里，<Num s="S1">300 g</Num> 以内，每多 <Num s="S1">50 g</Num>，伸长就多 <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num>：<Num s="S1">200 g</Num> 时伸长 <Num s="S1">4.0 cm</Num>。把每次的载荷和伸长记下来。图上的点连成一条直线，所以两点之间也能读：<Num s="S1">175 g</Num> 大约伸长 <Num s="S1">3.5 cm</Num>。

弹簧秤也是这个道理。拉力越大，里面的弹簧伸得越长；指针旁边的刻度标的不是厘米，而是牛顿。

弹簧也会坏。挂上 <Num s="S1">400 g</Num>，这个模型里的弹簧伸长 <Num s="S1">9.2 cm</Num>，比照那条直线算出来的多得多。取下砝码，它比原来长了 <Num s="S1">1.2 cm</Num>。拉过了头，弹簧就回不到原来的长度了。

<More title={{"en": "Elastic limit", "zh": "弹性限度"}}>

超过某个载荷，弹簧就回不到原来的长度，这个载荷叫弹性限度。在这个模型里是 <Num s="S1">300 g</Num>；挂过 <Num s="S1">350 g</Num> 后弹簧长了 <Num s="S1">0.4 cm</Num>，挂过 <Num s="S1">400 g</Num> 后长了 <Num s="S1">1.2 cm</Num>。真实的弹簧各有各的限度。不要故意挂太重来试课室里的弹簧。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Springs that push", "zh": "往外推的弹簧"}}>

把弹簧压短，它就往外推，想回到原来的长度。按压式圆珠笔里有一根被压短的小弹簧，把笔尖推回去；弹簧床垫里有好几百根这样的弹簧。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Stored energy", "zh": "储存的能量"}}>

被拉长或压短的弹簧储存着能量：一放手，它就能把东西弹出去，或者让东西动起来。储存的能量怎样变成运动、声音和热，见<TopicLink slug="energy-conversion" ch="stored-energy">储存的能量</TopicLink>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About this model", "zh": "关于这个模型"}}>

“在一定限度内，载荷每增加同样多，伸长也增加同样多”，这条规律以罗伯特·胡克的名字命名，以后的科学课还会学到。模型弹簧的数字（每 <Num s="S1">50 g</Num> 伸长 <Num s="S1">1.0 cm</Num>，限度 <Num s="S1">300 g</Num>）是示意值，不是在哪一根具体的弹簧上量出来的。每次加了载荷后的弹跳，是为了让你看到弹簧怎样停稳。

</More>
