<Lang en>

A solid has a definite shape and a fixed volume. Move the stone from the bench to the tray and it stays the same shape and the same size; put it in a box and it does not spread out to fill the box. The glass marble and the ball of clay behave the same way.

To measure a solid's volume, use the water in measuring cylinder B. It starts at <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num>. Lower the stone in until it is completely under the water and the reading rises to <Num s="S1">125 ml</Num>. The stone takes up space, so the water has to move aside and rise. Subtract the start: the stone's volume is <Num s="S1">25 cm³</Num>. A volume of <Num s="S11">1 ml</Num> is the same as <Num s="S11">1 cm³</Num>, so the rise in millilitres is the volume in cubic centimetres.

A force can change the shape of some solids. Roll the ball of clay into a long shape, keeping all of the clay. In this model either shape raises the water to <Num s="S1">120 ml</Num>: the shape changed, the volume did not. A sponge gets smaller when you squeeze it only because the air in its holes is pushed out; its thin solid walls fold but keep their own volume.

Sand and sugar pour, so they can look like liquids. They are not. Poured onto a dish, sand piles up in a heap with sloping sides, while water spreads out flat. Magnified, each <Num s="S13">0.0625–2 mm</Num> grain of sand has its own shape and volume: each grain is a solid. Pouring only lets the grains roll past each other.

<More title={{"en": "Measuring by displacement", "zh": "排水法"}}>

Measuring a volume this way is called <Term id="displacement">displacement</Term>: read the cylinder before and after, then subtract the first reading from the second. The object must be completely under the water, and it must not float, dissolve, soak up water or trap bubbles. The primary syllabus doesn't cover the method by name; you'll learn it in secondary school. Here the rising water is the evidence that a solid takes up space.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why the sponge stays out", "zh": "为什么不把海绵放进量筒"}}>

A sponge floats, and it soaks up water, so dropping it into a measuring cylinder would not give the volume of its outside. Its holes are full of air: in this model its <Num s="S1">384 cm³</Num> has a mass of only <Num s="S1">11.5 g</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Grains", "zh": "颗粒"}}>

Grains of sand differ in size and colour, and their corners are worn round. A dry sand heap slopes at about <Num s="S14">32.5°</Num>; the model draws <Num s="S1">33°</Num>. The slope depends on the sand and on how it is poured. Granulated sugar is made of small crystals, on average about <Num s="S40">0.4–1.0 mm</Num> across.

</More>

<Lang zh>

固体有固定的形状和固定的体积。把石头从实验台拿到托盘上，它还是那个形状、那么大；把它放进一只盒子，它也不会散开去填满盒子。玻璃弹珠、黏土球也是这样。

要量固体的体积，就用量筒 B 里的水。开始时是 <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num>。把石头放进去，让它整个没入水中，读数升到 <Num s="S1">125 ml</Num>。石头占据了空间，水只好让开、升上来。减去开始时的读数，石头的体积就是 <Num s="S1">25 cm³</Num>。<Num s="S11">1 ml</Num> 和 <Num s="S11">1 cm³</Num> 一样大，所以水面升高了多少毫升，体积就是多少立方厘米。

用力可以改变某些固体的形状。把黏土球搓成长条，一点黏土也不少。在这个模型里，两种形状都让水面升到 <Num s="S1">120 ml</Num>：形状变了，体积没变。海绵一捏就变小，只是因为孔里的空气被挤了出去；薄薄的固体孔壁折了起来，它们自己的体积并没有变。

<More title={{"en": "Measuring by displacement", "zh": "排水法"}}>

这样量体积的方法叫<Term id="displacement">排水法</Term>：放进去之前读一次，放进去之后再读一次，两数相减。物体要整个没入水中，不能浮、不能溶、不能吸水，也不能带着气泡。小学课纲没有讲到这个方法的名称，中学会学。在这里，水面升高就是固体占据空间的证据。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why the sponge stays out", "zh": "为什么不把海绵放进量筒"}}>

海绵会浮，还会吸水，所以把它丢进量筒，量不出它外形的体积。它的孔里全是空气：在这个模型里，<Num s="S1">384 cm³</Num> 的海绵只有 <Num s="S1">11.5 g</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Grains", "zh": "颗粒"}}>

沙粒大小、颜色各不相同，棱角被磨圆了。干沙堆的坡约 <Num s="S14">32.5°</Num>；模型画的是 <Num s="S1">33°</Num>。坡度取决于沙的种类和倒的方法。砂糖是一粒粒小晶体，平均约 <Num s="S40">0.4–1.0 mm</Num>。

</More>
