<Lang en>

Two questions sort most matter. Does it keep its own shape? Does it keep its own volume? A solid keeps both; a liquid keeps its volume but takes the shape of its container; a gas keeps neither and spreads out to fill whatever holds it.

Measuring cylinder C holds <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num> of dry sand, with <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num> of water beside it. Pour the water in. At first, bubbles of air come up from between the grains and the reading does not move: water is filling spaces that held air. In this model, after <Num s="S1">20 ml</Num> of water the reading is still <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num>. With all the water in, it reads <Num s="S1">165 ml</Num>, not <Num s="S1">200 ml</Num>. About <Num s="S1">35 ml</Num> of the dry sand was air, which escaped as bubbles. The grains themselves did not shrink: each is a solid.

Now look at the small table. In an unopened can of drink, the metal can is a solid, the drink is a liquid, and the small space above the drink holds gas. The drink also has carbon dioxide gas dissolved in it; you see it only when the can is opened and bubbles form.

Ice, the water in juice and the invisible water vapour in the air show that one substance can be a solid, a liquid or a gas. <TopicLink slug="water" ch="the-water-cycle">Water in Singapore: the water cycle</TopicLink> follows how water changes between them. The air in a bicycle tyre and in a basketball is a gas squeezed into a container.

Use the Try it card to pour the water in steps of <Num s="S1">20 ml</Num>, keeping the same dry sand. Record each step: the line stays flat while water fills the gaps between the grains, then rises once the water stands above the sand.

For a heap of sand, look at the grains, not at the heap: each grain has a definite shape and a fixed volume, and the spaces between them hold air.

Kaya is a sweet, thick spread made with coconut milk or cream, eggs and sugar. It spreads and flows slowly, yet a scoop of it holds its shape for a while. Toothpaste and jelly are like that too. The simple examples in the table do not describe them well, and scientists use more words for such materials. The primary syllabus doesn't cover how to sort them; you'll learn more about how materials behave in secondary school. Be curious: watch what a sample does before you give it a name.

Water vapour is a gas and cannot be seen. The white mist above a kettle or a cold drink is made of tiny drops of liquid water, not vapour. How water melts, evaporates, boils and condenses is in <TopicLink slug="water" ch="the-water-cycle">Water in Singapore: the water cycle</TopicLink>.

<More title={{"en": "Look closer (beyond the syllabus)", "zh": "凑近看（课纲之外）"}}>

Scientists use a model in which matter is made of tiny <Term id="particle">particles</Term>. In a solid the particles are packed close and stay in place, only jiggling; in a liquid they are close but can slide past each other; in a gas they are far apart and move about freely. This is a model scientists use: the primary syllabus doesn't cover it; you'll learn it in secondary school. Every question in this topic can be answered without it, using mass, space, shape and volume.

用两个问题，就能给大多数物质分类：它有没有自己固定的形状？有没有自己固定的体积？固体两样都有；液体体积固定，形状随容器变；气体两样都没有，会散开，充满装它的东西。

量筒 C 装着 <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num> 干沙，旁边有 <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num> 水。把水倒进去。一开始，沙粒之间冒出气泡，读数却不动：水正在填原来装着空气的空隙。在这个模型里，倒进 <Num s="S1">20 ml</Num> 水以后，读数还是 <Num s="S1">100 ml</Num>。水全倒进去，读 <Num s="S1">165 ml</Num>，不是 <Num s="S1">200 ml</Num>。干沙里约有 <Num s="S1">35 ml</Num> 是空气，冒着泡跑掉了。沙粒本身并没有变小：每一粒都是固体。

再看小桌。一罐没开的汽水里，金属罐是固体，汽水是液体，汽水上面那一小段空间里是气体。汽水里还溶着二氧化碳，要等罐子打开、冒出气泡才看得见。

冰、果汁里的水、空气里看不见的水蒸气，说明同一种物质可以是固体、液体，也可以是气体。水怎样在这三态之间变化，在<TopicLink slug="water" ch="the-water-cycle">“新加坡的水”主题的水循环一章</TopicLink>里讲。脚车轮胎和篮球里的空气，是压进容器里的气体。

用“试一试”卡片每次倒 <Num s="S1">20 ml</Num> 水，沙始终是那一量筒干沙。每倒一次记一行：水还在填沙粒之间的空隙时，折线是平的；水面升到沙面上以后，折线才往上走。

<More title={{"en": "Look closer (beyond the syllabus)", "zh": "凑近看（课纲之外）"}}>

科学家用一种模型来解释物质：物质由极小的<Term id="particle">微粒</Term>组成。固体里的微粒挨得很紧，待在原位，只在那里振动；液体里的微粒也挨着，但能互相滑过；气体里的微粒相隔很远，到处自由运动。这是科学家用的一种模型：小学课纲没有讲，中学会学。本主题的每个问题，不用它也都答得出，用质量、空间、形状和体积就够了。
