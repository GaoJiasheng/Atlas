<Lang en>

Look across the science bench. You can weigh a stone, pour water from one vessel into another, trap air under water in a cup and press on sealed syringes. Each of these gives you evidence about <Term id="matter">matter</Term>. In science, matter is anything that has <Term id="mass">mass</Term> and <Term id="occupy-space">takes up space</Term>. Mass is how much matter something contains, measured in grams or kilograms.

The bench has five stations. At the first, an electronic <Term id="balance">balance</Term> and a beam balance measure and compare masses. At the second, a <Term id="measuring-cylinder">measuring cylinder</Term> measures how much space a liquid or a solid takes up: its <Term id="volume">volume</Term>. The third tests whether air takes up space and has mass. At the fourth, three sealed syringes are squeezed. The fifth compares a heap of sand with the water beside it. A small table holds things from home.

Each chapter uses shape and volume to tell apart the three <Term id="state-of-matter">states of matter</Term>: <Term id="solid">solid</Term>, <Term id="liquid">liquid</Term> and <Term id="gas">gas</Term>. Look for evidence in the readings, not only in how things look. A can of drink holds all three.

From chapter 1 on, each chapter has a Try it card. It names what you change, what you keep the same and what you observe. Make a prediction, change the setting and record a row; a bar graph compares choices and a line graph follows a number as it goes up. Readings marked SIM are worked out by this model. Real apparatus gives slightly different readings each time, so real experiments are repeated.

<More title={{"en": "How this model works", "zh": "这个模型怎样算"}}>

The bench is drawn life size; the sizes of the vessels and the objects are design choices for this topic. The readings follow rules that use measured values for air and water, such as about <Num s="S6">1.2 g</Num> for a litre of air, and the sizes of the apparatus. For the same settings the model always gives the same reading. The sand and sugar grains in chapter 2 are drawn <Num s="S1">40 times</Num> life size, on their own, away from the bench.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Matter in everyday words", "zh": "日常说法里的“物质”"}}>

In everyday English, “it does not matter” means it is not important. In science, matter is the stuff things are made of. A stone, the water in a glass and the air in a room are all matter, whether or not you can see them or hold them.

</More>

看一看这张科学实验台。你可以称一块石头，把水从一个容器倒进另一个容器，在杯子里把空气关进水下，再压一压封了口的针筒。每一件事都给你一些关于<Term id="matter">物质</Term>的证据。在科学里，物质是任何有<Term id="mass">质量</Term>、<Term id="occupy-space">占据空间</Term>的东西。质量就是东西里有多少物质，单位是克或千克。

实验台上有五个工位。第一个工位用<Term id="balance">电子秤</Term>和天平称质量、比质量。第二个工位用<Term id="measuring-cylinder">量筒</Term>量液体或固体占据多少空间，也就是它的<Term id="volume">体积</Term>。第三个工位检验空气是不是占据空间、有没有质量。第四个工位压三支封了口的针筒。第五个工位把一堆沙和旁边的一碟水放在一起比较。旁边还有一张小桌，放着家里常见的东西。

每一章都从形状和体积两方面，区分<Term id="state-of-matter">物质的三态</Term>：<Term id="solid">固体</Term>、<Term id="liquid">液体</Term>和<Term id="gas">气体</Term>。判断时要看读数这些证据，不能只看样子。一罐汽水里，三态都有。

从第 1 章起，每章都有一张“试一试”卡片。卡片写明改变什么、保持什么不变、观察什么。先猜一猜，再改变设置，记下一行；条形图用来比较几种选择，折线图用来看一个数增大时读数怎样变。标 SIM 的读数是这个模型算出来的。真实的器材每次读数都会差一点，所以真实的实验要重复做。

<More title={{"en": "How this model works", "zh": "这个模型怎样算"}}>

实验台按实物大小画；容器和物体的尺寸是本主题的设计取值。读数按一些规则算出来，规则用到空气和水的实测数值，比如每升空气约 <Num s="S6">1.2 g</Num>，也用到器材的尺寸。设置相同，模型给出的读数每次都一样。第 2 章的沙粒和糖粒放大了 <Num s="S1">40 倍</Num>，单独画，不在实验台上。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Matter in everyday words", "zh": "日常说法里的“物质”"}}>

日常说的“物质生活”“物质条件”，指的是吃的、用的东西和钱，不是科学里的“物质”。科学里的物质，是构成东西的材料：一块石头、一杯水、一屋子的空气，都是物质，不管你看不看得见、拿不拿得住。

</More>
