The cup above the basin looks empty, but it is full of <Term id="air">air</Term>. A dry tissue is pushed into its bottom, and the cup is held upside down. You cannot see the air, but you can see what it does.

Push the cup straight down into the water. The air cannot get out, so the air stops the cup filling and the tissue stays dry. In this model the water outside rises by about <Num s="S1">9 mm</Num>: the cup and the air trapped in it take up space, and the water cannot be in the same place at the same time. The water pushes the trapped air into a slightly smaller space, so it creeps only about <Num s="S1">1 mm</Num> above the rim inside the cup.

Now make a hole in the bottom of the cup. The air bubbles out through the hole, water rushes in, and the tissue gets wet. The water outside falls back, to only about <Num s="S1">1.3 mm</Num> above where it started: that is the space the glass itself takes up. Tilting the cup lets the air out in the same way, as bubbles escape from under the rim.

Air also has mass. On balance B, the basketball reads <Num s="S1">600.0 g</Num> before it is pumped, in this model. After <Num s="S1">20</Num> pumps it reads about <Num s="S1">604.7 g</Num>. The ball keeps the same outside size, so the extra <Num s="S1">4.7 g</Num> is the air pumped in. Air takes up space and has mass, so air is matter, even though you cannot see it.

Use the Try it card to change the number of pumps, keeping the same ball and the same balance. Each pump in this model pushes in the same amount of air, so the graph rises in a straight line.

把杯子竖直压进水里。空气出不去，挡住了水，杯子灌不满，纸巾还是干的。在这个模型里，外面的水面升高了约 <Num s="S1">9 mm</Num>：杯子和关在里面的空气占据了空间，水不能同时待在同一个地方。水把关在里面的空气压紧了一点，所以杯里的水只比杯口高约 <Num s="S1">1 mm</Num>。
