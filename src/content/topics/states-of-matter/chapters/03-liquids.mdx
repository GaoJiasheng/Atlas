A liquid has no definite shape, but it has a fixed volume: the same water keeps its volume when it is poured from one container to another, as long as none is spilt. In every vessel its surface settles flat and level. Juice, oil and syrup behave the same way, although thick liquids flow more slowly.

Where water touches the glass wall of a cylinder, its surface curves up a little. This curve is the <Term id="meniscus">meniscus</Term>. Keep your eye level with the water and read the bottom of the curve. In a real pour a few drops cling to the walls; this model pours every drop.

A fixed volume here means that the same sample keeps its volume when it is moved from one container to another.
