<Lang en>

On 12 April 1961, Yuri Gagarin flew in Vostok 1, becoming the first human in space and the first to circle Earth. An <Term id="term-orbit">orbit</Term> is a path around a body under gravity. Reaching a great height is not enough: a spacecraft also needs the appropriate sideways motion. Gravity continues to act while it travels around Earth.

Alan Shepard’s Mercury flight on 5 May was <Term id="term-suborbital">suborbital</Term>. He reached space without completing a circuit of Earth. John Glenn’s flight on 20 February 1962 was the first US human orbital mission. These flights addressed different tasks; calling each simply “a trip into space” hides an important difference in the journey.

On 25 May 1961, President John F. Kennedy proposed landing a person on the Moon and returning that person safely before the decade ended. This was a political goal requiring further engineering, not evidence that the necessary spacecraft already existed. The return was part of the stated goal from the beginning. Meanwhile, Soviet human flights continued: Valentina Tereshkova became the first woman in space in Vostok 6 on 16 June 1963.

Voskhod 1 carried the first multi-person crew in October 1964. On 18 March 1965, Aleksei Leonov made the first <Term id="term-spacewalk">spacewalk</Term> from Voskhod 2, working outside his spacecraft. Human flight was expanding from getting a person into space to testing how people could live and work there. The location badges identify these as space missions associated with launch centres on Earth.

<More title={{ en: "Gravity remains in space", zh: "太空中仍有引力" }}>

An orbiting spacecraft is continually accelerated by gravity. Floating inside it is not proof that gravity has disappeared. The related Sun–Earth–Moon topic provides the physical setting; this chapter distinguishes the historically different orbital and suborbital flights.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A goal and its evidence", zh: "目标与证据" }}>

Kennedy’s proposal is evidence of an announced goal in 1961. The later mission record must establish whether, when and how it was achieved. A speech cannot substitute for a flight report, and “landing” leaves out the promised safe return.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Women and work outside the cabin", zh: "女性与舱外工作" }}>

Tereshkova’s flight and Leonov’s spacewalk belong to different missions. A later comparison entry can mention both Glenn and Tereshkova without moving Glenn’s 1962 flight to 1963. The name, date and task remain separate pieces of evidence.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

1961 年 4 月 12 日，尤里·加加林乘东方1号飞行，成为首位进入太空并环绕地球的人。<Term id="term-orbit">轨道</Term>是在引力作用下绕天体运动的路径。飞得高还不够，飞船也要有合适的横向运动；围绕地球飞行时，引力仍在作用。

5 月 5 日，艾伦·谢泼德完成水星计划的<Term id="term-suborbital">亚轨道</Term>飞行，进入太空但没有绕地球一周。约翰·格伦在 1962 年 2 月 20 日完成美国首次载人轨道飞行。两者解决的问题不同，只说“进入太空”，就会漏掉旅程的重要差别。

1961 年 5 月 25 日，肯尼迪总统提出在这个十年结束前，让人登月并安全返回。这是需要后续工程验证的政治目标，不代表所需飞船当时已经具备；安全返回从一开始就在承诺之中。苏联也继续载人飞行，瓦莲京娜·捷列什科娃于 1963 年 6 月 16 日乘东方6号成为首位进入太空的女性。

1964 年 10 月，上升1号首次搭载多人乘组。1965 年 3 月 18 日，阿列克谢·列昂诺夫从上升2号进行首次<Term id="term-spacewalk">太空行走</Term>。任务逐渐从把人送上去，扩展到验证人在太空怎样生活与工作。同样是载人任务，舱内飞行与舱外工作仍需分辨。地点说明仍须读清：这些任务发生在太空，地球上的点只是关联发射中心。

<More title={{ en: "Gravity remains in space", zh: "太空中仍有引力" }}>

轨道飞船不断受到引力加速；人在舱内漂浮，不能证明引力消失。相关的日地月主题提供物理背景，本章则分辨历史上轨道飞行与亚轨道飞行的不同任务。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A goal and its evidence", zh: "目标与证据" }}>

肯尼迪的讲话证明 1961 年宣布了什么目标。何时、怎样实现，要读后来的任务记录；讲话不能代替飞行报告，只说“着陆”也会漏掉原先承诺的安全返回。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Women and work outside the cabin", zh: "女性与舱外工作" }}>

捷列什科娃的飞行与列昂诺夫的舱外活动属于不同任务。回顾条目可以并提格伦与捷列什科娃，却不能把格伦的 1962 年飞行挪到 1963 年；姓名、日期和任务要各自保留。
