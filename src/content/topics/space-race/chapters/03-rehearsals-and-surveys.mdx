<Lang en>

A journey to the Moon required skills that a simple flight around Earth had not yet demonstrated. Gemini missions developed longer stays, work outside the cabin and the ability to bring spacecraft together. In December 1965, Gemini VI-A and VII achieved <Term id="term-rendezvous">rendezvous</Term>: they approached one another and matched their motion in orbit. They did not connect mechanically.

That connection is <Term id="term-docking">docking</Term>. Gemini VIII achieved the first docking in March 1966, joining a target vehicle. A stuck thruster then caused an emergency. The same flight therefore supplied evidence of a successful docking and of a serious control problem. Keeping both results in the account helps explain why an achievement cannot stand for an entirely trouble-free mission.

Robotic craft investigated the destination while crews practised near Earth. On 3 February 1966, Luna 9 made the first successful <Term id="term-soft-landing">soft landing</Term> on the Moon, controlling its arrival so it could work on the surface. Luna 10 became a lunar orbiter in April. Landing and circling the Moon require different operations and provide different kinds of observations. The map associates both with Baikonur; neither event occurred on the ground there.

US Ranger, Surveyor and Lunar Orbiter missions also investigated the Moon before human landings. Surveyor 1 soft-landed in June 1966. These were complementary ways of gathering evidence about a place people planned to visit. The historical importance of a robot is the question it investigated and the result it achieved, rather than whether a human crew was aboard.

<More title={{ en: "Meeting is not connecting", zh: "靠近不等于连接" }}>

Rendezvous concerns relative position and motion. Docking adds a mechanical connection. Apollo would need both. The related Saturn V engineering topic examines Apollo’s later turn, docking and extraction arrangement; Gemini’s missions remain separate historical events.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Reading a lunar mission table", zh: "怎样读探月任务表" }}>

A mission table may list launch and arrival in separate columns. Luna 9’s February landing date is an arrival event, not its launch date. Luna 10’s lunar-orbit result must not be described as a surface landing.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Before people arrived", zh: "在人抵达之前" }}>

Ranger, Surveyor and Lunar Orbiter are programme names for robotic investigations. Surveyor 1’s surface arrival preceded Apollo’s human landing. The August 1966 comparison entry looks back across that work; it does not place every US lunar investigation on the same date.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

绕地球飞行，还没有证明人已具备登月所需的全部技能。双子座任务练习较长时间飞行、舱外活动，以及让飞船在轨道会合。1965 年 12 月，双子座 VI-A 与 VII 完成<Term id="term-rendezvous">交会</Term>：相互靠近并匹配运动，却没有机械连接。

机械连接称为<Term id="term-docking">对接</Term>。1966 年 3 月，双子座 VIII 与目标飞行器完成首次对接，随后因推进器卡住而遇上紧急情况。同一趟飞行既证明对接成功，也暴露严重控制问题。两项结果都保留，才不会把一次成就误写成全程没有危险。

乘组在地球附近练习时，机器人也在调查目的地。1966 年 2 月 3 日，月球9号首次成功在月球<Term id="term-soft-landing">软着陆</Term>，控制抵达速度，让仪器能在表面工作。4 月，月球10号成为绕月轨道器。着陆与绕行需要不同操作，取得的观察证据也不同；地图把它们关联到拜科努尔，并非说任务发生在当地地面。

美国的徘徊者、勘测者和月球轨道器也在载人登月前研究月球；勘测者1号于 1966 年 6 月软着陆。这些方法互相补充，调查人类计划前往的地方。机器人的历史意义，要看它研究什么、取得什么结果，不能只看有没有载人。机器人结果与乘组练习各自提供证据，并不是同一种验证。

<More title={{ en: "Meeting is not connecting", zh: "靠近不等于连接" }}>

交会涉及相对位置与运动，对接还要完成机械连接。阿波罗后来都要用到。相关的土星五号工程主题讨论阿波罗转身、对接和抽出登月舱的构型，双子座仍是独立的历史任务。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Reading a lunar mission table", zh: "怎样读探月任务表" }}>

任务表可能把发射与抵达分别列出。月球9号的 2 月日期指着陆，不是发射；月球10号进入月球轨道，也不能写成抵达月面。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Before people arrived", zh: "在人抵达之前" }}>

徘徊者、勘测者与月球轨道器都是机器人调查项目。勘测者1号先于阿波罗载人任务抵达月面。1966 年 8 月的比较条目回顾这些工作，并不表示它们都在同一天进行。

</More>
