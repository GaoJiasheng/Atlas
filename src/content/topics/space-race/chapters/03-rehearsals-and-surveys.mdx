<Lang en>

A journey to the Moon required skills that a simple flight around Earth had not yet demonstrated. Gemini missions developed longer stays, work outside the cabin and the ability to bring spacecraft together. In December 1965, Gemini VI-A and VII achieved <Term id="term-rendezvous">rendezvous</Term>: they approached one another and matched their motion in orbit. They did not connect mechanically.

A mechanical connection between spacecraft is called <Term id="term-docking">docking</Term>. Gemini VIII achieved the first docking in March 1966 by joining a target vehicle. A thruster then stuck, causing an emergency. The flight proved that docking was possible, but also revealed a serious control problem.

Robotic craft investigated the Moon while crews practised near Earth. On 3 February 1966, Luna 9 made the first successful <Term id="term-soft-landing">soft landing</Term> there. It slowed enough for its equipment to keep working on the surface. In April, Luna 10 became a lunar orbiter. From the surface and from orbit, instruments could make different observations.

US Ranger, Surveyor and Lunar Orbiter missions also studied the Moon before people landed there. Surveyor 1 soft-landed in June 1966. These robotic missions investigated the place that crews hoped to visit. Their observations complemented the skills that astronauts were practising close to Earth.

<More title={{ en: "Meeting is not connecting", zh: "靠近不等于连接" }}>

Rendezvous brings spacecraft close together with matching motion. Docking joins them mechanically. Apollo would need both. The related Saturn V engineering topic explains Apollo's later turn, docking and extraction of its lander.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Reading a lunar mission table", zh: "怎样读探月任务表" }}>

Mission tables often give separate dates for launch and arrival. Luna 9 landed in February, after launching earlier. Luna 10 entered lunar orbit rather than landing on the surface.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Before people arrived", zh: "在人抵达之前" }}>

Ranger, Surveyor and Lunar Orbiter were robotic investigation programmes. Surveyor 1 reached the surface before Apollo's human landing. Their work took place over many missions, rather than on a single date.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

绕地球飞行，还没有证明人已具备登月所需的全部技能。双子座任务练习较长时间飞行、舱外活动，以及让飞船在轨道会合。1965 年 12 月，双子座 VI-A 与 VII 完成<Term id="term-rendezvous">交会</Term>：相互靠近并匹配运动，却没有机械连接。

飞船之间的机械连接称为<Term id="term-docking">对接</Term>。1966 年 3 月，双子座 VIII 与目标飞行器完成首次对接。随后，一个推进器卡住，引发险情。这次飞行证明了对接能够完成，也暴露出严重的控制问题。

乘组在地球附近练习时，机器人正在调查月球。1966 年 2 月 3 日，月球9号首次成功在月球<Term id="term-soft-landing">软着陆</Term>。它控制抵达速度，让仪器能够在表面继续工作。4 月，月球10号成为绕月轨道器。仪器从月面和轨道取得不同的观察资料。

美国的徘徊者、勘测者和月球轨道器，也在载人登月前研究月球。勘测者1号于 1966 年 6 月软着陆。这些机器人调查人类希望前往的地方。它们取得的资料，与宇航员在地球附近练习的技能相互补充，为后来的旅程作准备。载人任务练习飞船怎样靠近、连接和在舱外工作；无人任务则从月球表面与轨道观察。不同工作共同支持后来的载人登月。

<More title={{ en: "Meeting is not connecting", zh: "靠近不等于连接" }}>

交会让飞船靠近，并保持相匹配的运动；对接则把它们连接起来。阿波罗后来两者都要用到。相关的土星五号工程主题介绍阿波罗转身、对接与抽出登月舱的过程。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Reading a lunar mission table", zh: "怎样读探月任务表" }}>

任务表常把发射日期与抵达日期分开列出。月球9号先发射，再于 2 月着陆。月球10号则进入月球轨道，没有在表面着陆。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Before people arrived", zh: "在人抵达之前" }}>

徘徊者、勘测者和月球轨道器都是机器人调查项目。勘测者1号比阿波罗载人任务更早抵达月面。这些研究由多次任务完成，分布在不同时候。

</More>
