<Lang en>

The <Term id="term-space-race">space race</Term> was a competition in space technology during the Cold War. The United States and the Soviet Union sought scientific knowledge, technical capability and national prestige. Rockets also had military roots. A mission could gather useful measurements while serving a government’s political aims; these purposes could exist together.

This history follows both programmes from early satellites to the meeting of spacecraft in 1975. It includes machines that carried people and machines that worked without them. A first human landing is an important event, but it cannot tell the whole story of lunar exploration, research in space or the people who made those projects possible.

The engineers’ earlier lives belong in the account. Wernher von Braun worked on the German V-2 during the Second World War. Its production at Mittelwerk used forced labour. Sergei Korolev was imprisoned during Stalin’s purges and worked in the Soviet prison system before leading later space development. Technical achievement does not erase the coercion and suffering connected with these histories.

Read the map as a guide to Earth, not a drawing of space. Its points identify associated launch centres. A Moon mission remains a Moon mission even when its evidence is attached to a centre on Earth. Read each date and source as carefully as the point: a launch plan describes intentions, a flight record describes what happened, and a later memoir offers a participant’s perspective.

<More title={{ en: "What the map can show", zh: "地图能说明什么" }}>

The coastlines come from Natural Earth; centre coordinates come from Wikidata. No coloured national territory or guessed flight path is drawn. Static site markers are reference points throughout the topic, so their visibility does not establish when a facility opened.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "People and institutions", zh: "人物与制度" }}>

The von Braun and Korolev biographies support different statements about different systems. Forced labour at Mittelwerk and Korolev’s imprisonment must each be described directly. Neither is an explanation that makes later achievements inevitable or justifies the harm.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Different questions need different evidence", zh: "不同问题要读不同证据" }}>

A press kit is a plan, not proof that every planned action occurred. Mission summaries and financial histories answer other questions. The related Sun–Earth–Moon topic concerns the physical setting; this topic asks how dated historical evidence describes decisions and results.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

<Term id="term-space-race">太空竞赛</Term>是冷战时期的太空技术竞争。美国和苏联追求科学知识、技术能力与国家声望，火箭也有军用渊源。一项任务可以收集有用数据，同时服务政府的政治目标。这些目的可能并存，不能只凭一张庆祝照片判断。

本题从早期卫星讲到 1975 年飞船会合，既看载人的机器，也看无人工作的机器。首次载人登月是重要事件，却不能代替全部探月史、太空研究史，以及参与项目的人所经历的事。不同任务的成果，要按各自目标阅读。

工程师早年的处境也属于这段历史。第二次世界大战期间，韦恩赫尔·冯·布劳恩参与德国 V-2 火箭工作，米特尔工厂的生产使用强迫劳动。谢尔盖·科罗廖夫在斯大林清洗中被囚，曾在苏联监狱体系内工作，后来才领导航天研发。技术成果不能抹去这些经历中的强制与苦难。

地图供地球定位，不是太空示意图。点代表关联发射中心；月面任务不会因为证据连着地球上的点，就变成在地球发生。日期和来源也要细读：计划记录原定安排，飞行记录说明实际过程，后来的回忆则提供当事人的视角。

<More title={{ en: "What the map can show", zh: "地图能说明什么" }}>

海岸来自 Natural Earth，中心坐标来自 Wikidata。本题不画国家控制区，也不猜画飞行路线。静态地点标记始终是参照物，显示某个中心不等于证明它当时已经成立。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "People and institutions", zh: "人物与制度" }}>

冯·布劳恩与科罗廖夫的传记支持的是不同制度下的不同事实。米特尔工厂的强迫劳动、科罗廖夫的被囚，都应直接说明；不能把苦难写成成就必经的道路，或用后来的成果替伤害辩护。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Different questions need different evidence", zh: "不同问题要读不同证据" }}>

新闻资料包记的是计划，不证明每个动作都如期发生。任务汇编与财务史又回答其他问题。相关的日地月主题讨论物理背景，本题则用注明日期的历史证据，了解决策与结果。

</More>
