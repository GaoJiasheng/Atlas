<Lang en>

You have read a weighing scale to find how heavy something is. Now you will measure how much liquid there is: its <Term id="volume">volume</Term>.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the full bottle and the full bowl</Beat>. The bottle is tall and thin; the bowl is short and wide. The bottle looks as if it holds more because its water reaches higher, but the bowl is much wider. Looking is not enough.

Now <Beat n={2}>pour each into the same kind of jug</Beat>. The water from the bottle comes up to the 3rd mark; the water from the bowl comes up to the 5th. The jugs are the same, so the higher level means more water. The bowl held more, even though it is shorter.

Next, <Beat n={3}>watch Aisha fill a big measuring jug</Beat>. She pours in three cartons of <Qty u="1 l" /> each. The <Term id="litre">litre</Term> is a unit of volume; we write ℓ for litre. <Qty u="1 l" /> + <Qty u="1 l" /> + <Qty u="1 l" /> = <Qty u="3 l" />, and the water comes up to the mark 3. With a <Term id="scale">scale</Term> on the jug, you can say how much, not only which is more.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>read the water in the pail</Beat>. Look from the side, with your eyes level with the top of the water, and read the mark beside it. The numbers go up in twos with one short mark between, so each <Term id="small-part">small part</Term> stands for <Qty u="1 l" />. The water is one part above 6: <Qty u="7 l" />.

Thinking that a taller container always holds more is a mistake, because width matters too. To compare, pour into the same kind of container, or measure both in litres.

<More title={{ en: "Same water, different shape", zh: "水量相同，形状不同" }}>

Pouring water into a different container changes its shape, while its volume stays the same if none spills. The same water poured from vessel to vessel is in <TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="liquids">States of Matter: liquids</TopicLink>.

</More>

<Lang zh>

上一步用指针秤称东西有多重。这一步要量的是液体有多少，也就是<Term id="volume">容量</Term>。

<Figure src="figures/measuring-sugarcane" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of a sugar cane press with two ridged rollers, gear wheels and a large hand wheel. How much is there? To say so, you need marks on the jug and a unit. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "压出来的甘蔗水流进水壶。有多少？要说清楚，壶上得有刻度，还要有单位。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

先看<Beat n={1}>装满水的瓶子和碗</Beat>。瓶子又高又细，碗又矮又宽。瓶子里的水位高，看起来好像装得多；可是碗宽得多。光用眼睛看，是比不出来的。

再<Beat n={2}>把两边的水各倒进一样的量杯</Beat>。瓶子里的水只升到第 3 条线，碗里的水升到第 5 条线。量杯一模一样，水位高的水就多。所以碗装得多，虽然它比瓶子矮。

接着看<Beat n={3}>Aisha 往大量杯里倒水</Beat>。她倒进 3 盒水，每盒 <Qty u="1 l" />。<Term id="litre">升</Term>是容量的单位，写作 ℓ。<Qty u="1 l" /> + <Qty u="1 l" /> + <Qty u="1 l" /> = <Qty u="3 l" />，水正好升到 3 那条线。量杯上有了<Term id="scale">刻度</Term>，不但能比出哪个多，还能说出到底有多少。

最后，<Beat n={4}>读桶里的水</Beat>。读的时候要从侧面看，眼睛和水面一样高，再看水面对着哪条刻度线。这个桶上的数一次跳 2，中间一条短线，所以<Term id="small-part">每一小格</Term>表示 <Qty u="1 l" />。水面在 6 上面 1 格：<Qty u="7 l" />。

常见的错误是以为高的容器一定装得多。其实宽窄也有关系。要比容量，就倒进一样的容器里比，或者都用升量出来再比。

<More title={{ en: "Same water, different shape", zh: "水量相同，形状不同" }}>

把水倒进不同的容器，水的形状会改变；只要没有洒掉，体积就保持不变。同一份水在几个容器之间倒来倒去，在<TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="liquids">“物质的三态”主题的“液体”</TopicLink>里讲。

</More>
