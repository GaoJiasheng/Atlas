<Lang en>

You have read a <Term id="measuring-cup">measuring jug</Term> in <Term id="millilitre">millilitres</Term> and seen how they relate to <Term id="litre">litres</Term>. Now you will use two units together to describe one measurement.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the two brackets beside Siti's height</Beat>. The first covers <Qty u="1 m" />; the second covers <Qty u="32 cm" /> more. Each <Term id="small-part">small part</Term> stands for <Qty u="2 cm" />. Count on past the whole <Term id="metre">metre</Term> to read <Qty u="1 m 32 cm" />. This is written in <Term id="compound-units">compound units</Term>: whole big units first, then what is left in small units. Both parts belong to the same height.

Next, <Beat n={2}>follow the wet-market scale's needle</Beat>. Between <Qty u="2 kg" /> and <Qty u="3 kg" />, ten equal parts share <Qty u="1000 g" />, so each small part stands for <Qty u="100 g" />. Count the parts, not the marks. The needle is three parts past <Qty u="2 kg" />, so the fish has a <Term id="mass">mass</Term> of <Qty u="2 kg 300 g" />. Writing <Qty u="2 kg 3 g" /> is a mistake: each part here is <Qty u="100 g" />, not <Qty u="1 g" />.

Now <Beat n={3}>follow the posts along Arjun's path</Beat>. Long distances are measured in <Term id="kilometre">kilometres</Term>, written km, and <Qty u="1 km" /> = <Qty u="1000 m" />. This path has a post every <Qty u="100 m" />. The arrow is four posts past the first kilometre, so Arjun has walked <Qty u="1 km 400 m" />. Read the whole kilometres first, then the metres past them.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>compare the head boards on the two height charts</Beat>. Each small part stands for <Qty u="5 cm" />. Head board A is at <Qty u="1 m 5 cm" />, just one part past the metre mark. Head board B is at <Qty u="1 m 50 cm" />, halfway to <Qty u="2 m" />. Writing the small part as 50 when it is 5 is a mistake: it makes that extra <Term id="length">length</Term> ten times as long. Write the small part as it is.
