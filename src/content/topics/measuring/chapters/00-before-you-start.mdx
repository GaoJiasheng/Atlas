You measure things every day: whether a shelf is long enough, how heavy a bag of rice is, how much water is left in a bottle. This lesson is about reading the marks and numbers on the tools that measure them.

You will meet five tools: a ruler, a measuring tape, a weighing scale with a round dial, a <Term id="measuring-cup">measuring cup</Term> and a <Term id="balance">balance</Term> with two pans. Every one of them has a <Term id="scale">scale</Term>: marks the same distance apart, and numbers on some of them. Reading any of these tools is the same skill. On the stage now is an empty weighing scale, with its needle at 0. That is the first thing to check before you weigh anything.

There are 13 steps and a practice. Steps 01 and 02 start with a ruler, including one with its end broken off. Step 03 finds what <Term id="small-part">each small part</Term> of a scale stands for: the idea the whole lesson leans on. Steps 04 to 10 use it for <Term id="length">length</Term> in metres, <Term id="mass">mass</Term> on a balance and a dial, and <Term id="volume">volume</Term> in litres and millilitres; they also choose units, make estimates and compare. Steps 11 and 12 read two units together and change between units. Step 13 goes further and reads scales in decimals; its chapter is marked "Going further". The last chapter is a practice of eight questions.

Each chapter explains first. The circles on the chapter axis at the bottom are its beats: tap one, or press **,** and **.**, and the stage slides, fills, tips, swings a needle or zooms in, while the reading panel says what to look at.

The square at the end of each chapter is one short question, drawn from a set of about ten. In the reading panel you can:

- **Try it**: read a scale, tap the small parts, draw a line, pour to a mark, load a scale or put weights on a balance.
- **Check** (C): if it is not right yet, the feedback says why and where to look. After two tries, **Show me** draws the answer.
- **Hint** (I): a clue at any time, up to three, each one closer to the answer.
- **Another one**, or **Skip**: nothing is forced.

Every question can be done by tapping alone, or with the keyboard alone. Nothing is timed and nothing is saved.

<Lang zh>

我们每天都在量东西：书架够不够长，一袋米有多重，瓶子里还剩多少水。这节课讲的，就是怎样读懂量这些东西的器具上的刻度和数。

你会用到五种器具：尺子、卷尺、带圆盘的指针秤、<Term id="measuring-cup">量杯</Term>，还有两个盘的<Term id="balance">天平</Term>。它们都有<Term id="scale">刻度</Term>：一条条间隔一样的线，其中一些线旁边标了数。所以读这些器具，用的是同一种本领。舞台上现在是一台空着的指针秤，指针指着 0。称东西以前，第一件事就是看看它。

全课有 13 步和一章练习。第 01、02 步从尺子讲起，也包括断了一截的尺子。第 03 步讲<Term id="small-part">每一小格</Term>表示多少，这是整节课都要用到的想法。第 04 到 10 步用它来量米为单位的<Term id="length">长度</Term>，用天平和指针秤量<Term id="mass">质量</Term>，用升和毫升量<Term id="volume">容量</Term>，还要选单位、先估一估、比一比。第 11、12 步同时读两个单位，并在单位之间换算。第 13 步再往前一步，读刻度上的小数，这一章的章头标着“再往前一步”。最后一章是 8 道练习题。

每一章都先讲。屏幕底部章节轴上的小圆点就是这一章的拍：点一下，或者按 **,** 和 **.**，舞台上的东西就会滑动、倒水、天平倾斜、指针转动或者放大，阅读面板告诉你该看哪里。

每章最后的小方块是一道小题，从十道左右的题里挑一道。在阅读面板里你可以：

- **动手做**：读刻度、点出每一小格、画一条线、倒水到某条刻度、往秤上加东西，或者在天平上放砝码。
- **检查**（C）：还没对时，反馈会说明原因和该看哪里；试两次以后，可以按“看答案”，舞台会画出答案。
- **提示**（I）：随时可以看，最多三条，一条比一条接近答案。
- **再来一道**或**跳过**：做不做都由你。

每道题都能只用点按完成，也能只用键盘完成。不计时，也不保存。
