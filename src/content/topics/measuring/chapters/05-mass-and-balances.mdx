<Lang en>

You have used <Term id="metre">metres</Term> to say how long things are. Now you will compare how heavy things are: their <Term id="mass">mass</Term>.

<Figure src="figures/measuring-ball-and-stone" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of an old two-pan balance on a wooden base: a large smooth ball sits in the left pan, which has risen, and a small stone sits in the right pan, which has gone down.", zh: "一架老式双盘天平的铅笔画，底座是木头的：左盘里放着一个光滑的大球，这一边翘了起来；右盘里放着一块小石头，这一边沉了下去。" }} caption={{ en: "The big ball's pan rises and the small stone's pan goes down: bigger is not always heavier. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "大球那边翘起来，小石头那边沉下去：大的不一定重。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

Look at <Beat n={1}>the beach ball and the stone</Beat> on the two pans. The ball takes up much more room, but the pan with the stone is lower. On a <Term id="balance">balance</Term>, the heavier side goes down and the lighter side goes up. So the stone is heavier. Bigger is not always heavier.

Next, <Beat n={2}>balance the pineapple against a weight</Beat>. The pans are level, so the two sides have the same mass. The weight is <Qty u="1 kg" />, so this pineapple has a mass of <Qty u="1 kg" /> too. We write kg for <Term id="kilogram">kilogram</Term>. You find a mass you do not know by matching it with masses you do know.

Now <Beat n={3}>compare the big weight with smaller ones</Beat>. The other pan holds <Qty u="500 g" />, <Qty u="200 g" />, <Qty u="200 g" /> and <Qty u="100 g" />, which make <Qty u="1000 g" /> together. We write g for <Term id="gram">gram</Term>. The level beam shows that <Qty u="1 kg" /> = <Qty u="1000 g" />. Grams are for lighter things; kilograms are for heavier things.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>find the mass of Farah's flour</Beat>. Put weights on the other pan until the beam is level, then add them up: 500 + 200 + 50 = 750. The bag balances all three weights together, so it has a mass of <Qty u="750 g" />.

Thinking that the bigger thing must be heavier is a mistake: the small stone made its pan go down. Choosing the pan that has gone up as the heavier one is also a mistake. The heavier side always goes down.

<Lang zh>

上一步用米说东西有多长。这一步要比的是东西有多重，也就是它们的<Term id="mass">质量</Term>。

<Figure src="figures/measuring-ball-and-stone" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of an old two-pan balance on a wooden base: a large smooth ball sits in the left pan, which has risen, and a small stone sits in the right pan, which has gone down.", zh: "一架老式双盘天平的铅笔画，底座是木头的：左盘里放着一个光滑的大球，这一边翘了起来；右盘里放着一块小石头，这一边沉了下去。" }} caption={{ en: "The big ball's pan rises and the small stone's pan goes down: bigger is not always heavier. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "大球那边翘起来，小石头那边沉下去：大的不一定重。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

先看<Beat n={1}>天平两边的沙滩球和石头</Beat>。沙滩球大得多，可是放石头的那个盘更低。<Term id="balance">天平</Term>上，重的一边往下沉，轻的一边往上翘。所以石头更重。大的不一定重。

再<Beat n={2}>用砝码和黄梨比一比</Beat>。两个盘一样高，说明两边一样重。砝码是 <Qty u="1 kg" />，所以这个黄梨的质量也是 <Qty u="1 kg" />。kg 是<Term id="kilogram">千克</Term>的写法。不知道一样东西有多重，就拿知道重量的砝码去和它比，比到一样重为止。

接着<Beat n={3}>拿大砝码和几个小砝码比</Beat>。另一边放的是 <Qty u="500 g" />、<Qty u="200 g" />、<Qty u="200 g" /> 和 <Qty u="100 g" />，合起来是 <Qty u="1000 g" />。g 是<Term id="gram">克</Term>的写法。横梁平了，说明 <Qty u="1 kg" /> = <Qty u="1000 g" />。轻一些的东西用克，重一些的东西用千克。

最后，<Beat n={4}>称一称 Farah 的面粉</Beat>。在另一个盘里放砝码，放到横梁平了为止，再把砝码加起来：500 + 200 + 50 = 750。这袋面粉和三个砝码合起来一样重，所以它的质量是 <Qty u="750 g" />。

这一步要小心两个错误。一是以为个子大的东西一定重，可刚才那块小石头就让它那边的盘沉了下去。二是把翘起来的那一边当成重的。记住：天平上，重的一边总是往下沉。

准备好了，就<Task id="mass-and-balances">做一道小题</Task>：比轻重，或者用砝码称出东西有多重。
