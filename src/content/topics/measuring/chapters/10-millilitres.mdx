<Lang en>

You have compared measurements by keeping track of their units. Now go back to liquids and use a smaller unit, for amounts less than a whole <Term id="litre">litre</Term>.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the beaker filled to its top mark</Beat>. The number there is 1000. Small amounts of liquid are measured in <Term id="millilitre">millilitres</Term>, written ml. Filled to that mark, this beaker holds <Qty u="1000 ml" />: exactly <Qty u="1 l" />. So <Qty u="1 l" /> = <Qty u="1000 ml" />, two names for the same amount.

Now <Beat n={2}>look between 300 and 400</Beat> on the <Term id="measuring-cup">measuring cup</Term>. Its <Term id="scale">scale</Term> counts in hundreds, with one short mark between, making two equal parts. 100 ÷ 2 = 50, so each <Term id="small-part">small part</Term> stands for <Qty u="50 ml" />, not <Qty u="1 ml" />.

Next, <Beat n={3}>read Daniel's sugar cane juice</Beat>. Look from the side, with your eyes level with the juice. It reaches the mark just above 300: one small part more, so 300 + 50 = 350. Daniel has <Qty u="350 ml" />.

Then <Beat n={4}>look at another cup's marks</Beat>. Here four short marks split the space from 200 to 300 into five equal parts. Count the parts, not the marks: 100 ÷ 5 = 20, so each small part stands for <Qty u="20 ml" />. The water is three parts above 200: 220, 240, 260. It measures <Qty u="260 ml" />.

Finally, <Beat n={5}>measure water for a recipe</Beat>. This jug is numbered every <Qty u="250 ml" />, and each small part stands for <Qty u="50 ml" />. To measure <Qty u="750 ml" />, pour until the water is level with the mark 750, with your eyes at the same height as the water.

Taking every small part as <Qty u="1 ml" /> is a mistake, because cups are marked in different ways. Each time, work out the small part from two numbered marks and the parts between them.

上一步比较量的时候，数和单位要一起看。这一步回到液体，学一个更小的单位，用来量不到 1 <Term id="litre">升</Term>的液体。

先看<Beat n={1}>装到最上面那条线的量杯</Beat>。那条线旁边写着 1000。少量的液体用<Term id="millilitre">毫升</Term>量，毫升写作 ml。这个量杯装到那条线是 <Qty u="1000 ml" />，正好 <Qty u="1 l" />。所以 <Qty u="1 l" /> = <Qty u="1000 ml" />，同样多的水，两种说法。

再看这个<Term id="measuring-cup">量杯</Term>上<Beat n={2}>300 到 400 这一段</Beat>。它的<Term id="scale">刻度</Term>一次跳 100，中间只有一条短线，分出 2 格。100 ÷ 2 = 50，所以<Term id="small-part">每一小格</Term>表示 <Qty u="50 ml" />，不是 <Qty u="1 ml" />。

接着<Beat n={3}>读 Daniel 的甘蔗水</Beat>。从侧面看，眼睛和水面一样高。甘蔗水升到 300 上面那条线，多了一格，300 + 50 = 350。Daniel 有 <Qty u="350 ml" /> 甘蔗水。

然后<Beat n={4}>看另一个量杯的刻度</Beat>。这个量杯在 200 和 300 之间有 4 条短线，分出 5 格。数格子，不数刻度线：100 ÷ 5 = 20，每一小格表示 <Qty u="20 ml" />。水在 200 上面 3 格：220、240、260，一共 <Qty u="260 ml" />。

最后，<Beat n={5}>做菜要量水</Beat>。这个量杯每 <Qty u="250 ml" /> 标一个数，每一小格表示 <Qty u="50 ml" />。要量 <Qty u="750 ml" /> 水，就倒到水面和 750 那条线一样高，眼睛也放在和水面一样的高度。

这一步常见的错误，是把每一小格都当成 <Qty u="1 ml" />。可是不同的量杯，刻度画法不一样。每拿到一个量杯，都要先用两个相邻的标数和中间的格数，算出每一小格表示多少。
