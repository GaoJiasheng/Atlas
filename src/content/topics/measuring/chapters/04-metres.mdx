You can now find what each <Term id="small-part">small part</Term> on a measuring tape stands for. The same skill works with a bigger unit, for longer things.

Look along <Beat n={1}>the metre rule</Beat> from 0 to 100. Its <Term id="length">length</Term> is <Qty u="100 cm" />: one <Term id="metre">metre</Term>, written m. So <Qty u="1 m" /> = <Qty u="100 cm" />, two names for the same length. Laying your <Qty u="15 cm" /> ruler along it six times covers <Qty u="90 cm" />, and <Qty u="10 cm" /> is left over.

Next, <Beat n={2}>measure Hui Min's whiteboard</Beat>. This tape is marked in metres. The board starts at 0 and its other end reaches 3. Here each small part stands for <Qty u="1 m" />, so the whiteboard is <Qty u="3 m" /> long.

Now <Beat n={3}>look at the corridor on the long tape</Beat>. Its numbers go up in tens with four short marks between, so each small part stands for <Qty u="2 m" />. The corridor reaches 30: it is <Qty u="30 m" /> long. A pencil, by comparison, might be <Qty u="14 cm" />. <Term id="centimetre">Centimetres</Term> suit short things and metres suit long things. The number alone does not tell you the length; the unit says how big each count is.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>use the door to make a guess</Beat>. A classroom door is about <Qty u="2 m" /> tall. Something as tall as two doors is about <Qty u="4 m" />. A sensible guess like this, made before you measure, is an <Term id="estimate">estimate</Term>. Measure afterwards to check it.

Writing <Qty u="14 m" /> for a pencil is a mistake: a pencil that long would be longer than a bus. An estimate of <Qty u="40 m" /> for something two doors high is too big as well: that is about twenty doors stacked up.

先沿着<Beat n={1}>这把米尺</Beat>从 0 看到 100。它长 <Qty u="100 cm" />，也就是 1 <Term id="metre">米</Term>，米写作 m。所以 <Qty u="1 m" /> = <Qty u="100 cm" />：同一段<Term id="length">长度</Term>，两种说法。把你那把 <Qty u="15 cm" /> 的尺子沿着它接着放 6 次，一共 <Qty u="90 cm" />，还剩 <Qty u="10 cm" />。

再<Beat n={2}>量一量 Hui Min 班上的白板</Beat>。这条卷尺以米为单位，白板从 0 开始，另一端到 3。这里<Term id="small-part">每一小格</Term>表示 <Qty u="1 m" />，所以白板长 <Qty u="3 m" />。

接着看<Beat n={3}>长卷尺上的走廊</Beat>。这条卷尺一次跳 10，两个数中间有 4 条短线，分出 5 格，每一小格表示 <Qty u="2 m" />。走廊一直到 30，所以长 <Qty u="30 m" />。铅笔呢，大约是 <Qty u="14 cm" />。短的东西用<Term id="centimetre">厘米</Term>，长的东西用米。光有一个数还说不清有多长，单位告诉我们每“一个”有多大。

最后，<Beat n={4}>拿门来估一估</Beat>。教室的门大约 <Qty u="2 m" /> 高。有两扇门那么高的东西，大约就是 <Qty u="4 m" />。量之前先这样合理地猜一个大约的数，叫作<Term id="estimate">估计</Term>。估完再量，看看估得准不准。

把铅笔写成 <Qty u="14 m" /> 是个错误：那么长的铅笔，比一辆巴士还长。把两扇门高的东西估成 <Qty u="40 m" /> 也不对，那差不多是二十扇门叠起来的高度。
