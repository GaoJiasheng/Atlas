<Lang en>

The Cretaceous followed the Jurassic. The chart places its beginning at <Num s="S1">143.1</Num> million years ago and its end at <Num s="S1">66</Num> million years ago. Our maps at <Num s="S29">130</Num> and <Num s="S29">100</Num> million years ago both fall within this period. Compare the land and water: each map shows one age in a much longer period. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Source S1">S1</button></sup>

The large maps use the same reconstruction model and the same rule for drawing land. That helps us compare the changing geography. They are separate model results, with uncertain details, rather than photographs or exact measurements of ancient coastlines. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</button></sup>

Open the Thailand card in the small map. It describes Sirindhorna from the Early Cretaceous Khok Kruat Formation in Nakhon Ratchasima Province. The Laos card describes Early Cretaceous Ichthyovenator. The rock layers named in each study give clues to the fossils’ ages. The background map does not date them, and these regional cards do not give exact dig locations. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S20" aria-label="Source S20">S20</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S21" aria-label="Source S21">S21</button></sup>

Fossil dots keep the age limits given in their publications. A wide range may overlap several browsing windows. It describes what we know about the fossil’s age, not how long one animal lived. Its middle is not a known date of death. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S24" aria-label="Source S24">S24</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S65" aria-label="Source S65">S65</button></sup>
