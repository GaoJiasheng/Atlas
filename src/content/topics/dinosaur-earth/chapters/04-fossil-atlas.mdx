<Lang en>

A fossil dot opens a record. It does not count animals. The Paleobiology Database, or PBDB, groups fossil information from related places into a <Term id="term-collection">collection</Term>. A collection can contain several <Term id="term-occurrence">occurrences</Term>, each recording a particular kind of fossil. Our map draws one dot for each selected collection. Open it to read the separate records. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S24" aria-label="Source S24">S24</button></sup>

Each fossil has an <Term id="term-age-range">age range</Term>, with an older and a younger limit. Choose a time, and the map shows records whose ranges overlap the browsing window. It checks each record separately, keeping any gaps between their ranges. This window helps us browse; it is not a new measurement of age uncertainty. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S65" aria-label="Source S65">S65</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S67" aria-label="Source S67">S67</button></sup>

Our saved selection includes records from a limited global search and a regional search for Thailand, Laos and Malaysia. The global search was sorted by the width of each age range. Malaysian fossil locations are left out of the public map to protect them. The public selection has <Num s="S65,S66">1,274</Num> collections. This counts the selected records, rather than all dinosaurs, species or places where dinosaurs lived. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S65" aria-label="Source S65">S65</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S66" aria-label="Source S66">S66</button></sup>

Why do some areas have many dots and others none? Fossils must be buried, preserved, exposed and studied before they can enter a database. Our selection also leaves out some records. You cannot rank countries by dinosaur numbers from these dots, or draw the edges of a habitat around them. Open a dot to check its ages and sources. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S2" aria-label="Source S2">S2</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S24" aria-label="Source S24">S24</button></sup>

Collection 24178 has <Num s="S65">3</Num> occurrence records in this selection. Each has age limits of <Num s="S65">121.4</Num> to <Num s="S65">100.5</Num> Ma. The browsing window extends <Num s="S67">5</Num> Ma on either side of your chosen age. Atlas chose this width. A record appears if its range overlaps the window, even when they just meet at an endpoint.

</More>

化石点可以打开一份记录，不代表动物只数。古生物数据库 PBDB 把有地理联系的化石资料归为一个<Term id="term-collection">采集记录组</Term>。一组可以包含多条<Term id="term-occurrence">化石出现记录</Term>，每条记录某类化石。本图为每个选中的组画一个点，打开后便能逐条阅读。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S24" aria-label="来源 S24">S24</button></sup>

每件化石有一个<Term id="term-age-range">年龄范围</Term>，包括较老和较年轻的界限。选择年代后，小图显示范围与浏览窗相交的记录。每条记录分开检查，区间之间的空档会保留。浏览窗方便查阅，并不是新测出的年代误差。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S65" aria-label="来源 S65">S65</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S67" aria-label="来源 S67">S67</button></sup>

这里保存了有限的全球查询，以及泰国、寮国和马来西亚的区域查询。全球查询按年龄范围的宽度排序。为保护地点，公开地图不显示马来西亚化石位置。公开资料有 <Num s="S65,S66">1274</Num> 个采集记录组。这只是所选资料的规模，并不等于所有恐龙、物种或恐龙曾生活的地点数量。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S65" aria-label="来源 S65">S65</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S66" aria-label="来源 S66">S66</button></sup>

为什么有些地区点很多，有些没有？化石要先被埋藏、保存、露出地表，再被研究，才可能进入数据库。本题还只选取部分记录。因此，不能按点数给各国的恐龙数量排名，也不能沿着点群画出栖息地边界。打开一个点，先读它的年代和来源。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S2" aria-label="来源 S2">S2</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S24" aria-label="来源 S24">S24</button></sup>
