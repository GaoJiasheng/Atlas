<Lang en>

About 66 Ma, a great <Term id="term-extinction">extinction</Term> ended the Cretaceous. All <Term id="term-non-avian">non-avian dinosaurs</Term>—dinosaurs other than birds—died out. Some bird lineages survived. At this selected age, our large map still shows the 70 Ma sample because an exact 66 Ma map is not available. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S11" aria-label="Source S11">S11</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S8" aria-label="Source S8">S8</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</button></sup>

An <Term id="term-asteroid">asteroid</Term> hit Earth in what is now Mexico, leaving the <Term id="term-chicxulub">Chicxulub crater</Term>. The asteroid was about <Num s="S13">10</Num> kilometres across. The crater was about <Num s="S13">180</Num> to <Num s="S13">200</Num> kilometres across. The small map marks its approximate position today, not its outline or its position on ancient land. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S61" aria-label="Source S61">S61</button></sup>

How do scientists connect the impact to the extinction? They find a worldwide layer of ejecta: material thrown out by the impact. Its age and the pattern of changes in living things support the impact as the trigger. Together, these clues connect the impact with widespread damage. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S12" aria-label="Source S12">S12</button></sup>

Deccan volcanoes were also erupting around this time. Scientists study when they erupted and how released gases affected climate. Computer models help test possible effects on climate and dinosaur habitats. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S51" aria-label="Source S51">S51</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S13" aria-label="Source S13">S13</button></sup>

Chiarenza and colleagues modelled climate and habitats suitable for dinosaurs. In their model, strong cooling after the impact could remove suitable habitat worldwide. Their volcanic scenarios did not cause a similar global loss. These are calculations under the study’s conditions, not measured ancient temperatures. They cannot tell us what happened to every animal. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S13" aria-label="Source S13">S13</button></sup>

Sprain and colleagues also studied their timing, and warned that gas release may not follow the amount of rock erupted. Rock volume, eruption dates and gas release each need evidence. Different dating results cannot be joined into one exact burst of activity. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S15" aria-label="Source S15">S15</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S51" aria-label="Source S51">S51</button></sup>
