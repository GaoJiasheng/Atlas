<Lang en>

A date such as 250 <Term id="term-ma">Ma</Term> means 250 million years ago. Larger numbers take us further into the past; moving towards zero brings us towards today. The <Term id="term-mesozoic">Mesozoic</Term> includes the <Term id="term-triassic">Triassic</Term>, <Term id="term-jurassic">Jurassic</Term> and <Term id="term-cretaceous">Cretaceous</Term> periods. They have different boundaries. The first map sample sits within the Triassic, rather than marking the beginning of that period. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Open source S1">S1</button></sup>

The large map is a <Term id="term-palaeogeography">palaeogeographic reconstruction</Term>: an attempt to recover past land and sea using evidence and a model. Its shapes are derived from reconstructed elevations, not photographed from an ancient spacecraft. Read the selected age and the map sample age separately. A change in selected time may leave the same sample on screen because the sequence contains separate frames. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S29" aria-label="Open source S29">S29</button></sup>

The small map answers a different question: where were these finds recorded on today's Earth? A modern find location cannot simply be pasted onto ancient land. Singapore is a modern reference point here, not a marked dinosaur excavation. The regional cards also describe material from different times; opening them together does not make them a single ancient community. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S64" aria-label="Open source S64">S64</button></sup>

A <Term id="term-fossil">fossil</Term> preserves evidence of past life. A bone or tooth is body evidence; a footprint records activity. Preservation is incomplete, and the dots show selected published records. An empty patch does not establish that dinosaurs never lived there. Keep asking what each image records: reconstructed land, a modern location, or a particular piece of evidence. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S2" aria-label="Open source S2">S2</button></sup>

The selected age belongs to the story or browsing window. The sample age belongs to the actual reconstructed frame. At a selected age of 66 Ma, this sequence holds the older 70 Ma frame; it has no exact 66 Ma sample. The younger 65 Ma frame appears separately later. The map does not slide its coastline into an invented intermediate shape. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S29" aria-label="Open source S29">S29</button></sup>

A fossil can preserve a body part or evidence of activity. A footprint can show that an animal contacted a surface, while a tooth provides a different kind of anatomical evidence. Neither a generic footprint illustration nor an isolated body part supplies every detail of a complete animal. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S2" aria-label="Open source S2">S2</button></sup>
