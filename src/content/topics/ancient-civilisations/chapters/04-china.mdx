<Lang en>

The Yellow River takes its name from the yellow silt it carries, and it is often called the cradle of Chinese civilisation; the Yangtze flows further south. In the early second millennium BCE, Erlitou, in the Yellow River basin, was a large, neatly laid-out town with a walled palace area, and the earliest known bronze ritual vessels were cast there.

The Shang kings ruled from about 1600 to 1046 BCE. From about 1300 BCE their capital was Yin Xu, near today's Anyang. There they had records of divination carved on turtle shells and the shoulder blades of cattle. These <Term id="oracle-bones">oracle bones</Term> are the earliest known Chinese writing. The Shang kings also killed people as offerings to their ancestors, and sacrificial pits lie among the royal tombs at Yin Xu.

The Zhou took power in 1046 BCE. Over the following centuries their realm split into rival states; see <TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="eastern-zhou">The Spring and Autumn and Warring States</TopicLink>. In 221 BCE Qin <TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="qin-conquest">conquered the last of them</TopicLink>. The Qin made writing, weights and measures, and even the width of roads, the same everywhere, joined older walls into one long wall in the north and built a vast tomb for the First Emperor. The work and the wars were paid for with heavy taxes and the labour of very many people, as <TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="after-221">the chapter after 221 BCE</TopicLink> tells.

The Han dynasty ruled from 206 BCE to 220 CE. In this time the overland Silk Roads to Central Asia took shape. By tradition, about 105 CE the court official Cai Lun made paper from bark, hemp, rags and old fishing nets.

<More title={{"en": "The Xia: was there such a dynasty?", "zh": "夏：有没有这个朝代"}}>

Later Chinese histories name the Xia as the first dynasty, before the Shang. Since Erlitou was found in 1959, archaeologists in China have debated whether it belonged to the Xia or to the early Shang. The historian Sarah Allan notes that recent Chinese scholarship tends to accept the Xia as a real dynasty; she argues that the burden of proof lies with those who say it existed. Britannica calls the Xia "quasi-legendary".

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What the oracle bones record", "zh": "甲骨上记了什么"}}>

Since the end of the 19th century, about <Num s="S225">150,000</Num> inscribed shoulder blades and turtle shells have been found at Yin Xu. In 1899 the scholar Wang Yirong showed that their writing was the language of the Shang. They record divination, and they note eclipses of the sun and moon and positions of stars. They also name people offered in sacrifice.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Sacrifice by the Shang kings", "zh": "商王的祭祀"}}>

UNESCO describes the Royal Tombs area at Yin Xu as including sacrificial pits that contain chariots and human remains "considered to have been sacrificial victims". Among the large royal tombs are more than <Num s="S225">2,000</Num> smaller graves, most of them sacrificial pits, believed to hold people the Shang kings offered to their ancestors. In the royal cemetery at Xibeigang alone, excavators found more than <Num s="S226">1,000</Num> sacrificial pits, each with up to <Num s="S226">12</Num> skeletons.

The oracle bones mention at least <Num s="S226">14,197</Num> human victims. This is one scholar's count of mentions, and one event may be counted more than once. At least <Num s="S226">7,426</Num> of them are named as Qiang, a people living outside the Shang state.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Paper, printing and the compass: what the dates say", "zh": "纸、印刷和指南针：年代怎么说"}}>

Paper: tradition credits Cai Lun, about 105 CE. Printing: a printed scroll of the Diamond Sutra dated 868 CE is now in the British Library. Compass: Britannica says that in the 12th century sailors in China and in Europe found the compass's use for finding the way, apparently each on their own. More inventions that are still in use are gathered in chapter 08.

</More>

<Lang zh>

黄河因挟带黄色的泥沙而得名，常被称为“中华文明的摇篮”；长江在它的南方奔流。公元前 2 千纪早期，黄河流域的二里头已是一座布局整齐的大城，有围起来的宫殿区，已知最早的青铜礼器就铸在这里。

商约从公元前 1600 年延续到前 1046 年。约公元前 1300 年起，商的都城在今天安阳附近的殷墟。商王让人把占卜的记录刻在龟甲和牛肩胛骨上，这就是<Term id="oracle-bones">甲骨文</Term>，已知最早的汉字。商王也杀人祭祀祖先，殷墟的王陵之间分布着许多祭祀坑。

公元前 1046 年，周取代了商。此后几百年间，周的天下分裂成相互争战的诸侯国，这段历史见<TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="eastern-zhou">春秋战国</TopicLink>。公元前 221 年，秦<TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="qin-conquest">灭掉最后一个诸侯国</TopicLink>，统一了文字、度量衡，连道路的宽度也统一了；它把早先的城墙连成北方的长城，又为始皇帝修了巨大的陵墓。这些工程和战争，靠的是沉重的赋税和大批被征发的劳力，详见<TopicLink slug="warring-states" ch="after-221">“前 221 年以后”一章</TopicLink>。

汉朝从公元前 206 年延续到公元 220 年。这一时期，陆上丝绸之路逐渐形成，把中国和中亚连在一起。按传统说法，约公元 105 年，宫廷官员蔡伦用树皮、麻头、破布和旧渔网造出了纸。

<More title={{"en": "The Xia: was there such a dynasty?", "zh": "夏：有没有这个朝代"}}>

后世的中国史书把夏列为商以前的第一个朝代。1959 年发现二里头以来，中国考古学界一直在争论它属于夏还是早商。历史学家艾兰指出，近年中国学界趋于承认夏是真实存在过的朝代；她自己则认为，证明夏存在的责任在主张它存在的一方。《不列颠百科全书》称夏为“半传说中的”朝代。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What the oracle bones record", "zh": "甲骨上记了什么"}}>

19 世纪末以来，殷墟共出土约 <Num s="S225">15 万</Num>片刻字的牛肩胛骨和龟甲。1899 年，学者王懿荣认出上面的文字是商代的文字。甲骨上记的是占卜，也记下了日食、月食和星辰的位置，还写下了被用来祭祀的人。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Sacrifice by the Shang kings", "zh": "商王的祭祀"}}>

联合国教科文组织说，殷墟王陵区有一些祭祀坑，坑里有车和人骨，这些人“被认为是祭祀的牺牲”。在几座大王陵之间，有 <Num s="S225">2,000</Num> 多座小墓，大多是祭祀坑，被认为埋的是商王献给祖先的人。仅在西北冈王陵区，考古学者就发掘出 <Num s="S226">1,000</Num> 多个祭祀坑，每坑最多埋着 <Num s="S226">12</Num> 具人骨。

甲骨文里提到的人牲至少有 <Num s="S226">14,197</Num> 人。这是一位学者统计的提及次数，同一件事可能被算了不止一次。其中至少 <Num s="S226">7,426</Num> 人被写明是羌人，他们是生活在商国之外的人群。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Paper, printing and the compass: what the dates say", "zh": "纸、印刷和指南针：年代怎么说"}}>

纸：传统上归功于蔡伦，约公元 105 年。印刷：一卷标着公元 868 年的印本《金刚经》，今藏大英图书馆。指南针：《不列颠百科全书》说，12 世纪时，中国和欧洲的水手显然各自发现了它可以用来辨认方向。今天仍在用的更多发明，集中在第 08 章。

</More>
