<Lang en>

Greece and Rome grew up around the Mediterranean Sea rather than along one great river. Mountains and the sea divided Greece into <Num s="S300">several hundred</Num> small states, each a town with its own countryside, a citadel and a marketplace. Greece was not one country, and its story is told mostly through a few of these states, above all Athens and Sparta.

In Athens, reforms of about 508–507 BCE put power in an assembly of citizens, an early <Term id="democracy">democracy</Term>. Only free adult men whose parents were both citizens could take part. The Greeks had adapted the Phoenician alphabet and added letters for vowels. To the east lay the Persian Empire. Alexander of Macedon overthrew it, and when he died at Babylon in 323 BCE he ruled from Greece and Egypt to the edge of India.

Rome began as one city on the Tiber. By tradition it became a <Term id="republic">republic</Term> in 509 BCE: instead of a king, two consuls were elected each year. Its laws were put on public display about 449 BCE as the Twelve Tables, and in 312 BCE its first aqueduct brought water into the city. Roman armies conquered Italy, then Greece and much else around the sea. In 27 BCE Octavian took the name Augustus and became the first emperor. By 117 CE the empire reached from Britain to Egypt, and later historians called the long quiet from Augustus to 180 CE the Roman Peace. The conquered and the enslaved saw it differently.

In the west the empire lost land for decades; 476 CE, when its last emperor was deposed, is the year usually given for its end. The eastern half, ruled from Constantinople, lasted until 1453.

<More title={{"en": "Who counted as “the people”?", "zh": "谁算“人民”"}}>

In Athens a citizen had to have two citizen parents, according to Aristotle's account of the city's laws. In the 4th century BCE there were perhaps <Num s="S303,S304">30,000</Num> adult male citizens, a modern estimate. Living alongside them were their wives and children, about <Num s="S304">20,000</Num> foreigners who had settled in the city and, at most, <Num s="S304">150,000</Num> slaves, the upper estimate of the historian M. H. Hansen; an ancient figure of <Num s="S305">400,000</Num> slaves is now thought far too high. Slaves, mostly brought from abroad, worked on farms, in workshops and mines, and in houses.

Sparta's farmland was worked by helots, people held as serfs who belonged to the state. Thucydides tells how the Spartans picked out about <Num s="S307">2,000</Num> helots who had been promised freedom, and then did away with them.

Rome let masters free their slaves, and a freed slave became a citizen, which the historian M. J. Perry calls unique among the states of the time. Slaves in the silver mines of Spain, the writer Diodorus says, were given no rest and died in large numbers.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What the conquered said", "zh": "被征服者怎么说"}}>

The Roman historian Tacitus wrote a speech for a chief in northern Britain he calls Calgacus. Tacitus says only that the chief “is said to have” spoken, so the words are his own: “where they make a desert, they call it peace.”

Rome destroyed the Greek city of Corinth in 146 BCE. Pausanias, writing about <Num s="S320">300</Num> years later, says most people found in the city were killed and the women and children were sold as slaves. The historian Polybius, who was there, saw soldiers playing draughts on paintings thrown on the ground.

In 73–71 BCE an army of runaway slaves led by Spartacus fought Rome in Italy. When it was defeated, Appian says, about <Num s="S323,S324">6,000</Num> captured rebels were crucified along the road between Capua and Rome. Appian also writes that Spartacus sacrificed <Num s="S324">300</Num> Roman prisoners and crucified a Roman captive.

希腊和罗马都在地中海边成长起来，而不是沿着一条大河。山脉和大海把希腊分隔成<Num s="S300">好几百个</Num>小国，每国都是一座城加上周围的乡村，城里有卫城和市集。希腊并不是一个国家，它的历史多半靠雅典、斯巴达等少数城邦讲下来。

约公元前 508 至前 507 年，雅典的改革把权力交给公民大会，这是早期的<Term id="democracy">民主</Term>。只有父母都是公民的自由成年男子才能参加。希腊人借用了腓尼基字母，又加上表示元音的字母。东边是波斯帝国。马其顿的亚历山大推翻了它；公元前 323 年他在巴比伦去世时，统治的地方从希腊、埃及直到印度的边缘。

罗马起初只是台伯河边的一座城。按传统说法，罗马在公元前 509 年成为<Term id="republic">共和国</Term>：不再有国王，每年选出两名执政官。约公元前 449 年，罗马把法律刻成十二铜表公开展出；公元前 312 年，第一条水道通进城里。罗马军队先后征服意大利、希腊和地中海周围许多地方。公元前 27 年，屋大维获得“奥古斯都”的称号，成为第一位皇帝。到公元 117 年，帝国从不列颠延伸到埃及；后世史家把奥古斯都到公元 180 年的长期太平称为“罗马和平”。被征服的人和奴隶，看法却不一样。

西罗马帝国几十年间不断丢失土地，公元 476 年末代皇帝被废，通常以这一年为它的结束。以君士坦丁堡为都城的东半部，一直延续到 1453 年。

<More title={{"en": "Who counted as “the people”?", "zh": "谁算“人民”"}}>

按亚里士多德对雅典法律的记述，雅典公民必须父母双方都是公民。公元前 4 世纪，成年男性公民大约 <Num s="S303,S304">3 万</Num>人，这是现代学者的估计。和他们住在一起的，还有他们的妻子儿女、约 <Num s="S304">2 万</Num>在城里定居的外邦人，以及最多 <Num s="S304">15 万</Num>名奴隶（这是史家汉森估计的上限）；古书里“<Num s="S305">40 万</Num>奴隶”的数字，如今被认为高得不可信。奴隶多是从外地带来的，在农田、作坊、矿山和家里干活。

斯巴达的田地由希洛人耕种，他们是属于国家的农奴。修昔底德记载，斯巴达人挑出约 <Num s="S307">2,000</Num> 名曾被许诺给予自由的希洛人，随后把他们除掉了。

罗马允许主人释放奴隶，被释放的奴隶成为公民；史家佩里说，这在当时的国家里是独一无二的。狄奥多罗斯则写道，西班牙银矿里的奴隶得不到一刻休息，大批死去。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What the conquered said", "zh": "被征服者怎么说"}}>

罗马史家塔西佗替不列颠北部一位他称为卡尔加库斯的首领写了一篇演说。塔西佗只说这位首领“据说”发表过演说，所以这些话是塔西佗写的：“他们造出一片荒凉，却称之为和平。”

公元前 146 年，罗马摧毁了希腊城市科林斯。约 <Num s="S320">300</Num> 年后的保萨尼亚斯说，城里找到的人大多被杀，妇女和儿童被卖为奴隶。当时在场的史家波利比乌斯，看见士兵在扔在地上的画上下跳棋。

公元前 73 至前 71 年，斯巴达克斯率领一支逃亡奴隶组成的军队在意大利与罗马作战。阿庇安说，起义失败后，约 <Num s="S323,S324">6,000</Num> 名被俘的起义者沿着卡普阿与罗马之间的路被钉上十字架。阿庇安也写到，斯巴达克斯曾献祭 <Num s="S324">300</Num> 名罗马俘虏，并把一名罗马俘虏钉上十字架。
