<Lang en>

Sumer lay in southern Mesopotamia, the land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in what is now Iraq. Its towns grew up in a marshy delta, where river water could be led onto fields. The coast was not where it is today: between 5000 and 3000 BCE, UNESCO explains, the sea reached some <Num s="S106">200</Num> kilometres inland of the present shoreline. The map shows today's coast.

By about 3200 BCE the largest settlement in southern Mesopotamia was Uruk, which has been called "the first city in world history". The earliest known writing comes from cities like it. Large temple estates had to record what came in and went out, and the marks pressed into clay tablets for these accounts grew into <Term id="cuneiform">cuneiform</Term>.

Each Sumerian <Term id="city-state">city-state</Term> had its own ruler and temples, and the cities were rivals. About 2300 BCE the kings of Akkad, to the north, conquered them; their capital, Agade, has still not been found. About 2100 BCE Sumerian kings ruled again from Ur. King Ur-Nammu built the great <Term id="ziggurat">ziggurat</Term> there, and the oldest law code yet found carries his name. It already deals with slaves who ran away.

Counting in sixties very probably began with the Sumerians; chapter 08 follows where it went. After 2000 BCE, UNESCO explains, the sea drew back, the marshes dried and the great southern cities declined. Uruk, Ur and Eridu are now part of a <Term id="world-heritage-site">World Heritage Site</Term>, listed in 2016.

<More title={{"en": "Why did writing appear? Two explanations", "zh": "文字为什么出现：两种说法"}}>

The Metropolitan Museum of Art explains writing at Uruk by bookkeeping: large temple estates needed to record what came in and went out, and the records were kept on clay tablets. The historian Joshua Mark of World History Encyclopedia gives trade as the main reason.

The Met also notes that states using early cuneiform on clay and wood may have existed in what are now Syria and Turkey by the middle of the fourth millennium BCE. Uruk may be one place where writing developed, not the only one.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Who did the work? And who invented the wheel?", "zh": "谁在干活？轮子是谁发明的？"}}>

Texts from about 2130 to 2000 BCE, the time of Gudea of Lagash and of the kings of Ur, record slaves. Some were captives brought back by royal troops, such as the people from Elam brought to Lagash under Gudea; some were foreigners bought by the palace; some were people of the city who fell into slavery through debt or crime. Scholars generally agree that slaves were a small part of the workforce. Many other workers depended on temples and palaces, and the texts do not always show who was free.

The wheel is often said to be Sumerian. A picture-sign from Uruk of about 3500 BCE shows a sledge on wheels, but a pot from Bronocice in Poland with a wagon on it is about <Num s="S116">200</Num> years older, and the archaeologist David Anthony notes that the oldest radiocarbon-dated wooden wheels are European. Where the wheel was first invented is not known.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why the dates do not agree", "zh": "年代为什么对不上"}}>

For early Mesopotamia, scholars use more than one scheme of dates. In the one followed by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Akkadian period runs from about 2350 to 2150 BCE and King Sargon reigned from about 2340 to 2285 BCE. Another scheme places the same events several decades later. This topic writes "about 2300 BCE" for the Akkadian conquest and "about 2100 BCE" for the kings of Ur. The coloured areas on the map follow the years of the Cliopatria dataset, which may differ again.

</More>

<Lang zh>

苏美尔位于美索不达米亚南部，也就是底格里斯河和幼发拉底河之间的土地，在今天的伊拉克。城镇建在多沼泽的三角洲上，河水可以引到田里。当时的海岸也不在今天的位置：据联合国教科文组织，公元前 5000 年到前 3000 年间，海水一度深入今天海岸线以内约 <Num s="S106">200</Num> 公里。地图画的是今天的海岸。

约公元前 3200 年，美索不达米亚南部最大的聚落是乌鲁克，它被称作“世界历史上第一座城市”。已知最早的文字就出自这样的城市。大神庙的田产要记下收进和支出，人们为记账在泥板上压出的符号，后来发展成<Term id="cuneiform">楔形文字</Term>。

每个苏美尔<Term id="city-state">城邦</Term>都有自己的统治者和神庙，各城之间互相竞争。约公元前 2300 年，北边阿卡德的国王征服了这些城市；阿卡德的都城阿加德至今还没有找到。约公元前 2100 年，苏美尔人的国王又在乌尔掌权。乌尔纳姆王在乌尔建起大<Term id="ziggurat">塔庙</Term>，迄今发现最古老的法典也以他命名，里面已经有关于逃亡奴隶的条文。

以 60 为单位计数，很可能始于苏美尔人（见第 08 章）。据联合国教科文组织，公元前 2000 年以后海水后退、沼泽干涸，南部的大城市随之衰落。乌鲁克、乌尔和埃利都今天属于一处<Term id="world-heritage-site">世界遗产</Term>，2016 年列入名录。

<More title={{"en": "Why did writing appear? Two explanations", "zh": "文字为什么出现：两种说法"}}>

美国大都会艺术博物馆用“记账”来解释乌鲁克的文字：大神庙的田产要记下收进和支出，记录写在泥板上。世界历史百科（World History Encyclopedia）的作者约书亚·马克则认为，贸易是主要的推动力。

大都会博物馆还指出，在今天的叙利亚和土耳其一带，早在公元前 4 千纪中叶，可能已经有在泥板和木板上使用早期楔形文字的国家。乌鲁克也许只是文字发展起来的地方之一，而不是唯一的地方。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Who did the work? And who invented the wheel?", "zh": "谁在干活？轮子是谁发明的？"}}>

约公元前 2130 年至前 2000 年，也就是拉格什的古地亚和乌尔诸王的时代，文献里记有奴隶。有的是王室军队带回的俘虏，例如古地亚时期从埃兰带到拉格什的人；有的是王宫从外地买来的外族人；也有本城的居民，因欠债或犯罪沦为奴隶。学者一般认为，奴隶只占劳动力的一小部分。还有许多劳动者依附于神庙和王宫，文献并不总能说清谁是自由人。

常有人说轮子是苏美尔人发明的。乌鲁克一个约公元前 3500 年的图画符号，画的是装着轮子的雪橇；可是波兰布罗诺奇采出土的一只画着车的陶罐，比它早约 <Num s="S116">200</Num> 年，考古学家戴维·安东尼也指出，用碳十四测定年代的最古老木轮出自欧洲。轮子最早在哪里发明，至今没有定论。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why the dates do not agree", "zh": "年代为什么对不上"}}>

讲早期美索不达米亚时，学者用的年代体系不止一种。按美国大都会艺术博物馆采用的体系，阿卡德时期约为公元前 2350 年至前 2150 年，萨尔贡王约在公元前 2340 年至前 2285 年在位；另一种体系把同样的事排在晚几十年的位置。本主题把阿卡德的征服写作“约公元前 2300 年”，乌尔诸王写作“约公元前 2100 年”。地图上的色块按 Cliopatria 数据集的年份画，与这些年份也可能不同。

</More>
