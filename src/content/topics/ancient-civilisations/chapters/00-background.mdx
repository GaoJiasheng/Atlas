<Lang en>

Between about 3200 BCE and 1511 CE, people in many parts of Asia, Africa and Europe built cities, wrote things down and set up governments. Textbooks compare such societies using five features: a government, writing, cities with public buildings, the arts, crafts and sciences, and economic activities. A society with all of them is called a <Term id="civilisation">civilisation</Term>. In this topic the five features are a checklist for comparing societies, not a ladder for ranking the people in them.

The first chapters follow river valleys: the Tigris and Euphrates, the Nile, the Indus, and the Yellow River and Yangtze. There, yearly floods or monsoon rains watered farmland that could feed towns. The story then turns to Greece and Rome, which grew up around a sea rather than a great river, and to the ports and rice plains of Southeast Asia, ending at Melaka in 1511 CE. The last chapter looks at the <Term id="legacy">legacy</Term> of these civilisations: what is still in use today.

Dates are written BCE, before the Common Era, and CE, the Common Era. Dates before about 2000 BCE are approximate, and experts' dates for them can differ by decades. Where the sources give two dates, both are shown.

The map shows land and rivers as they are today. The coloured areas are the states this topic follows; their edges are approximate, so they are dashed. Blank land was not empty. Southeast Asian kingdoms appear as spheres of influence that could overlap, and a few civilisations appear only as points.

<More title={{"en": "The five features in one table", "zh": "五个特征：一张表"}}>

| Feature | Examples in this topic | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Kings of the Sumerian city-states; the citizens' assembly of Athens; Rome's elected consuls | 01, 05 |
| Writing | Cuneiform on clay; Egyptian hieroglyphs; Shang oracle bones; Old Malay inscriptions | 01, 02, 04, 06 |
| Cities with public buildings | The ziggurat of Ur; the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro; the temples of Angkor | 01, 03, 07 |
| The arts, crafts and sciences | The pyramids; Greek pottery; counting in sixties; the zero | 02, 05, 01, 08 |
| Economic activities | Farming on river floodplains; trade by sea with the monsoon winds | 01–04, 06 |

The same features can be found in many other societies that this topic has no room for. A society that lacked one of them, such as writing, was not for that reason less able or less human.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Who calls whom \"civilised\"", "zh": "谁说谁是“文明”"}}>

The early Greeks called all foreigners *barbaroi*, the Romans included, and the word soon took on a deeply negative meaning, according to Britannica. The Romans later used it for all peoples outside Greek and Roman rule. The English word "barbarian" comes from it.

Writers in China had similar words. The fourteenth-century travel record *Daoyi zhilue* by Wang Dayuan, which describes Temasek, has the character *yi* in its title, a word for foreign peoples; Singapore's National Library Board translates the title as "Description of the Barbarians of the Isles".

Words like these tell us who was writing and how they saw others, not what the people described were like. In this topic they appear only in quotation marks, with the speaker named.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "BCE and CE", "zh": "公元前与公元"}}>

This topic writes BCE, "before the Common Era", and CE, "Common Era", with the same year numbers as the older labels "BC" and "AD". That count of years goes back to a chronology that the monk Dionysius Exiguus prepared in 525 at the request of Pope John I, according to Britannica. It runs from 1 BCE straight to 1 CE: as the United States Naval Observatory puts it, "there is no year 0".

Years before about 2000 BCE are not known exactly. For Mesopotamia, experts use more than one scheme of dates, and the same king can be placed decades apart. Chapter 01 explains which dates this topic uses.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How this map is drawn", "zh": "这张地图是怎么画的"}}>

The coloured areas come from Cliopatria, an open dataset of dated outlines of past states made by the Seshat Global History Databank. Its authors say the outlines were redrawn by hand from published atlases and show "only one version" of the territories of past states. This topic takes them year by year without changing them, cuts them to today's coastline and draws every edge as dashed.

Southeast Asian kingdoms are drawn differently: as spheres of influence that appear and disappear with Cliopatria's dates and may overlap, because their power faded with distance from the capital rather than stopping at a border.

The Indus cities, Funan, Sukhothai, Melaka and the Greek city-states are shown only as points. Chapters 03, 05, 06 and 07 explain why in each case.

Land, coast and rivers come from Natural Earth and are today's. The two dashed courses of the Yellow River are reconstructions of older channels. The sea routes are schematic: they join ports known from archaeology or texts, and are not the track of any ship.

</More>

约从公元前 3200 年到公元 1511 年，亚洲、非洲和欧洲许多地方的人建起城市、写下文字、设立政府。课本用五个特征来比较这样的社会：政府、文字、有公共建筑的城市、艺术工艺与科学、经济活动。五样都具备的社会，叫作<Term id="civilisation">文明</Term>。在这个主题里，五个特征只是一张用来比较的检查表，不是给人排高下的梯子。

前几章沿着大河走：底格里斯河与幼发拉底河、尼罗河、印度河，以及黄河与长江。那里每年的洪水或季风雨水浇灌农田，种出的粮食养得起城镇。接着，故事转到靠海而不靠大河的希腊和罗马，再到东南亚的港口与稻田，最后停在公元 1511 年的马六甲。最后一章看这些文明留下的<Term id="legacy">遗产</Term>，也就是今天还在用的东西。

年代写成“公元前”和“公元”。公元前 2000 年以前的年代都是约数，专家给出的年份可能相差几十年；来源给出两个年份时，两个都列出。

地图上的陆地和河流按今天的样子画。色块是本主题讲到的国家，范围只是大致的，所以边界画成虚线；空白的地方并不是没有人。东南亚的王国画成可以互相重叠的势力范围，还有几个文明只画成点。

<More title={{"en": "Who calls whom \"civilised\"", "zh": "谁说谁是“文明”"}}>

据《不列颠百科全书》，早期希腊人把所有外族人都叫作 barbaroi，连罗马人也在内；这个词很快就带上了强烈的贬义。后来罗马人用它称呼希腊、罗马势力范围以外的所有民族。英文的 barbarian（“野蛮人”）就是从这里来的。

中国的作者也有类似的字眼。14 世纪汪大渊的游记《岛夷志略》记载了单马锡，书名里的“夷”就是对外族的称呼；新加坡国家图书馆管理局把书名英译为 Description of the Barbarians of the Isles。

这类词说明的是写书的人是谁、怎样看待别人，而不是被描写的人是什么样子。在本主题里，这类词只出现在引号里，并写明是谁说的。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "BCE and CE", "zh": "公元前与公元"}}>

本主题英文写 BCE（“公元前”）和 CE（“公元”），年份数字与旧的 BC、AD 写法相同。据《不列颠百科全书》，这套纪年来自修士狄奥尼修斯·埃克西古斯在 525 年应教宗约翰一世之请编出的年表。它从公元前 1 年直接跳到公元 1 年：用美国海军天文台的话说，“没有公元 0 年”。

公元前 2000 年以前的年份没法精确知道。讲美索不达米亚时，专家用的年代体系不止一种，同一位国王可能被排在相差几十年的位置上。第 01 章说明本主题采用哪一种。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How this map is drawn", "zh": "这张地图是怎么画的"}}>

色块来自 Cliopatria：塞沙特全球历史数据库（Seshat Global History Databank）制作的一套开放数据，记录历代国家按年份的范围。它的作者说明，这些轮廓是照已出版的历史地图手工重描的，只代表历代国家疆域的“一种版本”。本主题按年份原样取用，只把它们裁到今天的海岸线，边界一律画成虚线。

东南亚的王国画法不同：画成势力范围，按 Cliopatria 的年份出现和消失，彼此可以重叠，因为这些王国的权力是离都城越远越弱，而不是在一条国界上截然停下。

印度河城市、扶南、素可泰、马六甲和希腊城邦只画成点，原因分别在第 03、05、06、07 章说明。

陆地、海岸和河流取自 Natural Earth，都是今天的样子。黄河两条虚线河道，是对古河道的复原。海上航线是示意：它们连接考古或文献里有记载的港口，不是哪一艘船的航迹。

</More>
