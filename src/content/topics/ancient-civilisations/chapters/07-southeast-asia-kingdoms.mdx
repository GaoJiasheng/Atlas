<Lang en>

On the plains around the Tonlé Sap lake, Khmer kings built a state on rice and water. In 802 Jayavarman II began to rule as a “universal king”. His successors built reservoirs and temples at Angkor, overlooking a rice-growing plain. Angkor Wat was built in the 12th century under Suryavarman II. A 2021 study estimates that the Greater Angkor region held <Num s="S415">700,000 to 900,000</Num> people at its 13th-century peak. Khmer and Cham kings took each other's capitals. Thai chronicles say Ayutthaya attacked Angkor in 1431, the date usually given for the empire's end, but people went on living there.

In the mid-13th century, traditionally in 1238, a local Tai ruler rose against Khmer rule and founded Sukhothai. King Ramkhamhaeng spread its influence, and his inscription of 1292 is the earliest known in the Thai language. In 1438 Sukhothai was absorbed into Ayutthaya.

In 1292 the Mongol emperor sent a fleet to punish the king of East Java. A prince of the old royal house used it to defeat his rival, then drove it out; his kingdom, Majapahit, began about 1293. A court poem of 1365 lists more than <Num s="S424">80</Num> places as its territory and shows the king urging his people to keep up the rice fields.

About 1400 a ruler who had left Singapura founded Melaka. From 1405 it sent missions to Ming China, and it became the leading trading port of Southeast Asia, until Portuguese forces took it in 1511.

<More title={{"en": "Why the map shows spheres, not borders", "zh": "为什么画成势力范围"}}>

These kingdoms did not have borders like countries today. A ruler's power was strongest near the court and faded with distance, and neighbours' claims could overlap. This topic therefore draws Srivijaya, the Khmer Empire and Majapahit as <Term id="mandala">spheres of influence</Term>, from the dated outlines of the Cliopatria dataset, and lets them overlap. Melaka is a point: the dataset's outline for its sultanate covers the southern tip of the peninsula and nearby islands but not the city of Melaka itself, so it is not drawn.

The written claims are often a court's view. The Majapahit poem of 1365 praises the king; historians still disagree whether Majapahit ruled today's Indonesia and part of Malaysia, or only eastern Java and Bali. Ramkhamhaeng probably did not rule all the lands linked with his name: local rulers recognised him as overlord. In the late 1980s the historians Michael Vickery and Piriya Krairiksh questioned whether his inscription was genuine, and Thailand's own nomination of it to UNESCO says the question exists. UNESCO calls Sukhothai “the capital of the first Kingdom of Siam”. The map shows Sukhothai as a point.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Angkor and Champa: each took the other's capital", "zh": "吴哥与占婆：两边都打赢过"}}>

Champa, on the coast of today's central Viet Nam, was the Khmer kingdom's eastern neighbour. In 1145 (or 1144–45) a Khmer army took the Cham capital, Vijaya, and occupied it for <Num s="S418">four</Num> years. In 1177 a Cham fleet sailed up the Mekong into the Tonlé Sap and took and destroyed the Khmer capital at Angkor. Jayavarman VII drove the Cham out and was crowned in 1181. No figures for the dead are known for either attack, and neither side can be called the winner of the long struggle.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Temasek", "zh": "淡马锡"}}>

Temasek is not one of the six kingdoms of these chapters, but it lay at the centre of their world. In 1349 the Chinese traveller Wang Dayuan described a place he called Danmaxi, and the Javanese poem of 1365 names Tumasik. Gold ornaments, coins and porcelain have been dug up on Fort Canning Hill.

The Malay Annals tell of two Javanese attacks on Singapura. The first was beaten off. In the second, a treasurer opened the gate of the fort; the city fell, and its last king, Iskandar Shah, fled and later founded Melaka. Portuguese writers, among them Tomé Pires, say instead that Parameswara, a prince who had fled Palembang, killed the ruler of Singapura. The two accounts cannot be reconciled. The legend of Sang Nila Utama and the rest of the story are told in <TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="background">Singapore, 1819–1965</TopicLink>.

</More>

<Lang zh>

在洞里萨湖周围的平原上，高棉国王靠稻米和水建立了国家。802 年，阇耶跋摩二世开始以“转轮王”的身份统治。后来的国王在吴哥修建水库和寺庙，俯瞰着一片稻作平原。吴哥窟建于 12 世纪苏利耶跋摩二世在位时。一项 2021 年的研究估计，大吴哥地区在 13 世纪鼎盛时约有 <Num s="S415">70 万至 90 万</Num>人。高棉和占婆曾互相攻取对方的都城。泰国编年史说，阿瑜陀耶在 1431 年攻击吴哥，人们通常以这一年为帝国的终结，但这一带后来仍有人居住。

13 世纪中叶，按传统说法在 1238 年，一位本地泰人统治者起兵反抗高棉统治，建立了素可泰。兰甘亨王扩大了它的影响，他 1292 年的碑文是已知最早的泰文碑文。1438 年，素可泰并入阿瑜陀耶。

1292 年，蒙古皇帝派舰队远征东爪哇的国王。旧王室的一位王子借它打败对手，随后又把它赶走；他建立的满者伯夷约始于 1293 年。1365 年的一首宫廷长诗把 <Num s="S424">80</Num> 多个地方列为它的属地，还写到国王叮嘱臣民照料好稻田。

约 1400 年，一位离开新加坡拉的统治者建立了马六甲。从 1405 年起，马六甲向明朝派遣使团，后来成为东南亚首屈一指的贸易港，直到 1511 年被葡萄牙军队攻占。

<More title={{"en": "Why the map shows spheres, not borders", "zh": "为什么画成势力范围"}}>

这些王国没有今天国家那样的国界。统治者的权力在王都附近最强，离得越远越弱，邻国的范围也可能互相重叠。所以本主题依据 Cliopatria 数据集里带年份的轮廓，把室利佛逝、高棉帝国和满者伯夷画成可以重叠的<Term id="mandala">势力范围</Term>。马六甲只画成一个点：这个数据集给马六甲苏丹国画的轮廓，覆盖的是半岛南端和附近的岛屿，却不包括马六甲城本身，所以不画。

文字记载往往是宫廷的说法。1365 年的满者伯夷长诗是歌颂国王的；满者伯夷究竟统治了今天的印度尼西亚和马来西亚的一部分，还是只统治东爪哇和巴厘，史家至今意见不一。兰甘亨很可能没有直接统治与他名字相连的全部地方，而是各地统治者承认他是宗主。1980 年代末，史家 Michael Vickery 和 Piriya Krairiksh 质疑过他的碑文是否真迹，泰国向联合国教科文组织申报这块碑时也承认有这个疑问。联合国教科文组织称素可泰为“暹罗第一王国的都城”。地图上的素可泰只画成一个点。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Angkor and Champa: each took the other's capital", "zh": "吴哥与占婆：两边都打赢过"}}>

占婆在今天越南中部沿海，是高棉的东邻。1145 年（也有记作 1144—1145 年的），高棉军队攻取占婆都城毗阇耶，占领了 <Num s="S418">4</Num> 年。1177 年，占婆舰队沿湄公河而上，驶进洞里萨湖，攻取并毁坏了吴哥的高棉都城。后来阇耶跋摩七世把占人赶走，1181 年加冕。两次进攻都没有伤亡数字留下，这场漫长的争斗也说不上哪一方是赢家。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Temasek", "zh": "淡马锡"}}>

淡马锡不在这两章讲的六个王国之中，却正处在它们的世界中央。1349 年，中国旅行家汪大渊记下一个叫“单马锡”的地方；1365 年的爪哇长诗写到“淡马锡”。福康宁山上出土过金饰、钱币和瓷器。

《马来纪年》说，爪哇人两次进攻新加坡拉。第一次被击退。第二次，一位司库官打开城门，城池陷落，末代国王伊斯干达沙出逃，后来建立了马六甲。托梅·皮列士等葡萄牙作者却说，从巨港出逃的王子拜里米苏拉杀了新加坡拉的统治者。两种说法无法调和。山尼拉乌他玛的传说和这段故事的其余部分，在<TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="background">新加坡 1819–1965</TopicLink>里讲。

</More>
