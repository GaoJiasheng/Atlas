<Lang en>

The model's hind thigh has been made see-through. Inside is a large extensor muscle, which straightens the shin, and a much smaller flexor, which folds it. The measurements in this chapter come from the desert locust, *Schistocerca gregaria*, a close relative whose jump has been studied in detail. <FlyTo preset="hind-leg">Look into the hind leg</FlyTo>

The problem is speed. In the desert locust the extensor can pull with more than <Num s="S13">15 newtons</Num>, hundreds of times the animal's weight, but it needs about <Num s="S13">0.3 seconds</Num> to build that force. A jump is over far faster: the leg pushes against the ground for only <Num s="S13">25–30 thousandths of a second</Num>. So the muscle does not throw the body itself. It loads springs first, like the arms of a catapult.

The jump has four steps, shown as the four poses in this chapter. Fold: the flexor pulls the shin in tight against the thigh. Load: both muscles contract together for about <Num s="S14">0.3–0.6 seconds</Num>, and the extensor's pull bends two half-moon plates at each knee, the semilunar processes, and stretches its own tendon. The plates are hard cuticle combined with <Term id="resilin">resilin</Term>, a rubbery protein that springs back. Lock: the flexor's tendon is caught over a small bump inside the knee, which gives the small flexor enough leverage to hold the shin folded while the extensor pulls. Release: the flexor relaxes, the catch slips, and the stored energy straightens both hind legs at once.

The desert locust leaves the ground at about <Num s="S13">3.2 m/s</Num>, using about <Num s="S13">9 mJ</Num> of energy for a male and <Num s="S13">11 mJ</Num> for a female. The model shows the release slowed down, so that it can be followed.

<More title={{ en: "Measuring a jump", zh: "测量一次起跳" }}>

In 1975 H. C. Bennet-Clark measured the desert locust's jump piece by piece. The half-moon plates of each knee store about <Num s="S13">4 mJ</Num> and the extensor tendon about <Num s="S13">3 mJ</Num>, about <Num s="S13">14 mJ</Num> in both legs, a little more than a jump uses. The muscle's own peak power is about <Num s="S13">36 milliwatts</Num>, but the jump needs about <Num s="S13">0.75 watts</Num>, roughly <Num s="S13">20 times</Num> more, delivered in a burst. At take-off the acceleration peaks at about <Num s="S13">180 m/s²</Num>, about <Num s="S13">18 times</Num> the acceleration of a falling stone.

Later studies differ in the details: a 2025 study found adults of about <Num s="S19">1.8 g</Num> taking off at up to about <Num s="S19">2.5 m/s</Num>, and found the stored energy split about half and half between plates and tendon, rather than <Num s="S13">4 to 3</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How far?", zh: "能跳多远" }}>

A rough estimate: something launched at <Num s="S13">3.2 m/s</Num> at the best angle, with no air resistance, would travel about <Num s="S35">1.0 m</Num> (3.2 × 3.2 ÷ 9.8). That matches the author of the knee-lock study, who gives up to about <Num s="S15">a metre</Num> for a long jump by a desert locust and about <Num s="S15">25 cm</Num> for its height. Air resistance takes only a small share of a locust's jump energy; a flea, which is far smaller, loses much more.

Nymphs jump too, from the first stage on. They take off at about <Num s="S18">0.9–1.2 m/s</Num> and cover <Num s="S18">20–30 cm</Num> on average. Their springs are rebuilt at every moult.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why a person cannot jump like a grasshopper", zh: "人为什么跳不出蚱蜢那样的成绩" }}>

It is often said that a grasshopper jumps many times its own length, so a person "scaled up" would leap over a building. Size does not work like that. The energy an animal can store grows with its size, but so does the mass it has to throw, so animals that jump with a catapult reach about the same take-off speed whatever their size. Equal take-off speeds give similar jump heights: a locust reaches about <Num s="S21">0.35 m</Num>. A small animal looks spectacular only because its body is short.

The same idea, a spring loaded slowly by muscle and released by a catch, is used by fleas and by froghoppers. A froghopper only a few millimetres long jumps up to <Num s="S23">700 mm</Num> high, taking off in less than a thousandth of a second.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "After take-off", zh: "起跳以后" }}>

An adult desert locust takes off at about the slowest speed at which it can fly, about <Num s="S18">2.5 m/s</Num>, so a jump can carry it straight into flight. Nymphs have no working wings and fall back to the ground.

On screen the release pose takes <Num s="S35">0.15 seconds</Num>, about five times slower than the real push, so that the movement can be seen.

</More>

模型把后足的股节变成了透明的。里面有一块很大的伸肌，负责伸直小腿；还有一块小得多的屈肌，负责收起小腿。本章的数据来自沙漠蝗 *Schistocerca gregaria*：它是黑角瓦兰蝗的近亲，跳跃已经被研究得很细致。<FlyTo preset="hind-leg">看看后足里面</FlyTo>

难处在于速度。沙漠蝗的伸肌能产生超过 <Num s="S13">15 牛</Num>的拉力，是它体重的几百倍；但要把力建立起来，需要约 <Num s="S13">0.3 秒</Num>。而一次起跳快得多：后足蹬地只有<Num s="S13">千分之 25–30 秒</Num>。所以肌肉并不直接把身体抛出去，而是先给“弹簧”上弦，就像弹弓。

起跳分四步，就是本章的四个姿态。屈：屈肌把小腿收紧，贴住大腿。上弦：两块肌肉同时收缩约 <Num s="S14">0.3–0.6 秒</Num>，伸肌的拉力压弯膝部两侧的半月形骨板，即半月形突，也拉长伸肌自己的腱。半月形突由硬表皮和<Term id="resilin">节肢弹性蛋白</Term>组成，后者像橡胶一样能弹回原状。锁：屈肌腱卡在膝内的一个小突起上，这让小小的屈肌有足够的杠杆优势，在伸肌用力拉时仍把小腿收住。放：屈肌一放松，卡扣滑脱，储存的能量让两条后足同时伸直。

沙漠蝗离地时速度约 <Num s="S13">每秒 3.2 米</Num>；一次起跳，雄虫约用 <Num s="S13">9 mJ</Num> 的能量，雌虫约 <Num s="S13">11 mJ</Num>。模型把“放”这一步放慢了，好让人看清。

<More title={{ en: "Measuring a jump", zh: "测量一次起跳" }}>

1975 年，Bennet-Clark 把沙漠蝗的起跳一部分一部分地测量清楚。每个膝的半月形突约储存 <Num s="S13">4 mJ</Num>，伸肌腱约 <Num s="S13">3 mJ</Num>，两条腿合计约 <Num s="S13">14 mJ</Num>，比一次起跳所需稍多。肌肉自身的最大功率约 <Num s="S13">36 毫瓦</Num>，而起跳需要约 <Num s="S13">0.75 瓦</Num>，大约多 <Num s="S13">20 倍</Num>，而且要在一瞬间放出。起跳时加速度最高约 <Num s="S13">180 m/s²</Num>，约为石头自由下落加速度的 <Num s="S13">18 倍</Num>。

后来的研究在细节上有出入：2025 年的一项研究测得约 <Num s="S19">1.8 克</Num>重的成虫，起跳速度最高约 <Num s="S19">每秒 2.5 米</Num>；储存的能量在半月形突和肌腱之间大约各占一半，而不是 <Num s="S13">4 比 3</Num>。

<More title={{ en: "How far?", zh: "能跳多远" }}>

粗略估算一下：以 <Num s="S13">每秒 3.2 米</Num>的速度、按最佳角度抛出，不计空气阻力，能飞出约 <Num s="S35">1.0 米</Num>（3.2 × 3.2 ÷ 9.8）。这与膝部锁扣研究的作者给出的数字相符：沙漠蝗跳远可达约 <Num s="S15">1 米</Num>，跳高约 <Num s="S15">25 厘米</Num>。空气阻力只消耗蝗虫起跳能量的一小部分；小得多的跳蚤损失就大得多。

若虫一孵出就会跳。它们起跳速度约 <Num s="S18">每秒 0.9–1.2 米</Num>，平均跳出 <Num s="S18">20–30 厘米</Num>。每蜕一次皮，它们的“弹簧”都要重建。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why a person cannot jump like a grasshopper", zh: "人为什么跳不出蚱蜢那样的成绩" }}>

常听人说，蚱蜢能跳出体长的许多倍，人要是按比例放大，就能跳过一栋楼。体型并不是这样起作用的。动物能储存的能量随体型增大，要抛出去的质量也同样增大，所以用“弹弓”起跳的动物，不论大小，起跳速度都差不多。起跳速度相近，跳的高度也就相近：蝗虫能跳到约 <Num s="S21">0.35 米</Num>高。小动物显得厉害，只是因为它的身体短。

同样的办法：肌肉慢慢上弦、卡扣一松就释放，跳蚤和沫蝉也在用。一只只有几毫米长的沫蝉，能跳到 <Num s="S23">700 毫米</Num>高，起跳用时不到千分之一秒。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "After take-off", zh: "起跳以后" }}>

沙漠蝗成虫的起跳速度约 <Num s="S18">每秒 2.5 米</Num>，约等于它能飞起来的最低速度，所以一跳就能接着飞起来。若虫没有能用的翅，只能落回地面。

画面里“放”这个姿态用了 <Num s="S35">0.15 秒</Num>，比真实的蹬地慢了约五倍，好让人看清动作。
