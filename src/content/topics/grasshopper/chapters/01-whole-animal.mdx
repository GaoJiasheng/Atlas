This is an adult female Javanese grasshopper, *Valanga nigricornis*, rebuilt from published descriptions and drawn <Num s="S35">40 times</Num> life size. A real female is about <Num s="S1">64 mm</Num> long; the ruler on the ground and the scale bar in the ARCHITECTURE panel give real sizes. <FlyTo preset="side">See it from the side</FlyTo>

Like every insect, its body has three parts. The head carries the eyes, the feelers and the mouth. The thorax in the middle carries all <Num s="S7">six</Num> legs and all <Num s="S7">four</Num> wings. The abdomen behind holds most of the gut, the eggs and the muscles that pump air.

There are no bones inside. The whole body is covered by a hard <Term id="exoskeleton">exoskeleton</Term>, a skeleton on the outside. It is made of stiff plates joined by soft membrane, so the body can bend, and the muscles pull on it from within. Inside are the organs of six body systems, each with its own colour in the model. The LAYERS control switches them on and off one at a time, like the pages of an anatomy atlas. <FlyTo preset="systems">Look inside</FlyTo>

This species is the largest grasshopper in Singapore, <Num s="S6">39–70 mm</Num> long, and is common across the main island and the offshore islands. The specimen that now defines the species, its neotype, came from Singapore. Its young, the <Term id="nymph">nymphs</Term>, and the adults feed on the leaves of many plants, among them coconut, rubber and oil palm.

The female is bigger than the male and ends in short, hard prongs for digging egg holes. The model shows a female because her eggs lead into the life cycle in the last chapter.

<More title={{ en: "Where it sits in the tree of life", zh: "它在生命之树上的位置" }}>

Animals → arthropods (jointed legs and an exoskeleton) → insects (three body parts, six legs) → Orthoptera, the grasshoppers and crickets → Caelifera, the short-horned grasshoppers → Acrididae → the subfamily Cyrtacanthacridinae → *Valanga* → *Valanga nigricornis* (Burmeister, 1838). There are <Num s="S2">23</Num> recognised subspecies; the one in Singapore is the nominate form, *V. n. nigricornis*.

Crickets and katydids, the other main branch of the Orthoptera, have long thread-like antennae, ears on their front legs and make sound by rubbing one forewing on the other. Grasshoppers have short antennae and ears on the abdomen.

A locust is a grasshopper that can change its behaviour when crowded, from living alone to moving in groups, and can migrate over great distances in swarms. The desert locust is one; *Valanga* is usually called a grasshopper. In Chinese, 蚱蜢 and 蝗虫 are both used for grasshoppers; 蚱 on its own usually means the small groundhoppers, a different family.

<More title={{ en: "Seen in Singapore", zh: "在新加坡" }}>

Singapore is where the species was first described from, and it is still common here: on 10 October 2026 iNaturalist held <Num s="S37">2,676</Num> research-grade records from Singapore. Surveys have found its nymphs in East Coast Park, Pasir Ris Park and Sengkang Riverside Park. Gardeners often treat it as a pest, and in oil-palm and rubber plantations it can strip leaves.

It lives in a warm, wet climate: monthly mean temperatures stay between about <Num s="S31">27 and 29 °C</Num> and rain falls on about <Num s="S31">171</Num> days a year.

The best way to meet one is to watch it on a shrub in a park. In nature reserves it is illegal to take animals away; insects count as animals, and the fine can reach <Num s="S39">$50,000</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Do grasshoppers make sounds?", zh: "蚱蜢会发声吗" }}>

Many do. Most grasshoppers make sound by <Term id="stridulation">stridulation</Term>: they rub a ridge or a row of small pegs on the inside of the hind thigh against a vein of the forewing, which makes the wing vibrate. Different species have different songs, and males and females use them to find each other. They hear with eardrums on the abdomen (chapter 6).

No published description of sound from *Valanga nigricornis* was found for this topic, so the model makes no claim about whether it sings.

</More>

黑角瓦兰蝗是新加坡最大的蝗虫，体长 <Num s="S6">39–70 mm</Num>，在本岛和离岛都很常见。现在用来代表这个种的“新模标本”，就采自新加坡。它的幼体<Term id="nymph">若虫</Term>和成虫都吃多种植物的叶子，包括椰子、橡胶和油棕。

<More title={{ en: "Where it sits in the tree of life", zh: "它在生命之树上的位置" }}>

动物界 → 节肢动物门（分节的足、外骨骼）→ 昆虫纲（身体三部分、六条足）→ 直翅目（蝗虫和蟋蟀）→ 蝗亚目（触角短的蝗虫）→ 蝗科 → 刺胸蝗亚科 → 瓦兰蝗属 → 黑角瓦兰蝗 *Valanga nigricornis* (Burmeister, 1838)。这个种有 <Num s="S2">23</Num> 个亚种，新加坡的是指名亚种 *V. n. nigricornis*。

直翅目的另一大支是蟋蟀和螽斯：触角细长如丝，耳朵长在前足上，靠两片前翅互相摩擦来发出声音。蝗虫的触角短，耳朵在腹部。

“飞蝗”一类的蝗虫，密度高时会改变行为，从独居变成群居，成群长距离迁飞，沙漠蝗就是一种；黑角瓦兰蝗一般叫作蚱蜢。中文里“蚱蜢”和“蝗虫”都可以指这类昆虫；单说“蚱”，一般指另一个科的小型蚱科昆虫。

<More title={{ en: "Do grasshoppers make sounds?", zh: "蚱蜢会发声吗" }}>

很多会。多数蝗虫靠<Term id="stridulation">摩擦发声</Term>：用后足大腿内侧的一条脊或一排小钉，刮前翅上的一条翅脉，使翅振动发声。不同的种叫声不同，雌雄靠叫声互相寻找。它们用腹部的“耳朵”听声音（见第 6 章）。

本主题没有找到关于黑角瓦兰蝗发声的公开描述，所以不说它会不会“唱歌”。
