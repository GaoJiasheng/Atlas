<Lang en>

You have folded paper many times, and in the fractions lesson you folded a strip in half to make two equal parts. This lesson asks a different question about a fold: when one side turns over, does it land exactly on the other side?

<Figure src="figures/symmetry-folded-heart" alt={{ en: "A paper heart partly folded along its middle, with its right half lifted.", zh: "一个剪纸心形沿中线折起一部分，右半边微微掀起。" }} caption={{ en: "The heart folds along its middle so the two matching halves can lie on each other.", zh: "心形沿中线对折，两半相同的轮廓就能叠在一起。" }} />

Start with <Beat n={1}>a house shape</Beat> on <Term id="square-grid">square grid</Term> paper. A dashed line runs down its middle. Imagine folding the paper along that line, so that the left side turns over onto the right.

Now <Beat n={2}>watch the fold</Beat>. The left half turns over and lands on the right half. The two roof slopes meet, the walls meet, and nothing sticks out. The halves <Term id="match-exactly">match exactly</Term>. A figure like this is a <Term id="symmetric-figure">symmetric figure</Term>, and the fold line is its <Term id="line-of-symmetry">line of symmetry</Term>.

Not every fold works. Try <Beat n={3}>an L shape</Beat> folded along a line through its middle. The long bottom row lands on itself, but the upright part of the L lands where there is nothing. The stage hatches that part. Because something sticks out, this line is not a line of symmetry.

The inside counts too. Look at <Beat n={4}>a cross with one shaded square</Beat>. Fold it along the middle line: the outline of the cross lands exactly on itself. But the shaded square lands on a blank square on the other arm. The two halves look different, so the figure is not symmetric about this line. Every part has to match, not only the edge.

The mistake to watch for is looking only at the outline. A <Term id="fold">fold</Term> tests everything at once: corners, edges and any shading or pattern inside.

When you are ready, <Task id="fold-and-match">try one question</Task>: fold a figure in your head and decide whether its halves match.

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

你折过很多次纸。在分数那一课里，你把一张纸条对折，分成一样大的两份。这一课对“折”问的是另一个问题：一边翻过去以后，能不能正好落在另一边上？

<Figure src="figures/symmetry-folded-heart" alt={{ en: "A paper heart partly folded along its middle, with its right half lifted.", zh: "一个剪纸心形沿中线折起一部分，右半边微微掀起。" }} caption={{ en: "The heart folds along its middle so the two matching halves can lie on each other.", zh: "心形沿中线对折，两半相同的轮廓就能叠在一起。" }} />

先看<Beat n={1}>一个小屋图形</Beat>，画在<Term id="square-grid">方格</Term>纸上。一条虚线从它中间竖着穿过。想一想沿这条线把纸<Term id="fold">对折</Term>，让左边翻到右边。

再看<Beat n={2}>折过去的样子</Beat>。左半边翻过去，落在右半边上：两边的屋顶斜坡碰在一起，墙也碰在一起，没有一点露出来。两半<Term id="match-exactly">完全重合</Term>。这样的图形叫<Term id="symmetric-figure">对称图形</Term>，折痕那条线叫它的<Term id="line-of-symmetry">对称轴</Term>。

不是每次折都行。看<Beat n={3}>一个 L 形</Beat>，沿穿过它中间的一条线对折。底下长的一排折过来还落在自己身上，可是 L 竖起来的那一截，翻过去落在了空白的地方。舞台用斜线把这一截标出来。有露出来的部分，这条线就不是对称轴。

里面也要算。看<Beat n={4}>一个十字形</Beat>，左臂上有一个涂色的格。沿中线对折，十字的外形正好叠在一起；可是那个涂色格翻过去，落在另一条臂的空白格上。两半看上去不一样，所以对这条线来说，它不是对称图形。每一部分都要重合，不能只看边。

这一步最容易犯的错，是只看外形。对折一下，什么都检查到了：角、边，还有里面的涂色和花纹。

准备好了，就<Task id="fold-and-match">做一道小题</Task>：在脑子里把图形折一折，判断两半是否重合。

</Lang>
