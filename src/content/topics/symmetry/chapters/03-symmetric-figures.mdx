<Lang en>

You can now test a line by folding. In this step you look at many figures and ask whether each one has any line of symmetry at all, in any direction.

Look at <Beat n={1}>six figures</Beat>. The isosceles triangle, with two equal sides, folds onto itself along the upright line through its top corner. The <Term id="kite">kite</Term> folds along the line through its top and bottom corners, and the trapezium with equal slanting sides folds along its middle. The other three, a triangle with three different sides, a parallelogram and an L shape, have no fold that works.

Now <Beat n={2}>turn the kite</Beat>. Its line of symmetry turns with it and is slanted now. Nothing about the kite has changed, so it still folds onto itself, just along a slanted line. A line of symmetry does not have to stand straight up. When a figure lies on a slant, turn the page in your head before you decide.

Some figures have <Beat n={3}>more than one line of symmetry</Beat>. An equilateral triangle, with three equal sides, has 3: one through each corner. A square has 4, as you saw in step 02, and a rectangle has 2. A figure needs only one line of symmetry to be a symmetric figure.

Symmetry is everywhere <Beat n={4}>in things people make</Beat>: a kite, a paper cutting, a floor tile pattern. All of these are drawn here on square grid paper. Things in nature, like leaves and faces, are only nearly symmetric: fold a photo of a leaf and the halves never match exactly.

Two mistakes are common here. One is to look only for an upright line and miss a slanted one. The other is to say a figure on a slant is not symmetric just because it is tilted.

<More title={{ en: "What about a circle?", zh: "圆呢？" }}>

Fold a paper circle along any straight line through its centre and the two halves always match. There is no end to the lines you could choose, so a circle has endlessly many lines of symmetry.

</More>

<Lang zh>

你已经会用“折一折”来检验一条线了。这一步看很多图形，问每个图形有没有对称轴，方向不限。

看<Beat n={1}>这六个图形</Beat>。等腰三角形有两条边一样长，沿穿过顶角的竖线折，能和自己重合。<Term id="kite">风筝形</Term>沿穿过上下两个角的线能折得重合；两条斜边一样长的梯形，沿它的中线也能折得重合。另外三个，三条边都不一样长的三角形、平行四边形和 L 形，怎么折都不行。

再<Beat n={2}>把风筝形转一转</Beat>。它的对称轴跟着转，变成了斜的。风筝形本身一点没变，所以还是能折得重合，只不过是沿一条斜线折。对称轴不一定是竖的。图形斜放着的时候，先在脑子里把纸转一转，再下结论。

有的图形<Beat n={3}>不止一条对称轴</Beat>。等边三角形三条边一样长，有 3 条对称轴，每个角各有一条。正方形有 4 条，这在第 02 步看过；长方形有 2 条。只要有一条对称轴，就是对称图形。

<Beat n={4}>人做的东西</Beat>里到处是对称：风筝、剪纸窗花、地上花砖的图案。这里都画在方格纸上。大自然里的东西，比如树叶和人脸，只是差不多对称：把一片树叶的照片对折，两半从来不会完全重合。

这一步有两个常见的错。一个是只找竖的对称轴，漏掉斜的；另一个是看到图形斜放着，就说它不对称。

<More title={{ en: "What about a circle?", zh: "圆呢？" }}>

把一张圆形的纸沿任何一条穿过圆心的直线对折，两半总是重合的。这样的线怎么选都选不完，所以圆有无数条对称轴。

</More>
