<Lang en>

The eyepiece and objectives now explain <Term id="magnification">magnification</Term>: how much larger an image appears than the object. In a compound light microscope, total power is the eyepiece power multiplied by the objective power. This instrument model uses a <Num s="S20">10×</Num> eyepiece. Its lowest objective is <Num s="S20">4×</Num>, giving a simulated total of <Num s="S20">40×</Num>. These values describe this design, not every microscope.

Keep the onion specimen and selected target in place, then select the <Num s="S20">40×</Num> objective. Total power becomes <Num s="S20">400×</Num>. The target’s image becomes larger, while the circular field contains less of its surroundings. In this simulation the object-field diameter falls from <Num s="S20">4,500 micrometres</Num> to <Num s="S20">450 micrometres</Num>. The specimen itself has not grown.

Move the slide gently to the right. In a conventional compound view, the image moves left. The simulation reverses both horizontal and vertical directions. This is why centring a target at low power helps before increasing power: a target near the edge may fall outside the smaller field. Changing power does not move its nucleus to another position within the cell.

Finally, compare an enlarged but unfocused image with a clear one. Magnification and resolution are different: a larger blur does not automatically reveal close details. Use the Try it card to predict the field change, hold the other conditions steady, and record the resulting power and diameter.

<More title={{"en": "Field calculation in this model", "zh": "本模型的视野计算"}}>

The chosen field number is <Num s="S20">18 mm</Num>. Object-field diameter is field number divided by objective power. Thus <Num s="S20">18 ÷ 40 = 0.45 mm</Num>, or <Num s="S20">450 micrometres</Num>. This geometric result is a SIM value, not a measurement from a classroom slide.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Focus is a teaching model", "zh": "焦点是教学模型"}}>

The simulation assigns different focus tolerances to the objectives and changes the drawn blur. It does not calculate a calibrated optical resolution or provide a measured image-quality score. Illumination changes visibility without changing the geometric magnification.

</More>

现在从目镜与物镜解释<Term id="magnification">放大率</Term>，也就是图像相对物体放大多少。复式光学显微镜的总倍数，等于目镜倍数乘以物镜倍数。本模型的目镜为 <Num s="S20">10 倍</Num>，最低物镜为 <Num s="S20">4 倍</Num>，模拟总倍数就是 <Num s="S20">40 倍</Num>。这些数值属于这台设计模型。

保持洋葱标本和目标不动，换成 <Num s="S20">40 倍</Num>物镜，总倍数升到 <Num s="S20">400 倍</Num>。目标的图像变大，圆形视野里的周围范围却缩小。本模拟的物方视野直径从 <Num s="S20">4,500 微米</Num>降到 <Num s="S20">450 微米</Num>；细胞本身没有长大。

把玻片向右移，常规复式显微图像会向左移；上下方向也会相反。因此要先低倍居中，再提高倍数，否则边缘目标可能离开缩小的视野。图像位置改变，不表示核移到了细胞的另一处。

最后比较放大但模糊的图像与调清楚的图像。放大率和分辨细节的能力不同，较大的模糊影像不会自动增加细节。用“试一试”先预测视野怎样变，再保持其他条件相同，记录总倍数和直径。

移动玻片、改变倍数和调焦是不同操作。先说明正在改变哪个条件，再解释画面上的变化。保持同一个目标，才能把视野缩小与目标离开视野区分开来。

选定的视场数为 <Num s="S20">18 毫米</Num>。物方视野直径等于视场数除以物镜倍数，因此 <Num s="S20">18 ÷ 40 = 0.45 毫米</Num>，也就是 <Num s="S20">450 微米</Num>。这是模拟的几何结果，不是课堂玻片测量值。
