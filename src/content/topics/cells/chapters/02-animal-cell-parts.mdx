<Lang en>

The animal cell now fills the view. Follow its outside boundary before opening the interior. The <Term id="cell-membrane">cell membrane</Term> separates the cell’s contents from its surroundings and regulates the passage of substances into and out of the cell. It is a boundary with a function, not an open doorway or a rigid plant wall. The model draws its thickness large enough to select.

Inside the boundary is the <Term id="cytoplasm">cytoplasm</Term>, the region outside the <Term id="nucleus">nucleus</Term> where many chemical reactions take place. It contains more structures than this simplified model displays. The transparent appearance lets you locate the selected parts; it does not mean a real cell is an empty glass container. Notice that the cytoplasm occupies space within a volume, rather than lying on a flat page.

The nucleus contains genetic instructions and helps control cell activities. In this example it is displaced slightly from the centre. Its position is not a rule for every cell. These typical plant and animal examples share a membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus, although that description should not be extended to every possible kind of cell.

Rotate to the oblique view and find the same boundary and nucleus again. Their appearance changes with the viewing angle while their identity remains the same. A cell’s shape alone cannot tell us everything about its type. Use the relationship between parts and functions, together with evidence about the specimen, to make a comparison.

<More title={{"en": "Selection and observation", "zh": "点选不等于观察到"}}>

A cutaway can label a membrane and nucleus separately. Under a light microscope the contrast, preparation and focal plane determine which boundaries you can distinguish. A part being hard to see does not prove it is absent.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Simplified animal cell", "zh": "简化的动物细胞"}}>

Animal cells can contain small fluid-filled compartments and vesicles. They are omitted here, together with much other cell machinery, so that the shared structures remain readable. Their omission must not become the claim that animal cells lack those compartments.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

动物细胞现在占据主要画面。先沿外缘看一圈，再打开内部。<Term id="cell-membrane">细胞膜</Term>分隔细胞内外，并调节物质进出。认识它，既要找到位置，也要说明作用。模型把它画厚，方便点选；真实细胞膜并不是敞开的大门，也不是植物细胞外那种支持性的壁。

膜以内、核以外的区域叫<Term id="cytoplasm">细胞质</Term>，许多化学反应在这里进行。细胞质还包含本图省略的结构。半透明画法让我们看见选中的部位，不表示真实细胞是空玻璃盒。它有厚度，占据一定空间，不是纸面上一层平面的颜色。

<Term id="nucleus">细胞核</Term>保存遗传指令，并参与控制细胞活动。本例的核稍微偏离中央；这个位置不是所有细胞必须遵守的规则。这里的典型植物和动物例子，都有膜、质和核；比较时仍要保留“典型例子”的范围，不能无限推广。

切到斜视角，再找同样的膜和核。观察角度改变，看到的轮廓会变，结构本身没有换成另一样东西。只凭外形，不能充分判断细胞种类。应当结合标本来源、结构及其作用，再作比较。

这时先说清看到的结构，再说明结构有什么作用；转动模型，是从另一个方向检查同一个对象。

<More title={{"en": "Selection and observation", "zh": "点选不等于观察到"}}>

剖开模型能分别标出膜和核。光学显微镜下能否辨清边界，受到对比度、制片方法和焦平面的影响。一个部位看不清，不足以证明它不存在。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Simplified animal cell", "zh": "简化的动物细胞"}}>

动物细胞也可以有较小的含液腔室和囊泡。模型省略这些结构及许多其他细胞结构，集中说明共同部位；不能把“这里没有画”说成“动物细胞绝对没有这类腔室”。

</More>
