<Lang en>

The leaf and the separate enlarged models introduce a change of scale. A <Term id="cell">cell</Term> is a basic unit of life. Some living things consist of a single cell; plants and animals contain many. We cannot usually see their individual cells with our eyes alone. An enlarged model helps us connect a structure with its function, but its clear outlines and open interior are drawing choices, not what an observer necessarily sees.

Cells work together in <Term id="tissue">tissues</Term>. An <Term id="organ">organ</Term>, such as a leaf, contains tissues that contribute to its function. Looking at a whole leaf and looking at one of its cells therefore answer different questions. Neither view replaces the other. The plant and animal models let us compare selected structures without pretending that every cell has the same shape or contents.

The 2023 MOE syllabus states: “Recognise that a cell is a basic unit of life.” This outcome appears on printed page <Num s="S1">43</Num>. Detailed cell structures and instrument practice in the following chapters extend that basic idea. They are enrichment requested for this topic, rather than a list of detailed Cell System outcomes in that syllabus.

Use the chapter rail to move between views. The body of each model has depth: rotate it as well as looking from the front. Later, compare these explanatory cutaways with a simulated observation field. The field keeps the limits of observation visible instead of filling in everything shown by the model.

<More title={{"en": "Models have a scale", "zh": "模型的尺度"}}>

The cells are enlarged separately by <Num s="S20">2,000 times</Num>. Wall and membrane thicknesses are exaggerated again for visibility. The instrument and cells therefore do not share a single scale. These are design choices, not measured specimens.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Large organism, large cell", "zh": "大生物与大细胞"}}>

The size of a whole organism does not tell us the size of an individual cell. A body contains many cells. Comparing whole-body outlines is not evidence that every cell in the larger organism must be larger.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

叶的整体与另外放大的细胞模型，展示了观察尺度的改变。<Term id="cell">细胞</Term>是生命的基本单位。有的生物由一个细胞组成，植物和动物则包含许多细胞。多数细胞很小，单靠眼睛不容易辨认。清楚的轮廓和打开的内部，帮助我们把结构与作用联系起来；这样的模型不是直接观察到的影像。

细胞共同构成<Term id="tissue">组织</Term>，不同组织组成<Term id="organ">器官</Term>。叶就是器官。看一片叶，可以认识它在整株植物中的作用；看叶的一个细胞，则能解释更小尺度的结构。两个层次要连起来，不能彼此代替。这里选用植物和动物细胞作为例子，并不表示所有细胞的形状、内容都一样。

2023 年 MOE 科学课纲在印刷页 <Num s="S1">43</Num>，要求认识细胞是生命的基本单位。后文介绍细胞部位和显微镜操作，是围绕这个核心概念展开的深化内容；不能把详细的“细胞系统”清单说成该课纲的明确要求。
