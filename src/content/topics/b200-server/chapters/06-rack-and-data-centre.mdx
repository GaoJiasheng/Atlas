A server like this lives in a rack in a data centre. NVIDIA's reference design puts <Num s="S17">two</Num> DGX B200 servers in each rack, one above the other, and expects each rack to need more than <Num s="S17">25 kW</Num>. <FlyTo preset="in-rack">Step back to the rack</FlyTo>

Training a large model takes far more than one server. In the same design, <Num s="S17">32</Num> servers, <Num s="S17">256</Num> GPUs, form one building block, and the blocks are joined by a separate network in which every server's first GPU is cabled to one group of switches, every second GPU to another, and so on. The design describes clusters of up to <Num s="S17">2,048</Num> servers with <Num s="S17">16,384</Num> GPUs. For a sense of scale, Meta reported that training its Llama 3.1 model with <Num s="S22">405 billion</Num> parameters took about <Num s="S22">30.84 million</Num> GPU-hours on an earlier GPU, the H100, using up to <Num s="S23">16,000</Num> of them. Once trained, answering questions with the model, called <Term id="inference">inference</Term>, takes far less computing per answer.

Another design packs more GPUs closer together. NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 joins <Num s="S18">72</Num> GPUs in one rack into a single NVLink group, at <Num s="S18">130 TB/s</Num> in all. A rack like that gives off about <Num s="S19">120 kW</Num> of heat, too much for air, so it is cooled by liquid flowing through plates on the chips.

All of this uses a lot of electricity. The International Energy Agency estimates that data centres used about <Num s="S21">415 TWh</Num> in 2024, about <Num s="S21">1.5 %</Num> of the world's electricity, and that this may roughly double to about <Num s="S21">945 TWh</Num> by 2030.

<More title={{ en: "What \"training\" means at this scale", zh: "这种规模的\"训练\"是什么意思" }}>

The Llama 3.1 figures are for H100 GPUs, the generation before the B200; there is no published figure for a model trained on DGX B200 servers. If <Num s="S23">16,000</Num> GPUs had run without stopping, <Num s="S22">30.84 million</Num> GPU-hours would take about <Num s="S22,S23">1,930</Num> hours, roughly <Num s="S22,S23">80</Num> days; real schedules differ, and the paper reports <Num s="S23">419</Num> unexpected interruptions in <Num s="S23">54</Num> days of training. Meta estimated the emissions from the electricity used at about <Num s="S22">8,930</Num> tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, counted from the electricity grids where the training ran.

<More title={{ en: "NVL72 in numbers", zh: "NVL72 的数字" }}>

A GB200 NVL72 rack holds <Num s="S18">72</Num> Blackwell GPUs and <Num s="S18">36</Num> Grace CPUs on <Num s="S20">18</Num> compute trays, with <Num s="S20">9</Num> NVLink switch trays between them, and <Num s="S18">13.4 TB</Num> of HBM3e memory in all. Because all 72 GPUs share one NVLink group, a model can be spread over far more GPUs before it has to use the slower network between racks. NVIDIA says liquid cooling lets it pack this much computing into one rack and use less water than some cooling systems; that is the maker's claim.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "One server's electricity", zh: "一台服务器的用电" }}>

At the simulated full-load point of chapter 5, <Num s="S1">12.5 kW</Num>, one server uses <Num s="S1">12.5 × 24 = 300 kWh</Num> in a day. If it ran at its maximum, <Num s="S3">14.3 kW</Num>, for a whole year, it would use about <Num s="S1">125 MWh</Num>. On top of that the data centre spends electricity on cooling the room, on power conversion and on lighting. The ratio of a data centre's total electricity to the electricity its computers use is called <Term id="pue">PUE</Term>; the lower it is, the less is spent on everything else.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Energy and Singapore", zh: "能源与新加坡" }}>

Singapore hosts many data centres on very little land. In 2019 they used about <Num s="S36">5.3 %</Num> of the country's electricity, and about <Num s="S36">7 %</Num> in 2020, according to a government reply in Parliament based on seven months of 2020 data; no newer official share has been published. In 2024 IMDA's Green Data Centre Roadmap set out to add at least <Num s="S24">300 MW</Num> of new data-centre capacity in the near term, favouring efficient designs and green energy, and noted that running a data centre <Num s="S24">1 °C</Num> warmer can save <Num s="S24">2 to 5 %</Num> of its energy. For comparison, an average four-room HDB flat used about <Num s="S28">381 kWh</Num> a month in 2024, so the simulated server's <Num s="S1">300 kWh</Num> a day is close to a month of such a flat.

这样的服务器放在数据中心的机柜里。NVIDIA 的参考设计每个机柜放 <Num s="S17">2</Num> 台 DGX B200，上下叠放，并估计每个机柜要用电 <Num s="S17">25 kW</Num> 以上。<FlyTo preset="in-rack">退后看整个机柜</FlyTo>

训练一个大模型，一台服务器远远不够。在同一个设计里，<Num s="S17">32</Num> 台服务器、<Num s="S17">256</Num> 块 GPU 组成一个单元，单元之间用一套单独的网络相连：每台服务器的 1 号 GPU 都接到同一组交换机，2 号 GPU 接到另一组，依此类推。这个设计描述的集群最多有 <Num s="S17">2,048</Num> 台服务器、<Num s="S17">16,384</Num> 块 GPU。举个规模的例子：Meta 报告说，训练它有 <Num s="S22">4050 亿</Num>个参数的 Llama 3.1 模型，用了上一代 H100 GPU 约 <Num s="S22">3084 万</Num> GPU 小时，最多同时用了 <Num s="S23">16,000</Num> 块。模型训练好以后，用它回答问题叫<Term id="inference">推理</Term>，每次回答需要的计算要少得多。

另一种做法把更多 GPU 挤得更近。NVIDIA 的 GB200 NVL72 把一个机柜里的 <Num s="S18">72</Num> 块 GPU 用 NVLink 连成一个整体，合计每秒 <Num s="S18">130 TB</Num>。这样一个机柜要散掉约 <Num s="S19">120 kW</Num> 的热，空气带不走，所以改用液体：冷却液流过贴在芯片上的冷板。

这一切都很费电。国际能源署估计，2024 年全世界数据中心用电约 <Num s="S21">415 太瓦时</Num>，约占全球用电的 <Num s="S21">1.5 %</Num>；到 2030 年可能大约翻一番，达到约 <Num s="S21">945 太瓦时</Num>。
