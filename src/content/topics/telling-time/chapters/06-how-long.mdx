<Lang en>

You already know that one <Term id="hour">hour</Term> is 60 <Term id="minute">minutes</Term>, and how to change hours and minutes into minutes only. Now you can use that knowledge to find how long an activity lasts.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the two concert marks</Beat> on the <Term id="timeline">timeline</Term>. The <Term id="starting-finishing-time">starting and finishing times</Term> are <Time t="09:40" /> and <Time t="11:15" />. You need the time between them, called the <Term id="duration">duration</Term>. The line shows the whole concert as a length that you can split into smaller parts.

First, follow <Beat n={2}>the jump to the next full hour</Beat>, from <Time t="09:40" /> to <Time t="10:00" />. There are 40 minutes past the hour already, so 60 − 40 = 20 minutes remain. This first jump is <Dur d="20m" />; it takes you to an hour mark.

Next, follow <Beat n={3}>the whole hour jump</Beat> from <Time t="10:00" /> to <Time t="11:00" />. The gap between these marks is <Dur d="1h" />. A mistake is to count the two marks as two hours. Count the gap you travel across: there is only one hour between them.

Now look at <Beat n={4}>the last short jump</Beat>, from <Time t="11:00" /> to <Time t="11:15" />. It adds <Dur d="15m" />. Together, the jumps cover the concert from start to finish, with no gaps or overlaps. Add them: <Dur d="20m" /> + <Dur d="1h" /> + <Dur d="15m" /> = <Dur d="1h35m" />. The two short jumps make 35 minutes, alongside the whole hour.

Finally, compare the jumps with <Beat n={5}>the subtraction under the line</Beat>. A common mistake is to subtract clock times like ordinary numbers and read the result, 1.75, as hours and minutes. That treats an hour as 100 minutes, but each hour has only 60 minutes, so use the jumps to keep track.

When you are ready, <Task id="how-long">try one question</Task> on finding a duration by jumping forwards from start to finish.

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

知道了 <Dur d="1h" />有 <Dur d="60m" />，就可以算一件事用了多长时间。这一步用一条<Term id="timeline">时间线</Term>，跳着数。

学校音乐会从上午 9 点 40 分开始，到上午 11 点 15 分结束。<Beat n={1}>时间线</Beat>把这两个时刻放在同一条线上：左边的标记是开始，右边的标记是结束。我们要求的是两个标记之间有多长，也就是<Term id="duration">经过的时间</Term>。

第一跳，<Beat n={2}>先跳到下一个整点</Beat>。从 9 点 40 分到 10 点，是 <Dur d="20m" />。先跳整点，后面的路就好走了。

第二跳，<Beat n={3}>跳整小时</Beat>。从 10 点到 11 点，是 <Dur d="1h" />。注意，要数的是整点之间跳了几段，而不是线上有几个整点。10 点和 11 点是两个整点，中间只有一段，是 <Dur d="1h" />，不是 <Dur d="2h" />。

最后一跳，<Beat n={4}>跳剩下的分钟</Beat>：从 11 点到 11 点 15 分，是 <Dur d="15m" />。把三跳加起来：<Dur d="20m" /> + <Dur d="1h" /> + <Dur d="15m" /> = <Dur d="1h35m" />。

为什么不直接相减？<Beat n={5}>把 <Time t="11:15" as="face" /> − <Time t="9:40" as="face" /> 当成普通的数来减</Beat>，会得到 1.75。可是“<Dur d="1h" /> <Dur d="75m" />”这样的说法不对，分钟满 60 就要进一小时。普通的数满 100 才进位，时间满 60 就进位，所以不能照普通减法来算。

常见的错误有两个：一是像上面那样直接相减；二是数整点，把 9 点到 12 点算成 <Dur d="4h" />。跳着数，就能避开这两个坑。

准备好了，就<Task id="how-long">做一道小题</Task>：在时间线上跳一跳，算出经过了多长时间。

</Lang>
