<Lang en>

The water starts hotter than its surroundings. Its <Term id="cooling-curve">cooling curve</Term> slopes down as time passes. At first, the temperature difference is large and the curve falls steeply. Later, the difference shrinks and the curve becomes flatter. Compare the temperature drop over equal time intervals to see cooling slow down.

A temperature difference is the difference between two readings. In this model, a smaller difference from the surroundings means slower energy loss. The water approaches the surrounding temperature. It does not keep cooling towards zero or the freezing point simply because time passes.

In this simulation, the water starts at <Num s="S27">85 °C</Num> and the surroundings stay at <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num>. After <Num s="S27">300 s</Num>, the bare cup reaches about <Num s="S27">54.4 °C</Num>. With the same start and time, the wrapped cup reaches about <Num s="S27">75.2 °C</Num>. The wrap slows energy loss, leaving the water warmer.

Compare early and late sections of one curve. If you change the starting temperature or cover, make a new comparison and keep water mass and surroundings the same. This curve flattens as the temperature difference shrinks. A change of state can also give a flat section, but for a different reason. No change of state occurs here. Explore evaporation and condensation in <TopicLink slug="water" ch="the-water-cycle">the water cycle</TopicLink>.

<More title={{"en":"Approaching and rounding","zh":"靠近与取整"}}>

The calculation brings the water ever closer to the surrounding temperature. Rounding may make the readings look equal while a small difference remains. Matching displayed digits alone do not show that heat transfer has stopped.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Real cooling has more influences","zh":"真实冷却还有其他因素"}}>

This model uses Newton’s cooling rule: the water has one uniform temperature and the surroundings stay at a fixed temperature. Real cooling also depends on energy absorbed by the container, evaporation and air movement. The transfer settings were chosen for teaching comparisons.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

水从比环境更高的温度开始。<Term id="cooling-curve">冷却曲线</Term>随时间向下。起初温差大，曲线下降较陡；后来温差缩小，曲线便较平缓。比较相同长短的时间内降温多少，就能看出冷却减慢。

温差就是两个温度读数的差。在这个模型中，与环境温差越小，水失去能量越慢。水温逐渐接近环境温度，并不会因为时间继续经过，就一直降到零度或冰点。

在这个模拟中，水从 <Num s="S27">85 °C</Num> 开始，环境维持 <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num>。经过 <Num s="S27">300 s</Num>，裸杯水温约为 <Num s="S27">54.4 °C</Num>。相同初温和时间下，包覆杯约为 <Num s="S27">75.2 °C</Num>。包覆层减慢能量损失，使水保持较暖。

先比较同一曲线的前后段。改变初温或包覆条件时，要另作比较，并保持水量和环境相同。这里曲线变平，是因为温差缩小。物态变化也可能出现水平段，但原因不同；这里没有物态变化。到<TopicLink slug="water" ch="the-water-cycle">“水循环”</TopicLink>继续了解蒸发与凝结。

<More title={{"en":"Approaching and rounding","zh":"靠近与取整"}}>

计算出的水温越来越接近环境温度。取整后，两个读数可能看起来相同，实际仍有微小差距。只看显示的数字相同，不能判断传热已经停止。

</More>

<More title={{"en":"Real cooling has more influences","zh":"真实冷却还有其他因素"}}>

模型采用牛顿冷却规律，假设水温处处均匀，环境温度固定。真实冷却还受容器吸热、蒸发和空气流动影响。这里的传热参数是为教学比较选定的。

</More>
