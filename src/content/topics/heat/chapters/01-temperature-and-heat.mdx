<Lang en>

Look at the metal and plastic spoon tips. They start at the same model room temperature. Advancing time while the bath stays at that temperature leaves both readings unchanged. Metal can still feel cooler than plastic under ordinary room conditions: it transfers energy away from warmer skin more readily. A cold feeling alone does not establish a lower temperature.

<Term id="heat">Heat</Term> is energy transferred because of a <Term id="temperature-difference">temperature difference</Term>. Temperature describes a thermal condition; heat describes a transfer. A thermometer gives a temperature reading. It does not count the energy stored inside an object. Measuring and feeling therefore answer different questions.

Now turn to the heated water. At this simulated operating point, the heater supplies <Num s="S27">12,000 J</Num> over <Num s="S27">120 s</Num>. Starting with <Num s="S27">200 g</Num> of water at <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num>, the calculated temperature becomes about <Num s="S27">39.3 °C</Num>. Joules describe the energy supplied; degrees Celsius describe temperature. The quantities have different units because they tell different things.

Keep the same energy input but double the water amount. The temperature rises less in this model. That comparison shows why a temperature reading alone cannot describe the whole energy change. Use the prediction card to compare the unchanged spoon readings, then reveal the evidence. The card records your observations without giving a score. Choose matching conditions when you make a comparison.

<More title={{"en":"Energy and amount of water","zh":"能量与水量"}}>

For the same substance undergoing the same temperature rise, a larger amount needs more energy. Total internal energy also depends on the substance and state. Heat is the name for energy in transfer, rather than a stock held inside an object.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The probe needs time","zh":"探头也需要时间"}}>

A real contact thermometer must settle to the temperature of what it measures. Its sensing end should sample the intended water. The model displays that water temperature immediately and does not calculate probe delay. <TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="mass-and-space">States of matter</TopicLink> develops the distinction between matter and its properties.

<Lang zh>

先看金属勺和塑料勺的远端。它们从相同的模拟室温开始，温浴也维持同温。推进时间，两个读数仍不变。不过，普通室温下金属可能比塑料摸起来冷，因为它更容易把能量从较暖的皮肤传走。手感较冷，不能单独证明温度较低。

<Term id="heat">热</Term>是因为<Term id="temperature-difference">温差</Term>而传递的能量。温度描述冷热状态，热描述传递过程。温度计给出温度读数，并不计算物体内部储存多少能量。因此，手感和测量回答的是不同问题。

再看加热杯。这个模拟运行点中，加热器在 <Num s="S27">120 s</Num> 内供能 <Num s="S27">12,000 J</Num>。<Num s="S27">200 g</Num> 水从 <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num> 开始，计算水温达到约 <Num s="S27">39.3 °C</Num>。焦耳表示供能，摄氏度表示温度；它们描述的量不同，单位也不同。

保持供能相同，把水量加倍，模拟水温上升得较少。只凭温度，不能说明完整的能量变化。用预测卡比较同温勺子的读数，先留下想法，再揭示证据。卡片不评分；比较时要保持相关条件相同。两个同温远端并不需要继续升温；经过时间本身不是能量来源。看读数时也要交代测的是哪一处，不能把水温和勺柄远端温度当成同一个位置的测量。

<More title={{"en":"Energy and amount of water","zh":"能量与水量"}}>

同一种物质升高相同温度，水量较多时需要较多能量。物体的总内能也和物质种类、状态有关。热是传递中的能量，不能把它当作储存在物体内的一份“热”。
