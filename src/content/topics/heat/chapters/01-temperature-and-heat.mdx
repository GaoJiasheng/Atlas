<Lang en>

The metal and plastic spoon tips start at the same room temperature. Keep the bath at that temperature and move the time control forward. Both readings stay the same. Yet metal can feel cooler than plastic because it carries energy away from warmer skin faster. Touch alone cannot tell you which object has a lower temperature.

<Term id="heat">Heat</Term> is energy transferred because of a <Term id="temperature-difference">temperature difference</Term>. Temperature tells you how hot or cold something is. Heat describes energy passing from one place to another. A thermometer measures temperature, rather than the energy stored in an object.

Now look at the heated water. In this simulation, the heater supplies <Num s="S27">12,000 J</Num> in <Num s="S27">120 s</Num>. It heats <Num s="S27">200 g</Num> of water from <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num> to about <Num s="S27">39.3 °C</Num>. Joules measure energy. Degrees Celsius measure temperature.

Supply the same energy to twice as much water. The temperature rises less. So temperature alone cannot tell you the whole energy change. Use the card to predict the spoon readings, reveal them and record your observations. The card gives no score. Compare the same locations under matching conditions; the water and the far end of a handle can have different temperatures. Time passing by itself supplies no energy.

<More title={{"en":"Energy and amount of water","zh":"能量与水量"}}>

A larger amount of the same substance needs more energy for the same temperature rise. The energy stored inside an object also depends on its substance and state. Heat means energy being transferred.

</More>

<More title={{"en":"The probe needs time","zh":"探头也需要时间"}}>

A real contact thermometer needs time to reach the temperature of the water around its sensing end. This model shows the water temperature immediately. <TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="mass-and-space">States of matter</TopicLink> explains more about matter and its properties.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

先看金属勺和塑料勺的远端。两者从相同室温开始，温浴也保持这个温度。推进时间，两个读数都不变。不过，金属摸起来可能较冷，因为它把能量从较暖的皮肤传走得更快。单凭手感，不能判断哪个物体温度较低。

<Term id="heat">热</Term>是因<Term id="temperature-difference">温差</Term>而传递的能量。温度告诉我们物体有多热或多冷，热则描述能量从一处传向另一处的过程。温度计测量温度，不测量物体内部储存的能量。

再看加热杯。在这个模拟中，加热器用 <Num s="S27">120 s</Num> 供能 <Num s="S27">12,000 J</Num>，把 <Num s="S27">200 g</Num> 水从 <Num s="S27">25 °C</Num> 加热到约 <Num s="S27">39.3 °C</Num>。焦耳是能量的单位，摄氏度是温度的单位。

把相同能量供给两倍的水，升温就较少。因此，只看温度不能知道完整的能量变化。用卡片先猜勺子的读数，再揭示结果并记录，卡片不评分。比较时要保持条件相同，也要测同一位置：水温可能与勺柄远端温度不同。时间经过本身不会供给能量。

<More title={{"en":"Energy and amount of water","zh":"能量与水量"}}>

同一种物质升高相同温度，分量较多时需要较多能量。物体内部储存的能量也与物质种类和状态有关。热指的是传递中的能量。

</More>
