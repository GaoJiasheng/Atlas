<Lang en>

Southeast Asia is a <Term id="term-region">region</Term>: a way of grouping places to study them together. The United Nations statistical grouping includes <Num s="S3">11</Num> countries, from Myanmar in the west to the Philippines farther east and Timor-Leste in the south. The country index gives the complete list. Begin with the world view, then move closer to see how mainland land, islands and surrounding seas fit together.

The country shapes come from Natural Earth, a published map dataset. They are a present-day cartographic snapshot, rather than a reconstruction of an earlier century. A large country occupies more of the map than a small country. Singapore's marker makes a small place easier to find without making its land larger. A country outline and a city point answer different questions: one shows an area, while the other helps locate a named place.

Watch what changes when the reading context changes. The ordinary geography view keeps the country shapes in place. The climate view shows monthly averages for 1991–2020. The <Term id="term-asean">ASEAN</Term> view changes membership shading according to admission dates, using the same present-day shapes throughout. A membership date therefore does not date the coastline beside it.

Some chapters move from the regional map to a local example. Selecting a park or terrace case highlights its country as a location reference. It does not draw that site's boundary or promise directions to it. Read the map, the case description and any separate drawing together: each supplies information at a different scale.

<More title={{"en": "The map has a source", "zh": "地图也有资料来源"}}>

Natural Earth makes its map data public domain. Small bends, islands and other details are generalised for map reading. That allows a useful overview, but a classroom regional map cannot replace a survey or a legal boundary document.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A region and an organisation", "zh": "区域与组织"}}>

The United Nations regional grouping and ASEAN membership are different kinds of list. One is used for geographical statistics; the other records states joining an organisation. The last chapter follows those admission dates. Water also connects places beyond country outlines: explore the <TopicLink slug="water">water cycle</TopicLink> alongside this map.

</More>

<Lang zh>

东南亚是一个<Term id="term-region">区域</Term>，把地方放在一起，方便比较和研究。联合国的统计分组列有 <Num s="S3">11</Num> 个国家，完整名单在本章索引中。先从世界地图找亚洲，再移近东南亚，观察大陆、岛屿和海洋怎样排列。国名名单帮助认地方，陆地轮廓则帮助理解它们的相对位置，两者要一起读。

这张图的国形取自 Natural Earth 制图资料，采用今天的地图快照。新加坡在区域图上很小，所以另有可点的标记；标记让人容易找到它，并没有放大国土。国家的轮廓表示一片面积，城市点表示具名地点，不能把两种符号当成同一回事。

阅读时还要留意资料的时间。普通地理图展示固定国形；气候图采用 1991–2020 年的月平均；<Term id="term-asean">亚细安</Term>（也称东盟）图按入会日期改变颜色，仍沿用同一组今天的国形。因此，入会日不是旁边海岸线的年代，气候月份也不是某一天的天气。

后面会从区域图转向公园、梯田等案例。选择案例时，地图只用所在国家帮助定位，不显示实地边界，也不能据此寻找入口或田块。把位置图、文字和另附的示意图合起来读，并分别辨认它们的尺度。

<More title={{"en": "The map has a source", "zh": "地图也有资料来源"}}>

Natural Earth 的地图资料属于公有领域。为了便于读图，细小弯曲、岛屿等细节经过概括。这样的图适合看全局，不能代替实地测量或法律上的边界文件。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A region and an organisation", "zh": "区域与组织"}}>

联合国的区域分组与亚细安成员名单性质不同：前者用于地理统计，后者记录国家加入组织。最后一章会按日期看入会过程。水的联系也不限于国形，可以配合<TopicLink slug="water">水循环</TopicLink>一起读。
