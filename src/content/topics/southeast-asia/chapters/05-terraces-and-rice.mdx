<Lang en>

Locate the Philippines, then move towards northern Luzon. UNESCO's listed Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras include Ifugao terrace clusters there. The country outline gives a regional reference, not the outline of a rice field. Turn to the separate section drawing to examine how a slope can hold a cultivated surface. Its stepped arrangement is a teaching diagram, rather than a survey of an Ifugao site.

A rice <Term id="term-terrace">terrace</Term> is a field retained along a slope. UNESCO describes nearly level pond fields following hillside contours, with retaining walls of stone or earth. In the drawing, compare the relatively flat field surfaces with the ground falling away beside them. The walls and retained surfaces are parts of the agricultural construction, rather than natural steps that simply happen to contain rice.

<Term id="term-irrigation">Irrigation</Term> supplies water to cultivated fields. FAO describes water collected from forested uplands and conveyed to Ifugao fields. Its account connects forests, farms, terraces, settlements and water infrastructure. Look at the drawing’s higher and lower fields together, while remembering that their connections are schematic. They do not give measured channels, water depths or flow rates for a real hillside.

The terraces are a living system of work and knowledge, not an empty pattern on a mountain. FAO's account places communities within that system. Reading only the field shape would leave out the people and management that make it agricultural land. The landscape's age is a separate question that needs evidence beyond its appearance.

<More title={{"en": "Different accounts of terrace age", "zh": "梯田年代：来源范围不同"}}>

UNESCO’s heritage description gives an age of <Num s="S9">2,000</Num> years. A UCLA report describes research placing the wet-rice terraces it studied at about <Num s="S11">400</Num> years old. The research account concerns particular evidence and studied terraces, not a new date for every field. These accounts should be compared with their scope stated, rather than averaged into a single age.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A section cannot stand in for a whole hillside", "zh": "剖面不能代替整座山坡"}}>

The retained levels and downhill links make a general mechanism easier to inspect. Their number and proportions are choices made for this drawing. FAO describes a wider managed landscape, including forested uplands. Follow the <TopicLink slug="water">water cycle</TopicLink> to consider how rain, ground and moving water connect beyond the edge of a field.

</More>

先在地图上找到菲律宾，再移到吕宋北部。联合国教科文组织列入世界遗产的菲律宾科迪勒拉山水稻梯田，包括这一带的伊富高梯田群。国形只提供区域参考，没有描出某块稻田。要理解斜坡怎样留住田面，可以另看剖面图，但图中的台阶是教学示意，并非实地测绘。

水稻<Term id="term-terrace">梯田</Term>是在坡地修成、受到挡护的田面。教科文组织介绍，接近水平的蓄水田沿山坡等高线分布，用石或土筑墙挡护。比较图上平坦的田面与旁边下落的坡地，就能看出田面和墙是农业建设的一部分，并不是天然台阶恰好长着稻。

<Term id="term-irrigation">灌溉</Term>把水送到耕作田地。粮农组织说明，伊富高的用水从有森林的高地收集，再引向农田；森林、农地、梯田、聚落和水利设施彼此联系。图中高处与低处要一起读，但连接方式经过简化，没有提供真实水道、蓄水深度或流量。

梯田也是社区持续劳动和运用知识的地方。只看山坡上的形状，会漏掉农业系统里的人和管理。地景的年代又是另一道问题，必须比较文字、考古等证据，不能凭外观判断。
