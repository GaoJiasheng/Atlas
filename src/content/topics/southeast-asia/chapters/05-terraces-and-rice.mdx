<Lang en>

Find the Philippines, then move towards northern Luzon. The Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, listed by UNESCO as World Heritage, include Ifugao terrace clusters there. The map locates the region. The separate drawing below shows how people can build a field on a slope.

A rice <Term id="term-terrace">terrace</Term> is a field held in place along a hillside. UNESCO describes nearly level pond fields that follow the hillside's contours, with stone or earth retaining walls. Compare the flat field surfaces with the sloping ground beside them. People build the walls to hold the fields in place.

<Term id="term-irrigation">Irrigation</Term> supplies water to crops. FAO describes water collected from forested uplands and brought to Ifugao fields. Forests, farms, terraces, settlements and water channels form a connected system. Follow the drawing's higher and lower fields to see the general arrangement.

The terraces also depend on community knowledge, work and management. FAO includes people in its account of this farming system. A hillside of rice fields is a place where people continue to live and work. Its appearance alone cannot tell us when the terraces were built.

<More title={{ en: "How old are the terraces?", zh: "梯田有多久的历史" }}>

UNESCO's heritage description gives an age of <Num s="S9">2,000</Num> years. A UCLA report describes research dating the wet-rice terraces it studied to about <Num s="S11">400</Num> years ago. That research concerns particular terraces and evidence. It does not date every field. The accounts cover different evidence and places, so their ages cannot be averaged into one answer.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Beyond the edge of a field", zh: "田块以外的联系" }}>

The drawing simplifies field levels and downhill links. Its number of levels, proportions, channels and water depths are not measurements of an Ifugao hillside. FAO describes a wider landscape that includes upland forests. Explore the <TopicLink slug="water">water cycle</TopicLink> to follow rain and moving water beyond the fields.

</More>

找到菲律宾，再移向吕宋北部。联合国教科文组织列入世界遗产的菲律宾科迪勒拉山水稻梯田，包括这一带的伊富高梯田群。地图帮助找到区域，下面的插图则展示人们怎样在坡地修田。

水稻<Term id="term-terrace">梯田</Term>是沿山坡修成、用墙挡护的田地。教科文组织介绍，接近水平的蓄水田沿山坡等高线分布，挡墙用石或土建成。比较平坦的田面和旁边倾斜的地面，看看人们怎样用墙把田留在坡上。

<Term id="term-irrigation">灌溉</Term>把水送到农作物。粮农组织介绍，伊富高的水从有森林的高地收集，再引到田里。森林、农地、梯田、聚落和水道构成相连的系统。沿着图上高处和低处的田块，观察大致的安排。

梯田也依靠社区的知识、劳动和管理。粮农组织介绍这个农业系统时，也把人包括在内。种满水稻的山坡，是人们持续生活和工作的地方。只看外观，无法知道梯田建于什么时候。
