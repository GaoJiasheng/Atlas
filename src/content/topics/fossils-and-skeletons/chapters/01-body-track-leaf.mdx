<Lang en>

The bone on the left preserves the form of an animal's body part. It is a <Term id="body-fossil">body fossil</Term>, even if its original material has changed. A fossil bone is evidence from the past; it is not simply a recently dead bone placed on a shelf. Its shape can help us describe anatomy, but cannot by <Num s="S2,S4,S58">itself supply an exact age</Num>.

The footprint slab records an activity. A foot pressed into a surface and left a mark; the teaching slab contains no foot bone. Such a record is a <Term id="trace-fossil">trace fossil</Term>. Notice the depression rather than imagining a foot standing above the rock. This generic model does not identify the animal that <Num s="S2,S7,S90">made a particular fossil track</Num>.

Move the upper slab aside to reveal the leaf form beneath it. This example is an <Term id="impression">impression</Term>, a shallow imprint without retained organic material. A leaf is part of a plant's body. Its imprint therefore belongs with body evidence, while the word impression tells us how that evidence was preserved. The model does <Num s="S54,S90">not represent a carbon film</Num>.

These descriptions answer different questions. Body or activity identifies what was recorded; impression describes a preservation mode. Compare the bone, footprint and leaf by stating what each records and how it is preserved, without giving the answers a score. None of these teaching shapes alone fixes a precise age or settles every <Num s="S2,S7,S58">question about the original organism</Num>.

<More title={{"en": "An imprint or a carbon film", "zh": "印痕与碳膜"}}>

An impression has no retained organic material. A compression retains altered organic material, sometimes as a carbon-rich film. The leaf model shows the former, so do not add a <Num s="S54,S90">carbon-film interpretation to its colour</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Natural casts and museum casts", "zh": "自然铸型与博物馆复制"}}>

A natural mould is a cavity or imprint; material filling it can form a natural fossil cast. An artificial museum replica records a fossil shape through a different process. The shared English word does not <Num s="S3,S55">make them the same object</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "What a footprint cannot settle", "zh": "足迹的判断边界"}}>

Footprints provide evidence of activity. Identifying a maker requires more than recognising a generic outline. Our slab is an original teaching shape, with no claim <Num s="S2,S7,S90">to a particular track-maker species</Num>.

</More>

<Lang zh>

左边的骨保留动物身体部分的形态，属于<Term id="body-fossil">实体化石</Term>。即使原来的物质已经改变，它记录的仍是身体。化石骨来自过去的地质记录，不能把刚死动物的骨放上架子，就当作同一回事。形状有助于描述身体结构，<Num s="S2,S4,S58">但单看外形不能读出精确年龄</Num>。

足迹板记录活动。脚压在表面上留下凹痕，这块教学石板里并没有脚骨。这类活动记录称为<Term id="trace-fossil">遗迹化石</Term>。观察凹下去的轮廓，不要把它想成站在石板上的实体脚。<Num s="S2,S7,S90">这里的通用足形也不能用来鉴定某件真实足迹属于哪一种动物</Num>。

移开覆盖片，下面露出叶形。这个例子是<Term id="impression">印痕</Term>，没有保留原来的有机物质。叶是植物身体的一部分，因此叶印痕仍是身体证据。“印痕”说明形态如何保存，并不在身体与活动之外，另设一个互斥的类别。<Num s="S54,S90">本模型没有画碳膜</Num>。

看这些物件时，可以分别说清“记录什么”与“怎样保存”，不必做正误评分。骨、足迹与叶形各能回答不同问题，也各有边界。它们不能单独给出精确年代，<Num s="S2,S7,S58">不能仅凭教学形状补齐原来生物的全部资料</Num>。

<More title={{"en": "An imprint or a carbon film", "zh": "印痕与碳膜"}}>

印痕不保留有机物质；压型仍保存变化后的部分有机物质，有时呈富碳薄膜。本课叶模型画的是前者，<Num s="S54,S90">不能因颜色而把它解释成碳膜</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Natural casts and museum casts", "zh": "自然铸型与博物馆复制"}}>

自然印模可以是空腔或印迹，填入其中的物质可形成自然铸型化石。人工博物馆复制品则通过另一过程记录化石形体。英文同用 cast 一词，<Num s="S3,S55">不表示它们是相同物件</Num>。

</More>
