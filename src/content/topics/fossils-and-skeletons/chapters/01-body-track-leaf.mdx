<Lang en>

The bone on the left preserves the shape of an animal's body part. It is a <Term id="body-fossil">body fossil</Term>, even if minerals have changed its original material. Fossils belong to the geological record; a recently dead animal's bone is different. A fossil's shape helps us study body structure, but <Num s="S2,S4,S58">does not give an exact age</Num>.

The footprint records an activity. A foot pressed into sediment and left a hollow mark. No foot bone remains in this slab. This kind of record is a <Term id="trace-fossil">trace fossil</Term>. The model has a general footprint shape; it does not identify <Num s="S2,S7,S90">a particular animal species</Num>.

Move the upper slab aside to see the leaf shape below. This is an <Term id="impression">impression</Term>: a shallow imprint with no original organic material left. A leaf is part of a plant's body, so its imprint is body evidence too. <Num s="S54,S90">This leaf model has no carbon film</Num>.

We can describe fossils in two ways. Body or trace tells us what was recorded. Impression tells us how it was preserved. Compare what each object records and how it was preserved. Shapes alone cannot answer <Num s="S2,S7,S58">every question about an organism or its age</Num>.

<More title={{"en": "An imprint or a carbon film", "zh": "印痕与碳膜"}}>

An impression has no organic material left. A compression still contains some changed organic material, sometimes as a carbon-rich film. The model shows <Num s="S54,S90">a leaf impression</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Natural casts and museum casts", "zh": "自然铸型与博物馆复制"}}>

A natural mould is a cavity or imprint. Material filling it can form a natural fossil cast. A museum cast is an artificial copy of a fossil's shape. Both use the English word cast, but <Num s="S3,S55">they form in different ways</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Reading a footprint", "zh": "读懂足迹"}}>

A footprint records activity. To identify the animal that made it, scientists need more evidence than a general outline. This slab shows <Num s="S2,S7,S90">a teaching example</Num>.

</More>

<Lang zh>

左边的骨保留了动物身体部分的形状，属于<Term id="body-fossil">实体化石</Term>。即使矿物改变了原来的物质，它仍记录身体结构。化石属于地质记录，与刚死动物留下的骨不同。它的形状能帮助研究身体结构，<Num s="S2,S4,S58">却不能给出精确年龄</Num>。

足迹记录的是活动。脚压在沉积物上，留下凹痕，石板里并没有脚骨。这类记录叫<Term id="trace-fossil">遗迹化石</Term>。模型采用通用足形，<Num s="S2,S7,S90">不代表某一种动物</Num>。

移开覆盖片，就能看到下面的叶形。这是<Term id="impression">印痕</Term>：只留下浅浅的形状，没有原来的有机物质。叶是植物身体的一部分，所以叶印痕也属于身体证据。<Num s="S54,S90">这个叶模型没有碳膜</Num>。

描述化石时，可以分别说明记录了什么、怎样保存。实体或遗迹说明记录的内容；印痕说明保存方式。比较这几件物体，试着分别说出这两点。单凭形状，<Num s="S2,S7,S58">无法回答关于生物及其年龄的所有问题</Num>。

<More title={{"en": "An imprint or a carbon film", "zh": "印痕与碳膜"}}>

印痕没有保留有机物质。压型仍含有部分发生变化的有机物质，有时形成富含碳的薄膜。模型展示的是<Num s="S54,S90">叶印痕</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Natural casts and museum casts", "zh": "自然铸型与博物馆复制"}}>

自然印模是空腔或印迹，填入其中的物质可形成自然铸型化石。博物馆复制件则是人工复制的化石形体。英文都用 cast，<Num s="S3,S55">形成方式却不同</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Reading a footprint", "zh": "读懂足迹"}}>

足迹记录活动。科学家要辨认留下足迹的动物，还需要通用轮廓以外的证据。这块石板展示的是<Num s="S2,S7,S90">教学例子</Num>。

</More>
