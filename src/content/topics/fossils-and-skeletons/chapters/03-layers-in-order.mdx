<Lang en>

Find the fossil bed between the ash layers. A <Term id="stratum">stratum</Term> is one rock layer. To read their order, we use an upright sequence that has not been disturbed. The ash was deposited here directly, and the fossil was not carried in from an older deposit. These conditions matter because folding and movement can <Num s="S58,S59,S90">change the order we see</Num>.

Start at the lower ash and move upwards. The lower layer formed first. The upper ash formed later. This tells us the order in which the layers were deposited. Their colours and thicknesses <Num s="S5,S59">do not give their ages</Num>.

The fossil bed is younger than the lower ash and older than the upper ash. This is its <Term id="relative-age">relative age</Term>: how old it is compared with something else. We still do not know <Num s="S5,S58">how many years lie between the deposits</Num>.

Layers can form at different rates. Some deposits are later eroded away, and sometimes deposition stops. <Num s="S59">The rock record can have gaps</Num>, so thickness alone cannot tell us elapsed time. Next, we will use mineral measurements to add ages. Dinosaur Earth will connect these ranges with geological time and discovery sites.

<More title={{"en": "When up is not younger", "zh": "上方未必较新"}}>

Layers can be overturned or disturbed. A fossil washed out of an old deposit can be older than its new surroundings. These cases need more evidence. The simple model <Num s="S58,S59,S90">leaves out these complications</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Missing pieces of the record", "zh": "记录的空缺"}}>

Deposition can stop, and erosion can remove rock. Some remains are never preserved as fossils. Together, these processes leave <Num s="S2,S59">gaps in the geological record</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Geological time on the map", "zh": "地图里的地质时间"}}>

The layers let us compare order. Their numerical ages are <Num s="S90">invented examples</Num>. Dinosaur Earth will show dated discovery sites in a wider view of time and place.

</More>

先找两层火山灰之间的含化石层。<Term id="stratum">地层</Term>就是一层岩层。这里采用没有倒转、没有受扰动的层序。火山灰直接沉积在原地，化石也没有从更老的沉积中被搬来。这些条件很重要，因为褶皱和搬运会<Num s="S58,S59,S90">改变我们看到的先后关系</Num>。

从下方灰层往上看。下层先形成，上方灰层后来才形成。这告诉我们各层沉积的顺序。岩层的颜色和厚度<Num s="S5,S59">不能直接给出年龄</Num>。

含化石层比下方灰层新，比上方灰层老。这就是<Term id="relative-age">相对年龄</Term>，也就是与其他东西比较所得的先后。我们仍不知道<Num s="S5,S58">各次沉积之间相隔多少年</Num>。

岩层形成的速度可以不同。有时沉积会中断，有时岩层后来被侵蚀掉。<Num s="S59">地质记录会有空缺</Num>，所以不能只凭厚度换算年数。下一章会加入矿物测年。《恐龙时代的地球》将把年龄范围、地质时间和发现地点联系起来。

<More title={{"en": "When up is not younger", "zh": "上方未必较新"}}>

岩层可能倒转或受到扰动。老地层中的化石被冲出来，再埋入新沉积后，会比周围岩层更老。这些情况需要更多证据。简单模型<Num s="S58,S59,S90">没有加入这些复杂情况</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Missing pieces of the record", "zh": "记录的空缺"}}>

沉积可能中断，侵蚀可能移走岩石。有些遗存从未保存成化石。这些过程共同造成<Num s="S2,S59">地质记录的空缺</Num>。

</More>
