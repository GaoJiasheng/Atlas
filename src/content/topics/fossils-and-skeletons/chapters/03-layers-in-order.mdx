The section has an important set of conditions. It is upright and undisturbed; the ash layers are primary deposits, and the fossil has not been carried here from an older deposit. These conditions make its simple order readable. Check them before applying the same reasoning to rocks elsewhere, where folding, disturbance or movement of <Num s="S58,S59,S90">fossils may complicate the record</Num>.

A <Term id="stratum">stratum</Term> is a layer in the sequence we are examining. Start at the lower ash and move upwards. In this example, the lower layer was deposited before the layers above it. The upper ash came later. The statement concerns the order of deposition, rather than an age read directly from <Num s="S5,S59">the layer's colour or thickness</Num>.

The fossil-bearing bed lies between the ash layers. Its position gives a <Term id="relative-age">relative age</Term>: it is younger than the lower deposit and older than the upper deposit, under our stated conditions. This comparison does not yet say how many years separate the deposits or provide a <Num s="S5,S58">measured date for the bone</Num>.

Thickness is not a fixed measure of elapsed time. <Num s="S59">The geological record can also have missing intervals</Num>, so a simple stack is not a complete diary. The next chapter adds measurements from suitable minerals while retaining the conditions that make the comparison valid. Dinosaur Earth will place age ranges within a wider view of geological time and discovery sites.

Superposition needs the right geological conditions. Overturned or disturbed layers require additional interpretation, and a fossil moved from an older deposit can be older than its new surroundings. The simple section <Num s="S58,S59,S90">assumes these complications are absent</Num>.

<Term id="stratum">地层</Term>是所观察层序中的一层。这里从下方灰层往上看，下层先沉积，上方灰层较晚形成。这是在说明沉积的顺序，并不是从岩层颜色直接读出年代。<Num s="S5,S59">画面里的层厚也不表示每层花了相同时间形成</Num>。
