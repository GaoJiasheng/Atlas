<Lang en>

Volcanic ash can contain minerals that work like clocks. Radioactive material inside them changes over time. Measuring these changes gives a <Term id="radiometric-age">radiometric age</Term>, an estimated age with some uncertainty. The clock dates an event in the mineral's history. Different materials need <Num s="S5,S6,S58">different dating methods</Num>.

In this invented example, the lower ash's central age is <Num s="S90">150.00 Ma</Num> and the upper ash's is <Num s="S90">148.00 Ma</Num>. The fossil between them has an <Term id="age-bracket">age bracket</Term>: a range between younger and older limits. Taking the average of the dates <Num s="S58,S90">does not measure the fossil's exact age</Num>.

Use the Ash age bracket card to compare the examples. Closer ages give a narrower range between the central dates. Smaller errors keep the same central dates but narrow the outer range. The table lists every pair of ages and errors. These SIM values are invented; <Num s="S90">they do not date the Singapore museum fossils</Num>.

The same geological conditions still apply: <Num s="S58,S59,S90">upright, undisturbed layers, ash deposited here directly, and a fossil not moved from an older deposit</Num>. We use the minerals' crystallisation ages to limit when the layers formed. The errors in this exercise have fixed limits. They show possible bounds, without assigning probabilities. Dinosaur Earth will place discovery-site ages in a wider setting.

| SIM case | Lower ash ± set error (Ma) | Upper ash ± set error (Ma) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide interval | <Num s="S90">150.00 ± 0.20</Num> | <Num s="S90">148.00 ± 0.20</Num> |
| Closer beds | <Num s="S90">149.40 ± 0.10</Num> | <Num s="S90">148.60 ± 0.10</Num> |
| Smaller errors | <Num s="S90">150.00 ± 0.05</Num> | <Num s="S90">148.00 ± 0.05</Num> |

<More title={{"en": "The mineral clock", "zh": "矿物时钟"}}>

A mineral clock can date crystallisation: when crystals formed. To use that age for a rock layer, scientists must check the ash's history. Here the ash was deposited directly, and <Num s="S5,S58,S90">the fossil was not brought from an older deposit</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Carbon fourteen dating", "zh": "碳十四测年"}}>

Carbon fourteen can date relatively recent organic remains, on the scale of <Num s="S6">tens of thousands of years</Num>. It cannot date Mesozoic dinosaur bones. <Num s="S6">Its useful age range depends on the material and method</Num>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Calculating the outer range", "zh": "计算外包范围"}}>

Each ± error gives the distance from the central age to a limit. For the younger outer limit, subtract the upper ash's error from its age, stopping at <Num s="S90">zero</Num>. For the older outer limit, add the lower ash's error to its age. These exercise bounds are not confidence intervals: they assign no probability. <Num s="S90">Real dating errors need not have such fixed limits</Num>.

</More>

<Lang zh>

火山灰里有些矿物能像时钟一样记录时间。矿物中的放射性物质会随时间改变。测量这些变化，可以估计年龄，称为<Term id="radiometric-age">放射性测年年龄</Term>。这个估计带有误差，记录的是矿物历史中的某个事件。不同材料需要<Num s="S5,S6,S58">不同的测年方法</Num>。

在这个虚构例子中，下灰层的中心年龄是 <Num s="S90">150.00 Ma</Num>，上灰层是 <Num s="S90">148.00 Ma</Num>。中间化石的年龄落在两者之间，形成<Term id="age-bracket">年龄夹定范围</Term>。把两个年龄取平均，<Num s="S58,S90">并不能测出化石的精确年龄</Num>。

用灰层年龄夹定卡比较算例。两个中心年龄越接近，中间范围越窄。选择较小误差，中心年龄不变，外包范围会缩小。下表列出所有年龄和误差。这些 SIM 数值都是虚构的，<Num s="S90">不用于给新加坡馆藏化石定年</Num>。

仍要满足相同条件：<Num s="S58,S59,S90">地层没有倒转或受扰动，灰层直接沉积于原地，化石没有从老沉积中被搬来</Num>。我们用矿物结晶的年龄限制地层形成的时间。练习中的误差设有固定界限，只表示范围，不表示概率。《恐龙时代的地球》会进一步介绍发现地点的年龄背景。

<More title={{"en": "The mineral clock", "zh": "矿物时钟"}}>

矿物时钟可以测出结晶，也就是形成晶体的时间。要用这个年龄判断地层，科学家还须检查灰层的历史。这里的灰直接沉积于原地，<Num s="S5,S58,S90">化石也没有从老沉积中被搬来</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Carbon fourteen dating", "zh": "碳十四测年"}}>

碳十四可以为较近期的有机遗存测年，适用时间为<Num s="S6">数万年量级</Num>。它不能用于中生代恐龙骨。<Num s="S6">具体适用范围取决于材料和方法</Num>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Calculating the outer range", "zh": "计算外包范围"}}>

每个 ± 误差表示中心年龄到界限的距离。外包年轻界限等于上灰层年龄减去误差，最低为<Num s="S90">零</Num>；外包古老界限等于下灰层年龄加上误差。这些练习界限不是置信区间，也没有给出概率。<Num s="S90">真实测年的误差不一定有这样的固定界限</Num>。

</More>
