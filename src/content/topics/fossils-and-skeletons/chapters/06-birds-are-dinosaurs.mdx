This bird model represents a Javan myna, a bird found in Singapore. Birds belong to <Term id="theropod">theropod</Term> dinosaurs, and theropods belong to the wider dinosaur group. A Javan myna is therefore <Num s="S8,S23">a living dinosaur you can see locally</Num>.

Look at the wing and hindlimbs. The wing is a bird's forelimb. These body regions are useful starting points for comparison. Scientists use many fossil and body features to study relationships; a similar outline alone is not enough. The myna is <Num s="S7,S8,S90">not a direct descendant of Tyrannosaurus</Num>.

The enlarged feather shows a modern-bird comparison. Archaeopteryx fossils preserve feathers and a wishbone, along with features different from living birds. Such combinations help scientists study early birds. Archaeopteryx is an early bird, but it is not a proven <Num s="S10">direct ancestor of every living bird</Num>.

About <Num s="S11">sixty-six million years ago</Num>, non-avian dinosaurs became extinct. <Num s="S8,S11">Some bird lineages survived</Num>. Observe a nearby bird quietly. Describe its visible body parts without chasing or feeding it. Observation helps you compare structures; broader evidence establishes ancestry. Dinosaur Earth will explore the wider environment and extinction event.

<More title={{"en": "Nested groups", "zh": "一层套一层的分支"}}>

Birds belong within theropods, and theropods belong within dinosaurs. A living branch remains part of its larger group. <Num s="S8">This is a classification</Num>, rather than a family tree with branch dates.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Comparing Archaeopteryx", "zh": "比较始祖鸟"}}>

Archaeopteryx has feathers and a wishbone, together with features unlike living birds. Comparing this combination helps researchers study early birds. It does not prove that <Num s="S10">all modern birds descended directly from Archaeopteryx</Num>.

</More>
