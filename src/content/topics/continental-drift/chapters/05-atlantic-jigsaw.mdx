<Lang en>

Two hundred million years ago Pangaea was still whole. People had long noticed that the coasts of South America and Africa look as if they fit; in 1596 the mapmaker Abraham Ortelius suggested the Americas had been torn away from Europe and Africa. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S5" aria-label="Source S5">S5</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S4" aria-label="Source S4">S4</button></sup>

The fit is better along the edges of the continents under the sea than along today’s coasts. The pale band on the map is the model’s outline of continental crust, which reaches beyond the shoreline. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="Source S6">S6</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S3" aria-label="Source S3">S3</button></sup>

Rocks match too. A continuous mountain range, the Caledonides, once ran from the Appalachians through Newfoundland and Greenland to Scotland and Scandinavia; with the Atlantic closed, its pieces line up. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S23" aria-label="Source S23">S23</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="Source S6">S6</button></sup>

Wegener set out the fit of the coasts, matching rocks, fossils and old ice in his 1915 book. Most geologists did not accept it. He could not name a force strong enough to move continents, and he said himself that the forces he suggested were too weak; at a meeting in New York in 1926 most speakers argued against him. Wegener died on an expedition in Greenland in 1930. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Source S1">S1</button></sup>

<More title={{ en: "Two sides of the argument", zh: "争论的两方" }}>

For drift: coasts, rocks, fossils and glacial traces from separate continents fit into one picture. The South African geologist Alexander du Toit was one of its strongest supporters, and in 1931 Arthur Holmes published the idea that slow currents in the mantle could carry continents. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Source S1">S1</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S5" aria-label="Source S5">S5</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S35" aria-label="Source S35">S35</button></sup>

Against drift: no one could show what force could push continents through the ocean floor, and the forces Wegener suggested were far too weak. Decades later, new evidence came from the ocean floor. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Source S1">S1</button></sup>

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How the same evidence looks today", zh: "今天看同一批证据" }}>

Wegener’s “continental drift” differs from plate tectonics: plates include the ocean floor, and continents do not plough through it. His proposed driving forces were rejected, but the conclusion that continents move was supported by later evidence. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="Source S1">S1</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="Source S6">S6</button></sup>

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

2 亿年前，盘古大陆仍然完整。人们很早就注意到，南美洲和非洲的海岸看上去能拼合；1596 年，地图学家奥特柳斯就提出美洲是从欧洲和非洲撕裂出去的。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S5" aria-label="来源 S5">S5</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S4" aria-label="来源 S4">S4</button></sup>

沿着海下的大陆边缘拼，比沿今天的海岸拼得更合。地图上的浅色带是模型画出的大陆地壳范围，它比海岸线伸得更远。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="来源 S6">S6</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S3" aria-label="来源 S3">S3</button></sup>

岩石也对得上。一条连续的山脉——加里东山——曾从阿巴拉契亚经纽芬兰、格陵兰一直延伸到苏格兰和斯堪的纳维亚；把大西洋合拢，这些碎段就连成一线。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S23" aria-label="来源 S23">S23</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="来源 S6">S6</button></sup>

魏格纳在 1915 年的书里，把海岸拼合、岩石对应、化石和古冰川放在一起。多数地质学家不接受。他说不出有什么力足以推动大陆，他自己也承认，他提出的力太弱；1926 年在纽约的一次会议上，多数发言者反对他。1930 年，魏格纳在格陵兰考察时去世。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="来源 S1">S1</button></sup>

<More title={{ en: "Two sides of the argument", zh: "争论的两方" }}>

支持漂移：分开的大陆上的海岸、岩石、化石和冰川痕迹，能拼成同一幅图。南非地质学家杜托伊特是最坚定的支持者之一；1931 年，霍姆斯发表了地幔里缓慢的对流可能带动大陆的想法。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="来源 S1">S1</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S5" aria-label="来源 S5">S5</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S35" aria-label="来源 S35">S35</button></sup>

反对漂移：没人能说明什么力能推着大陆穿过洋底，魏格纳提出的力又远远不够。几十年后，新的证据来自洋底。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="来源 S1">S1</button></sup>

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How the same evidence looks today", zh: "今天看同一批证据" }}>

魏格纳的“大陆漂移”和板块构造不同：板块也包括洋底，大陆并不是在洋底上犁过去。他提出的驱动力被否定了，但“大陆会移动”这个结论得到了后来证据的支持。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S1" aria-label="来源 S1">S1</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="来源 S6">S6</button></sup>

</More>
