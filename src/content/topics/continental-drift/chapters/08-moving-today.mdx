<Lang en>

Between 1965 and 1968 a series of papers by J. Tuzo Wilson, Dan McKenzie and Robert Parker, Jason Morgan and Xavier Le Pichon brought these ideas together as <Term id="term-plate-tectonics">plate tectonics</Term>. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="Source S6">S6</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S36" aria-label="Source S36">S36</button></sup>

Today the movement can be measured directly. A <Term id="term-gnss">GPS</Term> station fixed to the ground and measured for years shows how its position changes. The station at Nanyang Technological University in Singapore moves about <Num s="S27">2.8</Num> centimetres a year, towards the east and slightly south, relative to a global reference frame. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S27" aria-label="Source S27">S27</button></sup>

Australia moves about <Num s="S28">7</Num> centimetres a year towards the north-east; its official coordinates, GDA2020, were shifted by <Num s="S28">1.8</Num> metres to keep up. In Iceland, stations on the two sides of the Atlantic ridge move apart, one towards the north-west and one towards the north-east. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S28" aria-label="Source S28">S28</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S27" aria-label="Source S27">S27</button></sup>

Plates usually move a few centimetres a year relative to each other, about as slowly as fingernails grow, about <Num s="S29">4</Num> centimetres a year. Over 200 million years that adds up to thousands of kilometres. The plate model is not meant for spans as short as a human lifetime, so these speeds come from GPS, not from the model. For where earthquakes and volcanoes cluster, see <TopicLink slug="plates-and-hazards" ch="mapping-plates">mapping the plates</TopicLink>. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S38" aria-label="Source S38">S38</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S29" aria-label="Source S29">S29</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S30" aria-label="Source S30">S30</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S3" aria-label="Source S3">S3</button></sup>

<More title={{ en: "A future supercontinent?", zh: "未来的超大陆？" }}>

Some scientists have modelled where today’s plates might gather in about 200 to 300 million years. Four scenarios have been compared: Novopangea, Pangea Ultima, Aurica and Amasia; one study proposed Amasia, with the continents gathering around the North Pole. These are predictions under different assumptions, not observations, so this topic does not draw them on the map. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S32" aria-label="Source S32">S32</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S33" aria-label="Source S33">S33</button></sup>

A GPS speed is measured against a global reference frame (here IGS14), not against the neighbouring plate. The speed at which Iceland is pulled apart is the difference between stations on its two sides. <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S27" aria-label="Source S27">S27</button></sup>

1965 至 1968 年间，图佐·威尔逊、丹·麦肯齐与罗伯特·帕克、杰森·摩根和格扎维埃·勒皮雄的一系列论文，把这些想法归纳成<Term id="term-plate-tectonics">板块构造</Term>。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S6" aria-label="来源 S6">S6</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S36" aria-label="来源 S36">S36</button></sup>

今天，这种移动可以直接测量。固定在地面、长期测量的 <Term id="term-gnss">GPS</Term> 站，能显示自身位置怎样变化。新加坡南洋理工大学的测站，相对全球参考框架每年移动约 <Num s="S27">2.8</Num> 厘米，方向朝东、略偏南。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S27" aria-label="来源 S27">S27</button></sup>

澳大利亚每年向东北移动约 <Num s="S28">7</Num> 厘米；它的官方坐标系 GDA2020 为此把坐标移动了 <Num s="S28">1.8</Num> 米。在冰岛，大西洋洋脊两侧的测站彼此远离，一个朝西北，一个朝东北。 <sup><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S28" aria-label="来源 S28">S28</button><button type="button" className="atlas-src" data-source="S27" aria-label="来源 S27">S27</button></sup>
