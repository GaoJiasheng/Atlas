<Lang en>

Singapore is a small island of about <Num s="S1">710 km²</Num>. It has no big rivers or lakes, and although some <Num s="S3">2,400 mm</Num> of rain falls in a year, there is little land to collect and store it. Today it uses about <Num s="S24">440 million gallons</Num> of water a day, enough to fill <Num s="S24">800</Num> Olympic swimming pools, and PUB expects demand to almost double by 2065. PUB, the national water agency, gets this water from the <Term id="four-national-taps">Four National Taps</Term>.

The first tap is water from local catchments. Rain falling on about <Num s="S1">two-thirds</Num> of the land runs through drains, canals and rivers into <Num s="S1">17</Num> <Term id="reservoir">reservoirs</Term>. The land that drains into a reservoir is its <Term id="catchment">catchment</Term>. From a reservoir the raw water goes to one of <Num s="S25">six</Num> <Term id="waterworks">waterworks</Term> to be treated, and only then enters the tap-water network.

The second tap is imported water from the Johor River in Malaysia, under an agreement signed in 1962 that runs until 2061. The third is NEWater, made by cleaning used water again and again. The fourth is desalinated water, made from seawater. Today these two taps are supplied by <Num s="S25">four</Num> NEWater factories and <Num s="S23">five</Num> desalination plants.

The map shows how these taps were added, from the first reservoir in 1867 to the newest plants. To see how water moves through the whole system, from rain to tap and back again, open the 3D model in the topic <TopicLink slug="water">Water in Singapore</TopicLink>.

<More title={{ en: "How to read the map", zh: "怎样读这张地图" }}>

Reservoirs are tinted by the tap they belong to: local catchment water, or imported water for Linggiu Reservoir in Johor. Each one appears in the year it was completed, with the outline it has today, because no open dataset records the older, smaller outlines. Small diamonds mark today's waterworks, NEWater factories, desalination plants and water reclamation plants. The thin lines are water pipelines. Catchment areas themselves are not drawn: PUB publishes them only as pictures, not as data.

<Lang zh>

新加坡是一座小岛，面积约 <Num s="S1">710 平方公里</Num>。岛上没有大河大湖；一年的雨量虽有约 <Num s="S3">2,400 毫米</Num>，能用来收集和储存雨水的土地却很少。今天新加坡每天用水约 <Num s="S24">4.4 亿加仑</Num>，足以注满 <Num s="S24">800</Num> 个奥运标准游泳池；PUB 预计，到 2065 年用水量可能接近翻倍。负责全国用水的公用事业局（PUB）从<Term id="four-national-taps">四大水喉</Term>取得这些水。

第一个水喉是本地集水。落在约<Num s="S1">三分之二</Num>土地上的雨水，经水沟、运河和河流流进 <Num s="S1">17</Num> 个<Term id="reservoir">蓄水池</Term>。雨水流向同一个蓄水池的那片土地，就是它的<Term id="catchment">集水区</Term>。蓄水池里的原水再送到 <Num s="S25">6</Num> 座<Term id="waterworks">水厂</Term>之一处理，处理好的水才进入自来水管网。

第二个水喉是进口水，按 1962 年签订、到 2061 年为止的协议，从马来西亚的柔佛河取水。第三个是新生水，把用过的水一再净化而成。第四个是淡化海水，由海水制成。今天，这两个水喉由 <Num s="S25">4</Num> 座新生水厂和 <Num s="S23">5</Num> 座海水淡化厂供应。

地图按年份展示这些水喉怎样一个个出现：从 1867 年的第一个蓄水池，到最新的水厂。想看水在整个系统里怎样流动——从雨水到水龙头再回到循环里——请打开三维模型主题<TopicLink slug="water">《新加坡的水》</TopicLink>。

<More title={{ en: "How to read the map", zh: "怎样读这张地图" }}>
