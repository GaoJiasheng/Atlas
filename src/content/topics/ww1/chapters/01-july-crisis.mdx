On 28 June 1914 Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary, and his wife Sophie were shot dead in Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia. The gunman, Gavrilo Princip, was a Bosnian Serb student, one of <Num s="S21">seven</Num> young conspirators of the Young Bosnia movement waiting along the route. They had been armed with help from the Black Hand, a secret society of Serbian army officers.

Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia. On 5 and 6 July Germany promised to stand by its ally whatever it decided, a promise later called the "blank cheque". On 23 July Austria-Hungary delivered an <Term id="ultimatum">ultimatum</Term> to Serbia with <Num s="S26">ten</Num> demands and <Num s="S27">48 hours</Num> to reply. Serbia's answer on 25 July accepted most of them but refused to let Austro-Hungarian officials take part in an investigation on Serbian soil.

Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July, and its river gunboats shelled Belgrade early the next morning. Russia, which saw itself as Serbia's protector, ordered general mobilisation on 30 July. Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August and on France on 3 August, and on 4 August invaded Belgium; Britain declared war the same day. In a week a crisis in the Balkans had become a war between the two blocs.

Britain's declaration covered its whole empire. India, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and colonies such as Singapore and Malaya were at war from 4 August, though none of them had been asked. Why the crisis ended in war is still argued over; two answers are set out below.

<More title={{ en: "Who was Gavrilo Princip?", zh: "加夫里洛·普林西普是谁？" }}>

Princip was the son of a poor Bosnian Serb family and had studied in Belgrade. Like the other members of Young Bosnia he wanted Bosnia freed from Austro-Hungarian rule and joined with Serbia in a state of the South Slavs. The weapons came through the Black Hand, led by Colonel Dragutin Dimitrijević, known as Apis, the head of Serbian military intelligence; how much the Serbian government knew is still debated. On the day, an earlier bomb missed, and Princip fired after the archduke's car took a wrong turn. He was a month short of his <Num s="S21">20th</Num> birthday, too young under Austro-Hungarian law to be executed, and was sentenced to <Num s="S22">20 years</Num>. He died of tuberculosis in prison at Theresienstadt in April 1918. In Bosnia today some remember him as a liberator and others as a terrorist; one account counts <Num s="S21">six</Num> armed conspirators rather than seven.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The July Crisis day by day", zh: "七月危机逐日表" }}>

- 28 June: Franz Ferdinand and Sophie are killed in Sarajevo.
- 5–6 July: Germany's "blank cheque" to Austria-Hungary.
- 23 July, 6 p.m.: the ultimatum is delivered in Belgrade, with a deadline of 6 p.m. on 25 July.
- 25 July: Serbia's reply accepts most points and rejects point 6; Austria-Hungary breaks off relations.
- 28 July: Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia.
- 29 July, 2.20 a.m.: the Danube monitors Temes, Bodrog and Számos open fire on Belgrade; Russia orders partial mobilisation.
- 30 July: Russian general mobilisation.
- 1 August: Germany mobilises and declares war on Russia.
- 3 August: Germany declares war on France.
- 4 August: Germany invades Belgium; Britain declares war on Germany.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Debate: who was responsible?", zh: "争议：谁该负责？" }}>

In 1961 the German historian Fritz Fischer argued in *Griff nach der Weltmacht* that Germany's leaders bore a considerable share of the responsibility: they encouraged Austria-Hungary to act and accepted, even welcomed, the risk of a great war in which Germany could expand its power. Abroad his book was read as showing that Germany had deliberately caused the war, and it set off a long debate inside Germany.

In 2012 the Australian historian Christopher Clark argued in *The Sleepwalkers* that responsibility was spread across all the capitals. Leaders in Vienna, Berlin, Saint Petersburg, Paris, London and Belgrade each misjudged the others, took risks they thought others would not match, and moved step by step towards a war few of them wanted in that form. Both books rest on the same archives; historians still disagree about how to weigh them. The peace treaty of 1919 gave its own answer, which Chapter 9 returns to.

</More>

1914 年 6 月 28 日，奥匈帝国皇储弗朗茨·斐迪南大公和妻子索菲在波斯尼亚首府萨拉热窝被开枪打死。开枪的加夫里洛·普林西普是一名波斯尼亚塞族学生，是“青年波斯尼亚”运动埋伏在大公车队路线上的 <Num s="S21">7</Num> 名青年之一。他们的武器是塞尔维亚军官的秘密组织“黑手会”帮忙弄到的。

奥匈帝国把矛头指向塞尔维亚。7 月 5 日和 6 日，德国向盟友保证：不论奥匈作出什么决定，德国都会支持。这个承诺后来被称为“空白支票”。7 月 23 日，奥匈帝国向塞尔维亚递交<Term id="ultimatum">最后通牒</Term>，提出 <Num s="S26">10</Num> 条要求，限 <Num s="S27">48 小时</Num>内答复。塞尔维亚 7 月 25 日答复，接受了大部分要求，但拒绝让奥匈官员到塞尔维亚境内参与调查。

7 月 28 日，奥匈帝国对塞尔维亚宣战，次日凌晨，它的内河炮舰炮击贝尔格莱德。俄国自认是塞尔维亚的保护者，7 月 30 日宣布总动员。德国 8 月 1 日对俄宣战，3 日对法宣战，4 日入侵比利时；英国当天对德宣战。短短一周，一场巴尔干危机变成了两大集团之间的战争。

英国的宣战适用于整个大英帝国。印度、加拿大、澳大利亚、新西兰、南非，以及新加坡、马来亚等殖民地，从 8 月 4 日起都处于战争状态，事先没有人征求它们的意见。这场危机为什么会走向战争，至今仍有争论，下面列出两种主要看法。

<More title={{ en: "Who was Gavrilo Princip?", zh: "加夫里洛·普林西普是谁？" }}>

普林西普出身于一个贫穷的波斯尼亚塞族家庭，曾在贝尔格莱德读书。和“青年波斯尼亚”的其他成员一样，他希望波斯尼亚摆脱奥匈统治，与塞尔维亚一起组成南方斯拉夫人的国家。武器来自“黑手会”，其首领是塞尔维亚军事情报首脑德拉古廷·迪米特里耶维奇上校，外号“阿皮斯”；塞尔维亚政府究竟知道多少，至今仍有争论。行刺当天，早些时候投出的炸弹没有命中；后来大公的汽车拐错了路，普林西普才开了枪。他离 <Num s="S21">20</Num> 岁生日还差一个月，按奥匈法律不能判死刑，被判 <Num s="S22">20 年</Num>徒刑，1918 年 4 月在特雷津的监狱里死于结核病。今天在波斯尼亚，有人把他看作解放者，也有人看作恐怖分子。另有一种记载认为持武器的密谋者是 <Num s="S21">6</Num> 人，而不是 7 人。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Debate: who was responsible?", zh: "争议：谁该负责？" }}>

1961 年，德国历史学家弗里茨·费舍尔在《争雄世界》一书中提出：德国领导人对战争爆发负有相当大的责任。他们鼓动奥匈帝国动手，并且接受甚至欢迎一场大战的风险，希望借此扩张德国的势力。这本书在国外被理解为证明了德国蓄意挑起战争，在德国国内引发了长期的争论。

2012 年，澳大利亚历史学家克里斯托弗·克拉克在《梦游者》一书中提出：责任分散在各国首都。维也纳、柏林、圣彼得堡、巴黎、伦敦和贝尔格莱德的领导人都误判了对方，都冒了自以为对方不敢跟进的风险，一步一步走向了一场他们大多并不想要的战争。两本书依据的是同一批档案，历史学家至今对如何权衡仍有分歧。1919 年的和约给出了自己的答案，第 9 章会再谈。

</More>
