The peace conference opened in Paris on 18 January 1919. Delegates of <Num s="S230">32</Num> countries took part, but the main decisions were made by four men: Woodrow Wilson of the United States, David Lloyd George of Britain, Georges Clemenceau of France and Vittorio Orlando of Italy. The defeated states could not negotiate. On 28 June Germany signed the Treaty of Versailles in the Hall of Mirrors.

Germany lost about <Num s="S230">13</Num> per cent of its territory and <Num s="S230">10</Num> per cent of its people, including Alsace-Lorraine, returned to France, and land given to Poland, as well as all its colonies. Its army was limited to <Num s="S231">100,000</Num> men, the Rhineland was occupied for <Num s="S230">15</Num> years and the Saar placed under the <Term id="league-of-nations">League of Nations</Term>. Article 231 held Germany and its allies responsible for the loss and damage of the war; on that basis a commission set <Term id="reparations">reparations</Term> in 1921 at <Num s="S232">132 billion</Num> gold marks. Treaties with Austria, Bulgaria, Hungary and the Ottoman Empire followed in 1919 and 1920.

Wilson's call for <Term id="self-determination">self-determination</Term> helped create Poland, Czechoslovakia and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, but not freedom for colonies. Germany's colonies and the Ottoman Arab lands became <Term id="mandate">mandates</Term>, mostly under British and French rule. The conference also gave Germany's rights in Shandong to Japan. On 4 May more than <Num s="S235">3,000</Num> students protested in Beijing, and China's delegates refused to sign.

About <Num s="S230">9.5 million</Num> soldiers had died; estimates range from <Num s="S244">9.2 million</Num> to <Num s="S244">10.1 million</Num>. Civilian deaths cannot be counted precisely: published estimates add up to about <Num s="S244">6</Num> to <Num s="S244">9 million</Num>. Serbia lost about <Num s="S245">16</Num> per cent of its people, the highest share of any country. Fighting went on in Russia, Poland and Anatolia. Poppies, memorials and war cemeteries, among them the graves of Chinese labourers in France, keep the dead in memory.

<More title={{ en: "Wilson, the League and the reparations debate", zh: "威尔逊、国际联盟与赔款之争" }}>

Wilson made the Covenant of the League of Nations the first part of every Paris treaty; the League came into being on 10 January 1920. But the United States never joined. Senators led by Henry Cabot Lodge attached reservations to the treaty, and on 19 March 1920 the Senate rejected the treaty <Num s="S238">49</Num> votes to <Num s="S238">35</Num>, <Num s="S238">7</Num> short of the two-thirds needed. The United States made a separate peace with Germany in 1921.

The British economist John Maynard Keynes, who left the conference in despair, published The Economic Consequences of the Peace in December 1919. He called the treaty a "Carthaginian peace" that would ruin Germany and with it Europe's economy; his view shaped how the treaty was judged for decades.

Many historians now answer that the real bill was smaller than it looked. Of the 1921 total, the C bonds, more than half of the sum, were never expected to be paid; the remaining <Num s="S232">50 billion</Num> gold marks (A and B bonds) were within British and American estimates of what Germany could pay. In the end Germany paid about <Num s="S231">20 billion</Num> gold marks, less than it received in American loans in the 1920s. Whether the peace failed because it punished Germany too harshly or not harshly enough is still debated.

<More title={{ en: "Shandong, May Fourth and the 'Wilsonian moment'", zh: "山东、五四与“威尔逊时刻”" }}>

At the conference China's young diplomat Wellington Koo argued that Germany's rights in Shandong, the birthplace of Confucius, should return to China, which had joined the war against Germany. But Britain and France had promised in secret wartime agreements to support Japan's claim, and Japan threatened not to join the League. On 30 April Wilson, Clemenceau and Lloyd George approved the transfer to Japan, after Japan said it would hand the peninsula back to China in sovereignty while keeping Germany's economic rights.

On 4 May more than <Num s="S235">3,000</Num> students from <Num s="S235">13</Num> colleges gathered at Tiananmen. They burned the house of the minister Cao Rulin, who had arranged loans from Japan, and <Num s="S235">31</Num> were arrested. Strikes and protests spread across China. Koo proposed signing with a reservation on Shandong; when the Allies refused, China's delegates did not sign. Shandong was returned to China under a treaty made at the Washington Conference in 1922.

China was not alone. In Korea, then a Japanese colony, <Num s="S239">33</Num> leaders declared independence on 1 March 1919 and more than <Num s="S239">1 million</Num> people joined protests, which were put down with thousands of casualties. In Egypt the arrest of nationalist leaders who wanted to go to Paris set off a revolution. In India, on 13 April, troops under Brigadier General Reginald Dyer fired without warning on a crowd of at least <Num s="S240">10,000</Num> in the Jallianwala Bagh at Amritsar. The official count was <Num s="S240">379</Num> dead and about <Num s="S240">1,200</Num> wounded; other estimates put the dead at about <Num s="S241">1,000</Num>.

<More title={{ en: "A peace to end all peace? Sèvres, mandates and the wars after the war", zh: "终结一切和平的和平？色佛尔、委任统治与战后的战争" }}>

The other treaties were signed at Saint-Germain with Austria on 10 September 1919, at Neuilly with Bulgaria on 27 November 1919, at Trianon with Hungary on 4 June 1920 and at Sèvres with the Ottoman Empire on 10 August 1920. Hungary lost two-thirds of its territory and <Num s="S230">58</Num> per cent of its population.

Sèvres recognised an independent Armenia and gave Greece Smyrna and its hinterland, where Greek troops had landed on 15 May 1919. Turkish nationalists under Mustafa Kemal fought on; in September 1922 the Turkish army took Smyrna, and a fire, whose origins are still disputed, destroyed its Christian districts. The Treaty of Lausanne of 24 July 1923 replaced Sèvres. Further north, Poland and Soviet Russia fought until the Treaty of Riga of 18 March 1921.

The mandates came in three classes. "A" mandates were the Ottoman Arab lands: Syria and Lebanon under France, Mesopotamia (Iraq), Palestine and Transjordan under Britain. "B" mandates were German colonies in Africa, divided among Britain, France and Belgium, and "C" mandates went to South Africa, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. Britain's wartime promises to Arab leaders, to France in the Sykes–Picot agreement and to the Zionist movement in the Balfour Declaration could not all be kept.

1919 年 1 月 18 日，和会在巴黎开幕。<Num s="S230">32</Num> 个国家派代表出席，但主要决定由四个人作出：美国的伍德罗·威尔逊、英国的劳合·乔治、法国的乔治·克列孟梭和意大利的维托里奥·奥兰多。战败国不被允许参加谈判。6 月 28 日，德国在凡尔赛宫镜厅签订了《凡尔赛和约》。

德国失去约 <Num s="S230">13%</Num> 的领土和 <Num s="S230">10%</Num> 的人口，其中阿尔萨斯—洛林归还法国，东部大片土地划给新生的波兰；德国还失去了全部殖民地。陆军限 <Num s="S231">10 万</Num>人，莱茵兰被占领 <Num s="S230">15</Num> 年，萨尔交给<Term id="league-of-nations">国际联盟</Term>管理。和约第 231 条规定德国及其盟国对战争造成的损失和损害负责；据此，一个委员会在 1921 年把<Term id="reparations">赔款</Term>定为 <Num s="S232">1,320 亿</Num>金马克。1919 年和 1920 年，协约国又先后同奥地利、保加利亚、匈牙利和奥斯曼帝国签订和约。

威尔逊倡导的<Term id="self-determination">民族自决</Term>促成了波兰、捷克斯洛伐克和塞尔维亚人—克罗地亚人—斯洛文尼亚人王国的建立，却没有给殖民地带来自由。德国的殖民地和奥斯曼的阿拉伯地区成了<Term id="mandate">委任统治</Term>地，大多归英法管理。和会还把德国在山东的权益交给日本。5 月 4 日，<Num s="S235">3,000</Num> 多名学生在北京游行抗议，中国代表团拒绝签字。

约 <Num s="S230">950 万</Num>军人死于这场战争，各种估计在 <Num s="S244">920 万</Num>到 <Num s="S244">1,010 万</Num>之间。平民死亡人数无法准确统计，已发表的估计合计约 <Num s="S244">600 万</Num>到 <Num s="S244">900 万</Num>。塞尔维亚失去了约 <Num s="S245">16%</Num> 的人口，比例为各国之首。战火在俄国、波兰和安纳托利亚继续燃烧。虞美人、纪念碑和战争公墓——包括法国的华工墓地——让人们记住死者。

威尔逊坚持把《国际联盟盟约》作为巴黎各和约的第一部分；国联于 1920 年 1 月 10 日正式成立。但美国始终没有加入。以亨利·卡伯特·洛奇为首的参议员给和约附加了保留条件；1920 年 3 月 19 日，参议院以 <Num s="S238">49</Num> 票对 <Num s="S238">35</Num> 票否决和约，离所需的三分之二多数差 <Num s="S238">7</Num> 票。1921 年，美国与德国另行媾和。

英国经济学家约翰·梅纳德·凯恩斯失望地离开和会，1919 年 12 月出版《和平的经济后果》。他说这是一份“迦太基式的和平”，会毁掉德国，也会毁掉欧洲经济；此后几十年，人们对和约的评价都受他的影响。

如今许多历史学者回答说，真正的账单没有看上去那么大。1921 年的总额中，占一半以上的 C 类债券从来没有人指望兑现；其余 <Num s="S232">500 亿</Num>金马克（A、B 两类债券）在英美估计的德国支付能力之内。德国最终支付了约 <Num s="S231">200 亿</Num>金马克，少于它在 20 年代得到的美国贷款。和约失败的原因，是对德国惩罚太重还是不够重，至今仍有争论。
