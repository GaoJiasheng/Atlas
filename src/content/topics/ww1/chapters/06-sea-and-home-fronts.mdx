<Lang en>

At sea, Britain's navy kept most of the German fleet in port. The one great fleet battle came off Jutland, Denmark, on 31 May 1916, with about <Num s="S142">250</Num> warships. Britain lost <Num s="S142">14</Num> ships and <Num s="S142">6,094</Num> men, Germany <Num s="S142">11</Num> ships and <Num s="S142">2,551</Num> men. Among the British battleships was HMS Malaya, paid for by the Federated Malay States.

Control of the sea let Britain and its allies <Term id="blockade">blockade</Term> Germany. From November 1914 the North Sea was treated as a military area, and cargoes bound for Germany, food included, were stopped. In the "turnip winter" of 1916–17 the daily ration fell below <Num s="S146">1,000</Num> calories. In December 1918 a German government study blamed the blockade for about <Num s="S145">763,000</Num> civilian deaths. Historians today estimate <Num s="S145">478,500</Num> to <Num s="S145,S146">800,000</Num> deaths from hunger-related disease, and debate how many were caused by the blockade and how many by Germany's own food policy.

Germany struck back with <Term id="u-boat">U-boats</Term>. On 7 May 1915 one of them sank the liner Lusitania off Ireland: <Num s="S147,S148">1,198</Num> of the <Num s="S147">1,959</Num> people aboard died, <Num s="S147,S148">128</Num> of them Americans. After American protests Germany held back, but on 1 February 1917 it declared <Term id="unrestricted-submarine-warfare">unrestricted submarine warfare</Term>.

The war was also fought in factories and fields. By 1918 about <Num s="S150">900,000</Num> British women made munitions. Empires drew on their colonies: about <Num s="S154">1.27 million</Num> Indians served as soldiers and labourers, France recruited about <Num s="S156">192,000</Num> West African soldiers and about <Num s="S157">93,000</Num> Indochinese soldiers and workers, and about <Num s="S151,S150">140,000</Num> Chinese workers came to France in a <Term id="labour-corps">labour corps</Term>.

In Dublin on 24 April 1916 Irish republicans rose against British rule. In six days <Num s="S158">485</Num> people died, more than half of them civilians, and <Num s="S160">16</Num> leaders were executed.

<More title={{ en: "Jutland: who won?", zh: "日德兰：谁赢了？" }}>

The German admiral Reinhard Scheer hoped to trap part of the British Grand Fleet and destroy it. Instead he met the whole of it, and during the night he brought his fleet home to Wilhelmshaven.

Germany celebrated a victory: its fleet had sunk more ships and killed more than twice as many men: over <Num s="S142">6,000</Num> British sailors died, against about <Num s="S142">2,500</Num> German.

In Britain the battle was soon seen as a strategic success: the German fleet stayed mostly in harbour for the rest of the war, and the blockade went on. Germany turned instead to its submarines. Historians still weigh the two sides of this verdict.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Hunger and the law of the sea", zh: "饥饿与海上法" }}>

Under the blockade Germany could no longer import enough food. The poor potato harvest of 1916 forced people to live on swede turnips through the winter, and the official ration fell below <Num s="S146">1,000</Num> calories a day. Historians also point to German failures: farmers in surplus regions held back potatoes or fed them to animals while cities went hungry.

Germany called the blockade a "hunger blockade" against international law, since it starved women and children. Its December 1918 study put the toll at <Num s="S145">762,796</Num>. The historian Alan Kramer concludes that the blockade did not break the letter of international law but broke its spirit, which was to protect civilians.

Britain answered that its blockade sank no ships and killed no one directly, while German U-boats sank merchant ships and liners without warning, drowning passengers and crews. Germany replied that the Lusitania carried war supplies; the ship did carry a quantity of ammunition. Both blockades, by surface ships and by submarines, aimed at the enemy's whole population.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The Chinese Labour Corps", zh: "华工团" }}>

Britain began recruiting Chinese workers in 1916, mostly in Shandong, through its leased territory at Weihaiwei; France recruited through Chinese companies. China was neutral, so the men were civilians on contracts: three years with Britain, five with France. Between 1916 and 1918 about <Num s="S151,S150">40,000</Num> worked for France and about <Num s="S151,S150">100,000</Num> for Britain.

The first British contingent of <Num s="S153">1,086</Num> men sailed from Weihaiwei on 18 January 1917 and went round the Cape of Good Hope to Le Havre. Most later contingents crossed the Pacific to British Columbia and travelled by train across Canada: between March 1917 and March 1918, <Num s="S152">84,422</Num> men passed through Canada to France.

They unloaded ships, built roads and railways, repaired tanks, dug trenches and, after the armistice, cleared the battlefields of shells and bodies. The British corps had its base and hospital at Noyelles-sur-Mer on the Somme, where many of its dead are buried. Nearly <Num s="S151">2,000</Num> Chinese workers are buried in France, and more than <Num s="S151">700</Num> died at sea when their ships were sunk, about <Num s="S151">3,000</Num> in all.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Soldiers and workers from the empires", zh: "来自各帝国的士兵与劳工" }}>

India sent the largest force from any colony: about <Num s="S155">1.3 million</Num> soldiers and labourers, of whom more than <Num s="S155">74,000</Num> died; <Num s="S154">140,000</Num> fought on the Western Front. France recruited <Num s="S156">192,000</Num> tirailleurs sénégalais in West Africa, of whom <Num s="S156">134,000</Num> fought in Europe and about <Num s="S156">30,000</Num> died; recruitment met resistance in West Africa. From Indochina came about <Num s="S157">44,000</Num> soldiers and <Num s="S157">49,000</Num> workers, mostly Vietnamese.

Not all came willingly: France set recruitment quotas for regions of its colonies, and colonial and Chinese workers soon had some of the lowest wages in French industry. On 21 February 1917 the troopship Mendi sank after a collision off the Isle of Wight; <Num s="S161">607</Num> black South African men of the Native Labour Corps died with her.

</More>

在海上，英国海军把德国舰队的大部分封锁在港内。唯一一次大规模舰队决战，1916 年 5 月 31 日发生在丹麦日德兰半岛外海，双方共约 <Num s="S142">250</Num> 艘军舰参战。英国损失 <Num s="S142">14</Num> 艘舰船、<Num s="S142">6,094</Num> 人，德国损失 <Num s="S142">11</Num> 艘、<Num s="S142">2,551</Num> 人。英国战列舰中有一艘“马来亚号”，是马来联邦出资建造的。

掌握了制海权，英国和它的盟国得以对德国实行<Term id="blockade">封锁</Term>。从 1914 年 11 月起，北海被当作军事区域，开往德国的货物——包括粮食——一律被截停。1916 至 1917 年的“萝卜之冬”，每天的配给热量降到 <Num s="S146">1,000</Num> 卡以下。1918 年 12 月，德国政府的一项研究把约 <Num s="S145">76.3 万</Num>名平民的死亡归咎于封锁；今天的历史学者估计，死于与饥饿有关的疾病的有 <Num s="S145">47.85 万</Num>到 <Num s="S145,S146">80 万</Num>人，并在争论其中多少应归于封锁、多少应归于德国自己的粮食政策。

德国用 <Term id="u-boat">U 艇</Term>反击。1915 年 5 月 7 日，一艘 U 艇在爱尔兰外海击沉邮轮“卢西塔尼亚号”：船上 <Num s="S147">1,959</Num> 人中有 <Num s="S147,S148">1,198</Num> 人遇难，其中美国人 <Num s="S147,S148">128</Num> 名。美国抗议后，德国一度收敛，但在 1917 年 2 月 1 日宣布实行<Term id="unrestricted-submarine-warfare">无限制潜艇战</Term>。

战争也在工厂和田地里进行。到 1918 年，英国约有 <Num s="S150">90 万</Num>名女工在生产军火。各帝国从殖民地征调人力：约 <Num s="S154">127 万</Num>印度人作为士兵和劳工服役；法国招募了约 <Num s="S156">19.2 万</Num>名西非士兵和约 <Num s="S157">9.3 万</Num>名印度支那士兵与劳工；约 <Num s="S151,S150">14 万</Num>名中国工人以<Term id="labour-corps">劳工团</Term>的形式来到法国。

1916 年 4 月 24 日，爱尔兰共和派在都柏林起义，反抗英国统治。六天里共有 <Num s="S158">485</Num> 人死亡，一半以上是平民；事后 <Num s="S160">16</Num> 名领导人被处决。

<More title={{ en: "Jutland: who won?", zh: "日德兰：谁赢了？" }}>

德国海军上将赖因哈德·舍尔原想引出英国大舰队的一部分加以歼灭，结果碰上了整个大舰队。当夜，他把舰队带回了威廉港。

德国庆祝胜利：它击沉的舰船更多，打死的人是自己损失的两倍多——英方 <Num s="S142">6,000</Num> 多人，德方约 <Num s="S142">2,500</Num> 人。

在英国，这一仗很快被看作战略上的成功：德国舰队在余下的战争里大多留在港内，封锁照旧进行。德国转而依靠潜艇。历史学者至今仍在权衡这两种评价。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Hunger and the law of the sea", zh: "饥饿与海上法" }}>

封锁之下，德国无法再进口足够的粮食。1916 年土豆歉收，人们整个冬天只能吃芜菁，官方配给每天不足 <Num s="S146">1,000</Num> 卡。历史学者也指出德国自身的失误：余粮地区的农民囤积土豆或拿去喂牲口，而城市却在挨饿。

德国称之为违反国际法的“饥饿封锁”，因为它让妇女和儿童挨饿。德国 1918 年 12 月的研究给出的死亡人数是 <Num s="S145">762,796</Num>。历史学者艾伦·克雷默的结论是：封锁没有违反国际法的条文，却违背了国际法保护平民的精神。

英国的回答是：封锁没有击沉船只，也没有直接杀死任何人；而德国 U 艇不加警告地击沉商船和邮轮，淹死了乘客和船员。德国则说，卢西塔尼亚号运载着战争物资；船上确实载有一批弹药。水面舰队的封锁和潜艇的封锁，针对的都是对方的全体人口。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The Chinese Labour Corps", zh: "华工团" }}>

英国从 1916 年起通过租借地威海卫招募华工，大多来自山东；法国则通过中国公司招募。中国当时是中立国，所以华工是签合同的平民：英国合同三年，法国合同五年。1916 年至 1918 年，约 <Num s="S151,S150">4 万</Num>人为法国工作，约 <Num s="S151,S150">10 万</Num>人为英国工作。

英方第一批 <Num s="S153">1,086</Num> 名华工于 1917 年 1 月 18 日从威海卫启航，绕过好望角抵达勒阿弗尔。此后大多数华工横渡太平洋到加拿大不列颠哥伦比亚，再乘火车横穿加拿大：1917 年 3 月至 1918 年 3 月，有 <Num s="S152">84,422</Num> 人经加拿大前往法国。

他们卸船、修路、修铁路、修理坦克、挖战壕；停战后又清理战场上的炮弹和遗体。英方华工团的大本营和医院设在索姆省的努瓦耶勒，许多死者就葬在那里。葬在法国的华工近 <Num s="S151">2,000</Num> 人，另有 <Num s="S151">700</Num> 多人因所乘船只被击沉而死在海上，共约 <Num s="S151">3,000</Num> 人。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Soldiers and workers from the empires", zh: "来自各帝国的士兵与劳工" }}>

印度派出的人数在各殖民地中最多：约 <Num s="S155">130 万</Num>名士兵和劳工，其中 <Num s="S155">7.4 万</Num>多人死亡；<Num s="S154">14 万</Num>人在西线作战。法国在西非招募了 <Num s="S156">19.2 万</Num>名塞内加尔步兵，其中 <Num s="S156">13.4 万</Num>人在欧洲作战，约 <Num s="S156">3 万</Num>人死亡；征兵在西非遭到反抗。印度支那来了约 <Num s="S157">4.4 万</Num>名士兵和 <Num s="S157">4.9 万</Num>名劳工，大多是越南人。

他们并非都是自愿而来：法国给殖民地各地区规定征募名额；殖民地劳工和华工的工资很快就成了法国工业中最低的一档。1917 年 2 月 21 日，运兵船“门迪号”在怀特岛外与他船相撞沉没，南非土著劳工团的 <Num s="S161">607</Num> 名黑人随船遇难。

</More>
