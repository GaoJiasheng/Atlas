The peace of Brest-Litovsk on 3 March 1918 took Russia out of the war. Russia gave up Poland, the Baltic lands and Finland and recognised an independent Ukraine; by one Russian newspaper's count it lost <Num s="S195">32</Num> per cent of its population and <Num s="S195">89</Num> per cent of its coal. German and Austro-Hungarian troops occupied Ukraine.

With Russia out of the war, Germany turned west to try to win before American troops arrived in large numbers. On 21 March, after a five-hour bombardment by more than <Num s="S196">6,600</Num> guns, <Term id="stormtroopers">storm troops</Term> broke through the British front; the British lost <Num s="S196">38,500</Num> men that day, almost <Num s="S196">21,000</Num> of them as prisoners. Further offensives struck Flanders in April and the Aisne in May. The Germans advanced up to <Num s="S197">64</Num> km but could not break the Allied armies, and lost about <Num s="S196,S197">240,000</Num> men in the first offensive alone.

On 18 July the Allies counter-attacked on the Marne, and on 8 August British Empire troops with about <Num s="S197">450</Num> tanks broke through at Amiens, "the black day of the German army". In the Hundred Days that followed the Allied armies advanced steadily; about <Num s="S192">1.2 million</Num> Americans attacked in the Meuse-Argonne. Germany's allies gave up one by one: Bulgaria signed an armistice on 29 September, the Ottoman Empire on 30 October and Austria-Hungary on 3 November.

Sailors in Kiel revolted, workers' and soldiers' councils spread, and on 9 November Germany became a republic. On 11 November German delegates signed the armistice in a railway carriage near Compiègne, and at 11 a.m. the guns fell silent. That autumn an influenza pandemic was at its deadliest. It killed between about <Num s="S217">17 million</Num> and at least <Num s="S216">50 million</Num> people, at least <Num s="S218">12 million</Num> of them in India and more than <Num s="S219">2,870</Num> in Singapore.

The German commander Erich Ludendorff did not try to destroy every trench before attacking. On 21 March a five-hour "hurricane" bombardment hit artillery and machine-gun positions, headquarters and telephone exchanges, then storm troops slipped through weak points and pushed on, leaving strongpoints to the troops behind them. The same infiltration tactics had broken the Italian front at Caporetto in 1917.

The first offensive, Michael, cost the British <Num s="S197">178,000</Num> men, the French <Num s="S197">77,000</Num> and the Germans <Num s="S197">240,000</Num>. From 23 March a long-range gun shelled Paris from about <Num s="S199">120</Num> km away; its shells killed <Num s="S199">256</Num> people, <Num s="S199">88</Num> of them in the church of Saint-Gervais on Good Friday.

At Amiens the Allies combined more than <Num s="S197">2,000</Num> guns, about <Num s="S197">450</Num> tanks and <Num s="S197">1,900</Num> aircraft with surprise. On the first day the Australians advanced <Num s="S200">11</Num> km and the Canadians nearly <Num s="S200">13</Num> km; by 11 August <Num s="S201">29,144</Num> German soldiers had been taken prisoner. The British crossed the St Quentin Canal and the Hindenburg Line on 29 September. Between 8 August and 11 November the British armies alone took <Num s="S202">187,000</Num> prisoners and <Num s="S202">2,850</Num> guns.

American troops attacked at St Mihiel on 12 September with <Num s="S203">14</Num> American and <Num s="S203">4</Num> French divisions and nearly <Num s="S203">1,500</Num> aircraft, then in the Meuse-Argonne from 26 September, where more than <Num s="S204">26,000</Num> Americans were killed. In all, <Num s="S205">116,516</Num> Americans died in service, <Num s="S205">63,114</Num> of them from disease and other causes.

On the other fronts, an Allied army of <Num s="S207">650,000</Num> at Salonika, including Vietnamese, Senegalese and Malagasy colonial troops, broke the Bulgarian line at Dobro Pole on 15 September. In Palestine and Syria the British-led army took <Num s="S206">75,000</Num> prisoners between 19 September and 30 October. In Italy the Italians reported <Num s="S211">400,000</Num> prisoners at Vittorio Veneto; the historian Manfried Rauchensteiner puts the true number at about <Num s="S211">360,000</Num>. <More title={{ en: "The armistice, and the 'stab-in-the-back' myth", zh: "停战协定与“背后一刀”传说" }}>

The armistice of 11 November was not yet a peace. Germany had to leave the occupied countries within <Num s="S214">15</Num> days and the left bank of the Rhine within <Num s="S214">31</Num> days, and hand over <Num s="S214">5,000</Num> guns, <Num s="S214">25,000</Num> machine guns, <Num s="S214">1,700</Num> aircraft, <Num s="S214">5,000</Num> locomotives, <Num s="S214">150,000</Num> railway wagons and all its submarines. The blockade went on until the peace treaty. In Africa, Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck heard of the armistice late and surrendered at Abercorn on 25 November with <Num s="S223">1,168</Num> African soldiers, <Num s="S223">1,522</Num> porters and <Num s="S223">819</Num> women.

After the war, German nationalists claimed that the army had not been beaten in the field but "stabbed in the back" by revolutionaries at home. Field Marshal Paul von Hindenburg repeated the claim before a parliamentary inquiry on 18 November 1919. The record does not support it. On 29 September 1918, after the breakthrough at the Hindenburg Line and the collapse of Bulgaria, Ludendorff himself told the Kaiser that the war was lost and demanded an immediate armistice; the army command asked for one in early October, a month before the sailors' revolt. The myth was used later to attack the new republic.

<More title={{ en: "The influenza pandemic", zh: "流感大流行" }}>

The pandemic came in three waves. One of the first recorded outbreaks was in March 1918, when more than <Num s="S216">100</Num> soldiers fell ill at Camp Funston in Kansas; the second wave, from late summer, was the deadliest, and a third followed in early 1919. About <Num s="S216">500 million</Num> people, a third of the world's population, were infected. Estimates of the dead differ widely: the US Centers for Disease Control give at least <Num s="S216">50 million</Num>, a 2018 study <Num s="S217">17.4 million</Num>. India lost at least <Num s="S218">12 million</Num>, more than any other country, and the United States about <Num s="S216">675,000</Num>.

Unusually, many of the dead were young adults, and the armies lost many men: about <Num s="S244">32,000</Num> French soldiers died of it. It was called the "Spanish flu" because neutral Spain's newspapers, free of wartime censorship, reported it openly, not because it began there.

In Singapore the disease arrived with wartime troops in June 1918 and returned, worse, in October and November. A 2007 study estimated more than <Num s="S219">2,870</Num> deaths in a population of about <Num s="S219">370,000</Num>, against the <Num s="S219">844</Num> influenza deaths in the official annual report.
