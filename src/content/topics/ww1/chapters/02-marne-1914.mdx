<Lang en>

Germany's war plan sent most of its army west through neutral Belgium to defeat France quickly. On 4 August German troops crossed the border near Liège, whose <Num s="S35">twelve</Num> forts held out until 16 August, <Num s="S36">eleven days</Num> instead of two. The Belgian army fell back to Antwerp.

As they advanced, German soldiers killed civilians, claiming they were francs-tireurs, guerrillas firing from houses. From August to October 1914 they deliberately killed <Num s="S37">5,521</Num> civilians in Belgium and <Num s="S37">906</Num> in France. At Tamines <Num s="S39">383</Num> people were killed, at Dinant <Num s="S37,S39">674</Num>, one in ten of the town, and at Leuven <Num s="S39">248</Num>, where the university library burned with some <Num s="S40">300,000</Num> books. Historians found almost no shooting by civilians. More than <Num s="S55">1.5 million</Num> Belgians fled their homes.

Further south, French attacks in Lorraine and the Ardennes failed; on 22 August alone more than <Num s="S41,S42">27,000</Num> French soldiers were killed. On 23 August the British Expeditionary Force of about <Num s="S45">70,000</Num> men met the German First Army at Mons. Then the French and British retreated for two weeks.

By early September German armies were east of Paris. On 5 September the French and British counter-attacked along the Marne, with about <Num s="S48">2 million</Num> men fighting. A gap opened between two German armies, and on 9 September they began to retreat. About <Num s="S48">500,000</Num> soldiers were killed or wounded.

Each side then tried to outflank the other. After the First Battle of Ypres in October and November a line of trenches about <Num s="S46">750 km</Num> long ran from the Channel to Switzerland: <Term id="trench-warfare">trench warfare</Term> had begun.

<More title={{ en: "Why go through Belgium?", zh: "为什么要借道比利时？" }}>

Germany expected to fight France and Russia at once. Its plan, drawn up under Alfred von Schlieffen and changed by his successor Helmuth von Moltke, was to defeat France in the time Russia needed to mobilise. The French border was short and fortified, so the German right wing would swing through flat Belgium and come down on Paris from the north. Belgium's neutrality had been guaranteed by the great powers, Prussia among them, in 1839. When Belgium refused to let German troops pass, Germany invaded, and Britain declared war. The Belgian army held at Liège for <Num s="S36">eleven days</Num>, then at Antwerp until October and finally behind the flooded Yser, where it stayed for the rest of the war.

</More>

Between August and October 1914 German troops killed civilians in <Num s="S37">129</Num> separate incidents of ten or more dead. Some of the largest:

| Place | Date | Killed |
|---|---|---|
| Liège and surrounding villages | 5–8 August | nearly <Num s="S36">850</Num> |
| Andenne | 20 August | <Num s="S56">217</Num> (with <Num s="S56">43</Num> more at Seilles) |
| Tamines | 22 August | <Num s="S39">383</Num> |
| Dinant | 23 August | <Num s="S37,S39">674</Num> |
| Leuven (Louvain) | 25–28 August | <Num s="S39">248</Num> |

The Belgian commission of inquiry counted <Num s="S37">5,517</Num> victims in Belgium; the historians John Horne and Alan Kramer, working from German as well as Belgian and French records, counted <Num s="S37">5,521</Num> in Belgium and <Num s="S37">906</Num> in France, about <Num s="S38">6,500</Num> in all. Victims included women, small children and old people. The German army said its soldiers had been shot at by civilians. Horne and Kramer found that the shots came from French and Belgian soldiers or from German troops firing on each other in panic, and that the belief in a guerrilla war spread among soldiers who had been told to expect one. In Germany the accusations were denied for decades; in 2001 a German government minister asked the people of Dinant for forgiveness. The names of the dead are on memorials in each town. They are remembered because the killings showed how quickly soldiers' fear could be turned against people who could not defend themselves.

Totals for the opening months differ with what is counted: more than <Num s="S44">250,000</Num> French soldiers died in the battles of August and September, and <Num s="S43">310,000</Num> in the first four months. At Mons on 23–24 August the British lost about <Num s="S45">4,200</Num> men and the Germans about <Num s="S45">4,900</Num>; at Le Cateau on 26 August the British lost <Num s="S47">7,812</Num>. Estimates of the BEF's size run from <Num s="S45">70,000</Num> to about <Num s="S46">90,000</Num>, and by the end of 1914 it had suffered over <Num s="S46">90,000</Num> casualties. On the Marne, some French reinforcements came from Paris in about <Num s="S49">600</Num> taxis, perhaps <Num s="S49">3,000</Num> to <Num s="S44">5,500</Num> men; most came by train. At First Ypres Germany lost about <Num s="S50">130,000</Num> men and the Entente about <Num s="S50">100,000</Num>: more than <Num s="S51">54,000</Num> British, at least <Num s="S51">50,000</Num> French and more than <Num s="S51">20,000</Num> Belgians. <More title={{ en: "The Christmas truce", zh: "圣诞休战" }}>

On 24 and 25 December 1914 soldiers in some sections of the Western Front stopped fighting on their own. British and German troops sang carols across no man's land, met between the trenches, exchanged food and buried their dead; in a few places they played football. It was not one organised truce but a series of local ones, and in many places the fighting went on. Commanders on both sides ordered that it must not happen again.

</More>

德国的作战计划，是让大部分军队向西穿过中立的比利时，迅速打败法国。8 月 4 日，德军在列日附近越过边境。列日的 <Num s="S35">12</Num> 座要塞一直守到 8 月 16 日，用了 <Num s="S36">11 天</Num>，而德国原计划只要两天。比利时军队退守安特卫普。

德军一路推进，一路杀害平民，声称他们是从房子里放冷枪的“游击队员”。1914 年 8 月到 10 月，德军在比利时蓄意杀害平民 <Num s="S37">5,521</Num> 人，在法国 <Num s="S37">906</Num> 人。塔明有 <Num s="S39">383</Num> 人被杀；迪南有 <Num s="S37,S39">674</Num> 人，占全城人口的十分之一；鲁汶有 <Num s="S39">248</Num> 人，大学图书馆连同约 <Num s="S40">30 万</Num>册藏书被焚毁。历史学家的研究表明，当时几乎没有平民开枪。超过 <Num s="S55">150 万</Num>比利时人逃离家园。

在更南面，法军在洛林和阿登的进攻失败了，仅 8 月 22 日一天，法军阵亡就超过 <Num s="S41,S42">2.7 万</Num>人。8 月 23 日，约 <Num s="S45">7 万</Num>人的英国远征军在蒙斯与德军第 1 集团军交战。随后，法军和英军连续撤退了两个星期。

9 月初，德军已经到了巴黎以东。9 月 5 日，法军和英军沿马恩河发起反击，双方投入约 <Num s="S48">200 万</Num>人。德军两个集团军之间出现缺口，9 月 9 日德军开始后撤。这一仗双方死伤约 <Num s="S48">50 万</Num>人。

此后双方都想从侧面包抄对方。10 月到 11 月的第一次伊普尔战役之后，一条约 <Num s="S46">750 公里</Num>长的堑壕线从英吉利海峡一直延伸到瑞士，<Term id="trench-warfare">堑壕战</Term>开始了。

<More title={{ en: "Why go through Belgium?", zh: "为什么要借道比利时？" }}>

德国预计要同时对法国和俄国作战。它的计划由施利芬主持制定、继任者小毛奇修改，要在俄国完成动员所需的时间里先打败法国。法德边境短、要塞多，于是德军右翼要穿过地势平坦的比利时，从北面南下直扑巴黎。比利时的中立在 1839 年由包括普鲁士在内的列强共同保证。比利时拒绝借道，德国便入侵比利时，英国随即宣战。比利时军队在列日坚守了 <Num s="S36">11 天</Num>，又在安特卫普守到 10 月，最后退到被河水淹没的伊瑟河后面，在那里一直坚持到战争结束。

<More title={{ en: "The Christmas truce", zh: "圣诞休战" }}>

1914 年 12 月 24 日和 25 日，西线部分地段的士兵自发停止了战斗。英军和德军隔着两军之间的无人区唱圣诞颂歌，走出战壕见面，交换食物，掩埋阵亡者；有几处还踢了足球。这不是一次统一安排的休战，而是一连串局部的停火，许多地方的战斗照常进行。双方的指挥官事后都下令，不许再发生这种事。
