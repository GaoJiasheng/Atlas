<Lang en>

Keep the air setting and repetition rate unchanged. The card now changes <Term id="amplitude">amplitude</Term>, the greatest displacement from a rest position. Watch the highlighted marker move farther from its tick at the larger setting. It still completes each cycle at the same pace. Extent of motion and rate of repetition are separate features. [S4]

The model’s relative settings are <Num s="S1">1</Num> and <Num s="S1">2</Num>. Selecting the larger one doubles the chosen displacement, while the input remains <Num s="S1">250 Hz</Num>. You can compare the largest excursions or pause at a visible instant. A point passing through its rest position has a small instantaneous displacement even when its full oscillation has a large amplitude. [S1, S4]

<Term id="loudness">Loudness</Term> is how strong or weak a sound seems to a listener. Under comparable conditions a sound wave with greater amplitude carries greater intensity and is generally heard as louder. This connection does not make loudness the same quantity as displacement, and it does not turn this silent model into a listening measurement. [S6]

Return to the smaller setting and record both observations. The displayed repetition rate stays unchanged. Comparing only the width of the motion prevents a larger excursion from being mistaken for a higher pitch. This is why the experiment holds frequency fixed while you change amplitude. [S1, S6]

The readout is relative motion, not a decibel level. Doubling this setting does not promise that a sound would seem exactly twice as loud. Keep device volume unchanged: the visual comparison supplies all the information needed for this experiment. [S1, S6]
