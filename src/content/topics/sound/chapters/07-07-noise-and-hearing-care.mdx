The final station has a speaker symbol and hearing protectors. <Term id="noise">Noise</Term> is sound that is unwanted or interferes with an activity. That everyday description is different from a judgment about harmful exposure. Very loud bursts and prolonged loud sound can damage hearing, including music you enjoy. [S10, S13]

Damage can follow a very intense brief sound or extended exposure to loud sound. Hearing changes may be temporary or permanent, and a gradual change can be difficult to notice at first. Being comfortable with a familiar sound does not establish that its level is suitable. The bench cannot measure what reaches a listener. [S13]

<Term id="hearing-care">Hearing care</Term> includes reducing sound level, limiting exposure time and moving away from loud sources. In noisy places, suitable hearing protection and quiet breaks can help. The farther speaker pose is a reminder to increase separation; no personal dose or safe duration is calculated from its position. [S1, S10]

If ringing persists or following conversations becomes difficult, tell a trusted adult and seek professional advice. This silent page offers no hearing test and cannot diagnose a change in hearing. A conversation about symptoms does not require first proving a cause with the model. [S10]

People use hearing, vision and other information in different ways. All of this topic’s comparisons can be completed with text and visible motion, without detecting any played sound. To follow the receiving system, continue to <TopicLink slug="eyes-and-ears" ch="vibration-to-signal">Eyes and Ears</TopicLink>. Keep the distinction between understanding sound and deciding whether real exposure is suitable. [S1, S23]

<More title={{"en": "Device reference modes", "zh": "设备参考模式"}}>

The WHO–ITU standard distinguishes an adult reference mode of <Num s="S11">80 dB for 40 hours per week</Num> and a child mode of <Num s="S11">75 dB for 40 hours per week</Num>. These are device-design reference exposures, not a personalised guarantee. This page does not convert them into a timer or infer decibels from a volume percentage.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Singapore construction controls", "zh": "新加坡施工噪音管理"}}>

NEA construction-noise controls use specified averaging intervals, affected-building categories and time periods. Work restrictions also have stated exceptions. These environmental rules have a different purpose from personal listening guidance; their limits cannot be read as a child’s headphone allowance. [S14, S15]

</More>
