<Lang en>

You have placed a <Term id="fraction">fraction</Term> on a <Term id="number-line">number line</Term> by counting equal jumps from 0. You used the stretch from 0 to 1 as one <Term id="whole">whole</Term>; now you will compare fractions of that same whole.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the rows of the fraction wall</Beat>. Each row is the same length, so each shows the same whole. The rows are cut into different numbers of <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>. As you look further down, there are more parts, and each part is narrower.

Now <Beat n={2}>compare the two highlighted pieces</Beat>. Each is a <Term id="unit-fraction">unit fraction</Term>, which names one equal part. A third is one of 3 parts; a fifth is one of 5. Cutting the same whole into more parts makes each part smaller. The third is wider, so <Frac n={1} d={3} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={5} />.

Next, <Beat n={3}>count the shaded sevenths</Beat> in the two bars. Both wholes are the same length and cut into 7 equal parts. These are <Term id="like-fractions">like fractions</Term>: their bottom numbers, or <Term id="denominator">denominators</Term>, match. Each part is the same size, so compare the top numbers, or <Term id="numerator">numerators</Term>. Three sevenths are fewer than five sevenths: <Frac n={3} d={7} /> &lt; <Frac n={5} d={7} />. The wide side of the sign faces the greater fraction.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>look at the half, quarter and eighth</Beat> on the wall. Each takes one part of the same whole. The half is widest, the quarter is next, and the eighth is narrowest. From greatest to smallest, you write <Frac n={1} d={2} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={4} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={8} />, because fewer equal parts mean larger pieces.

A common mistake is to think a bigger denominator always makes a greater fraction. For unit fractions, it means more equal parts in the same whole, so each part is smaller.

<More title={{ en: "Why the same whole matters", zh: "为什么要同一个整体" }}>

Half of a small prata can be less than a quarter of a very large one. That does not make <Frac n={1} d={4} /> greater than <Frac n={1} d={2} />: the two wholes are different. Fractions can only be compared when they are parts of the same whole, which is why every comparison in this lesson uses bars of the same length.

</More>

<Lang zh>

你已经会在<Term id="number-line">数轴</Term>上从 0 开始数段，放好一个<Term id="fraction">分数</Term>。那时你把 0 到 1 当作一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term>；现在来比较同一个整体的几个分数。

看<Beat n={1}>分数墙的每一行</Beat>。每一行一样长，所以表示的是同一个整体。各行被<Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>的份数不同：越往下，份数越多，每一份就越窄。

现在<Beat n={2}>比较亮起来的两块</Beat>。它们都是<Term id="unit-fraction">单位分数</Term>，各表示平均分出来的一份。三分之一是 3 份中的 1 份，五分之一是 5 份中的 1 份。同一个整体分得越多，每份就越小。三分之一那一块更宽，所以 <Frac n={1} d={3} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={5} />。

接着<Beat n={3}>数两条上涂色的七分之几</Beat>。两个整体一样长，都平均分成 7 份。这样的分数叫<Term id="like-fractions">同分母分数</Term>：下面的数，也就是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，相同。每份一样大，就比上面的数，也就是<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>。3 个七分之一比 5 个七分之一少：<Frac n={3} d={7} /> &lt; <Frac n={5} d={7} />。符号开口的一边朝着大的数。

最后看墙上的<Beat n={4}>二分之一、四分之一和八分之一</Beat>。它们都是同一个整体里的一份。二分之一最宽，四分之一其次，八分之一最窄。从大到小写作 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={4} /> &gt; <Frac n={1} d={8} />，因为平均分的份数越少，每份越大。

常见的错误是以为分母越大，分数就越大。对单位分数来说，分母大，说明同一个整体分成的份数多，每一份反而更小。

<More title={{ en: "Why the same whole matters", zh: "为什么要同一个整体" }}>

一张小 prata 的一半，可能比一张很大的 prata 的四分之一还少。但这并不说明 <Frac n={1} d={4} /> 比 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> 大：两个整体不一样大。只有同一个整体的几部分，才能这样比较，所以这一课里比较的纸条都一样长。

</More>
