<Lang en>

You already know how to divide a <Term id="whole">whole</Term> into <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>. Now you can give one of those parts a name.

Look at <Beat n={1}>one shaded part of the prata</Beat>. You can see 4 parts of the same size. One of them is one quarter, written <Frac n={1} d={4} />. Each part is a quarter because all 4 together make the whole prata. A <Term id="fraction">fraction</Term> with 1 on top is a <Term id="unit-fraction">unit fraction</Term>: it names one equal part.

Next, <Beat n={2}>match the two numbers to the prata</Beat>. Count all 4 equal parts first. The bottom number, the <Term id="denominator">denominator</Term>, is 4 because it counts every part in the whole. Then count the shaded part. The top number, the <Term id="numerator">numerator</Term>, is 1 because you take just one part. Keep these numbers in their places: swapping them changes what they say.

Now look at <Beat n={3}>one shaded part of the strip</Beat>. Count from one end to the other: there are 6 equal parts. One is shaded, so you write <Frac n={1} d={6} />, one sixth. The shape has changed, but you still count all the parts below and the part taken above.

Then <Beat n={4}>look at the strip in 8 equal parts</Beat>. Its total length stays the same. Each part must be smaller because more parts fit into that same length. One shaded part is now <Frac n={1} d={8} />, one eighth. So <Frac n={1} d={8} /> is smaller than <Frac n={1} d={6} />: a larger bottom number does not mean a larger part.

A common mistake is to count only the unshaded parts for the denominator. If a strip has 1 shaded part and 4 unshaded parts, count all 5 equal parts: it shows <Frac n={1} d={5} />, not <Frac n={1} d={4} />, because the shaded part belongs to the whole too.

<More title={{ en: "Saying fractions", zh: "分数怎么读" }}>

In English we say the top number first: one half, one third, one quarter, one fifth. In Chinese the bottom number comes first: 二分之一 means "of 2 parts, 1". The Chinese order follows the counting order of this step: first the parts in the whole, then the parts we take.

</More>

<Lang zh>

你已经会把一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term><Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>了。现在给其中的一份起个名字。

看<Beat n={1}>prata 上涂色的一份</Beat>。一共有 4 份，每份一样大。其中 1 份就是四分之一，写作 <Frac n={1} d={4} />。每一份是四分之一，因为 4 份合起来正好是整张 prata。分子是 1 的<Term id="fraction">分数</Term>叫<Term id="unit-fraction">单位分数</Term>，它说的是平均分出来的一份。

接着，<Beat n={2}>把两个数和 prata 对上</Beat>。先数一共有几份：4 份。下面的数叫<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，是 4，因为它数的是整体里所有的份。再数涂色的份：上面的数叫<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>，是 1，因为只取了 1 份。两个数的位置不能换：上下颠倒，意思就完全变了。

再看<Beat n={3}>纸条上涂色的一份</Beat>。从一头数到另一头，一共有 6 份。涂了 1 份，所以写作 <Frac n={1} d={6} />，读作六分之一。形状变了，方法没变：下面写一共几份，上面写取了几份。

然后看<Beat n={4}>平均分成 8 份的纸条</Beat>。纸条的长度没有变，可是同样长的纸条里要放下更多份，每一份就更小。现在涂色的 1 份是 <Frac n={1} d={8} />，八分之一。所以 <Frac n={1} d={8} /> 比 <Frac n={1} d={6} /> 小：分母大，并不说明每份大。

常见的错误是只数没涂色的份来写分母。如果纸条涂了 1 份，还有 4 份没涂，要数全部 5 份：它表示 <Frac n={1} d={5} />，不是 <Frac n={1} d={4} />，因为涂色的那一份也是整体的一部分。

<More title={{ en: "Saying fractions", zh: "分数怎么读" }}>

英语先读上面的数：one half、one third、one quarter、one fifth。中文先读下面的数："二分之一"的意思是"分成 2 份，取 1 份"。中文的读法正好和这一步数的顺序一样：先数整体有几份，再数取了几份。

</More>
