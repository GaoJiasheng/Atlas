<Lang en>

In the last chapter, you made the parts the same size before adding <Term id="related-fractions">related fractions</Term>. You use the same idea before taking an amount away.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the two rows</Beat> for <Frac n={7} d={8} /> − <Frac n={1} d={2} />. Both rows show the same <Term id="whole">whole</Term>, but their cuts are different. The top row shows seven eighths. The bottom row shows one half. A half is larger than an eighth, so you need to find how many eighths to take away.

Now <Beat n={2}>split the half into eighths</Beat>. Each half becomes four <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>, so <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={4} d={8} />. Look at the shaded length: it has not changed. These are <Term id="equivalent-fractions">equivalent fractions</Term>, two names for the same amount. Both rows now use eighths, so you can count parts of the same size.

Next, <Beat n={3}>look at the crossed-out parts</Beat>. Four of the seven shaded eighths are taken away. Count the three shaded eighths left: <Frac n={7} d={8} /> − <Frac n={4} d={8} /> = <Frac n={3} d={8} />. Subtract the top numbers, called the <Term id="numerator">numerators</Term>. Keep the bottom number, the <Term id="denominator">denominator</Term>, at eight because the whole is still cut into eighths.

You can also subtract to <Beat n={4}>find how much more</Beat>. Ravi drank <Frac n={3} d={4} /> of a bottle and Wei Jie drank <Frac n={5} d={8} /> of a bottle of the same size. Look at their bars, lined up on the left. Rename Ravi's amount: <Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={6} d={8} />. The extra piece is <Frac n={6} d={8} /> − <Frac n={5} d={8} /> = <Frac n={1} d={8} /> of a bottle.

A common mistake is to subtract both top and bottom numbers: taking parts away changes their number, not their size. Another is to take away just one eighth for one half; the half covers four eighths.

If asked, write your answer in <Term id="simplest-form">simplest form</Term> by dividing both numbers by the same number until only 1 divides both.

<More title={{ en: "Two kinds of model", zh: "两种模型图" }}>

A part–whole model has one bar: the parts sit side by side inside the whole, and the question mark is on a part or on the total. A comparison model has two bars of the same whole, lined up on the left; the question mark is on the extra piece of the longer one. The difference is the piece that sticks out.

</More>

<Lang zh>

上一章，你先把份化成一样大，再把<Term id="related-fractions">相关分数</Term>相加。拿走一个量之前，也用同样的办法。

看 <Frac n={7} d={8} /> − <Frac n={1} d={2} /> 的<Beat n={1}>两行纸条</Beat>。两行是同一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term>，只是分法不同。上面一行是八分之七，下面一行是二分之一。二分之一比八分之一大，所以要先弄清楚：拿走的是几个八分之一。

现在<Beat n={2}>把二分之一化成八分之几</Beat>。每个二分之一<Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>成 4 份，所以 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={4} d={8} />。看涂色部分的长短：没有变。这是<Term id="equivalent-fractions">等值分数</Term>，同一个量的两个名字。现在两行都是八分之几，可以数一样大的份了。

接着看<Beat n={3}>被划掉的部分</Beat>。涂色的 7 个八分之一里拿走 4 个，数一数还剩 3 个：<Frac n={7} d={8} /> − <Frac n={4} d={8} /> = <Frac n={3} d={8} />。上面的数，也就是<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>，相减；下面的数，也就是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，还是 8，因为整体还是分成八分之几。

减法也能<Beat n={4}>求多多少</Beat>。Ravi 喝了 <Frac n={3} d={4} /> 瓶水，Wei Jie 喝了 <Frac n={5} d={8} /> 瓶，两瓶一样大。看两人的长条，左端对齐。先把 Ravi 的量换个说法：<Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={6} d={8} />。多出来的一段是 <Frac n={6} d={8} /> − <Frac n={5} d={8} /> = <Frac n={1} d={8} /> 瓶。

常见的错误是把分子分母都相减：拿走一些份，变的是份数，不是每份的大小。另一个错误是把二分之一当成一个八分之一来拿；其实二分之一是 4 个八分之一。

如果题目要求，就把答案写成<Term id="simplest-form">最简分数</Term>：分子分母同时除以同一个数，直到除了 1 没有数能同时整除它们。
