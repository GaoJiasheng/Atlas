<Lang en>

You have compared <Term id="like-fractions">like fractions</Term> by counting parts of the same size in the same <Term id="whole">whole</Term>. Now you can add those parts or take some away.

Look at the <Beat n={1}>two shaded parts</Beat> of the strip. The strip has 7 <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>, so each part is one seventh. You have 2 sevenths shaded. The <Term id="fraction">fraction</Term> <Frac n={2} d={7} /> names those 2 parts out of all 7.

Next, <Beat n={2}>add three more sevenths</Beat> by shading 3 unshaded parts. Count the first 2 parts and the 3 added parts together. There are now 5 shaded parts, each still one seventh: <Frac n={2} d={7} /> + <Frac n={3} d={7} /> = <Frac n={5} d={7} />.

Look at the <Beat n={3}>labels on the parts</Beat>. Sevenths plus sevenths are still sevenths because you have not cut the strip again. The bottom number, the <Term id="denominator">denominator</Term>, stays 7. Only the top number, the <Term id="numerator">numerator</Term>, changes: 2 + 3 = 5. It counts the shaded parts.

For taking away, <Beat n={4}>start with seven ninths</Beat>. This strip has 9 equal parts, with 7 shaded, so it shows <Frac n={7} d={9} />. Each shaded part is one ninth. You will take away some shaded parts while keeping the same whole strip.

Now <Beat n={5}>cross out four shaded parts</Beat> to take away <Frac n={4} d={9} />. Count only the shaded parts without crosses: 3 are left. So <Frac n={7} d={9} /> − <Frac n={4} d={9} /> = <Frac n={3} d={9} />. The whole still has 9 equal parts, so the parts left are still ninths. Both examples stay within one whole.

A common mistake is to add or subtract the denominators too. Adding them would give <Frac n={5} d={14} />, but the strip still has 7 equal parts, not 14: shading or crossing out changes how many parts you count, not their size.

<More title={{ en: "Saying it in words", zh: "用话来说" }}>

Say the addition in words: "2 sevenths and 3 sevenths make 5 sevenths", just as 2 apples and 3 apples make 5 apples. "Sevenths" is the name of the parts, like "apples". The name stays the same; only the number in front of it changes. For taking away, say: "7 ninths take away 4 ninths leaves 3 ninths."

</More>

<Lang zh>

你已经会比较<Term id="like-fractions">同分母分数</Term>：数同一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term>里一样大的份。现在把这些份加起来，或者拿走一些。

看纸条上<Beat n={1}>涂色的 2 份</Beat>。纸条<Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>成 7 份，每份是七分之一。涂了 2 个七分之一，<Term id="fraction">分数</Term> <Frac n={2} d={7} /> 说的就是全部 7 份中的这 2 份。

接着，<Beat n={2}>再加 3 个七分之一</Beat>：在没涂色的地方再涂 3 份。把原来的 2 份和新涂的 3 份一起数，现在一共涂了 5 份，每份还是七分之一：<Frac n={2} d={7} /> + <Frac n={3} d={7} /> = <Frac n={5} d={7} />。

看<Beat n={3}>每份上的标注</Beat>。纸条没有再切，所以七分之几加七分之几，还是七分之几。下面的数是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，还是 7。变的只有上面的数，<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>：2 + 3 = 5，它数的是涂色的份数。

再看减法，<Beat n={4}>先看九分之七</Beat>。这条纸条平均分成 9 份，涂了 7 份，表示 <Frac n={7} d={9} />。每一份涂色的部分都是九分之一。我们要在同一条纸条上拿走几份涂色的部分。

现在<Beat n={5}>划掉 4 份涂色的部分</Beat>，也就是拿走 <Frac n={4} d={9} />。只数没被划掉的涂色部分：还剩 3 份。所以 <Frac n={7} d={9} /> − <Frac n={4} d={9} /> = <Frac n={3} d={9} />。整体还是 9 份，剩下的还是九分之几。两个例子的结果都没有超过一个整体。

常见的错误是把分母也加起来或减掉。把分母相加会得到 <Frac n={5} d={14} />，可是纸条还是 7 份，不是 14 份：涂色或划掉，改变的是数到几份，不改变每份的大小。

<More title={{ en: "Saying it in words", zh: "用话来说" }}>

把加法说出来："2 个七分之一加 3 个七分之一，是 5 个七分之一"，就像 2 个苹果加 3 个苹果是 5 个苹果。"七分之一"是每份的名字，就像"苹果"。名字不变，变的只是前面的数。减法也可以说："7 个九分之一拿走 4 个，还剩 3 个九分之一。"

</More>
