<Lang en>

You have learnt to write a <Term id="fraction">fraction</Term> in its <Term id="simplest-form">simplest form</Term> without changing its amount. Now you can use <Term id="equivalent-fractions">equivalent fractions</Term> to compare amounts when the parts are different sizes.

<Beat n={1}>Look at the two strips</Beat> showing <Frac n={2} d={3} /> and <Frac n={3} d={4} />. Both strips represent the same-sized <Term id="whole">whole</Term>. Each third is bigger than each quarter. The bottom numbers, or <Term id="denominator">denominators</Term>, differ, so these are unlike fractions. Comparing only the top numbers is a mistake: they count parts of different sizes. You need parts of the same size before you compare their counts.

<Beat n={2}>Cut both strips into twelfths</Beat>. Split each third into 4 <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>, and each quarter into 3. The 2 shaded thirds become 8 shaded twelfths; the 3 shaded quarters become 9. So <Frac n={2} d={3} /> = <Frac n={8} d={12} /> and <Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={9} d={12} />. You have added dividing lines, but the shaded lengths have not changed. Both strips now have matching parts.

<Beat n={3}>Compare the shaded twelfths</Beat>. The top numbers, or <Term id="numerator">numerators</Term>, are 8 and 9. Eight equal parts cover less than nine of the same parts, so <Frac n={8} d={12} /> &lt; <Frac n={9} d={12} />, and <Frac n={2} d={3} /> &lt; <Frac n={3} d={4} />. Thinking they are equal because both are one part short is another mistake. The missing third is bigger than the missing quarter, so less of that strip is shaded.

<Beat n={4}>Follow the points from left to right</Beat> on the <Term id="number-line">number line</Term>. The stretch from 0 to 1 has 8 equal jumps. You reach <Frac n={3} d={8} /> after 3 jumps, <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={4} d={8} /> after 4, and <Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={6} d={8} /> after 6. More jumps from zero mean a greater amount. From smallest to greatest, the order is <Frac n={3} d={8} />, <Frac n={1} d={2} />, <Frac n={3} d={4} />. Renaming them in eighths lets you compare all three using the same-sized parts.

<More title={{ en: "Same whole, same length", zh: "同一个整体，一样长" }}>

Every pair of bars on the stage is the same length, because both fractions are parts of the same whole. Lining them up, one under the other, is a quick first check: the shaded part that reaches further to the right is greater.

</More>

When you are ready, <Task id="comparing-unlike">try one question</Task> on comparing fractions by making their parts the same size.

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

你已经会在不改变大小的前提下，把<Term id="fraction">分数</Term>化成<Term id="simplest-form">最简分数</Term>。现在用<Term id="equivalent-fractions">等值分数</Term>来比较每份大小不同的两个量。

<Beat n={1}>看这两条纸条</Beat>，分别表示 <Frac n={2} d={3} /> 和 <Frac n={3} d={4} />。两条纸条是同样大的<Term id="whole">整体</Term>，可是三分之一比四分之一大。下面的数，也就是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，不一样，这叫异分母分数。只比上面的数是错的：它们数的是大小不同的份。先要让每份一样大，才能比份数。

<Beat n={2}>把两条都分成十二分之几</Beat>。每个三分之一分成 4 份，每个四分之一分成 3 份，都是<Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>。涂色的 2 个三分之一变成 8 个十二分之一，涂色的 3 个四分之一变成 9 个十二分之一。所以 <Frac n={2} d={3} /> = <Frac n={8} d={12} />，<Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={9} d={12} />。只是多画了分隔线，涂色部分的长短没有变。现在两条纸条的份一样大了。

<Beat n={3}>比较涂色的十二分之几</Beat>。上面的数，也就是<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>，是 8 和 9。8 份比 9 份少，所以 <Frac n={8} d={12} /> &lt; <Frac n={9} d={12} />，也就是 <Frac n={2} d={3} /> &lt; <Frac n={3} d={4} />。以为两个都差一份到整体、所以相等，也是错的。缺的三分之一比缺的四分之一大，所以那条纸条涂得少。

在<Term id="number-line">数轴</Term>上<Beat n={4}>从左往右看这几个点</Beat>。从 0 到 1 平均分成 8 段。跳 3 段到 <Frac n={3} d={8} />，跳 4 段到 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={4} d={8} />，跳 6 段到 <Frac n={3} d={4} /> = <Frac n={6} d={8} />。离 0 跳得越多，数越大。从小到大是 <Frac n={3} d={8} />、<Frac n={1} d={2} />、<Frac n={3} d={4} />。都化成八分之几，三个数就能用一样大的份来比了。

<More title={{ en: "Same whole, same length", zh: "同一个整体，一样长" }}>

舞台上每一对纸条都一样长，因为两个分数都是同一个整体的一部分。把它们上下对齐，是一个快速的检查：涂色部分伸得更靠右的那个更大。

</More>

准备好了，就<Task id="comparing-unlike">做一道小题</Task>：先把份化成一样大，再比较分数。
