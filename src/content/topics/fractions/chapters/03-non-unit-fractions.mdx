<Lang en>

You know that a <Term id="unit-fraction">unit fraction</Term> names one of the <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term> of a <Term id="whole">whole</Term>. Now you can name several of those parts together.

Look at <Beat n={1}>one shaded chocolate part</Beat>. The whole bar has 8 equal parts, so each part is <Frac n={1} d={8} /> of the bar. The shaded part is the same size as each unshaded part. You count all 8 parts to name one part, even though only one is shaded.

Next, look at <Beat n={2}>three shaded eighths</Beat> in the same bar. Each shaded part is still <Frac n={1} d={8} />. Together, they make <Frac n={1} d={8} /> + <Frac n={1} d={8} /> + <Frac n={1} d={8} /> = <Frac n={3} d={8} />. Read this <Term id="fraction">fraction</Term> as “three eighths”. The parts have not changed size, so the bottom number stays 8. The top number changes to 3 because you take three parts.

Now look at <Beat n={3}>five shaded cake slices</Beat>. The whole cake has 6 equal slices, and each is <Frac n={1} d={6} />. Five of these slices make <Frac n={5} d={6} /> of the cake. The top number, the <Term id="numerator">numerator</Term>, counts the 5 slices taken. The bottom number, the <Term id="denominator">denominator</Term>, counts all 6 slices, including the one that is not shaded.

Finally, look at <Beat n={4}>all four shaded quarters</Beat> of the strip. Each part is <Frac n={1} d={4} />, and no part is left unshaded. All 4 quarters together fill the strip, so <Frac n={4} d={4} /> = 1. When the numerator and denominator are the same, you have taken every equal part of one whole.

Counting only the 5 unshaded chocolate parts gives <Frac n={3} d={5} />, a mistake because the bottom number must count all 8 parts. Writing <Frac n={8} d={3} /> swaps the counts: 3 shaded parts belong on top, and all 8 parts belong at the bottom.

A rainbow kueh lapis is often made of 9 thin layers of the same thickness, so each layer is <Frac n={1} d={9} /> of its height. If 3 of the layers are red, the red part is <Frac n={3} d={9} /> of the height: 3 layers, each one ninth.

</More>

<Lang zh>

你已经知道，<Term id="unit-fraction">单位分数</Term>表示一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term>里<Term id="equal-parts">平均分</Term>出来的一份。现在把好几份合起来说。

看<Beat n={1}>巧克力上涂色的一块</Beat>。整条巧克力平均分成 8 块，每一块是整条的 <Frac n={1} d={8} />。涂色的那一块和没涂色的一样大。虽然只涂了一块，说出它的名字也要数全部 8 块。

再看同一条巧克力上<Beat n={2}>涂色的 3 个八分之一</Beat>。每一块还是 <Frac n={1} d={8} />。合起来是 <Frac n={1} d={8} /> + <Frac n={1} d={8} /> + <Frac n={1} d={8} /> = <Frac n={3} d={8} />，读作"八分之三"。每块的大小没有变，所以分母还是 8；取了 3 块，所以分子变成 3。这样的数也叫<Term id="fraction">分数</Term>，它是 3 个八分之一。

现在看<Beat n={3}>涂色的 5 块蛋糕</Beat>。整个蛋糕平均切成 6 块，每块是 <Frac n={1} d={6} />。5 块就是蛋糕的 <Frac n={5} d={6} />。上面的数是<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>，数的是取了的 5 块；下面的数是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，数的是全部 6 块，没涂色的那一块也算在里面。

最后看纸条上<Beat n={4}>涂满的 4 个四分之一</Beat>。每份是 <Frac n={1} d={4} />，一份也没剩下。4 个四分之一正好铺满纸条，所以 <Frac n={4} d={4} /> = 1。分子和分母相同，就是把一个整体的每一份都取了。

如果只数巧克力没涂色的 5 块，写成 <Frac n={3} d={5} />，就错了，因为分母要数全部 8 块。写成 <Frac n={8} d={3} /> 是把两个数放反了：涂色的 3 块写在上面，全部 8 块写在下面。
