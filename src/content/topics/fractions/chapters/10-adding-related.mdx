<Lang en>

You have compared <Term id="fraction">fractions</Term> by making their parts the same size. You can use the same idea to add amounts from one <Term id="whole">whole</Term>.

Look at <Beat n={1}>the half of the strip</Beat> in <Frac n={1} d={2} /> + <Frac n={1} d={4} />. A quarter is smaller than this half, so you cannot count them as two matching parts. Halves and quarters are <Term id="related-fractions">related fractions</Term>: 4 is a multiple of 2, so each half can be cut into 2 quarters.

Now <Beat n={2}>cut each half into two</Beat> <Term id="equal-parts">equal parts</Term>. Look at the given half: it covers 2 of the strip's 4 quarters. The cuts change the number of parts, but the amount stays the same. So <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={2} d={4} />. These are <Term id="equivalent-fractions">equivalent fractions</Term>, two names for the same amount.

Next, <Beat n={3}>add one more quarter</Beat> beside the two given quarters. You now have 3 quarters: <Frac n={2} d={4} /> + <Frac n={1} d={4} /> = <Frac n={3} d={4} />. Add the top numbers, the <Term id="numerator">numerators</Term>, to count the parts. Keep the bottom number, the <Term id="denominator">denominator</Term>, at 4 because the whole still has 4 equal quarters.

Use <Beat n={4}>the ribbon model</Beat> to see how much ribbon is used altogether. The whole bar is one ribbon. The bow uses <Frac n={3} d={10} />, and the bookmark uses <Frac n={2} d={5} />. Each fifth covers 2 tenths, so <Frac n={2} d={5} /> = <Frac n={4} d={10} />. Count the 3 tenths and 4 tenths under the question-mark bracket: <Frac n={3} d={10} /> + <Frac n={4} d={10} /> = <Frac n={7} d={10} />.

Adding tops and bottoms is a mistake: <Frac n={2} d={6} /> is less than the half you started with. Adding without renaming gives <Frac n={2} d={4} />, but that counts the half as one quarter instead of two.

If asked for <Term id="simplest-form">simplest form</Term>, join parts after adding: <Frac n={1} d={6} /> + <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={1} d={6} /> + <Frac n={3} d={6} /> = <Frac n={4} d={6} /> = <Frac n={2} d={3} />. Joining pairs of sixths makes thirds without changing the amount.

<Lang zh>

你已经会先把份化成一样大，再比较<Term id="fraction">分数</Term>。同样的办法也能用来把同一个<Term id="whole">整体</Term>里的两个量加起来。

看 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> + <Frac n={1} d={4} /> 里<Beat n={1}>纸条上的二分之一</Beat>。四分之一比这个二分之一小，所以还不能把它们当成一样大的两份来数。二分之一和四分之一是<Term id="related-fractions">相关分数</Term>：4 是 2 的倍数，所以每个二分之一都能分成 2 个四分之一。

现在<Beat n={2}>把每个二分之一平均分成两份</Beat>，分出<Term id="equal-parts">相等的部分</Term>。看给出的那一半：它盖住了纸条 4 个四分之一中的 2 个。切了几刀，份数变了，多少没变。所以 <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={2} d={4} />。这是<Term id="equivalent-fractions">等值分数</Term>，同一个量的两个名字。

接着，在给出的 2 个四分之一旁边<Beat n={3}>再加 1 个四分之一</Beat>。现在一共 3 个四分之一：<Frac n={2} d={4} /> + <Frac n={1} d={4} /> = <Frac n={3} d={4} />。把上面的数，也就是<Term id="numerator">分子</Term>，相加来数份数；下面的数，也就是<Term id="denominator">分母</Term>，还是 4，因为整体还是 4 个一样大的四分之一。

用<Beat n={4}>丝带的模型图</Beat>看看一共用了多少丝带。整条长方形代表一条丝带。蝴蝶结用了 <Frac n={3} d={10} />，书签用了 <Frac n={2} d={5} />。每个五分之一等于 2 个十分之一，所以 <Frac n={2} d={5} /> = <Frac n={4} d={10} />。数一数问号括号下面的 3 个十分之一和 4 个十分之一：<Frac n={3} d={10} /> + <Frac n={4} d={10} /> = <Frac n={7} d={10} />。

把分子和分母都相加是错的：<Frac n={2} d={6} /> 比一开始的一半还少。不先化就相加，会得到 <Frac n={2} d={4} />，那是把一半当成了一个四分之一，其实一半是两个四分之一。

如果题目要求写成<Term id="simplest-form">最简分数</Term>，加完以后再合并：<Frac n={1} d={6} /> + <Frac n={1} d={2} /> = <Frac n={1} d={6} /> + <Frac n={3} d={6} /> = <Frac n={4} d={6} /> = <Frac n={2} d={3} />。每 2 个六分之一合成一个三分之一，多少不变。
