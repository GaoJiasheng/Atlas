<Lang en>

The marked place begins on Earth's dark side. Earth turns while the Sun stays in the same direction. From space, we see the marker cross the boundary between darkness and light. A person at that place sees the Sun move across the sky because they turn with Earth. Earth spins about its axis, the imaginary line through its poles.<Num s="S3,S36" />

As the marker enters the lit side, its <Term id="daylight">daylight</Term> begins: the Sun is now above the horizon. Keep turning Earth. The place faces the Sun, then moves towards darkness again. Earth's rotation brings both day and night.<Num s="S3,S36" />

Use Try it to turn Earth and watch the Sun's altitude, or height above the horizon. In this experiment, Earth stays at one point on its yearly path and has no tilt. At the equatorial marker, sunlight comes almost straight down in the middle of the day. Night still follows, even while the yearly position stays fixed.<Num s="S36" />

A <Term id="solar-day">solar day</Term> lasts from one return of the Sun to the same daily position to the next. It averages about <Num s="S10,S4">24 hours</Num>. A full turn measured against distant stars takes slightly less time because Earth also moves around the Sun. See how the Sun's changing direction affects shadows in <TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows">Light and Shadows</TopicLink>.

<More title={{"en": "Studying one movement at a time", "zh": "一次研究一种运动"}}>

Here, only Earth spins. The Sun's direction, Earth's orbital position and the zero tilt stay fixed. A model turn takes <Num s="S36">24 hours</Num>. In the coupled experiment, the movements run together and Earth's spin follows the period measured against distant stars.<Num s="S36" />

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two ways to measure a turn", "zh": "两种自转计时方法"}}>

Earth turns once relative to distant stars in about <Num s="S10,S4">23 hours 56 minutes</Num>. During that time, Earth also moves along its orbit. It must turn a little farther to face the Sun in the same way again. The model uses <Num s="S10">86,164.09054 seconds</Num> for the turn relative to the stars.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Sunlight and clock time", "zh": "阳光与钟表时刻"}}>

Day and night depend on sunlight at a place. Clock readings also depend on the timekeeping rules people use. Practise comparing times in <TopicLink slug="telling-time">Telling Time</TopicLink>. Later, we will compare clock noon with solar noon in Singapore.<Num s="S33,S36" />

</More>

<Lang zh>

标记地点起初在地球暗面。太阳方向不变，地球继续转动。从太空看，标记穿过明暗交界；站在当地的人也随地球转动，所以看见太阳在天空中移动。地球绕地轴自转，地轴就是穿过两极的假想线。<Num s="S3,S36" />

标记进入受光面，太阳升到当地地平线以上，<Term id="daylight">白昼</Term>便开始了。继续转动，这个地点朝向太阳，随后又走向黑夜。地球自转带来了白昼和黑夜。<Num s="S3,S36" />

用“试一试”转动地球，同时观察太阳高度，也就是太阳在地平线上方的角度。这个实验把地球固定在公转轨道上的一个位置，并取消地轴倾角。因此，赤道标记在白昼中段几乎正对太阳。即使不推进公转，黑夜仍会到来。<Num s="S36" />

<Term id="solar-day">太阳日</Term>是太阳从一个日常位置回到同一位置的时间，平均约 <Num s="S10,S4">24 小时</Num>。以远方恒星为参照的一次自转略短，因为地球还在绕太阳公转。<TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows">《光与影》</TopicLink>介绍太阳方向改变时，地面影子怎样变化。

<More title={{"en": "Studying one movement at a time", "zh": "一次研究一种运动"}}>

这里仅让地球自转，太阳方向、公转位置和零倾角都固定。模型转一圈取 <Num s="S36">24 小时</Num>。联动实验让各项运动一起进行，自转则采用相对远方恒星测得的周期。<Num s="S36" />

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two ways to measure a turn", "zh": "两种自转计时方法"}}>

相对远方恒星，地球约 <Num s="S10,S4">23 小时 56 分钟</Num>转一圈。这段时间里，地球也沿公转轨道前进，所以还要多转一点，才能再次以相同方向朝向太阳。模型采用的恒星自转周期为 <Num s="S10">86,164.09054 秒</Num>。
