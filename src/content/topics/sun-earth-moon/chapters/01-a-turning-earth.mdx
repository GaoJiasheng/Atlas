<Lang en>

The marked place starts on Earth's dark side. The Sun stays in the same direction while Earth turns. From space, the marker moves across a changing light boundary. Someone standing at that place would instead see the Sun change position in the sky. The imaginary line through Earth's poles is its axis: Earth rotates about this line.<Num s="S3,S36" />

As the marker enters the lit side, its <Term id="daylight">daylight</Term> begins. Here daylight means the interval when the Sun is above the horizon, rather than the time a clock calls daytime. Continue turning and the marker faces towards the Sun; continue again and it returns towards darkness. One rotating Earth accounts for both changes. The Sun does not have to travel around Earth to produce this pattern.<Num s="S3,S36" />

Use Try it to change the spin angle and watch the altitude reading beside the day or night label. This demonstration holds the yearly position still and removes Earth's tilt. At the equatorial marker, the Sun therefore comes almost overhead at the middle of its lit interval. Keeping the yearly path frozen lets us test rotation by itself; night still arrives without Earth moving along that path.<Num s="S36" />

A <Term id="solar-day">solar day</Term> follows the Sun's return to the same daily position, averaging about <Num s="S10,S4">24 hours</Num>. A full turn measured against distant stars is slightly shorter. Real Earth turns and travels around the Sun together, so those comparisons differ. The model's frozen-path turn is a teaching choice. <TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows">Light and Shadows</TopicLink> follows the shadows that the same changing Sun direction makes on the ground.

<More title={{"en": "Why hold the yearly path still", "zh": "模型为什么冻结公转"}}>

In this experiment only Earth spins. The Sun direction, orbital position and zero tilt stay fixed. Its model turn takes <Num s="S36">24 hours</Num>. The coupled experiment instead advances all movements and uses the sidereal spin period. Switching experiments changes the question being tested.<Num s="S36" />

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two ways to measure a turn", "zh": "两种计量方法"}}>

Relative to distant stars, Earth turns in about <Num s="S10,S4">23 hours 56 minutes</Num>. During that time its orbital motion changes the direction towards the Sun, so reaching the same solar direction requires a little more turning. The exact model sidereal period is <Num s="S10">86,164.09054 seconds</Num>; this precision belongs to the calculation, not an everyday clock-reading task.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A clock is another convention", "zh": "钟表另有约定"}}>

Day and night describe sunlight at a place. Clock labels also depend on the timekeeping convention. Practise reading and comparing clock times in <TopicLink slug="telling-time">Telling Time</TopicLink>; the Singapore chapter later compares clock noon with solar noon.<Num s="S33,S36" />

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

标记地点起初在地球暗面。太阳方向保持不变，地球继续转动。从太空看，标记穿过明暗交界；站在当地看，太阳却像是在天空中移动。穿过两极的假想线叫地轴，地球绕着这条线自转。要分清天体本身的运动和观察者所见的变化：观察者也跟着地面一起转。<Num s="S3,S36" />

标记进入受光面，就进入<Term id="daylight">白昼</Term>。这里指太阳在地平线以上的一段时间，不是钟表规定的白天时段。继续转，地点朝向太阳；再继续转，又走向黑夜。同一个自转过程解释了两次转换，不需要太阳绕地球运行。<Num s="S3,S36" />

用“试一试”改变自转角，同时比较太阳高度与昼夜读数。本演示固定地球在一年轨道上的位置，并把倾角设为零，所以赤道标记在白昼中段几乎正对太阳。公转路线没有推进，明暗仍会改变，这让自转的作用单独显现。<Num s="S36" />

<Term id="solar-day">太阳日</Term>按太阳回到相同日常位置来计量，平均约 <Num s="S10,S4">24 小时</Num>；以远方恒星为参照的一次自转略短。真实地球同时自转和公转，两种计量才会有差别。模型把公转冻结，只是教学选择。<TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows">《光与影》</TopicLink>继续追踪太阳方向改变时地面影子的变化。

<More title={{"en": "Why hold the yearly path still", "zh": "模型为什么冻结公转"}}>

这个实验只让地球自转，太阳方向、公转位置和零倾角保持不变，模型转一圈取 <Num s="S36">24 小时</Num>。联动实验会推进各项运动，改用恒星自转周期。两种实验回答不同的问题。<Num s="S36" />

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Two ways to measure a turn", "zh": "两种计量方法"}}>

相对远方恒星，地球约 <Num s="S10,S4">23 小时 56 分钟</Num>转一圈。期间公转也改变了朝向太阳的方向，因此要再转一点，才回到同一太阳方向。模型恒星周期取 <Num s="S10">86,164.09054 秒</Num>，这个精度用于计算，不是日常读钟要求。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "A clock is another convention", "zh": "钟表另有约定"}}>

昼夜描述一个地点的受光，钟表标签还受计时约定影响。读钟和比较时刻可看<TopicLink slug="telling-time">《认识时间》</TopicLink>；后面的新加坡一章再比较钟表正午与太阳正午。<Num s="S33,S36" />

</More>
