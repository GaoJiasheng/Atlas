<Lang en>

The opening view shows a new Moon that misses the alignment needed for an eclipse. The lunar orbit tilts about <Num s="S8">5.1 degrees</Num> from Earth's orbital plane. This differs from Earth's axial tilt. A <Term id="solar-eclipse">solar eclipse</Term> needs more than a new Moon, and a <Term id="lunar-eclipse">lunar eclipse</Term> needs more than a full Moon: the bodies must also line up. <Num s="S6,S40" />

The two orbital planes meet along the drawn line. The lunar orbit crosses Earth's orbital plane at an <Term id="node">orbital node</Term>. Near a crossing, the bodies can line up closely enough for an eclipse. Turn the node control to bring the crossing towards that alignment. <Num s="S40,S36" />

In a solar eclipse, the Moon comes between the Sun and an observer on Earth. In a lunar eclipse, the Moon enters Earth's shadow near full Moon. The dark central shadow, or umbra, receives no direct sunlight. The surrounding penumbra receives light from only part of the Sun. The shadow diagrams compare eclipse cases and are schematic, not to scale; the observation above still uses the physical radii and distances. <Num s="S6,S36" />

Keep the bodies centrally aligned and change the Moon's distance. <Term id="apparent-diameter">Apparent diameter</Term> means how wide a body looks, measured as an angle. A nearer Moon looks larger and can cover the Sun. Farther away, it can leave a bright ring: an <Term id="annular-eclipse">annular eclipse</Term>. The Moon's physical radius stays the same. <Num s="S6,S8,S9,S36" />

The disc window shows the marked surface observer's view. The phase window looks from Earth's centre. These experiments explain eclipse conditions, but have no real dates, contact times or eclipse paths. Note which inputs you change when recording a result. <Num s="S36" />

During the same solar eclipse, people in different places can see different amounts of the Sun covered. Here, the marked surface point stays fixed. The controls change the orbital alignment or Moon distance. <Num s="S6,S36" />

<More title={{ en: "Where the shadows fall", zh: "影子落在哪里" }}>

In a solar eclipse, the Moon's shadow falls towards Earth. In a lunar eclipse, Earth's shadow falls towards the Moon. Beyond the tip of the Moon's umbra is the antumbra. An observer centrally aligned there can see a bright solar ring. The schematic uses converging umbra and diverging penumbra cones. The observation above calculates eclipse types from the physical radii and distances, rather than the enlarged scene. <Num s="S6,S36" />

</More>

开场是新月位置，月球却没有排到日食所需的位置。月球轨道相对地球轨道平面倾斜约 <Num s="S8">5.1 度</Num>，这与地轴倾角不同。<Term id="solar-eclipse">日食</Term>除了需要新月，<Term id="lunar-eclipse">月食</Term>除了需要满月，还需要天体排得足够齐。<Num s="S6,S40" />

两个轨道平面沿画出的直线相交。月轨道穿过地球轨道平面的位置叫<Term id="node">轨道交点</Term>。月球靠近交点时，才可能达到日月食需要的排列。转动交点方向控件，观察排列怎样改变。<Num s="S40,S36" />

日食时，月球来到太阳与地表观察者之间；月食时，满月附近的月球进入地影。中央较暗的本影收不到太阳的直接光，周围半影只收得到太阳部分区域的光。影图对照不同食型，是未按比例的示意；上方观察窗仍采用天体实际半径与距离。<Num s="S6,S36" />

保持中心对齐，改变地月距离。<Term id="apparent-diameter">视直径</Term>是天体看起来有多宽，用角度表示。移近月球，月盘变大，可以盖住太阳；移远月球，则可能露出太阳的亮环，形成<Term id="annular-eclipse">日环食</Term>。过程中，月球实际半径保持不变。<Num s="S6,S8,S9,S36" />

视盘窗口从标出的地表观察点看，月相窗口从地心看。实验解释食的条件，没有对应的真实日期、接触时刻或地面食带。记录结果时，写下改动了哪些条件。<Num s="S36" />
