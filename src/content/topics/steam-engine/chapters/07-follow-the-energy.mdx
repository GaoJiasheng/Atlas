<Lang en>

Start at the furnace and boiler. Fuel releases energy, and heat passes into the water. Steam pushes the piston. The mechanism passes mechanical energy to the shaft and machine drive. Water and steam are moving matter. Heat is energy being transferred. [S7, S10] <FlyTo preset="hero">Follow the energy through the engine</FlyTo>

At the condenser, steam gives heat to cooling water as it turns into liquid. The cooling water carries that energy away. Energy also passes from the boiler to its surroundings. So the heat that reaches the water is only part of the fuel’s chemical energy. [S7, S9, S10]

The separate energy display divides <Num s="S1">100</Num> equal heat-input units into <Num s="S1">20</Num> work units and <Num s="S1">80</Num> units of heat carried away. Each tile is an equal share. Compare the input row with both output rows together. In the try-it card, keep the input fixed and change the work share. The heat carried away changes too, keeping the total equal. These shares are teaching examples, not measured Lap engine efficiency. [S1]

Continue to <TopicLink slug="industrial-revolution" ch="ir-seas-signals">Seas and signals</TopicLink> for steamships and telegraph connections. <TopicLink slug="industrial-revolution" ch="ir-connected-world-debates">Connected world, debated history</TopicLink> connects workers, materials and environmental change. Return to the whole engine and trace the energy from boiler water to piston to machine drive. [S14]

<More title={{ en: "Balancing the energy account", zh: "算清能量的去向" }}>

Follow the working fluid, the water and steam inside the heat engine. After a complete cycle, its stored energy returns to the starting amount. Energy conservation gives heat input = work out + heat carried away, or Qh = W + Qc. Changing the tiles changes an example of this balance, rather than controlling a real engine. [S1, S10]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Energy carried away", zh: "被带走的能量" }}>

The card can show <Num s="S1">10 + 90</Num>, <Num s="S1">20 + 80</Num> or <Num s="S1">30 + 70</Num> output units. Each pair equals the input. Heat carried away still counts as energy, even though it does not turn the output shaft. These tiles count heat supplied to the working fluid, so they cannot tell us fuel consumption. [S1, S10]

</More>
