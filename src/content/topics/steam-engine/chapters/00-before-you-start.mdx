<Term id="steam-engine">A steam engine</Term> uses steam to do mechanical work. Start at the boiler. Follow the steam to the <Term id="cylinder">cylinder</Term>, the chamber where the <Term id="piston">piston</Term> moves. Above it, the beam passes the motion to the large wheel. The model is based on the preserved Lap engine of <Num s="S2">1788</Num>. [S2, S7] <FlyTo preset="hero">Look over the whole engine</FlyTo>

Steam engines pumped water from mines before Watt improved them. The smaller model shows an earlier engine powered by air pressure. Watt developed his working model, patented his condenser and built commercial machines at different times. The Lap engine includes several later improvements. [S11, S12, S13]

Follow three processes: water changes state, pressure pushes the piston, and connected parts pass on motion. <TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="gases">States of Matter: gases</TopicLink> introduces the gaseous state. Coloured dots trace the pipe routes. Their colours do not show temperature, and their numbers do not show molecules or measured flow rates. [S1, S9]

Choose a still view on the bottom chapter rail. Rotate the model to see how the parts connect. SIM marks a calculated teaching example. The animation follows a chosen position in the movement cycle; changing the pressure reading does not drive it. The links stay connected, and some gaps are enlarged to make movement visible. Read about workers, materials and industrial change in the linked history chapters. [S1]

<More title={{ en: "The engine’s size", zh: "蒸汽机的尺寸" }}>

The bore is the cylinder’s inside diameter. ASME gives <Num s="S3">18.75 in</Num>, about <Num s="S3">0.476 m</Num>. The stroke, or distance the piston travels from one end to the other, is <Num s="S3">4 ft</Num>, about <Num s="S3">1.219 m</Num>. The Birmingham catalogue gives a different bore: <Num s="S4">18 in</Num>, or <Num s="S4">0.4572 m</Num>. The reason for the difference is unknown. Model researcher David K. Hulse gives a flywheel diameter of <Num s="S5">16 ft</Num>, about <Num s="S5">4.88 m</Num>. These conversions use NIST length definitions. [S16]

</More>

<Term id="steam-engine">蒸汽机</Term>利用蒸汽做机械功。先找到锅炉，再沿着蒸汽流向找到<Term id="cylinder">汽缸</Term>，也就是<Term id="piston">活塞</Term>在里面移动的空间。上方的横梁把运动传给大轮。这个模型参考了保存至今的 <Num s="S2">1788</Num> 年 Lap 蒸汽机。[S2, S7]<FlyTo preset="hero">看看整台蒸汽机</FlyTo>

在瓦特改良蒸汽机之前，蒸汽机已经用来抽出矿井里的水。旁边的小模型展示较早的机器，靠空气的压力推动活塞。瓦特先后做出工作模型、取得冷凝器专利，再建造商用机器。Lap 蒸汽机结合了后来几项改进。[S11, S12, S13]

沿着机器，观察三个过程：水改变状态，压力推动活塞，连接的零件传递运动。<TopicLink slug="states-of-matter" ch="gases">“物质的状态”的“气体”</TopicLink>介绍气态。彩色小点标出管道路径；颜色不代表温度，小点的数量也不代表分子数或实测流量。[S1, S9]

Hulse 给出的飞轮直径是 <Num s="S5">16 英尺</Num>，约 <Num s="S5">4.88 米</Num>。这些换算采用 NIST 的长度定义。[S16]
