# Materials Around Us: review status (22 October 2026)

Published (`status: published`) on the spec's decisions (docs/37 §0, all taken). The build record is docs/37 §9.2; the sources and what was cut are in [data/SOURCES.md](data/SOURCES.md). Nothing below blocks publication. Each item is a choice made the usual way that you may want to change.

## For Gavin

1. **塑料, not 塑胶.** Every Chinese text uses 塑料, as all other Atlas topics do. The Singapore Mandarin Database lists 塑胶 as the Singapore word (S20), and the glossary entry for plastic says so. This reverses spec §0.1-1 default (a). If you want 塑胶, change it across the whole site in one pass.
2. **"Some light" through the plastic sheet is low confidence.** The only source is a supplier saying natural HDPE "has a translucent appearance" (S29). Two search snippets that could not be opened suggested a 2 mm natural sheet lets through very little. The chapter 05 More box "About this plastic" says this. The options are spec §0.1-4: keep it; switch to a clear plastic sheet (needs a source); or check a real 2 mm HDPE sheet on a torch.
3. **"No light" through the glazed tile and the rubber** rests on a Year 2 teaching statement that thick, dark materials stop light (S5). Wood and metal have their own sources (S30, S31). No source names a 2 mm tile or rubber sheet.
4. **The rubber is pure gum, so it floats.** Most school erasers are PVC or filled rubber and will sink. The text reasons this from one eraser's maker's sheet (S33) and plastics densities (S6), not from a float test, and the chapter 03 More box "Rubber differs" warns readers. Switching the sample to hard rubber is one constant plus one beat caption (§0.1-3).
5. **Soft rubber's stiffness has one source** (S26, about 5 MPa). Because of it, the rubber strip lies on the bench in the strength test before any mass goes on, the same as cotton. The chapter 01 beat 5 caption says so.
6. **Glass and ceramic snap at the same step:** the third mass and 1.5 cm of push. That follows from the as-cut glass edge value of 36 MPa (S22) and the porcelain minimum of 35 MPa (S13, S24). No text ranks them or names the step.
7. **Safety lines** in chapters 01, 02, 04 and 05 and in the snapped-strip details: "this test is done only in the model". The chapter 01 line quotes the Commissioner of Building Control on glass falling from height (S16). Check the tone for P3.
8. **The rivets beat** (chapter 06 beat 3) says "home owners in Singapore have had to replace aluminium rivets"; the body and the part detail name the Building and Construction Authority (S16). The BCA release itself spells it both "aluminum" and "aluminium".
9. **The kitchen corner is generic**, not a particular HDB flat (the counter, wall and part details say so). Three things differ from spec §3.2:
   - The window opening is 48 × 90 cm (spec 60 × 100), so that the window and the cabinet fit side by side over a 1.1 m counter.
   - The tea towel hangs from the cabinet's bottom board, as in the cover drawing, not over the counter's edge; that keeps it in the kitchen view.
   - The casement sash is drawn open 35° outwards with one friction stay and its four rivets. This is schematic: no window drawing was used.
10. **Teak is drawn at 650 kg/m³**, an illustrative air-dry value. The Wood Handbook's official host could not be reached; its values come from research pass 1 and were cross-checked with The Wood Database (S7, S35).
11. **Figures:** the three approved drawings are used (the cover, the flexible ruler in chapter 02 and the waterproof test in chapter 04). The waterproof figure is 134 KB, over the 120 KB figure target, as several existing figures are. No other figures were made.

## Drawing choices (not facts)

- Glass and HDPE strips, the snapped glass pieces, the glass float block and the capped bottle use the translucent `membrane` family. With the `glass` family, 2 mm pieces are nearly invisible on paper (engine note). The glass sample squares keep `glass` and get a green cut edge.
- The support blocks' inner faces stand exactly on the supports at ±10 cm, so a bending strip clears the wood and its ends lift off the blocks, as in the ruler drawing.
- The spec's G-clamp is drawn as a steel clamping plate with two wing screws on the 15 cm block. A G-clamp could not reach round a block that tall.
- The cotton strip and the cotton sample squares are drawn 2 mm thick, the same as the other samples.
- The torch beam is narrowed to 20° so that its spot fits the 30 × 22 cm screen.
