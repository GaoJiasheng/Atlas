<Lang en>

Raise something and you store energy in it. The lift car at the top floor has more potential energy than it had at the ground floor, even while it waits there without moving. A thing does not have to be moving to have energy. <FlyTo preset="lift-core">Look at the lift</FlyTo>

A tennis ball held one metre above the floor has potential energy too. Let go, and as it falls its potential energy changes into kinetic energy: it goes faster and faster, and is fastest just as it reaches the floor. The three bars beside the pole follow the change. The potential-energy bar shrinks as the kinetic-energy bar grows, and together they stay the same. <FlyTo preset="ball-drop">Look at the ball</FlyTo>

The ball bounces back up, but not to where it started: on tile, to about <Num s="S1">55 cm</Num> in this model. The rest of the energy has not vanished. As the ball squashed against the floor, some energy became heat in the ball and the floor, and some became the sound of the bounce. The three bars still add up to the whole. On softer floors more becomes heat and sound, and on sand the ball hardly bounces.

The swing changes potential energy into kinetic energy and back again. It is fastest at the lowest point and still for an instant at each highest point. Each swing is a little lower, because some energy becomes heat and sound at the hooks and in the air. Without a push, it slowly stops.

In the try-it card, change the drop height with the same ball on the same floor. Changing the floor at the same height is a second, separate fair test: keep each table to one change.

<More title={{"en": "Energy is not a force", "zh": "能量不是力"}}>

A force is a push or a pull. The raised ball falls because the Earth pulls it down; as it falls, its potential energy changes into kinetic energy. Force and energy are different things: a force can start or stop a movement, and energy is what lets things work. The forces on falling things are in <TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">Gravity and weight</TopicLink>.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How much energy is in the ball", "zh": "球里有多少能量"}}>

Energy can be measured in joules. Raising the <Num s="S1">58 g</Num> ball through <Num s="S1">1 m</Num> gives it about <Num s="S1">0.57 J</Num> of potential energy: its mass in kilograms, times <Num s="S22">9.8</Num>, times the height in metres. On tile, about <Num s="S1">55 %</Num> of that is left as potential energy at the top of the bounce, and <Num s="S1">45 %</Num> has become heat and sound. You do not need to work this out; you will meet joules again in later science lessons.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "The lift's counterweight", "zh": "电梯的对重"}}>

The lift's ropes go over the sheave to a counterweight, which goes down as the car goes up. A counterweight usually balances the car and part of the load it is built to carry, often about half, so the motor only has to make up the difference and overcome friction. To keep it simple, the energy chain in this model follows only the car and leaves the counterweight out.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About this model", "zh": "关于这个模型"}}>

The fall and the bounce are shown slowed down <Num s="S1">2 times</Num>, and the run stops at the top of the first bounce: a pause for looking, not a ball hanging in the air. The model leaves out the air. On tile the ball bounces back to <Num s="S1">55 %</Num> of its drop height, close to how a tennis ball bounces on a hard test block; the values for rubber, carpet and sand are illustrative. The swing takes about <Num s="S24">2.9 s</Num> to swing there and back. In this model it turns <Num s="S1">3 %</Num> of the energy it has left into heat and sound on every swing from one side to the other, an illustrative value, and the run pauses after <Num s="S1">8</Num> swings at a turning point, with about <Num s="S1">78 %</Num> left. Run again pulls it back and lets it go once more. In later science lessons you will learn separate names for the energy stored in raised things, in springs and in fuels; here they are all potential energy.

</More>

秋千把势能转换成动能，又转换回势能。它在最低点最快，在每个最高点停住一瞬间。每摆一次都低一点，因为一部分能量在挂钩处和空气里变成了热和声。没有人推，它就慢慢停下来。

在试一试卡片里，用同一只球、同一种地面，只改变放手的高度。在同样的高度换地面，是另一个公平测试：每张表只改变一样东西。

<More title={{"en": "Energy is not a force", "zh": "能量不是力"}}>

力是推或拉。举高的球会落下，是因为地球把它往下拉；它下落时，势能转换成动能。力和能量是两回事：力可以让运动开始或停止，能量则是让事物能够工作的东西。下落的物体受到什么力，见<TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">重力与重量</TopicLink>。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How much energy is in the ball", "zh": "球里有多少能量"}}>

能量可以用焦耳来量。把 <Num s="S1">58 g</Num> 的球举高 <Num s="S1">1 m</Num>，它就有大约 <Num s="S1">0.57 J</Num> 的势能：用千克表示的质量，乘以 <Num s="S22">9.8</Num>，再乘以用米表示的高度。落在地砖上，弹到最高点时大约还剩 <Num s="S1">55 %</Num> 是势能，<Num s="S1">45 %</Num> 已经变成热和声。这个不必会算；焦耳以后的科学课还会学到。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "The lift's counterweight", "zh": "电梯的对重"}}>

电梯的钢丝绳绕过曳引轮，另一头吊着对重；轿厢上升，对重就下降。对重一般平衡轿厢本身，再加上额定载重的一部分，常见的是一半左右，所以电动机只要补上两边的差额，再克服摩擦就行。为了简单，这个模型的能量链只跟着轿厢走，没有把对重算进去。

</More>
