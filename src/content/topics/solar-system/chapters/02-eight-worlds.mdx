<Lang en>

Read the paths outwards from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. The size view sets their diameters on a common scale. Mercury is the smallest planet; Jupiter is the largest. Compare Earth with its neighbours before switching views. The large differences in width remain, even though the gaps in this row are only a way to arrange the display. <Num s="S4,S5" />

The inner <Num s="S4">four</Num> planets have solid surfaces. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are ice giants. Those names group planets by their physical nature. An ice giant is not a frozen ball with a bare icy surface. The giant planets have no hard surface like the ground beneath our feet. Earth's internal layers have their own model in <TopicLink slug="inside-the-earth" ch="layers-of-earth">Inside the Earth</TopicLink>. <Num s="S4" />

Now use the distance ruler. The reference orbital distances are unevenly spaced: Mercury's is about <Num s="S6">0.39 AU</Num>, Earth's about <Num s="S6">1.00 AU</Num>, and Neptune's about <Num s="S6">30.07 AU</Num>. A reference distance describes the size of an orbit; it is not a promise that the planet is always exactly that far from the Sun. <Num s="S6,S37" />

Compare the starting snapshot with a snapshot after <Num s="S37">one model-year</Num>. Every orbit uses the same elapsed time, but the planets complete different fractions of a turn. Mercury takes about <Num s="S5">0.24 years</Num> per orbit, Earth about <Num s="S5">one year</Num>, and Neptune about <Num s="S5">165 years</Num>. Outer planets generally take much longer to complete a circuit. Choosing a later snapshot compares periods without pretending that the opening alignment is the real sky. <Num s="S5,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "Radius, diameter and distance", zh: "半径、直径与距离" }}>

The reference table supplies mean radii; a displayed diameter is twice a mean radius. The orbit input is the semimajor axis, a length describing an ellipse. This model borrows that length as a circular radius. The Earth input uses the Earth–Moon barycentre as a proxy, following the JPL table, rather than claiming an exact moving Earth–Sun distance. <Num s="S5,S6,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The teaching clock", zh: "教学时间尺" }}>

In this simulation a model-year contains <Num s="S37">365.25 days</Num>. Each planet's turn count is elapsed model-years divided by its reference period. Starting phases are chosen, the paths share a plane, and angular rates are constant. Real paths have inclinations and changing speeds. Switching size and distance displays leaves these physical reference calculations unchanged. <Num s="S5,S6,S7,S37" />

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

沿路径从太阳往外读，依次是水星、金星、地球、火星、木星、土星、天王星和海王星。大小视图让各行星直径共用比例：水星最小，木星最大。先找地球，再与相邻行星比较；宽度的差异可以直接读，行星之间的留白却只是排版，不能用来判断真实远近。 <Num s="S4,S5" />

内侧 <Num s="S4">四颗</Num>行星都有固体表面。木星和土星是气态巨行星，天王星和海王星是冰巨行星。这些名称描述天体的物理性质；“冰巨行星”并不表示外面有裸露的冰地面。巨行星没有像脚下地面一样可站立的硬表面。地球内部的结构另见<TopicLink slug="inside-the-earth" ch="layers-of-earth">《地球内部》的分层模型</TopicLink>。 <Num s="S4" />

切到距离尺，参考轨道距离并不等间隔。水星约 <Num s="S6">0.39 天文单位</Num>，地球约 <Num s="S6">1.00 天文单位</Num>，海王星约 <Num s="S6">30.07 天文单位</Num>。这些长度描述轨道尺度，不是说行星始终与太阳保持这个实际距离。 <Num s="S6,S37" />

把起始快照与经过 <Num s="S37">一个模型年</Num>后的快照比较：时间相同，各行星完成的圈数却不同。水星公转一周约 <Num s="S5">0.24 年</Num>，地球约 <Num s="S5">一年</Num>，海王星约 <Num s="S5">165 年</Num>。外侧行星通常要久得多。选择较晚快照是在比较周期，不是把开场排列当成真实星空。 <Num s="S5,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "Radius, diameter and distance", zh: "半径、直径与距离" }}>

参考表提供平均半径，画出的参考直径是它的两倍。轨道输入则是描述椭圆大小的半长轴，本模型借用这段长度作圆半径。依照 JPL 表，地球一行采用地月质心作代理，不代表精确、随时变化的日地距离。 <Num s="S5,S6,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The teaching clock", zh: "教学时间尺" }}>

本模拟规定一个模型年为 <Num s="S37">365.25 日</Num>；经过年数除以参考周期，就得到圈数。起始角度是设定值，各轨道共面，角速度不变。真实轨道有倾角，沿途速度也会改变。切换大小或距离画法，不会改变这些参考物理量的计算。 <Num s="S5,S6,S7,S37" />

</More>
