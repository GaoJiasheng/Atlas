<Lang en>

The large sphere in this view represents the Sun, our nearest <Term id="star">star</Term>. Move the viewpoint farther away and its apparent size shrinks. Distance changes how large a body looks; it does not turn that body into a different kind of object. The points seen at night are other, much more distant stars. The Sun is the only star in our solar system. <Num s="S3" />

Follow the light paths towards Earth. The Sun emits light, while a planet becomes visible to us by reflecting sunlight. The model marks that difference with an outgoing path and a reflected path. A bright-looking point in the sky therefore need not produce its own visible light. <TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows" ch="seeing-light">Seeing light</TopicLink> follows the same distinction between emitted and reflected light closer to home. <Num s="S1,S3" />

The cutaway marks the Sun's core. Nuclear fusion there joins light atomic nuclei and releases energy. This is different from the chemical burning of an ordinary flame. The Sun consists of hot plasma, matter containing electrically charged particles, chiefly hydrogen and helium. It has no solid surface to stand on. Its diameter is about <Num s="S3">1.4 million kilometres</Num>, although some other stars are far larger. <Num s="S3" />

The surrounding paths show another role of the Sun: its gravity holds the planetary system together. <Term id="orbital-motion">Orbital motion</Term> means moving around another body. The paths here are circular teaching paths, rather than predictions of today's positions. Light and gravity describe different relationships: sunlight carries energy to Earth, while gravity governs the motion of bodies. Follow the energy connection in <TopicLink slug="energy-conversion" ch="energy-from-the-sun">Energy from the Sun</TopicLink>. <Num s="S3,S26,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "The core marker", zh: "核心标记" }}>

The cutaway identifies a region; its bright material is a visual code. It does not measure temperature or show energy travelling instantly through the Sun. Fusion occurs in the core, and energy passes outward before escaping as radiation. The model leaves out the detailed transport layers and reactions. <Num s="S3,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Gravity and curved paths", zh: "引力与弯曲路径" }}>

A moving planet is continually deflected by the Sun's gravity. Real planetary orbits are ellipses, with the Sun at a focus, and their speed changes along the path. This lesson uses circles and uniform angular rates to make period comparisons clear. <TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">Gravity and weight</TopicLink> introduces the force itself. <Num s="S26,S37" />

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

眼前的大球代表太阳，也就是离地球最近的<Term id="star">恒星</Term>。把视点移远，太阳看起来就变小；改变的是视大小，天体的种类没有改变。夜空中的许多小光点，也是距离远得多的恒星。太阳系里只有太阳这一颗恒星，夜里的其他恒星并不属于太阳系。 <Num s="S3" />

沿着光路看向地球，可以分清发光与反光。太阳自己发出光，行星则反射太阳光，被我们看见。模型用射出的光路与反射的光路标明这层关系。天空里看起来很亮的天体，不一定自己产生可见光。<TopicLink slug="light-and-shadows" ch="seeing-light">《光与影》的看见光</TopicLink>一章，用身边物体继续解释这种区别。 <Num s="S1,S3" />

剖面标出了太阳核心。核心发生核聚变，较轻的原子核结合，释放能量；这与普通火焰的化学燃烧不同。太阳主要由氢和氦组成，处于含带电粒子的高温等离子体状态，没有可供站立的固体表面。太阳直径约 <Num s="S3">140 万千米</Num>，但它并不是最大的恒星。 <Num s="S3" />

外围路径显示太阳的另一种作用：引力维系着行星系统。<Term id="orbital-motion">公转</Term>指绕另一天体运动；这里的圆形路径方便比较，并不预报当天位置。阳光把能量带到地球，引力则影响天体运动，两者描述不同的关系。能量的后续过程可看<TopicLink slug="energy-conversion" ch="energy-from-the-sun">《能量的转化》的太阳能量</TopicLink>。 <Num s="S3,S26,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "The core marker", zh: "核心标记" }}>

剖面只标明区域，较亮的材质是视觉提示，不代表测得的温度，也不表示能量瞬间穿过太阳。核聚变在核心释放能量，能量向外传递，最后以辐射等形式离开。本模型省略了传能层次与核反应的细节。 <Num s="S3,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Gravity and curved paths", zh: "引力与弯曲路径" }}>

太阳引力持续改变行星的运动方向。真实行星轨道是椭圆，太阳在一个焦点上，行星沿途快慢也会改变。本主题采用圆形、匀角速路径，方便比较周期。引力本身的基础可看<TopicLink slug="forces" ch="gravity-and-weight">《力》的重力与重量</TopicLink>。 <Num s="S26,S37" />

</More>
