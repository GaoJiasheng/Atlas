<Lang en>

The local-sky station uses a near-equatorial teaching position in Singapore. A near-equatorial observer can explore directions into both the northern and southern celestial skies. Science Centre Singapore's observatory provides a local connection with this way of looking. The model's position is a chosen input, not a claim of official survey precision, and a star above the drawn horizon is not guaranteed to be visible outdoors. <Num s="S15,S37" />

Choose Orion, then Crux and Scorpius with their example dates and times. The star positions come from a small catalogue sample. Lines join selected bright stars to help the eye recognise a pattern. A <Term id="constellation">constellation</Term>, in modern astronomy, is a named area of sky with a defined boundary. The familiar figure inside it is a related idea, rather than a physical frame holding stars together. <Num s="S14,S28,S34,S37" />

Turn the lines off. The star positions remain, showing that the connections were added by people. Different cultures have connected and named sky patterns in different ways. Orion, Crux and Scorpius are examples for this lesson, rather than the only possible groupings. Crux belongs to the southern sky; which patterns can be seen depends on the observer's location, date, time and available horizon. <Num s="S14,S32,S37" />

The depth view spreads the sample along its catalogue directions using estimated distances. Stars that look close together in a pattern can be very far apart in space. This is why recognising a figure does not establish a physical star group. The distance estimates are historical catalogue values, and the local sky calculation is approximate. For outdoor observing, compare the pattern with what is actually visible, including gaps caused by cloud or buildings. <Num s="S32,S28,S34,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "Catalogue points and drawn lines", zh: "星表点位与人为连线" }}>

The selected star data come from HYG v4.1, credited to David Nash and Astronexus under CC BY-SA. The catalogue sample remains in a separate data file. Atlas adds its own sparse recognition lines, which can be switched off. These are not official constellation boundaries or a copy of a cultural sky chart. <Num s="S22,S28,S34,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "An approximate Singapore sky", zh: "近似的新加坡天空" }}>

The chosen input is latitude <Num s="S37">1.3342° N</Num>, longitude <Num s="S37">103.7357° E</Num>. Fixed example dates are interpreted in Singapore time. Catalogue directions use the J2000 reference frame and are not corrected for precession or proper motion. The model also omits extinction, weather and terrain. It is a geometry lesson, not a visibility service. <Num s="S28,S30,S31,S37" />

</More>

<Lang zh>

本地星空工位采用接近赤道的新加坡教学位置。近赤道的观察者，可以探索北天与南天的方向；新加坡科学馆天文台也让这种观察有了本地联系。模型地点是选定输入，不宣称官方测绘精度。星点高于画出的地平线，也不等于户外一定看得见。 <Num s="S15,S37" />

依次选择猎户座、南十字座和天蝎座，并配合示例日期与钟点。星点位置来自小份星表样本，连线帮助眼睛辨认亮星图案。现代天文学中的<Term id="constellation">星座</Term>，是有名称与边界的天区；熟悉的图形与它有关，却不是把恒星固定在一起的真实框架。 <Num s="S14,S28,S34,S37" />

关掉连线，星点仍在原处，说明连接关系是人画上去的。不同文化以不同方式连接和命名天空图案，本主题选的图案只是例子。南十字座位于南天；某个图案能否看见，还取决于地点、日期、钟点，以及眼前是否有无遮挡的地平。 <Num s="S14,S32,S37" />

深度视图按星表的距离估计，把样本沿原方向展开。看起来相邻的星，在太空中可能相隔很远；认出图案，不等于找到物理上聚在一起的星群。目录距离是历史估计，天空计算也只是近似。到户外观察时，记录真正可见的部分，云和建筑挡住的地方同样是观察条件。 <Num s="S32,S28,S34,S37" />
