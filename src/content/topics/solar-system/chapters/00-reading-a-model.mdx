<Lang en>

The opening view puts the Sun and the <Num s="S4">eight</Num> <Term id="planet">planets</Term> within reach. It is a map for finding things, with enlarged worlds and compressed gaps. The <Term id="solar-system">solar system</Term> includes the Sun and the bodies held by its gravity. These bodies occupy far more empty space than the opening picture can show. A useful model must say which relationships it preserves. <Num s="S3,S37" />

Select the size view. The planet diameters now share a scale, so their widths can be compared directly. The gaps between them are simply a display arrangement. Select the distance view instead, and the reference distances share a scale. The little bodies can become too small to see. A selectable marker points to a body; its screen width is not the body's physical width. <Num s="S37" />

The <Term id="au">astronomical unit</Term>, shortened to AU, is a defined length of about <Num s="S7">150 million kilometres</Num>, useful for distances across the planetary system. In this scale example, making Earth's diameter <Num s="S37">10 millimetres</Num> puts an AU about <Num s="S37">117 metres</Num> away. Neptune's reference distance would be about <Num s="S37">3.53 kilometres</Num>. A small desktop model cannot keep those gaps and still show every planet clearly. <Num s="S7,S37" />

An <Term id="orbit">orbit</Term> is a path around another body. The opening paths are simplified circles, and their starting arrangement is chosen for the lesson. Changing the view changes the drawing, not the physical reference data. Compare snapshots at a selected elapsed time. For the mechanisms of day and night, seasons, Moon phases and eclipses, continue to <TopicLink slug="sun-earth-moon">Sun, Earth and Moon</TopicLink>. <Num s="S6,S26,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "A shared ruler", zh: "共用一把尺" }}>

In this model-scale calculation, Earth's reference diameter is about <Num s="S5">12,742 kilometres</Num>. Divide every physical length by that diameter and multiply by <Num s="S37">10 millimetres</Num>. Jupiter then measures about <Num s="S37">110 millimetres</Num> across, and the rounded solar reference gives a Sun about <Num s="S37">1.10 metres</Num> across. These are calculated comparisons, not measured classroom objects. <Num s="S5,S7,S37" />

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A model date and a sky date", zh: "模型时间与天空日期" }}>

Elapsed years compare the simplified planetary paths from a chosen starting arrangement. They do not select today's planetary alignment. The separate local-sky station uses fixed example dates and catalogue directions. Neither view is a photograph, and the local-sky station omits weather and obstructions. Keep a model observation separate from something recorded outdoors. <Num s="S6,S37" />

<Lang zh>

开场画面把太阳和 <Num s="S4">八颗</Num><Term id="planet">行星</Term>放在眼前，方便逐个寻找。天体被放大，间距被压缩。太阳和受它引力束缚的天体组成<Term id="solar-system">太阳系</Term>；真实天体之间的空隙，远比这张图显示的大。读模型前，先看画面保留了哪种比例，才能知道哪些长度可以直接比较。 <Num s="S3,S37" />

切到大小并排视图，行星直径用同一比例绘制，左右间隔只是排版。换到距离尺，参考距离按同一比例展开，小球却可能小到看不清。屏幕上的可点标记负责指示位置，标记的宽度不等于天体的真实大小。不能把大小视图里的间距，当成太阳系里的实际距离。 <Num s="S37" />

<Term id="au">天文单位</Term>是规定好的长度，约 <Num s="S7">1.5 亿千米</Num>，适合描述行星间的大尺度。这个缩尺例子把地球直径设成 <Num s="S37">10 毫米</Num>，一天文单位就约有 <Num s="S37">117 米</Num>，海王星的参考距离约有 <Num s="S37">3.53 千米</Num>。桌面很难同时容下这样的间隔和看得清的行星。 <Num s="S7,S37" />

<Term id="orbit">轨道</Term>是天体绕另一天体运动的路径。开场采用简化圆轨道，起始排列也是教学设定。切换视图只改变画法，不改变参考数据；选择经过的时间，可以比较不同快照。昼夜、四季、月相与食的机制，继续看<TopicLink slug="sun-earth-moon">《太阳、地球与月球》</TopicLink>。 <Num s="S6,S26,S37" />

<More title={{ en: "A shared ruler", zh: "共用一把尺" }}>

地球的参考直径约为 <Num s="S5">12,742 千米</Num>。把所有真实长度除以这个直径，再乘 <Num s="S37">10 毫米</Num>，就得到同尺模型。按这项计算，木星直径约 <Num s="S37">110 毫米</Num>，采用取整太阳数据得到的太阳直径约 <Num s="S37">1.10 米</Num>。这些是比例计算，不是课堂物件的实测尺寸。 <Num s="S5,S7,S37" />

</More>
