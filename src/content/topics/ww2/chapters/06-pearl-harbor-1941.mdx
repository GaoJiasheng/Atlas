Japan's war in China had gone on since 1937. In July 1941 Japanese troops moved into southern French Indochina, within reach of Malaya and the Netherlands East Indies. On 26 July the United States froze Japanese assets, and Britain and the Dutch colonial government followed, cutting off Japan's oil. Japan produced only <Num s="S84">about a tenth</Num> of the oil it used, and its reserves would last <Num s="S84">about three years</Num> in peace. Talks in Washington failed: on 26 November the United States asked Japan to withdraw from China and Indochina. Japan's leaders chose to take the oil of Southeast Asia by force, and first to strike the American fleet that could stop them.

On 26 November a carrier force under Vice-Admiral Chuichi Nagumo, <Num s="S85">six</Num> aircraft carriers with escorts and tankers, sailed from Hitokappu Bay in the Kuril Islands. It crossed <Num s="S85">3,000 miles</Num> of the North Pacific without being detected. Early on Sunday 7 December, Hawaii time, it launched <Num s="S85">360</Num> aircraft against Pearl Harbor on Oahu. In <Num s="S85">less than two hours</Num> all <Num s="S33">eight</Num> battleships of the US Pacific Fleet were sunk or put out of action, and <Num s="S33">2,403</Num> Americans were killed. The <Num s="S85">three</Num> American fleet carriers were not in harbour.

In Asia it was already 8 December. About <Num s="S25">70 minutes</Num> before the first bombs fell on Pearl Harbor, Japanese troops had begun landing at Kota Bharu in Malaya (chapter 7). Within hours Japanese forces attacked Hong Kong, bombed Singapore, and caught the American air force in the Philippines on the ground.

On 8 December the United States declared war on Japan. On 11 December Germany and Italy declared war on the United States. China, at war with Japan since 1937, formally declared war on Japan, Germany and Italy on 9 December. The wars in Asia and Europe had become one world war.

At Pearl Harbor <Num s="S85">360</Num> carrier aircraft put all <Num s="S33">eight</Num> American battleships out of action for the loss of <Num s="S33">29</Num> Japanese aircraft.
