<Lang en>

On 6 August 1945 an American B-29 dropped an atomic bomb on Hiroshima. About <Num s="S152">half</Num> of the people within <Num s="S152">1.2 km</Num> of the blast died that day, and by the end of the year <Num s="S152">roughly 140,000</Num> were dead. On 9 August a second bomb fell on Nagasaki, where <Num s="S154">73,884</Num> had died by the end of 1945. That same day <Num s="S156">about 1.58 million</Num> Soviet troops invaded Manchuria. On 15 August Emperor Hirohito told his people by radio that Japan accepted the Allied terms, and on 2 September Japan signed the surrender in Tokyo Bay. China marks its victory on 3 September.

On 12 September, in Singapore's Municipal Building, Admiral Mountbatten accepted the surrender of the Japanese forces in Southeast Asia. <FlyTo preset="singapore-island">Singapore and the Municipal Building</FlyTo> The camps opened: by May 1946 <Num s="S161">80,419</Num> prisoners of war and <Num s="S161">16,156</Num> civilian internees had been brought home from the region. Of American and British prisoners held by Japan, <Num s="S162">27 per cent</Num> had died.

Nobody knows exactly how many people died in the war: at least <Num s="S163,S14">60 million</Num>, and up to <Num s="S14">70–85 million</Num> in wider counts, about <Num s="S57">3</Num> in every 100 people alive in 1940. China lost <Num s="S13,S14">14–20 million</Num>, the Soviet Union <Num s="S14,S83">24–27 million</Num>, Poland <Num s="S14,S63">5.6 million</Num>, half of them Jews, Germany <Num s="S14">6.6–8.8 million</Num> and Japan <Num s="S164">about 3.1 million</Num>.

Leaders were put on trial. In Tokyo <Num s="S165">seven</Num> of <Num s="S165">28</Num> defendants were sentenced to death; Singapore held <Num s="S115">131</Num> war-crimes trials, among them the Sook Ching trial of 1947.

Singapore was a British colony again, but not for long: the Emergency against a communist insurrection began in 1948, self-government came in 1959 and independence on 9 August 1965, the subject of the next topic. Each 15 February, Total Defence Day, a ceremony is held at the Civilian War Memorial, raised in 1967 over the remains of Sook Ching victims.

<More title={{ en: "The atomic bombs: the argument", zh: "原子弹：争论" }}>

**The case made for the bombs.** On 26 July the United States, Britain and China had called on Japan to surrender unconditionally or face "prompt and utter destruction", and Japan's government had not accepted. President Truman said on 9 August that the bomb was used "to shorten the agony of war" and to save the lives of "thousands and thousands of young Americans". In 1947 the former Secretary of War, Henry Stimson, wrote that an invasion of Japan might have cost <Num s="S172">over a million</Num> American casualties, and fighting into 1946. Supporters also point out that the war was still killing people every day across occupied Asia, and that the Emperor's own broadcast named the "new and most cruel bomb" as a reason to end it.

**The case against.** In June 1945 a group of the scientists who built the bomb asked that it first be shown "by a demonstration in an appropriately selected uninhabited area". In 1946 the US Strategic Bombing Survey concluded that Japan would in all probability have surrendered before <Num s="S174">1 November 1945</Num> even without the bombs, the Soviet entry or an invasion. Critics argue that the targets were cities full of civilians, that Stimson's casualty estimate was made after the event, and that the Soviet declaration of war weighed as heavily on Japan's leaders.

Historians still disagree. Atlas does not decide between them.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The trials", zh: "审判" }}>

The International Military Tribunal for the Far East sat in Tokyo from 3 May 1946 to 12 November 1948, with judges from <Num s="S165">eleven</Num> countries. Of <Num s="S165">28</Num> defendants, mostly generals and ministers, <Num s="S165">seven</Num> were hanged on 23 December 1948, among them General Itagaki Seishirō, who had surrendered in Singapore, and <Num s="S165">16</Num> were sentenced to life imprisonment. The tribunal's judgment is the source for many figures in this atlas, such as the Nanjing death toll and the death rate of prisoners of war.

In Singapore, British military courts held <Num s="S115">131</Num> trials between January 1946 and March 1948. The Double Tenth trial of 1946 tried <Num s="S115">21</Num> Japanese soldiers and interpreters for the torture and deaths of Changi internees. At the main Sook Ching trial, in the Victoria Memorial Hall from 10 March to 2 April 1947, all <Num s="S115">seven</Num> accused officers were found guilty: Lieutenant-General Kawamura Saburō and Lieutenant-Colonel Oishi Masayuki were sentenced to death and <Num s="S115">five</Num> others to life imprisonment.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Singapore after 1945", zh: "1945 年以后的新加坡" }}>

The British came back to a hungry, damaged city whose people had seen the colonial power defeated. The Malayan Communist Party, whose guerrillas had fought the Japanese, turned to armed struggle against British rule; after the killing of three European planters in Perak, Britain declared an Emergency in Malaya on 18 June 1948 and in Singapore on 24 June. It lasted until 1960. Singapore moved step by step to self-rule: after the People's Action Party won the 1959 elections, Lee Kuan Yew was sworn in as Prime Minister at City Hall, the old Municipal Building. On 16 September 1963 he read the proclamation of Malaysia from its steps, and on 9 August 1965 Singapore separated from Malaysia and became an independent republic. That story is the next topic.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Remembering", zh: "记住" }}>

In February 1962 workers found a mass grave at Siglap. The Singapore Chinese Chamber of Commerce searched for others, found more at Bedok, Bukit Timah, Changi, the East Coast and Yio Chu Kang, and asked Japan to pay a "blood debt". The remains were gathered beneath the Civilian War Memorial on Beach Road: four pillars <Num s="S168">68 metres</Num> high, known as "the chopsticks", unveiled by Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew on 15 February 1967, <Num s="S168">25</Num> years after the fall of Singapore. It was built for the civilians of every community who died in the occupation, and every 15 February a service is held there. Since <Num s="S169">1984</Num> the same date has been Total Defence Day, chosen because it was the day Singapore fell. Other countries keep their own days: 27 January for the Holocaust, 6 and 9 August in Hiroshima and Nagasaki, 3 September in China.

**支持投弹的理由。** 7 月 26 日，美、英、中三国要求日本无条件投降，否则将“迅速完全毁灭”，日本政府没有接受。杜鲁门总统 8 月 9 日说，使用原子弹是为了“缩短战争的痛苦”，拯救“成千上万美国年轻人的生命”。1947 年，前陆军部长史汀生写道，进攻日本本土可能让美军伤亡<Num s="S172">超过 100 万</Num>，战斗要打到 1946 年。支持者还指出，战争每天都在被占领的亚洲各地夺走人命，而天皇自己的广播也把“新的残虐炸弹”列为结束战争的理由。

**反对投弹的理由。** 1945 年 6 月，一些参与研制原子弹的科学家提出，应当“先在适当选择的无人区做示范”。1946 年，美国战略轰炸调查团得出结论：即使不投原子弹、苏联不参战、也不登陆，日本很可能在 <Num s="S174">1945 年 11 月 1 日</Num>以前投降。批评者认为，轰炸目标是满是平民的城市；史汀生的伤亡估计是事后写的；苏联参战对日本领导层的冲击同样巨大。

<More title={{ en: "Singapore after 1945", zh: "1945 年以后的新加坡" }}>

英国人回来时，城市饥饿、残破，而这里的人已经亲眼看到殖民宗主国被打败。马来亚共产党的游击队曾经抗击日军，这时转而武装反抗英国统治；三名欧洲种植园主在霹雳州被杀后，英国在 1948 年 6 月 18 日宣布马来亚进入紧急状态，新加坡 6 月 24 日跟进，一直持续到 1960 年。新加坡一步步走向自治：人民行动党赢得 1959 年选举后，李光耀在市政厅（即当年的市政大厦）宣誓就任总理。1963 年 9 月 16 日，他在市政厅台阶上宣读马来西亚成立宣言；1965 年 8 月 9 日，新加坡脱离马来西亚，成为独立的共和国。那是下一个主题的故事。
