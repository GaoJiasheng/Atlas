<Lang en>

On 18 September 1931 an explosion on a Japanese-owned railway near Mukden (Shenyang) gave the Japanese army a pretext to occupy north-east China, where Japan set up the state of Manchukuo in 1932. The post-war Tokyo <Term id="war-crimes-tribunal">tribunal</Term> found that army officers had planned the explosion. On the night of 7 July 1937 Japanese and Chinese troops clashed at the Marco Polo Bridge outside Beiping. Neither government stepped back, and the fighting spread across north China and, in August, to Shanghai. There <Num s="S4">about 700,000</Num> Chinese and <Num s="S4">300,000</Num> Japanese troops fought for <Num s="S5">three months</Num>. A Chinese report counted <Num s="S5">187,200</Num> Chinese killed and wounded by early November; Japanese records list <Num s="S6">over 40,000</Num> Japanese killed and wounded.

Japanese forces then took the capital, Nanjing, on 13 December 1937. What followed is known as the Nanjing Massacre. A <Term id="massacre">massacre</Term> is the deliberate killing of many people who cannot defend themselves. Over <Num s="S7">about six weeks</Num> Japanese soldiers killed <Term id="prisoner-of-war">prisoners of war</Term> and civilians and raped women and girls; the post-war tribunal recorded <Num s="S7">about 20,000</Num> cases of rape in the first month.

Two courts ruled on the scale. The International Military Tribunal for the Far East (1948) found that <Num s="S7">over 200,000</Num> people were killed; the Nanjing War Crimes Tribunal (1947) found <Num s="S8">over 300,000</Num>. The Japanese government's position is that the killing of non-combatants and looting cannot be denied, but that the number of victims is difficult to determine. Both tribunals heard survivors and foreign residents of the city, and their records are why these events can be told in detail today.

The war went on. In October 1938 Japanese armies took Wuhan, and China's government moved west to Chongqing and kept fighting. Europe was still at peace, but for China the Second World War had begun. By 1945 between <Num s="S13">14</Num> and <Num s="S13,S14">20 million</Num> Chinese had died.

<More title={{ en: "Why 'War of Resistance', not 'Second World War'?", zh: "为什么叫“抗日战争”，而不只是“二战”？" }}>

In China the war is called the War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression, and its start depends on who is counting. Chinese textbooks dated it from 7 July 1937 until 2017, when the Ministry of Education told schools to teach a <Num s="S16">14-year</Num> war beginning on 18 September 1931. Japan's government called the fighting the "China Incident" and never declared war; China formally declared war on Japan, and on Germany and Italy, only on 9 December 1941. In Europe the Second World War is usually dated from Germany's invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, and in the United States from Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941. These are one war seen from different places: from December 1941 the war in China, the Pacific and Europe were formally joined.

<More title={{ en: "Singapore's Chinese and the war in China", zh: "新加坡华人与中国抗战" }}>

Many Chinese in Singapore and Malaya had family in China and followed the war closely. On 10 October 1938 community leaders in Singapore founded the Nanyang Federation of China Relief Fund, with the businessman Tan Kah Kee as president, to raise money across Southeast Asia for China. In 1939 the federation recruited <Num s="S18,S19">about 3,200</Num> drivers and mechanics, the Nanyang Volunteers, to drive supply lorries on the Burma Road between Lashio in Burma and Kunming in China. <Num s="S19">More than 1,000</Num> of them died in that service. <More title={{ en: "The numbers", zh: "数字" }}>

No complete count of China's war dead exists. Estimates of Chinese deaths in 1937–1945, soldiers and civilians together, run from <Num s="S13">about 14 million</Num> to <Num s="S13,S14">about 20 million</Num>; the US National WWII Museum puts military deaths at <Num s="S14">3–4 million</Num> within its total. Many civilians died of famine, disease and flight rather than in battle, which is one reason the records are incomplete. Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare counts <Num s="S15">about 465,700</Num> Japanese war dead in China proper, not including the north-east. At Shanghai, the Chinese report of 5 November listed <Num s="S5">187,200</Num> killed and wounded; Japanese official records list <Num s="S6">42,202</Num> Japanese killed and wounded up to 8 November, <Num s="S6">11,072</Num> of them killed.

<Lang zh>

1931 年 9 月 18 日，沈阳附近日本经营的南满铁路发生爆炸，日本关东军以此为借口<Term id="occupation">占领</Term>中国东北，次年扶植成立“满洲国”。战后<Term id="war-crimes-tribunal">东京审判</Term>认定，这次爆炸是日本军官事先策划的。1937 年 7 月 7 日夜，日军与中国守军在北平城外卢沟桥一带交火。两国政府都没有退让，战火很快蔓延到华北各地，8 月又烧到上海。淞沪会战打了<Num s="S5">三个月</Num>，中国先后投入<Num s="S4">约 70 万</Num>人，日本<Num s="S4">约 30 万</Num>人。到 11 月初，中方统计的死伤已有 <Num s="S5">187,200</Num> 人；日方记录的日军死伤<Num s="S6">超过 4 万</Num>人。

上海失守后，日军向首都南京推进，1937 年 12 月 13 日攻占南京。随后发生的事，史称南京大屠杀。“<Term id="massacre">屠杀</Term>”指有意地成批杀害没有抵抗能力的人。在<Num s="S7">约六周</Num>里，日军成批杀害被俘的中国军人和平民，大量强奸妇女；战后东京审判认定，仅占领后第一个月，城内就发生了<Num s="S7">约 2 万</Num>起强奸案。

关于遇难人数，有两个法庭的认定：远东国际军事法庭（1948）认定被杀害者<Num s="S7">超过 20 万</Num>人；南京国防部审判战犯军事法庭（1947）认定在 <Num s="S8">30 万人以上</Num>。日本政府的立场是：杀害非战斗人员、掠夺等行为无法否认，但遇难人数说法很多，难以确定。两个法庭都听取了幸存者和当时留在南京的外国侨民的证词，今天能把这段历史讲清楚，靠的正是这些记录。

南京之后，战争并没有结束。1938 年 10 月武汉失守，国民政府西迁重庆，继续抵抗。这时欧洲还没有开战，而对中国来说，第二次世界大战已经开始了。到 1945 年，中国的死亡人数在 <Num s="S13">1,400 万</Num>到 <Num s="S13,S14">2,000 万</Num>之间。

<More title={{ en: "Why 'War of Resistance', not 'Second World War'?", zh: "为什么叫“抗日战争”，而不只是“二战”？" }}>

在中国，这场战争叫“中国人民抗日战争”，从哪天算起，要看谁在算。中国教材原来从 1937 年 7 月 7 日算起，2017 年教育部要求改为从 1931 年 9 月 18 日开始的<Num s="S16">十四年</Num>抗战。日本政府当时把这场战争叫作“支那事变”，始终没有宣战；中国直到 1941 年 12 月 9 日才正式对日宣战，同时对德国、意大利宣战。欧洲一般把 1939 年 9 月 1 日德国入侵波兰算作二战开始，美国则从 1941 年 12 月 7 日珍珠港事件算起。说法不同，指的是同一场战争：从 1941 年 12 月起，中国战场、太平洋战场和欧洲战场正式连成了一体。

新加坡和马来亚的许多华人在中国有亲人，一直关注着战局。1938 年 10 月 10 日，新加坡侨领成立“南洋华侨筹赈祖国难民总会”（南侨总会），由陈嘉庚任主席，在东南亚各地为中国募捐。1939 年，南侨总会招募了<Num s="S18,S19">约 3,200</Num> 名司机和修车工，也就是“南侨机工”，到缅甸腊戍和昆明之间的滇缅公路上开运输车，其中 <Num s="S19">1,000 多</Num>人死在这条路上。第 8 章会再讲到日据时期新加坡的华人社会。
