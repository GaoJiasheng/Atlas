<Lang en>

After the surrender Singapore was renamed Syonan-to, "Light of the South". Within days the occupiers began the Sook Ching, "purge through cleansing", which ran from 18 February to 4 March 1942. Chinese men aged <Num s="S100">18 to 50</Num> were ordered to screening centres. Kempeitai (military police) and army officers picked out those they suspected of being anti-Japanese, often on flimsy evidence, and took them by lorry to the coast to be shot. At Changi Beach <Num s="S98">66</Num> men were killed on 20 February; on Punggol beach <Num s="S99">some 400</Num> on 28 February.

Nobody counted the dead. At the 1947 war-crimes trial the Japanese figure was <Num s="S96">about 5,000</Num>. Singapore's Chinese community has put it far higher, at up to <Num s="S95">100,000</Num>; most estimates lie between <Num s="S95">25,000 and 50,000</Num>. Atlas lists them side by side. Afterwards the community was made to pay <Num s="S95">$50 million</Num> in "tribute money" to the occupiers.

Daily life became a struggle. The occupation's "banana money" lost almost all its value: an egg that cost <Num s="S101">3 cents</Num> before the war cost <Num s="S101">$100</Num> by 1945. Rice was rationed, and people ate tapioca and sweet potatoes instead. Recorded deaths <Num s="S101">more than doubled</Num> between 1942 and 1945.

From Changi, prisoners of war went north by train to build a railway between Thailand and Burma, <Num s="S102">415 km</Num> long and finished in October 1943: the Death Railway. Of the <Num s="S102">62,000</Num> Allied prisoners who worked on it, <Num s="S102">11,979</Num> died; of some <Num s="S103">200,000</Num> Asian labourers, between <Num s="S103">75,000</Num> and <Num s="S104">90,000</Num> died.

On 10 October 1943, after Allied commandos had attacked ships in Singapore harbour, the Kempeitai seized <Num s="S105">57</Num> civilian internees at Changi Prison; <Num s="S105">15</Num> died under interrogation. This was the Double Tenth. Resistance went on in Malaya's jungles, through the British Force 136 and the communist-led Malayan People's Anti-Japanese Army.

<More title={{ en: "Lim Bo Seng", zh: "林谋盛" }}>

Lim Bo Seng was a Singapore businessman and the father of eight. He left the island as it fell and reached India, where he joined Force 136, a secret unit of Britain's Special Operations Executive with its headquarters in Kandy, Ceylon. He recruited Chinese agents who could pass as locals, and they were landed in Malaya by submarine to build an intelligence network. In 1944 the network was betrayed. Lim was arrested and held at Batu Gajah Gaol in Perak, where the Japanese military police starved and tortured him; fellow prisoners said he still shared his food. He died there on 29 June 1944, aged <Num s="S107">35</Num>. After the war his remains were brought back and buried with military honours at MacRitchie Reservoir.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Elizabeth Choy", zh: "伊丽莎白·蔡" }}>

Elizabeth Choy and her husband, Choy Khun Heng, ran a canteen at the hospital that is now Woodbridge Hospital and secretly carried food, medicine, money, letters and radios to British prisoners of war. They were arrested in the round-up that followed the Double Tenth and taken to the Kempeitai's East District Branch in the old YMCA building on Stamford Road. She was held for <Num s="S109">193 days</Num> and tortured, including with electric shocks; her husband was held for longer. After the war she became the first and only woman member of the Legislative Council, and in 1955 she left politics to teach.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Daily life under occupation", zh: "日据下的日常生活" }}>

Singapore imported most of its food, and the war cut the shipping lanes; in 1943 the island had <Num s="S101">about 900,000</Num> people to feed. The rice ration started at <Num s="S101">20 katis</Num> (about <Num s="S101">12 kg</Num>) a month per person and fell to <Num s="S101">8</Num> for men, <Num s="S101">6</Num> for women and <Num s="S101">4</Num> for a child. People queued for hours, grew tapioca and sweet potatoes and bought on the black market, where a kati of pork that had cost <Num s="S101">30 cents</Num> reached <Num s="S101">$1,500</Num>.

Schools taught in Japanese. Pupils learned to speak and write the language, sang the Japanese national anthem and bowed to the Emperor's picture before lessons. Families who kept their children away received smaller rations, and pupils who did well in Japanese received an extra allowance.

</More>

<Lang zh>

投降之后，新加坡被改名为“昭南岛”，意思是“南方之光”。没过几天，占领当局就开始了“肃清”，从 1942 年 2 月 18 日持续到 3 月 4 日。<Num s="S100">18 岁至 50 岁</Num>的华人男子被勒令到各个检证中心集中。日本宪兵和军官从人群中挑出他们怀疑“抗日”的人，往往没有什么根据，然后用卡车把他们拉到海边枪杀。2 月 20 日，樟宜海滩有 <Num s="S98">66</Num> 名男子被杀；2 月 28 日，榜鹅海滩有<Num s="S99">约 400</Num> 人被杀。

遇难者的人数，当时没有人统计。1947 年战犯审判时，日方的数字是<Num s="S96">约 5,000</Num> 人；新加坡华人社会的估计要高得多，最高到 <Num s="S95">10 万</Num>人；多数估计在 <Num s="S95">2.5 万到 5 万</Num>人之间。这里把几种数字并列。肃清之后，华人社会还被迫向占领当局缴纳 <Num s="S95">5,000 万元</Num>“奉纳金”。

日常生活越来越难。占领当局发行的“香蕉钱”几乎成了废纸：战前 <Num s="S101">3 分钱</Num>一个的鸡蛋，到 1945 年要 <Num s="S101">100 元</Num>。大米实行配给，人们只好改吃木薯和番薯。1942 年到 1945 年间，登记的死亡人数<Num s="S101">增加了一倍多</Num>。

战俘从樟宜坐火车北上，去修建连接泰国和缅甸的铁路。这条铁路长 <Num s="S102">415 公里</Num>，1943 年 10 月完工，后来被叫作“死亡铁路”。参加修路的 <Num s="S102">6.2 万</Num>名盟军战俘中，<Num s="S102">11,979</Num> 人死亡；约 <Num s="S103">20 万</Num>名亚洲劳工中，死亡人数在 <Num s="S103">7.5 万</Num>到 <Num s="S104">9 万</Num>之间。

1943 年 10 月 10 日，盟军突击队刚刚袭击过新加坡港内的船只，宪兵队从樟宜监狱抓走 <Num s="S105">57</Num> 名被拘留的平民，其中 <Num s="S105">15</Num> 人死于审讯。这就是“双十事件”。抵抗也一直没有停：英国组织的 136 部队和马来亚共产党领导的马来亚人民抗日军，在马来亚的丛林里坚持了下来。

<More title={{ en: "Lim Bo Seng", zh: "林谋盛" }}>

林谋盛是新加坡商人，有八个孩子。新加坡沦陷时他离开了这座岛，辗转到了印度，加入 136 部队。这是英国特别行动处下属的秘密部队，总部设在锡兰的康提。他负责招募能混在当地人中的华人特工，再由潜艇把他们送进马来亚，建立情报网。1944 年，情报网遭人出卖，林谋盛被捕，关在霹雳州的华都牙也监狱，日本宪兵饿他、拷打他；同狱的人说，他还把自己的口粮分给别人。1944 年 6 月 29 日，他死在狱中，年仅 <Num s="S107">35</Num> 岁。战后，他的遗骸被运回新加坡，以军礼安葬在麦里芝蓄水池畔。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Elizabeth Choy", zh: "伊丽莎白·蔡" }}>

日据时期，伊丽莎白·蔡（蔡秀美）和丈夫蔡坤兴在今天的板桥医院经营食堂，暗中给英国战俘送食物、药品、钱、信件和收音机。双十事件之后的大搜捕中，两人被捕，押到史丹福路旧基督教青年会大楼里的宪兵队东区分队。她被关押了 <Num s="S109">193 天</Num>，受过包括电击在内的酷刑；她的丈夫被关得更久。战后，她成为立法议会第一位、也是唯一一位女议员；1955 年离开政坛，回去教书。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Daily life under occupation", zh: "日据下的日常生活" }}>

新加坡的粮食大多靠进口，战争切断了航运；1943 年岛上有<Num s="S101">约 90 万</Num>人要吃饭。大米配给起初每人每月 <Num s="S101">20 斤</Num>（约 <Num s="S101">12 公斤</Num>），后来降到男子 <Num s="S101">8</Num> 斤、女子 <Num s="S101">6</Num> 斤、儿童 <Num s="S101">4</Num> 斤。人们排几个小时的队，自己种木薯和番薯，或者到黑市上买；一斤原来 <Num s="S101">3 角</Num>的猪肉，涨到了 <Num s="S101">1,500 元</Num>。

学校改用日语教学。学生要学说日语、写日文，每天上课前唱日本国歌，向天皇像鞠躬。不送孩子上学的家庭，配给会减少；日语学得好的学生，可以多领一份津贴。

</More>
