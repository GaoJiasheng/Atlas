At 3 a.m. on 22 June 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union, its partner in the partition of Poland. About <Num s="S75">3 million</Num> German soldiers in <Num s="S75">145</Num> divisions attacked on a front from the Baltic to the Black Sea, with <Num s="S75">3,350</Num> tanks and <Num s="S75">625,000</Num> horses; Romanian and Finnish forces joined them. By most Soviet accounts the western military districts had <Num s="S76">2.9 million</Num> men. <Num s="S75">Three</Num> army groups advanced: North towards Leningrad, Centre towards Moscow and South into Ukraine.

The Red Army was caught by surprise and lost whole armies in vast encirclements. East of Kyiv a pocket closed in mid-September, and the Germans claimed <Num s="S75">665,000</Num> prisoners. On 8 September German and Finnish forces cut Leningrad off by land. The siege lasted <Num s="S79">872 days</Num>, and between <Num s="S77">about 630,000</Num> and <Num s="S78">800,000</Num> people died in the city, most of them of hunger.

Behind the front, SS and police units called Einsatzgruppen shot Jews, Communist officials and others. At the Babi Yar ravine outside Kyiv they killed <Num s="S80">33,771</Num> Jews on 29 and 30 September; the number is known because the killers reported it to Berlin themselves. Soviet prisoners of war died in huge numbers from hunger, cold and disease: of <Num s="S82">5.7 million</Num> taken during the war, <Num s="S82">3.3 million</Num> died.

On 2 October the Germans began their attack on Moscow. Autumn rain turned the roads to mud, then the frost came, and winter clothing had been ordered for only <Num s="S75">60</Num> divisions. By late November the German Army had lost <Num s="S75">more than 740,000</Num> men. On 5 December, with German troops on the approaches to the capital, the Red Army counter-attacked and drove them back. Hitler had planned to win within <Num s="S75">five months</Num>; Germany now faced a long war in the east.

<More title={{ en: "Why did Hitler attack the Soviet Union?", zh: "希特勒为什么要进攻苏联？" }}>

The decision was taken a year earlier. On 31 July 1940, with Britain still undefeated, Hitler told his generals that the Soviet Union should be attacked the following spring and smashed in one campaign of about <Num s="S75">five months</Num>. Ukraine, Belorussia and the Baltic states were to come under German control, and the oil of Baku was a later objective. Behind the armies came a war of annihilation: Einsatzgruppen followed to kill Jews and Communist officials, and Soviet prisoners of war became, after the Jews, the largest group of victims of Nazi racial policy.

German planners expected to win before the weather turned, so winter clothing was ordered only for the <Num s="S75">60</Num> divisions meant to stay as an occupation force. Soviet railways used a wider gauge, so every line had to be relaid before German trains could run, and supplies moved forward by truck and by the army's <Num s="S75">625,000</Num> horses. In October rain and mud slowed everything. On the morning of 6 December the temperature near Moscow fell to <Num s="S76">−38 °F</Num> (about <Num s="S76">−39 °C</Num>); later that winter it reached <Num s="S75">−50 °F</Num> (about <Num s="S75">−46 °C</Num>). The Red Army, closer to its own supplies, still had <Num s="S76">eight</Num> armies, <Num s="S76">about 800,000</Num> men, in reserve.
