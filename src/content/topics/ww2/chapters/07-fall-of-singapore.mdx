Shortly after midnight on 8 December 1941 Japanese troops began landing at Kota Bharu in north-east Malaya, <Num s="S25">about 70 minutes</Num> before the attack on Pearl Harbor, where it was still 7 December. Within hours larger forces came ashore at Singora and Patani in southern Thailand; the first landings totalled <Num s="S26">about 26,600</Num> men. Malaya and Singapore were British colonies, and the <Num s="S28">88,600</Num> troops defending them were British, Australian, Indian and locally recruited. On 10 December Japanese aircraft sank the battleships Prince of Wales and Repulse off Kuantan; <Num s="S30">about 840</Num> men died.

Japan's 25th Army, under Lieutenant-General Tomoyuki Yamashita, moved south along the west-coast trunk road, its infantry often on bicycles, with light tanks in front. It broke through at Jitra on 11–12 December and at the Slim River on 7 January, and entered Kuala Lumpur on 11 January. On 31 January the last Commonwealth troops crossed to Singapore island and blew a gap in the Causeway.

Singapore's big coastal guns had been sited to face the sea. On the night of 8 February Japanese troops crossed the Johor Strait and landed near Sarimbun in the north-west, held by the 22nd Australian Brigade, and by 11 February they held Bukit Timah. <FlyTo preset="singapore-island">Singapore island</FlyTo> On 14 February Japanese soldiers killed <Num s="S38">about 200</Num> patients and staff at Alexandra Hospital. With the city under shelling and its water supply expected to last <Num s="S35">48 hours</Num> at most, Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival signed the surrender at the Ford factory at 6.10 p.m. on 15 February.

The campaign had lasted <Num s="S35">70 days</Num>. British Commonwealth losses were <Num s="S31">138,708</Num>, <Num s="S31">more than 130,000</Num> of them prisoners of war; Japanese battle casualties were <Num s="S31">9,824</Num>. For the people of Singapore it was the start of <Num s="S43">three years and eight months</Num> of Japanese occupation. Singapore marks 15 February as Total Defence Day.

Malaya was one of several attacks Japan launched within hours. At Pearl Harbor <Num s="S33">2,403</Num> Americans were killed. Hong Kong, attacked on 8 December, surrendered on 25 December; its garrison of British, Canadian, Indian and local troops lost <Num s="S32">11,848</Num> men, most of them as prisoners, and Japanese official casualties were <Num s="S32">2,754</Num>. In the Philippines, American and Filipino troops held out on the Bataan peninsula until 9 April 1942 and on Corregidor until 6 May. On Java, in the Netherlands East Indies, Allied units were ordered to lay down their arms on 8 March 1942. On 9 December 1941 China formally declared war on Japan, Germany and Italy.

<More title={{ en: "Whose war? The colony and the Indian National Army", zh: "谁的战争？殖民地与印度国民军" }}>

Malaya and Singapore were British colonies, and most of the soldiers defending them were not British. Indian troops were the largest group: <Num s="S28">37,000</Num> of Malaya Command's <Num s="S28">88,600</Num> men, and <Num s="S31">67,340</Num> of the campaign's losses. The Malay Regiment fought on the Pasir Panjang ridge and at Bukit Chandu, where Second Lieutenant Adnan Saidi was captured and killed on 14 February. Chinese volunteers of all political views formed "Dalforce" to help defend the island.

Two days after the surrender the Japanese assembled the Indian prisoners at Farrer Park and urged them to join an Indian National Army. The INA was formed in 1942 with Japanese support, first under Captain Mohan Singh and later under Subhas Chandra Bose, with the aim of ending British rule in India. Japan encouraged, and sometimes forced, Indian prisoners to join. The INA was dissolved after Japan's defeat in 1945.
