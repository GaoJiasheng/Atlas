<Lang en>

The final expedition is usually dated 1431–1433. A commission dated 1430 belongs to its preparation, so the earlier year need not contradict the conventional sailing period. The distinction matters: a court order, a coastal departure and a return are different events. The year labels here follow the expedition sequence rather than claiming an exact departure day. [S1, S12]

<FlyTo preset="view-gulf">Hormuz</FlyTo> belongs to the final expedition's main-fleet account. A fleet could divide, however, and a destination associated with a detachment is not automatically a place visited by its commander. The published modern map used in preparation distinguishes a possible Hong Bao squadron route from the main fleet. Such proposals require their own evidence. The points here locate places; moving the camera does not trace a ship's wake. [S21]

The state expedition sequence ended in 1433. That ending should not stand for the disappearance of maritime exchange. A government fleet programme and the activities of merchants were different things, even when they interacted. Likewise, the end of the voyage sequence does not resolve Zheng He's biography: the circumstances and location of his death remain uncertain in the museum account. This map therefore gives no exact death location. [S28, S4]

The surviving record also has a history. Later navigational charts preserved earlier knowledge, including Temasek, but cannot by themselves prove a personal landing. Archaeology, inscriptions and travel accounts each preserve different traces. <FlyTo preset="view-straits">Melaka and the Singapore region</FlyTo> belong to histories that began before these fleets and continued beyond them. Returning to those local histories keeps the ports' inhabitants in view when the admiral's story ends. [S3, S7, S13, S17]

<More title={{ en: "An ending for a fleet, not for trade", zh: "船队的结束，不是贸易的终点" }}>

Nakajima Gakusho's research describes later mutual trade and smuggling within a changing East Asian order. This supports distinguishing the end of the expeditions from a complete end to Chinese seafaring. It does not supply a continuous set of trade totals, and this topic does not invent a measure of how much commerce increased or declined. [S28]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What survived, and when", zh: "留下了什么，何时留下" }}>

The Mao Kun chart tradition survives through a later publication of earlier navigational knowledge. The Galle inscription preserves a different kind of record, while excavated objects answer questions about material exchange. An account of a port, a charted name and an object found there do not each prove the same visit by the same person. [S3, S13, S14, S17]

</More>

<Lang zh>

最后一次远航通常记作 1431—1433 年；1430 年的颁诏属于准备阶段，不必把这两种纪年说成互相矛盾。朝廷下令、沿海启航与返回本来就是不同事件。本图采用航次的常用年份范围，不把它写成精确到日的离岸记录。[S1, S12]

最后一航次的主队记载包括<FlyTo preset="view-gulf">霍尔木兹</FlyTo>，但主队不等于全部分队，分队目的地也不等于统帅个人足迹。准备时参考的现代出版地图，把洪保支队的可能路线另行标出。推定路线需要自己的证据。这里的点只定位地点，镜头移动也不是船在海上留下的航迹。[S21]

1433 年，这组国家远航告一段落，不能因此宣布海上交换从此消失。国家船队与商人活动虽然相关，却不是同一套制度。航次结束也没有解开所有传记问题：博物馆资料仍把郑和逝世的地点与情形列为不确定，所以图中不标一个确定的死亡位置。[S28, S4]

留下的材料也有自己的年代。后出的航海图保存较早知识，其中出现淡马锡，并不能单独证明郑和登陆。考古、碑刻与行旅记述各留下一部分线索。<FlyTo preset="view-straits">马六甲和新加坡区域</FlyTo>的历史早于船队，也延续到船队之后；读到统帅故事的结尾，仍要看见当地居民更长久的生活。[S3, S7, S13, S17]

<More title={{ en: "An ending for a fleet, not for trade", zh: "船队的结束，不是贸易的终点" }}>

中岛乐章的研究讨论后来东亚秩序中的互市与走私，说明停止国家远航与停止所有中国海上活动不能画等号。不过，现有材料没有给出连续贸易总量，本主题不据此编造贸易增长或衰退的数字。[S28]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What survived, and when", zh: "留下了什么，何时留下" }}>
