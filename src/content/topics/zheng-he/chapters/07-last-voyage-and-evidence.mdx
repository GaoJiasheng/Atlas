<Lang en>

The final expedition is usually dated 1431–1433. The court ordered it in 1430, before the usual sailing period began. Ordering, preparing, sailing and returning were different stages of a voyage. Their dates can differ without contradicting one another. An order could come while ships were still being prepared, long before they sailed and eventually returned. [S1, S12]

The main fleet's final expedition included <FlyTo preset="view-gulf">Hormuz</FlyTo>. Ships could split into groups, so a smaller squadron's destination was not always its commander's destination. A modern published map proposes a separate route for Hong Bao's squadron. That route remains a possibility, rather than a confirmed journey. [S21]

The state expeditions ended in 1433. Maritime trade continued through merchants' activities, even as government policy changed. The end of the voyages also leaves questions about Zheng He himself. The museum account says the place and circumstances of his death remain uncertain. [S28, S4]

Later charts preserved older sailing knowledge, including the name Temasek. Archaeology, inscriptions and travellers' accounts provide other clues about the ports and their people. A place-name alone cannot prove that Zheng He landed there. <FlyTo preset="view-straits">Melaka and the Singapore region</FlyTo> had histories that began before his fleets arrived and continued after their final voyage. [S3, S7, S13, S17]

<More title={{ en: "An ending for a fleet, not for trade", zh: "船队的结束，不是贸易的终点" }}>

Nakajima Gakusho describes later trade and smuggling as East Asian relations changed. The state expeditions ended, but Chinese seafaring continued. Surviving figures do not provide a continuous total showing how much trade rose or fell. [S28]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What survived, and when", zh: "留下了什么，何时留下" }}>

The Mao Kun chart survives in a later publication that preserves earlier sailing knowledge. The Galle inscription records offerings, while excavated objects reveal material exchange. Together, these clues answer different questions about the past. They do not necessarily describe one person's visit. [S3, S13, S14, S17]

</More>

<Lang zh>

最后一次远航通常记作 1431—1433 年。朝廷在 1430 年先颁布诏令，随后才进入出航阶段。下令、准备、出海与返回，是远航的不同步骤，日期不同并不一定互相矛盾。诏令发出时，船队还要准备；出海以后，还经历远航与回程。[S1, S12]

最后一次远航的主队到过<FlyTo preset="view-gulf">霍尔木兹</FlyTo>。船队可以分组行动，分队的目的地未必就是统帅本人到过的地方。一幅现代出版地图为洪保支队另标可能路线，但这条行程仍是推测。[S21]

1433 年，国家远航告一段落。政府政策继续变化，商人的海上贸易也仍在进行。郑和本人的生平则留下未解问题：博物馆资料记述，他逝世的地点与情形仍不确定。[S28, S4]

后出的航海图保存了较早的知识，其中也有淡马锡的名字。考古、碑刻与旅行者的记述，则留下港口和居民的其他线索。单凭一个地名，不能证明郑和曾经登陆。文献中的港口名称、石碑上的文字和出土器物，各自留下不同的过去。把这些线索放在一起，才能逐步了解海上往来。<FlyTo preset="view-straits">马六甲和新加坡区域</FlyTo>的历史早于船队，也延续到最后一次远航之后。[S3, S7, S13, S17]

<More title={{ en: "An ending for a fleet, not for trade", zh: "船队的结束，不是贸易的终点" }}>

中岛乐章研究东亚关系变化中的互市与走私。国家远航结束后，中国的海上活动仍在继续。不过，现有数字没有提供连续的贸易总量，无法据此算出贸易增长或衰退了多少。[S28]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "What survived, and when", zh: "留下了什么，何时留下" }}>

《武备志》收录的航海图，在英文文献中称为 Mao Kun map，是后出书籍保存较早航海知识的例子。加勒碑刻记载献礼，出土器物则反映商品交换。这些线索回答不同问题，未必都对应同一个人的到访。[S3, S13, S14, S17]
