<Lang en>

Long before Zheng He sailed in 1405, ships carried goods across Asian seas. Singapore's Asian Civilisations Museum displays a Tang shipwreck from the ninth century. Its surviving cargo shows what people traded across long distances. The ship was probably Arab-built. Its final departure port in China is still uncertain. [S8, S9]

Ceramics, coins and other finds at Fort Canning show that early Singapore was a trading settlement. The name <Term id="term-temasek">Temasek</Term> also appears on later charts that preserve older sailing knowledge. These objects and charts are <Term id="term-evidence">historical evidence</Term>: clues that help us understand the past. They do not prove that Zheng He personally landed here. <TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="background">Early Singapore</TopicLink> explores the settlement's history. [S3]

<FlyTo preset="view-straits"><Term id="term-melaka">Melaka</Term> and the straits</FlyTo> belonged to a Malay trading world with local rulers and merchants. Farther west, India's southwest-coast ports joined wider exchanges. Across the ocean, Kilwa's merchants traded goods including Chinese porcelain. These places had their own histories and interests before a Ming fleet arrived. <TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-seas">Southeast Asia's connected seas</TopicLink> gives a longer view. [S1, S6, S7, S26]

The map uses modern coastlines and city points to locate these regions. It shows neither measured ship tracks nor historical borders. Some points mark trading places where a fleet visit is unconfirmed. Around Nanjing, symbols spread out so you can select each record. They all refer to the same city. [S21, S22, S23]

<More title={{ en: "Clues from objects", zh: "器物留下的线索" }}>

A Chinese ceramic found in Singapore or Kilwa shows that goods reached the port. It cannot tell us which ship, merchant or admiral brought it. Comparing objects with written accounts helps us learn more. [S3, S6, S17]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Ports had their own purposes", zh: "港口也有自己的打算" }}>

Melaka's rulers and merchants made their own decisions about trade. Kilwa belonged to Swahili coastal culture and had links with Arabia, India and China. Visitors entered societies with their own histories. [S7, S26]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Many networks across the sea", zh: "海上不只有一条路" }}>

“Maritime Silk Routes” is a name for many networks of exchange. The Tang wreck and the later port finds come from different periods. A line joining their locations would not show a single voyage. [S8, S9, S21]

</More>

<Lang zh>

早在郑和 1405 年出海以前，亚洲各地已有船只往来。新加坡亚洲文明博物馆展出的唐代沉船，年代早至九世纪。留存的货物让我们看见当时人们远距离交易的商品。这艘船很可能由阿拉伯工匠建造。它最后从中国哪个港口出发，至今仍不能确定。[S8, S9]

福康宁出土的陶瓷、钱币等，说明早期新加坡已有贸易聚落。<Term id="term-temasek">淡马锡</Term>的名字，也出现在保存较早航海知识的后出地图上。这些器物和地图都是<Term id="term-evidence">历史证据</Term>，也就是帮助我们了解过去的线索。它们不能证明郑和曾亲自登陆。<TopicLink slug="singapore-1819-1965" ch="background">早期新加坡</TopicLink>继续介绍这个聚落的历史。[S3]

把镜头移向<FlyTo preset="view-straits"><Term id="term-melaka">马六甲</Term>与海峡</FlyTo>，当地统治者和商人有自己的利益。印度西南岸港口也参与交换；海的另一边，基尔瓦商人交易的商品包括中国瓷器。海洋网络并不从明朝船队开始。<TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-seas">东南亚的海上交流</TopicLink>把时间拉得更长。[S1, S6, S7, S26]

地图用现代海岸和城市点帮助定位这些区域，没有画出实测航迹或历史国界。有些点标出贸易港口，但船队是否到访仍未确定。南京周围的符号分开排列，方便逐个点选；这些记录都对应同一座城市。[S21, S22, S23]

<More title={{ en: "Clues from objects", zh: "器物留下的线索" }}>

新加坡或基尔瓦的中国陶瓷，说明商品曾经抵达这些港口，却不能告诉我们是哪艘船、哪位商人或统帅把它带来。把器物与文献相互比较，可以找到更多线索。[S3, S6, S17]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Ports had their own purposes", zh: "港口也有自己的打算" }}>

马六甲的统治者和商人自行决定怎样开展贸易。基尔瓦属于斯瓦希里沿海文化，与阿拉伯、印度和中国都有联系。外来船只到访时，这些地方早已有自己的历史。[S7, S26]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Many networks across the sea", zh: "海上不只有一条路" }}>

“海上丝路”是许多交流网络的统称。唐代沉船和较晚的港口遗物属于不同年代，不能把它们的位置连成一条航线。[S8, S9, S21]

</More>
