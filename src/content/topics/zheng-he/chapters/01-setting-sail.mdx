<Lang en>

In 1405 the Yongle emperor sent a fleet into the Indian Ocean trading world. The court wanted to display Ming power and expand diplomatic relations. An <Term id="term-envoy">envoy</Term> represented a ruler; <Term id="term-tribute">tribute</Term> involved gifts and formal recognition within the court's order. A port ruler could seek useful exchanges without sharing every Ming understanding of the relationship. Trade, diplomacy and military authority travelled together. [S1, S4, S29]

Zheng He, originally named Ma He, came from a Muslim family in Yunnan. Ming forces captured him as a child. He was castrated and forced into court service. He later gained authority within that same court and commanded its expeditions. Remembering both parts of his life keeps his later power from hiding the violence done to him. [S1, S2]

The fleet depended on sailors, soldiers, medical staff, repair workers and diplomatic specialists. An <Term id="term-interpreter">interpreter</Term> helped people using different languages communicate. Such work made an expedition possible alongside command decisions. Navigators combined compasses, observations of the sky and written sailing knowledge; a compass supplied direction, rather than a ship's complete position. <FlyTo preset="view-china">Look toward the Chinese coast</FlyTo>, where this organisation connected court and sea. [S1]

Published accounts describe <Num s="S1,S4">hundreds</Num> of vessels and <Num s="S1,S4">tens of thousands</Num> of people, but their totals differ. The dimensions of the largest vessels, often called <Term id="term-treasure-ship">treasure ships</Term>, are also disputed. The cover shows a representative Chinese sailing vessel. It is an illustration of a vessel type, not a measured reconstruction of a particular ship. [S1, S4, S10, S11]

<More title={{ en: "Why fleet totals differ", zh: "船队到底有多少艘" }}>

Columbia's account gives <Num s="S1">317</Num> ships for the first expedition; the Mariners' Museum gives about <Num s="S4">208</Num>. The accounts do not establish a shared counting scope. Averaging them would create a total that neither source supports. [S1, S4]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "How large was a treasure ship?", zh: "宝船有多大" }}>

Late historical dimensions are often converted to about <Num s="S10">137</Num> metres long and <Num s="S10">56</Num> metres wide. Sally Church examines engineering objections and proposals for lengths nearer <Num s="S10">61–76</Num> metres. Wakeman reports arguments for large vessels based on written capacity references and an excavated rudder post. [S10, S11]

Those arguments concern how surviving evidence should be reconstructed. A rudder post is not a complete hull, and a late text is not a measured ship plan. This topic does not select a settled length. [S10, S11]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Direction is not position", zh: "方向不等于位置" }}>

The fleet's navigation used magnetic compasses together with celestial observation and sailing knowledge. Knowing the direction of north did not by itself reveal how far a vessel had travelled or which harbour lay ahead. No navigation instrument shown here is presented as a recovered object from a particular expedition. [S1]

</More>

<Lang zh>

1405 年，永乐皇帝派船队出海，希望显示明朝威望，扩大外交关系。代表统治者出使的人是<Term id="term-envoy">使节</Term>；<Term id="term-tribute">朝贡</Term>涉及献礼及朝廷秩序中的承认。港口统治者也可能借往来争取交易和自身利益，不一定与明廷有相同理解。船队同时承担外交、贸易和军事任务。[S1, S4, S29]

郑和原名马和，来自云南穆斯林家庭。他童年被明军俘获，遭到阉割，被迫进入宫廷服役。后来，他在这个朝廷取得权力，统领远航。讲述他的指挥地位，也要说明早年遭受的强制伤害，不能让后来的身份遮住这段经历。[S1, S2]

船队不只靠统帅。水手、士兵、医护、修船人员和外交人员都参与工作，<Term id="term-interpreter">通事</Term>帮助使用不同语言的人沟通。航海者把罗盘、观星与文字航海知识结合使用；知道方向，并不等于已经知道船的位置。<FlyTo preset="view-china">看中国沿岸</FlyTo>，朝廷组织与海上工作在这里相接。[S1]

远航规模达到<Num s="S1,S4">数百</Num>艘船、<Num s="S1,S4">数万</Num>人，但不同来源的总数不一致。大型船只所用的<Term id="term-treasure-ship">宝船</Term>名称，也不代表尺寸已经确定。封面画的是代表性的中国帆船，不是某艘船的精确复原；有关尺度的争论仍需保留。[S1, S4, S10, S11]
