<Lang en>

Melaka had ties with the Ming court before Zheng He's first expedition. Yin Qing was sent there in 1403. Local rulers used overseas connections to pursue their own interests. Court records describe official dealings. Malay royal family histories and other accounts show different views. <TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">Southeast Asian kingdoms</TopicLink> explores the wider region around this port. [S7, S12, S30]

The fleets used Melaka as a <Term id="term-depot">staging depot</Term>: a place to gather ships and store supplies. Voyages included waiting and restocking as well as sailing. Historian Wan Ming links Melaka's role to seasonal winds and return journeys. Geoff Wade stresses its military and commercial uses. Local rulers and the Ming court each sought benefits from the relationship. [S2, S12]

A stone stele found at Galle in Sri Lanka carries an <Term id="term-inscription">inscription</Term>, or writing cut into stone. It has Chinese, Tamil and Persian texts. The date carved on it is 1409; its installation date is uncertain. The Chinese text records offerings by imperial envoys to the Buddha. It shows how religious gifts formed part of these overseas encounters. [S13, S14]

In 1411, Zheng He's forces captured a local Sri Lankan ruler and members of his family after fighting. They took the captives to the Ming court. Ming records describe the fighting as a response to hostile action. Wade's <Term id="term-interpretation">interpretation</Term>, or explanation of the evidence, stresses the use of force to impose Ming power. Columbia's account says the captives were later released. <FlyTo preset="view-sri-lanka">Look at Sri Lanka</FlyTo>: the captive ruler did not govern the whole island. [S1, S2]

<More title={{ en: "How often did the fleets visit Melaka?", zh: "每次远航都停马六甲吗" }}>

Wan Ming argues for <Num s="S12">7</Num> fleet visits to Melaka, compared with the traditional count of <Num s="S12">5</Num>. The first visit is disputed. The surviving records are incomplete, so she also studies when a visit could have fitted into the voyages. [S12]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The stele's different texts", zh: "碑上的文字并不等同" }}>

The stele's Chinese, Tamil and Persian texts do not all say the same thing. The Chinese section records offerings to the Buddha. The carved date tells us when the text was dated, but not necessarily when the stone was set up. [S13, S14]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A local conflict on a diverse island", zh: "岛上的一场地方冲突" }}>

The captive ruler controlled only part of Sri Lanka. Ming records describe a response to hostile action. Wade stresses the imposition of Ming power, while Columbia's teaching guide describes support for recognised rulers. In each account, people were taken away by force. [S1, S2, S29]

</More>

<Lang zh>

郑和首次出海以前，马六甲已与明廷往来。1403 年，尹庆奉命前往。当地统治者借海外联系谋求自己的利益。明廷档案记下正式交往，马来王室的家族历史和其他记述，则留下不同角度。<TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">东南亚王国</TopicLink>介绍这个港口周围更广阔的区域历史。[S7, S12, S30]

船队把马六甲用作<Term id="term-depot">仓储据点</Term>，在此集合、存货和补给，也会停下来等待。万明把这些安排与季节风、回程时间联系起来。韦德则强调港口兼有军事和商业作用。当地统治者与明廷都希望从往来中获益。[S2, S12]

锡兰加勒发现一方石碑，上面有中文、泰米尔文和波斯文。刻在石头上的文字称为<Term id="term-inscription">碑刻</Term>。碑文日期是 1409 年，石碑何时安装则不确定。中文部分记载皇帝使节向佛陀献礼，可见宗教赠礼也是海外交往的一部分。[S13, S14]

1411 年战事之后，郑和军队俘获锡兰一位地方统治者及家人，把他们送到明廷。明方称船队是在遭到敌对行动后反击。韦德的<Term id="term-interpretation">历史解释</Term>，也就是对证据的说明，强调明朝用武力施加权威。哥伦比亚大学资料记述俘虏后来获释。<FlyTo preset="view-sri-lanka">看锡兰</FlyTo>，被俘者并非全岛的统治者。[S1, S2]

<More title={{ en: "How often did the fleets visit Melaka?", zh: "每次远航都停马六甲吗" }}>

万明认为船队到访马六甲 <Num s="S12">7</Num> 次，传统说法则是 <Num s="S12">5</Num> 次。首次到访尤其有争议。史料并不完整，因此她也考察各航次是否有时间到访。[S12]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The stele's different texts", zh: "碑上的文字并不等同" }}>

碑上的中文、泰米尔文和波斯文内容并不完全相同。中文部分记载向佛陀献礼。碑文日期说明文字何时署年，却未必就是石碑安装的时间。[S13, S14]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A local conflict on a diverse island", zh: "岛上的一场地方冲突" }}>
