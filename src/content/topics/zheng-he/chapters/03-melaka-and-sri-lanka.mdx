<Lang en>

Melaka's relations with the Ming court began before Zheng He's first expedition: Yin Qing was sent there in 1403. Its rulers pursued local interests through overseas connections. The court archive records official dealings, while Malay genealogies and other accounts offer different perspectives. <TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">Southeast Asian kingdoms</TopicLink> places this port polity within its regional history. [S7, S12, S30]

For the fleets, Melaka served as a <Term id="term-depot">staging depot</Term>, a place to assemble and store supplies. Waiting and provisioning were part of a voyage, alongside sailing. Wan Ming connects the port's use with seasonal winds and return journeys; Geoff Wade stresses its military as well as commercial role. Local cooperation and Ming deployment belonged to the same relationship without having identical purposes. [S2, S12]

In Sri Lanka, a stone stele found at Galle preserves an <Term id="term-inscription">inscription</Term> in Chinese alongside Tamil and Persian writing. It bears a date in 1409; that date does not automatically tell us when the stone was installed. Its Chinese text records offerings by imperial envoys to the Buddha. The object preserves religious giving across a multilingual encounter. [S13, S14]

In 1411, after fighting, Zheng He's forces captured a local Sri Lankan ruler and members of his family and took them to the Ming court. The court narrative presented the fighting as a response to hostile action. Wade's <Term id="term-interpretation">interpretation</Term> emphasises coercive intervention. The captives were subsequently released, according to Columbia's account. These differing explanations must still include the capture. <FlyTo preset="view-sri-lanka">Look at Sri Lanka</FlyTo>: this conflict did not mean the whole island came under Ming rule. [S1, S2]

Wan Ming argues that the fleets visited Melaka <Num s="S12">7</Num> times, against a traditional account of <Num s="S12">5</Num> visits. The first visit is disputed. Her argument draws on incomplete records and possible chronology; it is not a measured route proving identical landings on every expedition. [S12]

</More>

The stele carries Chinese, Tamil and Persian writing. Their presence does not mean that every text says exactly the same thing. This chapter uses the checked Chinese section to discuss offerings to the Buddha and keeps the inscription date distinct from the installation date. [S13, S14]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "A local conflict on a diverse island", zh: "岛上的一场地方冲突" }}>

The captive ruler did not govern all Sri Lanka. Ming accounts explain the fighting from the fleet's side; Wade examines how it imposed Ming power. Columbia's teaching guide frames interventions more broadly as supporting recognised rulers. None of these explanations turns the capture of people into a voluntary exchange. [S1, S2, S29]

</More>

<Lang zh>

马六甲与明廷的往来早于郑和出海。1403 年，尹庆已奉命前往。当地统治者也借海外联系谋求自身利益，不能只用明廷的目标解释关系。官档保存正式交往，马来王室谱系等材料另有视角。<TopicLink slug="ancient-civilisations" ch="southeast-asia-kingdoms">东南亚王国</TopicLink>把这个港口政权放回区域历史。[S7, S12, S30]

船队把马六甲用作<Term id="term-depot">仓储据点</Term>，在此集合、存货、等待与补给。万明将据点的使用与季节风、回程安排联系起来；学者 Geoff Wade 强调它兼有军事和商业作用。同一处港口，对当地统治者与明廷可以有不同意义，合作也包含各自的打算。[S2, S12]

锡兰加勒发现的石碑留下中文、泰米尔文和波斯文<Term id="term-inscription">碑刻</Term>。碑上有 1409 年的日期，但书写日期不能直接当成安装日期。已核的中文部分记载皇帝使节向佛陀献礼，让人看到跨语言接触中的宗教赠礼。[S13, S14]

1411 年战事之后，郑和军队俘获锡兰一位地方统治者及家人，送至明廷。明方叙述称船队遭到敌对行动后反击；学者 Geoff Wade 的<Term id="term-interpretation">历史解释</Term>强调强制干预。哥伦比亚大学材料记述俘虏后来获释。<FlyTo preset="view-sri-lanka">看锡兰</FlyTo>，献礼与俘虏都属于这段历史，却不能由此说全岛都被明朝征服。[S1, S2]
