<Lang en>

The fourth expedition, usually dated 1413–1415, sailed beyond India to <FlyTo preset="view-gulf"><Term id="term-hormuz">Hormuz</Term></FlyTo>. This island port lies near the entrance to the Persian Gulf. The town, island and nearby strait share a name. Today's Bandar Abbas is a different port on the mainland. [S1]

At a port, visitors could buy goods, exchange diplomatic gifts and recognise an emperor's authority. These activities often happened together. Interpreters helped visitors speak with local rulers and merchants. Travellers wrote down what they saw, but their accounts give only part of the story. Local people had their own reasons for taking part. [S1, S4, S17]

In 1414 the king of <FlyTo preset="view-bengal">Bengal</FlyTo> sent the Yongle emperor a giraffe. Ming courtiers linked the unfamiliar animal to the <Term id="term-qilin">qilin</Term>, a mythical creature thought to bring good fortune. The gift gained a special meaning at court. It came through Bengal's king, rather than directly from an African animal-catching expedition led by Zheng He. [S5]

The fleets also used force. During a struggle over who would rule <FlyTo preset="view-sumatra">Samudera</FlyTo> in 1415, Zheng He captured a claimant and took him to China. Accounts disagree about the succession dispute. Columbia's teaching guide describes support for recognised rulers. Historian Geoff Wade stresses efforts to impose Ming dominance. [S2, S29]

<More title={{ en: "An island, a town and a strait", zh: "岛、城与海峡" }}>

Hormuz can mean the town, the island or the strait. The expedition destination was on the island. Bandar Abbas lies on the mainland, across a different shore. The exact medieval harbour and ships' approach are unknown. [S1]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The animal and its political meaning", zh: "动物与宫廷寓意" }}>

A giraffe is real; a qilin is mythical. Ming courtiers saw the gift as a sign of good fortune. The Metropolitan Museum discusses the 1414 gift alongside a later Qing painting. That painting was made long after the event. [S5]

</More>

<Lang zh>

1413—1415 年的第四次远航，越过印度，抵达<FlyTo preset="view-gulf"><Term id="term-hormuz">霍尔木兹</Term></FlyTo>。这是波斯湾入口附近的岛上港口。城镇、岛屿和附近海峡共用这个名称。今天的阿巴斯港则在大陆上，是另一座港口。[S1]

到了港口，访客可以买卖商品、交换外交礼物，也可能承认皇帝的权威。这些活动常常同时发生。通事帮助访客与当地统治者、商人沟通。同行者记下自己的见闻，但只能讲述部分情况。当地人也有各自参与往来的理由。[S1, S4, S17]

1414 年，<FlyTo preset="view-bengal">孟加拉</FlyTo>国王赠给永乐皇帝一头长颈鹿。明廷官员把这头陌生的动物与<Term id="term-qilin">麒麟</Term>联系起来。麒麟是传说中带来吉祥的动物，因此礼物在宫廷中有了特殊含义。这头鹿经过孟加拉国王赠送，并非郑和在非洲捕获后直接运回中国。[S5]

船队也使用武力。1415 年，<FlyTo preset="view-sumatra">苏门答剌</FlyTo>发生继位争端，郑和俘获一位争夺统治权的人，把他带往中国。有关争端的记载互有矛盾。哥伦比亚大学教案解释为支持获承认的统治者，史家韦德则强调明朝施加支配。同一支船队带去礼物，也带走俘虏。宗教与外交赠礼、贸易和武力干预，共同构成了这些港口往来的历史。[S2, S29]

<More title={{ en: "An island, a town and a strait", zh: "岛、城与海峡" }}>

“霍尔木兹”可以指城镇、岛屿或海峡。远航记载中的目的地在岛上。阿巴斯港在大陆另一边，不能混为一处。当时码头的准确位置和船只进港的路线仍不清楚。[S1]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The animal and its political meaning", zh: "动物与宫廷寓意" }}>

长颈鹿是真实动物，麒麟则来自神话。明廷官员把这份礼物看成吉祥的象征。大都会艺术博物馆介绍 1414 年的事件时，配的是后来清代的画作，远晚于赠礼发生的年代。[S5]

</More>
