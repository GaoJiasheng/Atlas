<Lang en>

The fourth expedition, conventionally dated 1413–1415, extended the fleet's destinations beyond India to <FlyTo preset="view-gulf"><Term id="term-hormuz">Hormuz</Term></FlyTo>. This was an island port near the entrance to the Persian Gulf. The marker locates the town on the island; it does not stand for every shore around the strait or for today's mainland port of Bandar Abbas. [S1]

Trade, diplomatic gifts and recognition of an emperor's authority could meet in the same encounter, but they were different activities. Interpreters and other diplomatic specialists helped visitors communicate with rulers and merchants. The accounts left by travellers preserve what particular observers noticed. They cannot supply every local person's reasons for taking part in an exchange. [S1, S4, S17]

In 1414 a giraffe reached the Yongle emperor as a gift from the king of <FlyTo preset="view-bengal">Bengal</FlyTo>. Courtiers associated the unfamiliar animal with the <Term id="term-qilin">qilin</Term>, an auspicious creature from Chinese tradition. The gift therefore acquired a courtly meaning beyond the animal itself. Bengal's king belongs in this story: it cannot be shortened into an admiral catching an African animal and taking it directly to China. [S5]

Gifts did not remove coercion. In 1415, during a succession conflict in <FlyTo preset="view-sumatra">Samudera</FlyTo>, Zheng He captured a claimant to power and took him to China. The succession accounts conflict. Columbia's teaching guide frames interventions as support for recognised rulers; historian Geoff Wade stresses attempts to impose Ming dominance. Captivity remains part of the account under either interpretation. [S2, S29]

A place-name may describe different geographic things. Here Hormuz means the island destination in the expedition account. Moving the marker to Bandar Abbas would place that destination on a different shore. The map offers a town locator, without reconstructing a medieval harbour or a ship's approach. [S1]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "The animal and its political meaning", zh: "动物与宫廷寓意" }}>

A giraffe is a real animal; the qilin is a mythical creature. Their association tells us how courtiers interpreted a gift. The Metropolitan Museum's discussion explains the 1414 presentation alongside a much later Qing painting. That painting is not an eyewitness picture of the Ming event, and the illustration here is newly made. [S5]

</More>

<Lang zh>

1413—1415 年的第四航次，把目的地从印度延伸到波斯湾入口附近的<FlyTo preset="view-gulf"><Term id="term-hormuz">霍尔木兹</Term></FlyTo>。这是岛上的港口。图中标记定位岛上城镇，不能移到今天大陆上的阿巴斯港，也不能把城、岛和海峡当成同一个地方。地名相近，不表示地点可以互换。[S1]

到了港口，买卖、赠礼和承认皇帝威望可能同时发生，却不是同一件事。通事与外交人员帮助访客和当地统治者、商人沟通。同行者留下的记述各有观察角度，不能代替当地所有人的声音，也不能证明参加交换的人都有相同打算。[S1, S4, S17]

1414 年，孟加拉国王赠给永乐皇帝一头长颈鹿。明廷官员把它与象征祥瑞的<Term id="term-qilin">麒麟</Term>联系起来，真实动物因而获得了宫廷政治寓意。讲这件礼物，必须保留孟加拉国王这一环，不能缩写成郑和在非洲捕兽后直接运回中国。[S5]

赠礼之外也有强制。1415 年，<FlyTo preset="view-sumatra">苏门答剌</FlyTo>发生继位争端，郑和俘获争夺统治权的人，并把他带到中国；有关继位的记载互有矛盾。哥伦比亚大学教案强调支持获承认的统治者，史家韦德则强调明朝施加支配。解释不同，被俘和押送的事实仍须写清。[S2, S29]
