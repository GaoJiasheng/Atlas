<Lang en>

The first expedition, conventionally dated 1405–1407, reached ports on India's southwest coast. Its return brought the fleet through a trading world where rulers and armed groups competed for influence. <FlyTo preset="view-india">Calicut and Kochi</FlyTo> locate that western connection; the map does not give an unrecorded day-by-day itinerary between them and the straits. [S1]

In 1407 the Ming fleet fought Chen Zuyi's force at <Term id="term-old-port">Old Port</Term>, associated here with present-day Palembang. Ming records reported about <Num s="S2">5,000</Num> people killed. Chen was captured and taken to China for punishment. The death figure comes from the attacking fleet's records and has not been independently verified. It tells us what the Ming side reported, rather than providing a complete account from everyone caught in the fighting. [S1, S2]

That year, Ming records also reported about <Num s="S2">170</Num> Ming troops killed during a local conflict in Java. This report likewise comes through the court's account. It does not establish the number of local people killed, or make all Javanese people responsible. The sources available here do not provide matching totals for each side at either place. Missing numbers remain unknown. [S2]

The next expedition is conventionally dated 1407–1409. Voyages continued through existing ports and relationships after these killings. Calling them trading missions alone would leave out their armed power; describing only battles would leave out the exchanges and diplomatic work. Fleet command also does not establish that Zheng He personally attended every voyage or landed at every mapped point. [S1, S4]

<More title={{ en: "Whose description is “pirate”?", zh: "谁把谁称为“海盗”" }}>

Ming accounts treated Chen Zuyi as a pirate and an opponent. Geoff Wade places the action within struggles over ports and Ming efforts to impose authority. Keeping the court's label attributed makes it possible to examine the power behind that description. [S1, S2]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Reported deaths and missing totals", zh: "报告的死亡与缺少的总数" }}>

The Old Port figure of about <Num s="S2">5,000</Num> killed and the Java figure of <Num s="S2">170</Num> Ming troops killed are separate reports about different events. They are not a balanced comparison of total casualties. Neither report supplies all local and Ming losses; absence of a number does not mean nobody died. [S2]

</More>

<Lang zh>

第一次远航通常记作 1405—1407 年，船队抵达印度西南岸港口，再沿已有的海上网络回程。那里既有交换，也有不同统治者与武装势力的竞争。<FlyTo preset="view-india">古里与柯枝</FlyTo>帮助定位西面的联系；地图没有为港口之间补上逐日行程。[S1]

1407 年，明朝船队在<Term id="term-old-port">旧港</Term>与陈祖义的势力交战，本主题将旧港对应为今天的巨港。明方记录报告约有 <Num s="S2">5,000</Num> 人被杀。陈祖义被俘送往中国接受惩处。死亡数字来自进攻船队一方，没有独立核实；它不能代替所有卷入战事者的经历。[S1, S2]

同年，明方记录还报告约 <Num s="S2">170</Num> 名明军在爪哇一场当地冲突中被杀。两份报告来自同一方，但并不是同一场战斗。这个数字同样经过朝廷记载留下，不能据此推定当地人的死亡总数，也不能把责任归给所有爪哇人。两处事件都缺少可供对照的各方总伤亡，未知不能填成零。[S2]

第二次远航通常记作 1407—1409 年，船队在这些事件之后继续往返。贸易、外交与武力同时存在，只讲其中一面都会漏掉历史。统领远航也不等于郑和亲自参加每次航行，更不能由地图上的地点推定他处处登陆。[S1, S4]
