<Lang en>

The sixth expedition, dated 1421–1422, carried envoys home across the Indian Ocean. For the Ming fleet, sailing out from China was an outward journey. For some envoys aboard, it was the journey home. These exchanges linked courts and ports in many directions. Rulers understood the relationships differently, and some people took part under force. [S1, S4, S2]

At <FlyTo preset="view-straits">Melaka</FlyTo>, ships gathered, restocked supplies and waited to sail. Historian Wan Ming links the port's role to seasonal winds and return schedules. Waiting for suitable conditions was part of organising a voyage. Surviving records do not give daily sailing logs or show the same stop on every expedition. [S12]

A <Term id="term-monsoon">monsoon</Term> is a large seasonal change in wind circulation. Seasonal winds help explain why sailing schedules mattered. Modern Singapore weather records describe local conditions, however, rather than winds across the whole Indian Ocean. We do not know the wind beside a particular ship in 1422. [S16, S15]

The court had to decide how much money and attention to give the fleets. Columbia's account stresses imperial prestige, expense and competing priorities. Wade stresses control over ports and trade. After 1421–1422, the state expeditions paused until the final voyage of 1431–1433. Merchants continued to have their own changing networks. [S1, S2, S29, S28]

<More title={{ en: "Waiting at Melaka", zh: "在马六甲候风" }}>

Wan Ming links Melaka's location and seasonal winds to its role as a gathering and storage place. This helps explain why fleets waited there before returning. The exact departure days, daily speeds and stops of each fleet remain uncertain. [S12]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why winds change by season", zh: "风为何随季节变化" }}>

Meteorological Service Singapore explains seasonal wind changes and Singapore's modern climate. These are modern observations, rather than measurements from Ming ships. Even within a monsoon season, local winds vary. Sailors needed more than a season's name to plan their journey. [S16, S15]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Several possible futures", zh: "并非只有一种结局" }}>

Later European voyages led to colonial empires. That does not mean every earlier expedition was bound to follow the same path. The Ming fleets served the choices and needs of their own time, using diplomacy as well as force. [S18, S2, S29]

</More>

<Lang zh>

1421—1422 年的第六次远航，载着使节返回印度洋各地。从中国看是出海，从一些使节看却是回乡。船队把各地朝廷与港口连在一起，往来朝着多个方向展开。统治者对这些关系有不同理解，有些人也并非自愿参加。[S1, S4, S2]

在<FlyTo preset="view-straits">马六甲</FlyTo>，船只集合、补给，并等待适合出航的时机。万明把港口的作用与季节风、回程安排联系起来。等待也是组织航行的一部分。留下的史料没有逐日航海日志，也不能确定每次远航都在这里停靠。[S12]

<Term id="term-monsoon">季风</Term>是大范围随季节变化的风循环。季节风有助于解释船队为什么要安排航期。不过，现代新加坡气候资料描述的是本地情况，不能代表整个印度洋。1422 年某艘船旁边吹什么风，我们并不知道。[S16, S15]

朝廷要决定为远航投入多少金钱和精力。哥伦比亚大学资料强调皇帝威望、开支与其他事务；韦德则更重视港口和贸易控制。1421—1422 年以后，国家远航暂停，直到 1431—1433 年最后一次出航。商人的海上网络仍有各自的变化。朝廷决定是否派出船队，商人则沿着自己的关系往来。国家远航的间隔，不能说明海上所有船只都停了下来。[S1, S2, S29, S28]

<More title={{ en: "Waiting at Melaka", zh: "在马六甲候风" }}>

万明结合马六甲的位置与季节风，解释它作为集合和仓储据点的作用。这有助于理解船队为何在回程前候风，但各船队的确切离港日、日行速度和停靠处仍不清楚。[S12]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Why winds change by season", zh: "风为何随季节变化" }}>

新加坡气象署介绍季节风的变化与现代新加坡气候。这些是现代观察资料，并非明代船上的测量。即使同属一个季风季节，局地风向仍会变化。水手还要掌握更多资料，才能安排航行。[S16, S15]

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Several possible futures", zh: "并非只有一种结局" }}>

后来欧洲的远航发展成殖民帝国，却不表示更早的船队必然走向同样结局。明朝船队回应的是当时的需要与选择，既运用外交，也使用武力。[S18, S2, S29]

</More>
