<Lang en>

From the stomach the food passes into the small intestine, a narrow tube about <Num s="S6">3 m</Num> long, coiled in the middle of the abdomen. Most digestion and most absorption happen here. Its first part, the duodenum, curves in a C round the pancreas. The rest lies in loose coils that end low on the right, where the small intestine opens into the large intestine.

In the small intestine, digestive juices finish the work. They break starch, protein and fat down into simpler substances that are small enough to pass through the wall of the intestine. The simpler substances are absorbed into the blood, which carries them round the body. On the stage, olive dots leave the coils to show digested food going into the blood. Food takes about <Num s="S6">3 to 5 hours</Num> to pass along the small intestine.

The inside of the small intestine is covered in tiny finger-like <Term id="villi">villi</Term>, about <Num s="S6">0.5 to 1 mm</Num> tall, with tiny blood vessels inside them. Villi make the inside surface very large, so digested food passes into the blood quickly; the blood then carries it round the body, as <TopicLink slug="heart-and-lungs" ch="three-systems">Three systems working together</TopicLink> shows. Most of the water that enters the gut each day is taken back here too: about <Num s="S8">90%</Num> of the roughly <Num s="S8">9 litres</Num> that reach the small intestine. <FlyTo preset="abdomen">Look at the coils</FlyTo>

Food never passes through the liver, gallbladder or pancreas. The liver makes <Term id="bile">bile</Term>, about <Num s="S7">1 litre</Num> a day, and the gallbladder stores it. Bile breaks fat into tiny droplets. The pancreas makes over <Num s="S7">1 litre</Num> of pancreatic juice a day, which digests starch, protein and fat and neutralises the acid from the stomach. Both enter the duodenum through one opening. <FlyTo preset="back-organs">Look behind the stomach</FlyTo>

<More title={{ en: "How big is the inside?", zh: "里面有多大？" }}>

A textbook figure for the inside surface of the small intestine, counting the villi and the even smaller folds on their cells, is about <Num s="S6">200 square metres</Num>. A 2014 study that measured the lining in a different way put the whole gut at about <Num s="S15">32 square metres</Num>. The two numbers measure different things, but both show a very large surface packed into a small space. The model's coils are about <Num s="S1">2 m</Num> long; the source labels them "jejunum", but they run all the way to the large intestine, so they also stand for the last part, the ileum.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: fold a surface", zh: "试一试：把表面折起来" }}>

Lay a paper towel flat and draw round it. Then pleat it into tight folds and see how little space it takes. Folds and villi let a large surface fit inside a narrow tube.

</More>

<Lang zh>

食物离开胃，进入小肠。小肠是一条很细的管子，长约 <Num s="S6">3 米</Num>，盘绕在腹部中间。大部分消化和大部分吸收都在这里进行。小肠的第一段叫十二指肠，绕着胰弯成 C 形；其余部分松松地盘成很多圈，最后在腹部右下方通入大肠。

在小肠里，消化液完成剩下的工作。它们把淀粉、蛋白质和脂肪分解成更简单的物质，小到能穿过肠壁。这些更简单的物质被吸收进血液，再由血液运送到全身。舞台上，橄榄色的点离开肠圈，表示消化后的食物进入血液。食物通过小肠约需 <Num s="S6">3–5 小时</Num>。

小肠的内壁布满细小的指状<Term id="villi">绒毛</Term>，高约 <Num s="S6">0.5–1 毫米</Num>，里面有细小的血管。绒毛让内表面变得很大，消化后的食物能很快进入血液；血液再把它运到全身，见<TopicLink slug="heart-and-lungs" ch="three-systems">三个系统一起工作</TopicLink>。每天进入消化道的水，大部分也在这里被吸收：进入小肠的约 <Num s="S8">9 升</Num>液体中，约 <Num s="S8">90%</Num> 的水在小肠被吸收。<FlyTo preset="abdomen">看看肠圈</FlyTo>

<More title={{ en: "How big is the inside?", zh: "里面有多大？" }}>

按课本的估算，把绒毛和绒毛细胞上更小的褶都算进去，小肠的内表面约 <Num s="S6">200 平方米</Num>。2014 年的一项研究用另一种方法量内壁，估算整条消化道约 <Num s="S15">32 平方米</Num>。两个数字量的东西不同，但都说明很大的表面装在了很小的空间里。模型的肠圈约长 <Num s="S1">2 米</Num>；源文件把它标为“空肠”，但它一直连到大肠，所以也代表小肠的最后一段——回肠。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: fold a surface", zh: "试一试：把表面折起来" }}>

把一张厨房纸平铺，沿边描出它的大小。再把它折成密密的褶，看它只占多小的地方。褶皱和绒毛让很大的表面装进一条细管里。

</More>
