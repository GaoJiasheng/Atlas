<Lang en>

What is left after the small intestine passes into the large intestine. It is about <Num s="S6">7.6 cm</Num> wide, three times as wide as the small intestine, and runs round the coils like a frame. It starts low on the right with a pouch called the caecum, where the small intestine opens into it. From there the colon goes up the right side, across under the liver and stomach, and down the left side into the pelvis. The large intestine ends with the rectum and the anus.

By now most of the useful substances in the food have been absorbed. What arrives is mostly water, fibre from plant foods and other material that could not be digested. As it moves slowly along, the large intestine absorbs water, with some mineral salts, back into the blood, and the remains become firmer. Bacteria living in the large intestine break down some of the fibre. The remains usually stay in the large intestine for about <Num s="S6">12 to 24 hours</Num>.

The remains are now <Term id="faeces">faeces</Term>: undigested food, bacteria and cells from the lining of the gut. Faeces are stored in the rectum. Two rings of muscle keep the <Term id="anus">anus</Term>, the opening at the end of the digestive tube, closed until faeces are passed out of the body. On the stage, blue dots show water leaving the colon, and the dots inside turn brown as faeces form.

<More title={{ en: "Most water is taken back earlier", zh: "大部分水分在小肠就被吸收了" }}>

The syllabus says the large intestine absorbs water, and it does. But most of the water in the gut, about <Num s="S8">90%</Num> of what enters the small intestine, has already been absorbed before the large intestine. The large intestine absorbs most of what is left.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Water and fibre", zh: "水和膳食纤维" }}>

Singapore's Health Promotion Board advises choosing water instead of sugary drinks, and eating fibre from fruit, vegetables and wholegrains, which helps the bowels work regularly. The appendix, a narrow tube on the caecum, is closed at one end; food does not travel through it.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "About the model", zh: "关于模型" }}>

The source file has no separate caecum, rectum or anus. The caecum is the rounded lower end of the ascending colon. The source calls the lower loop of the colon "descending colon" and the last part "sigmoid colon"; Gray's Anatomy places the sigmoid colon as a loop in the pelvis and the rectum along the front of the sacrum, about <Num s="S10">12 cm</Num> long, so Atlas names that last part the rectum, ending at the anus.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: a water diary", zh: "试一试：喝水日记" }}>

For two days, write down each time you drink water, and which meals include fruit, vegetables or wholegrains. Compare your diary with the Health Promotion Board's advice on water and fibre. This is a record to notice habits, not a score.

</More>

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

食物经过小肠后，剩下的残渣会进入大肠。大肠的直径约为 <Num s="S6">7.6 厘米</Num>，约是小肠直径的三倍。它像一个框，围在盘曲的小肠外面。大肠从身体的右下腹开始，起点是一个叫盲肠的小袋子，小肠就在这里与大肠相连。从盲肠开始，结肠沿身体右侧向上，在肝脏和胃的下方横向延伸，再沿身体左侧向下，进入骨盆。大肠的末端是直肠和肛门。

到了这里，食物中大部分有用的物质已经被吸收。进入大肠的主要是水、来自植物性食物的纤维，以及其他无法消化的物质。残渣慢慢向前移动时，大肠将其中的水分和一些矿物盐吸收回血液中，残渣也就逐渐变硬。大肠里的细菌会分解部分纤维。残渣通常会在大肠里停留约 <Num s="S6">12–24 小时</Num>。

这时，残渣就形成了<Term id="faeces">粪便</Term>，其中包括未消化的食物、细菌，以及从肠道内壁脱落的细胞。粪便暂时储存在直肠里。<Term id="anus">肛门</Term>是消化道末端的开口，周围有两圈肌肉，平时让肛门保持关闭。排便时，粪便才从这里排出体外。模型中，蓝点表示结肠吸收的水分；肠内的点变成褐色，表示粪便逐渐形成。

<More title={{ en: "Most water is taken back earlier", zh: "大部分水分在小肠就被吸收了" }}>

课纲提到大肠会吸收水分。不过，消化道里的大部分水分在到达大肠之前就被吸收了：进入小肠的水分，约有 <Num s="S8">90%</Num> 会在小肠被吸收。大肠再吸收剩余水分中的大部分。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Water and fibre", zh: "水和膳食纤维" }}>

新加坡保健促进局建议用水代替含糖饮料，并从水果、蔬菜和全谷类食物中摄取膳食纤维。膳食纤维有助于让排便更规律。阑尾是一条与盲肠相连的细管，一端封闭，食物不会从这条管子通过。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "About the model", zh: "关于模型" }}>

原始模型文件没有把盲肠、直肠或肛门分开建模。模型中的盲肠是升结肠下端圆鼓鼓的部分。原始文件把结肠下方弯曲的部分称为“降结肠”，把最后一段称为“乙状结肠”。不过，《格雷解剖学》指出，乙状结肠在骨盆内弯曲，直肠则沿着骶骨（脊柱下端的一块骨头）前方向下延伸，长约 <Num s="S10">12 厘米</Num>。因此，Atlas 将模型的最后一段标为直肠，末端是肛门。

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Try it: a water diary", zh: "试一试：喝水日记" }}>

连续两天，记下每次喝水的时间，以及哪几餐吃了水果、蔬菜或全谷类食物。再把记录与保健促进局关于喝水和膳食纤维的建议作比较。这份记录是帮助你了解自己的习惯，不需要打分。

</More>
