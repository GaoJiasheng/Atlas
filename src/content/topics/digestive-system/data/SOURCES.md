# The digestive system — sources

P4 scope follows the MOE Science syllabus [S2]: the five body systems and their functions, and the parts of the digestive system (mouth, gullet, stomach, small intestine, large intestine) and what each does. The main text uses the syllabus wording: food is digested into simpler substances, which are absorbed into the blood; the large intestine absorbs water. The liver, gallbladder and pancreas are Further study / 再进一步. The chicken-rice chapter follows where a meal goes and how long it typically takes; it does not judge the meal's nutrition. Physiology follows OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e [S3–S8], checked against NIDDK [S9]; positions and the identification of the colon and rectum meshes follow Gray 1918 [S10]; health advice follows Singapore's Health Promotion Board [S13, S14, S19]. The chapter figures are Atlas teaching illustrations, not copies of OpenStax or Gray plates.

Every number in chapter prose is wrapped in `<Num s="S#">`; numbers in part details cite `[S#]`. Textbook values describe an adult, not a P4 pupil or this mesh. Display speeds, particle counts, magnifications and contraction timing are design values [S1], not physiological readings. No disease, diagnosis, prescribed intake or quiz is included.

## Anatomy review: how the source meshes are named on the page

- **Stomach.** The source mesh has a round window, about 5 cm across, cut in its front wall to show the lining. A pupil reads it as a hole, so the build closes that one opening with a patch whose interior solves a bi-harmonic system against the wall around it (936 triangles, seamless). The cardia and pylorus rings stay open, where the gullet and duodenum join.
- **Duodenum.** A 0.5 mm pinhole near the papilla had been taken as the tube's far end, so the first centre line stopped after 9 cm and left a 70 mm gap to the jejunum. Pinholes are no longer tube ends (`centrelines.py`); the duodenum's centre line now runs its whole C, 0.21 m, to the jejunum.
- **"Jejunum" = jejunum and ileum.** The coiled source mesh runs from the duodenum to the caecum, so it includes the ileum, which opens into the caecum [S6, S10]. Its centre line is 2.0 m, shorter than a living small intestine (about 3 m [S6]); text and S1 say so. At about a dozen tight bends neighbouring coils of the source overlap slightly; the food route passes straight through them.
- **Caecum.** Not a separate source mesh: it is the rounded lower end of the ascending colon, where the appendix hangs.
- **"Descending colon" includes the sigmoid colon.** The source mesh runs down the left side and then loops into the pelvis to the midline, where Gray places the sigmoid loop [S10].
- **"Sigmoid colon" is the rectum.** The mesh lies in the midline along the front of the sacrum and coccyx, its centre line is about 12–14 cm and its lower end widens and then narrows to the outlet: Gray's rectum (from the third sacral vertebra, in the sacrococcygeal curve, about 12 cm, widening into the ampulla) and anal canal [S10]; OpenStax also places the rectum in front of the sacrum and coccyx [S6]. The page names it "Rectum and anus" / 直肠与肛门; the glb node keeps its source name `sigmoid-colon`.
- **Teeth.** The source has 28 teeth, without third molars; the text gives the 32 of a full adult set [S4] and says the model has 28.

## Food route and flows

All four food legs (`swallow`, `stomach-pass`, `small-gut`, `large-gut`) join end to end (`ends: open`), so the food moves continuously from the mouth to the anus. `scripts/anatomy/digestive/food_route.py` traces them inside the meshes: consecutive tube meshes touch (gaps under 0.5 mm), and each leg runs between its contact patches with the segments before and after it (slice centroids of the surface-geodesic field; the jejunum keeps the pipeline's exact source-ring stations). The mouth-and-throat leg (over the tongue, down the middle of the throat meshes), the churning loop in the stomach, the saliva, bile and pancreatic-juice paths and the absorption branches are schematic and anchored to the meshes; bile and pancreatic juice end at the same point of the duodenum's second part, where the two ducts open together [S7]. `food-route.json` records the leg lengths and a six-ray wall-crossing check for every path point. Colours: olive = food, ochre = the mixture from the stomach, grey to brown = what is left becoming faeces, blue-grey = digestive juices, olive branches = digested food into the blood, blue = water taken back.

The engine has no travelling peristalsis wave and no speed change along one flow (docs/25 E-D3, E-D4): each organ pulses as a whole in a staggered sequence, and each leg of the route has its own speed. Both are said in the chapter notes.
