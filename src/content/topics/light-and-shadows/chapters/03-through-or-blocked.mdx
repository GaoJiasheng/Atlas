<Lang en>

Put clear glass in the holder and look at the screen. Most of the light goes straight through, so the glass leaves only a very faint shadow, and you can see clearly through it. A glass maker gives the light passing through clear glass of this kind as <Num s="S8">91 %</Num>.

Change to frosted glass. It lets a lot of light through as well: about <Num s="S9">83 %</Num> for the frosted glass in the maker's figures. But its rough face scatters the light in all directions, so you cannot see clearly through it, and less light reaches the screen straight behind. Its shadow is lighter, not dark. Tracing paper does the same: it lets some light through and scatters it, so its shadow is lighter than cardboard's.

Now try the cardboard. It lets no light through: all the light that reaches it is blocked, so its shadow is dark.

<More title={{"en": "Most, some or none", "zh": "大部分、一部分、完全不"}}>

You can sort materials by whether they let most light, some light or no light through. Some books use three words for this: <Term id="transparent">transparent</Term> for materials you can see clearly through, such as clear glass; <Term id="translucent">translucent</Term> for materials that let light through but blur what is behind, such as frosted glass and tracing paper; and <Term id="opaque">opaque</Term> for materials that let no light through, such as cardboard. You do not need these words; "most, some or none" says the same thing. Translucent does not mean exactly half the light.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Frosted glass lets most light through", "zh": "磨砂玻璃让大部分光穿过"}}>

Letting light through and letting you see through are not the same. Frosted glass lets about <Num s="S9">83 %</Num> of the light through, nearly as much as clear glass, but it spreads that light out. In this model, with the screen <Num s="S1">30 cm</Num> behind the sheet instead of <Num s="S1">10 cm</Num>, the sensor reads only <Num s="S1">18 %</Num> for frosted glass and <Num s="S1">14 %</Num> for tracing paper: the spread-out light thins out over the longer distance, so the shadow looks darker.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "About these numbers", "zh": "关于这些数字"}}>

The two glass figures are what a maker states for its products. For tracing paper, one measured sample of thick tracing paper let about <Num s="S10">65 %</Num> of the light through; thinner tracing papers let through more. The way this model spreads the light is deliberately simple, so its sensor readings are a guide, not a prediction for a sheet in your classroom. Cardboard is taken to let no light through. In a real test, first take a reading with nothing in the holder, then compare each sheet with it.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Most, some or none", "zh": "大部分、一部分、完全不"}}>

可以按“让大部分光、一部分光，还是完全不让光穿过”给材料分类。有些书用三个词来说：<Term id="transparent">透明</Term>，指隔着它看得清楚的材料，例如透明玻璃；<Term id="translucent">半透明</Term>，指让光穿过、但后面看不清的材料，例如磨砂玻璃和描图纸；<Term id="opaque">不透明</Term>，指完全不让光穿过的材料，例如硬纸板。不用这三个词也没关系，说“大部分、一部分、完全不”意思一样。“半透明”不是“正好一半的光”。

</More>

让光穿过和让人看得清，不是一回事。磨砂玻璃让约 <Num s="S9">83%</Num> 的光穿过，差不多和透明玻璃一样多，只是把光散开了。在这个模型里，屏幕从材料片后面 <Num s="S1">10 厘米</Num>移到 <Num s="S1">30 厘米</Num>，磨砂玻璃的读数只剩 <Num s="S1">18%</Num>，描图纸只剩 <Num s="S1">14%</Num>：散开的光走得越远越稀，影子看起来就更深。

</More>
