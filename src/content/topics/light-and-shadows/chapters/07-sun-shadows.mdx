Outdoors, the light source is the Sun. In an open courtyard a straight pole stands <Num s="S1">1 m</Num> high, and an arrow on the ground points north. The model follows the pole's shadow through one day, <Num s="S1">20 October 2026</Num>, at a place in Singapore. Like the torch and the card on the bench, the Sun and the pole make a shadow that points directly away from the light.

At <Time t="08:00" /> the Sun is low in the east. In this model the shadow is about <Num s="S1">3.25 m</Num> long, more than three times the height of the pole, and it points west. Use the arrow to read its <Term id="compass-direction">compass direction</Term>. As the Sun climbs, the shadow gets shorter.

Around the middle of the day the Sun is high and the shadow is short: about <Num s="S1">0.21 m</Num> at <Time t="13:00" />. On this day the Sun is highest at about <Num s="S1"><Time t="12:50" /></Num>, not at <Time t="12:00" />. In the afternoon the Sun is in the west, so the shadow points east and grows long again: about <Num s="S1">1.99 m</Num> at <Time t="17:00" />.

<More title={{"en": "Why the Sun seems to move", "zh": "太阳为什么看起来在动"}}>

The Sun does not go round us. The Earth turns, once a day, and as it turns the Sun seems to rise in the east, cross the sky and set in the west. The Sun is also very far away, so its light reaches the courtyard in almost parallel lines. That is why the pole's shadow is about as wide as the pole, instead of spreading out the way the card's shadow does near a torch.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Noon is not 12 o'clock here", "zh": "这里的正午不是十二点"}}>

Singapore's clocks are set <Num s="S14">8 hours</Num> ahead of world time (UTC). For Singapore's longitude that puts the clock about an hour ahead of the Sun, so the Sun reaches its highest point later than <Time t="12:00" />. In this model it happens between about <Num s="S1"><Time t="12:48" /></Num> and <Num s="S1"><Time t="13:19" /></Num>, depending on the time of year. For how to write morning and afternoon times, see <TopicLink slug="telling-time" ch="am-and-pm">am and pm</TopicLink>.

The midday shadow does not always point north either. From about late March to late September it points south. Around <Num s="S1">23–24 March</Num> and <Num s="S1">19–20 September 2026</Num> the Sun at midday passes almost straight overhead, and a pole has almost no shadow.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How these numbers were worked out", "zh": "这些数字是怎样算出来的"}}>

This goes beyond the syllabus. The Sun's height and direction come from NOAA's solar calculator. For an upright pole on flat ground, the length of the shadow is the height of the pole divided by the tangent of the Sun's height, a rule you will meet in secondary school mathematics. The Sun is not a point: it looks a little over <Num s="S17">0.5°</Num> across, so a real shadow's tip is slightly blurred. The model leaves that out and assumes a clear sky.

Watch shadows outdoors with an adult, and never look straight at the Sun.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Why the Sun seems to move", "zh": "太阳为什么看起来在动"}}>

太阳并没有绕着我们转。是地球在转，每天转一圈；地球一转，太阳看起来就从东边升起，越过天空，在西边落下。太阳又非常远，它的光照到空地上时几乎是平行的，所以杆子的影子差不多和杆子一样粗，不会像手电筒前的卡片那样越照越大。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "Noon is not 12 o'clock here", "zh": "这里的正午不是十二点"}}>

新加坡的时钟比世界标准时间（UTC）快 <Num s="S14">8 小时</Num>。按新加坡的经度，这让时钟比太阳快了大约一个小时，所以太阳到达最高点的时刻比 <Time t="12:00" /> 晚。在这个模型里，按一年里不同的时候，大约在 <Num s="S1"><Time t="12:48" /></Num> 到 <Num s="S1"><Time t="13:19" /></Num> 之间。上午、下午的时间怎样写，可以看<TopicLink slug="telling-time" ch="am-and-pm">上午和下午</TopicLink>。

中午的影子也不总是指向北边。大约从 3 月下旬到 9 月下旬，它指向南边。在 <Num s="S1">3 月 23–24 日</Num>和 <Num s="S1">2026 年 9 月 19–20 日</Num>前后，中午的太阳几乎从头顶正上方经过，杆子几乎没有影子。

</More>

<More title={{"en": "How these numbers were worked out", "zh": "这些数字是怎样算出来的"}}>

这部分超出了课纲。太阳的高度和方向来自 NOAA 的太阳计算器。竖直的杆子立在平地上时，影长 = 杆高 ÷ 太阳高度角的正切，这是中学数学才会学到的方法。太阳不是一个点，看上去略大于 <Num s="S17">0.5°</Num>，所以真实影子的尖端有点模糊。模型没有算这一点，并假设天空无云。

和大人一起到户外观察影子，千万不要直视太阳。
