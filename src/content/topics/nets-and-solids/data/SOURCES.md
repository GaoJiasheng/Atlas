# Sources · nets-and-solids

The syllabus lines quoted in the lesson come from S1 only (wording and page numbers checked against the PDF on
2026-10-21, docs/39 §1.1). The syllabus does not define "2D representations" (Nets 4.1); the line sits under the
heading Nets, so the lesson reads it as nets, and the nets of a cylinder and a cone are only recognised (step 08).
The syllabus has no line on counting edges or vertices, on surface area or on Euler's formula, and the lesson does
none of these. The misconception research and teaching sources (S9, S18, S19, S20, S21, S24) shape which mistakes the
beats name and which wrong answers the checks diagnose. None of them is Singapore data; S24 is a tuition website.
The lesson does not claim how often any mistake happens. The codes cube-cuboid and curved-unrolled are this lesson's
own diagnoses, with no research source.

The counts in the lesson are 11 nets of a cube (S3; the 35 shapes of six squares, 11 of them nets, S6), 54 nets of a
cuboid whose three lengths are all different (S7, S8; given only with that condition) and 2 nets of a triangular
pyramid made of four equal triangles (S3). All four numbers were also checked by computer when the engine was built
(`tests/math-scene/net.test.ts`). Every net and solid on the stage is drawn by the engine from its geometry, and
every "it folds" or "it does not" in a question is checked by folding the net, not written by hand. The cuboids are
measured in grid squares only, never in centimetres. Names in the questions are made up.

The cover drawing (a cross-shaped net of a cube) and the two chapter figures
(`public/illustrations/figures/nets-and-solids-{open-carton,cylinder-faces}.webp`: a tall box with its lid turned
open, and an empty tin with its lid beside it) were drawn earlier for this lesson in pencil style (`art/planned/`,
reviewed per docs/39 §6) and processed with `scripts/illustrate.ts`. None of them carries numbers, letters or brand
names. Chinese terms follow mainland primary usage and the other lessons on this site (正方体, 长方体, 棱柱, 棱锥);
S30 and S31 are checks only, and no official Chinese terms for Singapore primary mathematics were found.
