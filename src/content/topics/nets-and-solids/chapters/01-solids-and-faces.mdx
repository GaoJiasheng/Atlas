<Lang en>

Look around a classroom and you will find boxes, tins and balls. In this lesson you will open shapes like these out flat, and fold flat shapes back up. First, you need words for their parts.

Start with <Beat n={1}>the five solids in a row</Beat>: a <Term id="cube">cube</Term>, a <Term id="cuboid">cuboid</Term>, a <Term id="cylinder">cylinder</Term>, a <Term id="cone">cone</Term> and a <Term id="sphere">sphere</Term>. You have met them before. A <Term id="solid">solid</Term> takes up space. You can hold it and turn it round, and it looks different from each side.

Now <Beat n={2}>turn the cube and count its faces</Beat>. Each flat side of a solid is a <Term id="face">face</Term>. Count them as the cube turns: top, bottom, front, back, left and right. A cube has 6 faces, and all of them are squares of the same size. A dice is shaped like a cube.

Next, <Beat n={3}>look at the cuboid's faces in pairs</Beat>. A cuboid has 6 faces too, but they are rectangles. They come in 3 pairs: each face has a twin of the same size on the opposite side. The colours and the marks ●, ▲ and ■ show which faces go together. A cuboid can have one pair of squares, like a tall box with square ends. It is still a cuboid, because its other faces are rectangles.

Finally, <Beat n={4}>find the curved faces</Beat>. Some faces are not flat. A cylinder has 2 <Term id="flat-face">flat faces</Term> that are circles, and 1 <Term id="curved-face">curved face</Term> joining them. A cone has 1 flat circle and 1 curved face that narrows to a point. A sphere has only a curved face, so it rolls whichever way you put it down. On the stage, flat faces are drawn in one colour and curved faces in another.

A common mix-up is to call every box a cube. Look at the faces before you name it: a cube has 6 squares all the same size, and a box with rectangles on its sides is a cuboid.

When you are ready, <Task id="solids-and-faces">try one question</Task> on naming a solid or counting its faces.

</Lang>

<Lang zh>

在教室里看一圈，就能找到盒子、罐子和球。这节课要把这样的形状摊平，再把平的图形折回去。动手以前，先要知道它们的各个部分怎么叫。

先看<Beat n={1}>排成一排的五种立体</Beat>：<Term id="cube">正方体</Term>、<Term id="cuboid">长方体</Term>、<Term id="cylinder">圆柱</Term>、<Term id="cone">圆锥</Term>和<Term id="sphere">球</Term>。它们你以前都见过。<Term id="solid">立体图形</Term>占着空间，可以拿在手里转着看，从不同方向看，样子也不一样。这节课把它简称为“立体”。

再<Beat n={2}>转着正方体数一数它的面</Beat>。立体上每一块平平的面叫作<Term id="face">面</Term>。跟着它转，一个一个数：上、下、前、后、左、右。正方体有 6 个面，都是一样大的正方形。骰子就是正方体的形状。

接着<Beat n={3}>一对一对地看长方体的面</Beat>。长方体也有 6 个面，但面是长方形。它们成 3 对：每个面在对面都有一个一样大的。颜色和 ●、▲、■ 三个记号告诉你哪两个面是一对。有的长方体有一对面是正方形，比如两端是正方形的高盒子。它的侧面是长方形，所以它还是长方体。

最后<Beat n={4}>找一找弯的面</Beat>。有的面不是平的。圆柱有 2 个圆形的<Term id="flat-face">平的面</Term>，中间用 1 个<Term id="curved-face">曲面</Term>连起来。圆锥有 1 个圆形的平的面和 1 个曲面，曲面越往上越窄，最后收成一个尖。球只有一个曲面，不管怎么放都会滚。舞台上，平的面和曲面用两种不同的颜色画。

常见的错误是看到盒子就叫正方体。叫名字以前先看面：正方体的 6 个面是一样大的正方形；侧面是长方形的盒子，是长方体。

准备好了，就<Task id="solids-and-faces">做一道小题</Task>：说出一个立体的名字，或者数一数它有几个面。

</Lang>
