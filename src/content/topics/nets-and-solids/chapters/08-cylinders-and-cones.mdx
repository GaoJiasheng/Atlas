<Lang en>

Recognise only: in this chapter you learn to know the nets of a cylinder and a cone when you see them. You do not need to draw them or test them. Every solid so far had only flat faces. These two have a curved face as well.

An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "一个曲面加两个平的圆：罐底，和旁边的盖子。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

Start with <Beat n={1}>a closed cylinder</Beat>. It has 2 circles and 1 curved face. Now imagine cutting it straight down its side, and most of the way round its two ends, then opening it out.

Watch <Beat n={2}>the cylinder unroll</Beat>. The curved face unrolls into a rectangle. The two circles stay joined to it, one on the top edge and one on the bottom edge. So a cylinder's net is 1 rectangle and 2 circles.

Now look at <Beat n={3}>the long side of the rectangle</Beat>. It wraps once round the circle. So it is exactly as long as the distance round the circle, no shorter. If it were shorter, the curved face could not close, and a gap would be left.

Next, <Beat n={4}>a cone opens out</Beat>. A cone opens into a circle and a shape like an open fan, not a triangle. This shape is called a sector. The fan's curved edge wraps once round the circle, and its point becomes the tip of the cone. Ice-cream cones can be made this way: <Num s="S28">baked flat, then rolled up</Num>.

Last, see <Beat n={5}>four nets side by side</Beat>. Now you can name a solid from its net. Circles mean a cylinder or a cone. Rectangles joining two equal ends mean a prism. Triangles meeting at a point mean a pyramid.

The usual mix-up is to think the curved face of a cone opens into a triangle. A triangle has straight sides, so it could not wrap round the circle. And a sphere has no net at all: no flat shape folds up into it.

When you are ready, <Task id="cylinders-and-cones">try one question</Task> on which net folds into a cylinder or a cone.

<Lang zh>

只要认得：这一章只要求你看到圆柱和圆锥的展开图时能认出来，不用画，也不用判断对不对。前面的立体只有平的面，这两种还有一个曲面。

<Figure src="figures/nets-and-solids-cylinder-faces" alt={{ en: "Pencil drawing of an empty metal tin standing upright with its top open, and its round lid standing on its edge beside it.", zh: "一个空铁皮罐头的铅笔画：罐头立着，上口敞开；圆形的盖子竖着立在它旁边。" }} caption={{ en: "A curved face and two flat circles: the bottom, and the lid beside it. An illustration made for this lesson.", zh: "一个曲面加两个平的圆：罐底，和旁边的盖子。为本课画的示意图。" }} />

先看<Beat n={1}>一个完整的圆柱</Beat>。它有 2 个圆和 1 个曲面。想象把圆柱的侧面竖着剪开一刀，两个圆形的底也沿着边剪开大半圈，然后摊开。

再看<Beat n={2}>圆柱摊开</Beat>。曲面摊开成一个长方形，两个圆还连在它上面：一个在上边，一个在下边。所以圆柱的展开图是 1 个长方形和 2 个圆。

接着看<Beat n={3}>长方形的长边</Beat>。长方形的长边正好绕圆一圈，所以它和圆一圈的长度一样长，短一点都不行。要是短了，曲面就合不拢，会留下一条缝。

然后看<Beat n={4}>圆锥打开</Beat>。圆锥摊开是一个圆和一个像打开的扇子的形状，不是三角形。这个形状叫作扇形。扇形那条弯的边正好绕圆一圈，它的尖就成了圆锥的尖。冰淇淋蛋筒就有这样做的：<Num s="S28">先烤成平的，再卷起来</Num>。

最后看<Beat n={5}>并排的四个展开图</Beat>。现在你能看展开图说出立体了：有圆的，是圆柱或圆锥；长方形连着两个一样的底的，是棱柱；三角形的尖碰在一起的，是棱锥。

常见的错误，是以为圆锥的曲面摊开是一个三角形。三角形的边是直的，绕不住那个圆。还有，球根本没有展开图：没有哪个平面图形能折成球。

准备好了，就<Task id="cylinders-and-cones">做一道小题</Task>：看看哪个展开图能折成圆柱或圆锥。
