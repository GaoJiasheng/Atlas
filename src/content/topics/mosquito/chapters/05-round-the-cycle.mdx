<Lang en>

Follow the dots round: egg, larva, pupa, adult, and back to eggs. NEA says an adult mosquito lives about <Num s="S4">two weeks</Num> in nature. In that time a female can lay eggs about <Num s="S4">three times</Num>, so the cycle keeps turning, and young of different ages are often found in the same water.

In one laboratory study of male Aedes aegypti, the trip from hatching to adult took about <Num s="S16">7.2 days</Num> in water at <Num s="S16">28 °C</Num>. The clock starts at hatching: it leaves out the egg, which can wait for water for months, so the ring card marks the egg “waits for water” instead of giving it days.

Now change only the water temperature. At <Num s="S16">20 °C</Num> the same trip took <Num s="S16">13.4 days</Num>; at <Num s="S16">32 °C</Num>, <Num s="S16">6.4 days</Num>. In the model the dots slow down or speed up to match. This is a fair test: only the temperature changes, while the food and the way the larvae are kept stay the same.

Record the days at each temperature and look at the graph. Across these temperatures, warmer water meant faster growth. Singapore is warm all year round. In this model, at <Num s="S1">28 °C</Num>, still water left standing for about a week is long enough for new adults to come out of it.

Different living things have different life cycles. A grasshopper has three stages, egg, nymph and adult, and its whole cycle takes months. A mosquito, like a butterfly, has four, because it has a pupa. Knowing how a cycle runs helps you predict what you will find, and when.

<More title={{ en: "A fair test", zh: "公平测试" }}>

In the study, each larva was kept alone in a small well of water and given the same food every day, so that the water temperature was the only thing that changed. That matters, because food and crowding also change how fast larvae grow. The model scales each stage's measured average so that the stages add up to the study's measured total from hatching to adult; the stage on each day is worked out from that, not watched in one mosquito.

</More>

<More title={{ en: "Not warmer and warmer for ever", zh: "不是越热越好" }}>

跟着小点绕一圈：卵、幼虫、蛹、成虫，再回到卵。环境局说，成蚊在野外大约活 <Num s="S4">两周</Num>。这段时间里，雌蚊大约能产 <Num s="S4">三次</Num>卵，所以这一圈不停地转下去，同一处积水里常常能找到大小不一的孑孓。

在一项雄性埃及伊蚊的实验室研究里，水温 <Num s="S16">28 °C</Num> 时，从孵化到成虫大约用了 <Num s="S16">7.2 天</Num>。这个钟从孵化开始算，没有把卵算进去，因为卵可以等水等上好几个月；所以环卡在卵那一段写的是“等水来”，不写天数。

现在只改变水温。水温 <Num s="S16">20 °C</Num> 时，同样一圈用了 <Num s="S16">13.4 天</Num>；<Num s="S16">32 °C</Num> 时，用了 <Num s="S16">6.4 天</Num>。模型里的小点也跟着变慢或变快。这是一个公平测试：只改变水温，食物和养孑孓的方法都保持不变。

把每个水温下的天数记下来，看看图。在这几个温度之间，水越暖，长得越快。新加坡全年温暖。在这个模型里，水温 <Num s="S1">28 °C</Num> 时，积水放上一个星期左右，就足够让新的成虫钻出来。

不同的生物有不同的生活史。蝗虫有三个阶段：卵、若虫、成虫，整个生活史要好几个月。蚊子和蝴蝶一样有四个阶段，因为多了蛹。知道一个生活史怎样运转，就能预测会看到什么、什么时候看到。

<More title={{ en: "A fair test", zh: "公平测试" }}>

在这项研究里，每只孑孓单独养在一小格水里，每天喂同样的饲料，所以唯一改变的是水温。这很重要，因为食物多少和拥挤程度也会改变孑孓长得多快。模型把每个阶段测得的平均天数按比例缩放，让各阶段加起来等于研究测得的孵化到成虫的总天数；每一天在哪个阶段，是这样算出来的，不是盯着某一只蚊子看出来的。
