<Lang en>

Look closely at the small group of frog eggs. Each visible jelly coat surrounds an <Term id="embryo">embryo</Term>, a developing young animal. Only part of a clutch is shown. The amount drawn is a choice for the display, not a claim about how many eggs every frog lays. The eggs and the next specimen are also enlarged separately.

In this pond-frog example, a young frog emerges as a <Term id="tadpole">tadpole</Term>. Coming out of an egg is called <Term id="hatching">hatching</Term>. The selected swimming tadpole has a rounded body and a long tail, with no visible legs. Its tail moves it through the water. The model is a still observation of this form, not a film of an egg hatching. <FlyTo preset="frog-overview">Follow the frog observations</FlyTo>

Compare the tadpole with the adult frog on the same tray. The tadpole does not look like a small copy of the adult. Even so, it is a young frog, not a fish that later changes into a frog. To identify its place in the life cycle, follow the sequence from the egg and onwards through the changing body forms.

Use the frog observation control to move the pointer from egg to tadpole, then back to inspect the egg again. The other specimens remain available for comparison. The control chooses a view; it does not advance a day counter. Development takes different amounts of time in different frog species and conditions, so there is no single frog timetable on this display.

<More title={{"en": "One frog pathway", "zh": "一种青蛙的发育路线"}}>

Many frogs have a free-living tadpole stage, but not all do. Some develop inside their eggs and emerge as froglets. The pond-type sequence here is an example to study, not a rule that every frog species must follow.

</More>

<More title={{"en": "No shared frog clock", "zh": "没有统一的青蛙时钟"}}>

Published accounts describe different species and environments. This model uses their observations of changing form without borrowing a seasonal calendar from another place. A stage label tells you what can be seen, rather than the animal’s exact age.

</More>

</Lang>

近看这一小团蛙卵。每个可见的胶质包被都围着一个<Term id="embryo">胚胎</Term>，也就是发育中的幼体。这里仅画出卵团的一小部分，画多少是展示的选择，不表示每只蛙都会产这么多卵。卵和旁边的幼体标本也分别放大，不能直接比较大小。

在这个池塘型青蛙的例子里，幼体从卵中出来时是<Term id="tadpole">蝌蚪</Term>。从卵中出来叫<Term id="hatching">孵出</Term>。所选的游泳蝌蚪身体圆，有长尾巴，还看不到腿，靠尾巴在水中游动。模型展示的是这个阶段静止的样子，没有播放一枚卵正在孵出的影片。<FlyTo preset="frog-overview">沿着青蛙的阶段观察</FlyTo>

再看同一展台上的成蛙。蝌蚪并不像一只缩小的成蛙，但它仍然是青蛙的幼体，不是一条以后才变成青蛙的鱼。不能只凭外形有些相像，就把它归成鱼。沿着卵和之后的身体变化，才能看清它在生活史中的位置。

试着用观察控件把指示珠从卵拨到蝌蚪，再回头细看卵。其他标本仍留在展台上，方便对照。控件只选择视图，不把日历往前拨。不同蛙种、不同条件下，发育所需的时间并不相同，因此展台没有给所有青蛙共用的天数表。
