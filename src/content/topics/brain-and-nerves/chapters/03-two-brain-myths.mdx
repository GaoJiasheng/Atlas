<Lang en>

You may have heard that people use only ten per cent of their brains. It is not true. A neurologist asked about it answered that we use virtually every part of the brain <Num s="S17"></Num>. Different tasks use different areas: seeing uses the back of the brain, hearing the sides, moving the frontal lobes and the cerebellum, and the brainstem works all the time to keep you breathing <Num s="S8, S9"></Num>. The signals on the stage come and go in many parts at once.

The idea is widespread. In a survey in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands, <Num s="S18">48 %</Num> and <Num s="S18">46 %</Num> of teachers believed it. Nobody knows exactly where it came from <Num s="S17"></Num>.

The second idea sorts people into left-brained, logical people and right-brained, creative ones. A study looked at brain scans of <Num s="S19">1,011 people</Num> aged 7 to 29 and found that the data do not fit a whole brain that is more left-sided or more right-sided <Num s="S19"></Num>. In the same teacher survey, <Num s="S18">91 %</Num> in the UK and <Num s="S18">86 %</Num> in the Netherlands believed that left-brain or right-brain dominance explains differences between learners.

What is true is that some abilities rely more on one side. In about <Num s="S14">96 %</Num> of people, language relies mainly on the left half, where an area in the frontal lobe turns language into the movements of speech <Num s="S14"></Num>. 这种说法流传很广。在英国和荷兰的一项调查里，分别有 <Num s="S18">48%</Num> 和 <Num s="S18">46%</Num> 的教师相信它。没有人确切知道它是从哪里来的 <Num s="S17"></Num>。
